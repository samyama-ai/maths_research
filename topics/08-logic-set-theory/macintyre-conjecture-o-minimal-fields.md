# Macintyre's Conjecture on O-minimal Fields

> **Topic:** Mathematical Logic & Set Theory · **ID:** `08-logic-set-theory/macintyre-conjecture-o-minimal-fields` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Angus Macintyre's conjecture (developed with A. J. Wilkie) asserts that the naturally occurring o-minimal expansions of the real field have **decidable** first-order theories. Its sharpest instance:

> **Conjecture (Macintyre).** The first-order theory $\mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{R}_{\exp})$ of the real exponential field $\mathbb{R}_{\exp} = (\mathbb{R}, <, +, \cdot, -, 0, 1, \exp)$ is decidable: there is an algorithm that, given a sentence $\sigma$ in the language $L_{\exp} = \{<,+,\cdot,-,0,1,\exp\}$, outputs whether $\mathbb{R}_{\exp} \models \sigma$.

Broader form: every o-minimal expansion of $\overline{\mathbb{R}} = (\mathbb{R},<,+,\cdot)$ by a "tame" family of analytic functions (restricted analytic functions, exponentiation, real powers, Pfaffian functions) is decidable, and more strongly is *effectively o-minimal* — the cell-decomposition data are computable from the defining data.

A complete solution requires either (i) an explicit algorithm with a correctness proof that does not assume an unproved transcendence hypothesis, or (ii) a proof of undecidability, e.g. an interpretation of arithmetic or of a known undecidable set in $\mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{R}_{\exp})$. The conjecture is *conditionally settled*: Macintyre–Wilkie (1996) proved decidability assuming Schanuel's conjecture. The open content is the removal of that hypothesis.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**O-minimality.** A structure $\mathcal{M} = (M,<,\dots)$ expanding a dense linear order without endpoints is *o-minimal* if every definable (with parameters) subset of $M$ is a finite union of points and open intervals $(a,b)$ with $a,b \in M \cup \{\pm\infty\}$. By Pillay–Steinhorn, o-minimality is preserved under elementary equivalence for structures expanding an ordered field, and every o-minimal ordered field is real closed. The Cell Decomposition Theorem gives: every definable $X \subseteq M^n$ is a finite disjoint union of cells, and every definable $f$ is piecewise continuous (indeed $C^k$).

**The exponential field.** Wilkie's theorem: $\mathbb{R}_{\exp}$ is model complete and o-minimal. Model completeness takes the concrete form that every $L_{\exp}$-definable set is a projection of a zero set of an exponential polynomial: for definable $X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^m$ there are $n\ge m$ and $f \in E_n := \mathbb{Z}[x_1,\dots,x_n,e^{x_1},\dots,e^{x_n}]$ with
$$X = \{\,\bar{a}\in\mathbb{R}^m : \exists\, \bar{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-m}\ f(\bar a,\bar b)=0\,\}.$$
Formally $E_n$ is the ring of *exponential polynomials*, generated from $\mathbb{Z}[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ by closing under composition with $\exp$.

**Khovanskii finiteness.** For $f_1,\dots,f_n$ in the Pfaffian/exponential class of complexity (degree $d$, format $\le r$), the number of nondegenerate real solutions of $f_1=\cdots=f_n=0$ is bounded by an explicit function of $(n,d,r)$ alone — no transcendence input. This is the *geometric* half of decidability: the solution count is effectively bounded.

**Schanuel's conjecture.** If $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n \in \mathbb{C}$ are $\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent, then
$$\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}\big(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n, e^{\alpha_1},\dots,e^{\alpha_n}\big) \;\ge\; n .$$
Its role is the *arithmetic* half: it supplies a decision procedure for the **constant problem** — given $\alpha$ presented as an exponential-algebraic expression, decide whether $\alpha = 0$ — by bounding the transcendence degree of the field generated by a putative solution.

**Macintyre–Wilkie theorem.** If (the real case of) Schanuel's conjecture holds, then $\mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{R}_{\exp})$ is decidable. The proof combines Wilkie's model completeness (reducing sentences to $\exists$-statements about exponential polynomials), Khovanskii bounds (finitely many, uniformly bounded, solution components), and a Schanuel-driven zero-recognition procedure for the coordinates of nondegenerate solutions.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1951** — Tarski proves $\mathrm{Th}(\overline{\mathbb{R}})$ admits quantifier elimination and is decidable, and asks explicitly whether $\mathbb{R}_{\exp}$ is decidable ("Tarski's exponential function problem").
- **1968** — Richardson: the analogous problem for expressions involving $\pi$, $\exp$ and $\sin$ (unrestricted) is undecidable, marking the boundary of what can be hoped for.
- **1980s** — Khovanskii's fewnomial theory supplies uniform finiteness for exponential/Pfaffian systems; van den Dries proposes o-minimality as the right tameness framework; Pillay–Steinhorn and Knight–Pillay–Steinhorn found the theory.
- **1988** — Denef–van den Dries prove $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an}}$ (restricted analytic functions) admits quantifier elimination in the language with division, and its theory is decidable.
- **1991–1996** — Wilkie proves model completeness and o-minimality of $\mathbb{R}_{\exp}$; Macintyre–Wilkie derive decidability from Schanuel. This is the current SOTA: the conjecture is a theorem modulo one transcendence hypothesis.
- **1994** — van den Dries–Macintyre–Marker axiomatize $\mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\exp})$ via LE-series; van den Dries–Miller establish its o-minimality and quantifier elimination.
- **1996–1999** — Miller's growth dichotomy (every o-minimal expansion of an ordered field is polynomially bounded or defines $\exp$); Speissegger's Pfaffian closure gives large new o-minimal structures with no known decidability.
- **2004–2016** — Berarducci–Servi give an effective version of Wilkie's theorem of the complement; Servi extends conditional decidability to $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\exp}$; Macintyre's *Turing meets Schanuel* (2016) reframes the programme in computability terms.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Structure | Status |
|---|---|
| $\overline{\mathbb{R}} = (\mathbb{R},<,+,\cdot)$ | Decidable, unconditionally (Tarski 1951); doubly exponential complexity lower bound (Davenport–Heintz 1988) |
| $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an}}$ (restricted analytic, with computable coefficient data) | Decidable relative to the coefficient field; QE by Denef–van den Dries 1988 |
| $\mathbb{R}_{\exp}$ | Decidable **assuming Schanuel** (Macintyre–Wilkie 1996); the weaker "real Schanuel" suffices |
| $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\exp}$ | Decidable assuming Schanuel plus effectivity of the analytic data (Servi 2008) |
| $\mathbb{R}_{x^{\lambda}}$, $\lambda$ a *generic* real power | Decidable (Jones–Servi 2011) — unconditional for generic $\lambda$ |
| $\exists$-theory of $\mathbb{R}_{\exp}$ in one variable, $f(x,e^x)=0$, $f\in\mathbb{Z}[X,Y]$ | Root counting effective by Khovanskii/Rolle; sign-determination reduces to the constant problem |
| Pfaffian closure $\mathcal{P}(\overline{\mathbb{R}})$ | O-minimal (Speissegger 1999); decidability entirely open |

Negative/limiting results: unrestricted $\sin$ makes the structure non-o-minimal and the theory undecidable (it defines $\mathbb{Z}$); Richardson's undecidability applies to expression classes containing $\pi$ and $\sin$ on all of $\mathbb{R}$. Bianconi's nondefinability results ($\exp$ not definable in $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an}}$; restricted $\sin$ not definable in $\mathbb{R}_{\exp}$) show the hierarchy is strict.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **The constant problem is a transcendence problem.** Deciding whether a given exponential-algebraic real equals $0$ is not a geometric question. No unconditional effective lower bound is known for $|\alpha|$ when $\alpha \ne 0$ is an exponential polynomial evaluated at algebraic or exponential-algebraic points. Baker-type linear forms in logarithms handle $\sum \beta_i \log \alpha_i$ but not iterated exponentials or the full nonlinear systems produced by Wilkie's reduction.
- **Model completeness is non-uniform in the wrong way.** Wilkie's proof yields, for each formula, *some* exponential polynomial witness, but the passage from formula to witness in his original argument is not manifestly computable; Berarducci–Servi's effective theorem of the complement fixes this in Pfaffian settings but not in full generality for $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\exp}$.
- **Khovanskii bounds count but do not locate.** Fewnomial theory bounds the number of nondegenerate solutions independently of coefficients, but degenerate (tangential) intersections — exactly the cases where a sign is $0$ — are invisible to it. Perturbing away degeneracy requires knowing that a constant is nonzero: circular.
- **No interpretation route to undecidability.** O-minimality itself forbids defining $\mathbb{Z}$, so the standard undecidability method (interpret arithmetic) is blocked by the very tameness that makes the structure interesting. Any disproof must come from a genuinely new source of undecidability, such as coding a halting problem into transcendence data.
- **Schanuel is far out of reach.** It implies Lindemann–Weierstrass, the algebraic independence of $e$ and $\pi$, and much of open transcendence theory; even the case $n=2$ (e.g. algebraic independence of $\{\log 2,\log 3\}$-type data) is open.

## 6. The Gap

Proven (Section 4): $\mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{R}_{\exp})$ is decidable *given* an oracle deciding, for exponential polynomials $f \in E_n$ and points $\bar{a}$ arising as nondegenerate zeros of triangular exponential systems, whether $f(\bar a)=0$. Conjectured (Section 1): decidability outright.

The exact missing step is an **unconditional effective zero-recognition theorem** for the exponential ring: a computable function $B(n,d,H)$ such that for every nonzero $f \in E_n$ of degree $\le d$, height $\le H$, and every real solution $\bar a$ of a system of exponential polynomials of that complexity,
$$f(\bar a) \ne 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; |f(\bar a)| > B(n,d,H)^{-1}.$$
Schanuel yields this by a transcendence-degree count. Nothing weaker is currently known to yield it, and nothing is known to make it fail. Equivalently: separate the geometry (settled: Wilkie, Khovanskii) from the arithmetic (open: transcendence).

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Computability-theoretic reframing.** Macintyre's *Turing meets Schanuel* programme measures exactly which fragment of Schanuel is needed, and studies the degree-theoretic strength of $\mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{R}_{\exp})$ — e.g. whether it is Turing-reducible to a natural arithmetic set. Work at Queen Mary, London.
- **Exponential algebraic closedness (EAC) / Zilber pseudo-exponentiation.** Kirby, Bays, Gallinaro and collaborators (Manchester, East Anglia, Freiburg) prove unconditional cases of the Zilber "exponential-algebraic closedness" axiom for systems of exponential-polynomial equations, including free and raising-to-powers varieties; these results substitute for parts of Schanuel in solution-existence arguments *(frontier — verify current scope)*.
- **Effective o-minimality.** Continuation of Berarducci–Servi: effective cell decomposition and explicit bounds for Pfaffian and $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\exp}$-definable sets (Servi, Jones, Rolin, Speissegger).
- **Point-counting inputs.** Pila–Wilkie counting and its effective refinements (Binyamini–Novikov's sharply-o-minimal structures) supply effective bounds on rational points in definable sets; whether sharp o-minimality can be leveraged into an unconditional decision procedure is actively discussed *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Complexity side.** Even granting Schanuel, the Macintyre–Wilkie procedure has no elementary complexity bound; quantifying it is an open subproblem.

## 8. Future Work

- Isolate a **weakest sufficient hypothesis**: identify a statement strictly weaker than Schanuel (e.g. a "quantitative Ax–Schanuel" for real solutions) that still yields the constant-problem oracle, then attack that statement with transcendence methods.
- Prove **unconditional decidability of the existential theory** of $\mathbb{R}_{\exp}$, or even of the one-variable fragment $\exists x\, f(x,e^x)=0$, in full generality with algebraic coefficients.
- Transfer EAC results from the complex to the real setting, exploiting o-minimality and Khovanskii bounds to make existence statements effective.
- Develop **effective Ax–Schanuel / differential** analogues: Ax's theorem is the function-field Schanuel and is unconditional; find the real-analytic reduction that lets it substitute at the level of definable germs (Hardy fields, transseries).
- Settle decidability for the **Pfaffian closure** $\mathcal{P}(\overline{\mathbb{R}})$ and for $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\exp}$ conditionally-free of analytic-coefficient assumptions.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take the one-variable fragment: decide sentences $\sigma_f : \exists x \in \mathbb{R}\ \big(f(x,e^x) = 0\big)$ for $f \in \mathbb{Z}[X,Y]$.

**Step 1 — geometry is effective.** Let $f(X,Y) = Y - X - 1$, so $g(x) = e^x - x - 1$. Then $g'(x) = e^x - 1$ has exactly one real zero, at $x=0$. By Rolle, $g$ has at most two real zeros. Direct check: $g(0)=0$, $g''(0)=1>0$, and $g>0$ elsewhere, so $x=0$ is the unique zero and $\sigma_f$ is **true**. Khovanskii's theory generalizes precisely this Rolle-type count: for $f$ of degree $d$, the number of real zeros of $f(x,e^x)$ is bounded by an explicit function of $d$ alone — for one variable, at most $\binom{d+1}{2}$-type bounds — computed with no transcendence input.

**Step 2 — arithmetic is not.** Now perturb: let
$$g_c(x) = e^{x} - x - 1 - c, \qquad c = 2e^{\alpha} - e^{\alpha+\log 2},$$
where $\alpha$ is any real presented by an exponential-algebraic expression. Since $\min_{x} (e^x - x - 1) = 0$ attained at $x=0$:
$$\sigma_{g_c} \text{ holds} \iff c \ge 0 .$$
Here $c = 2e^{\alpha} - 2e^{\alpha} = 0$ by the exponential law, so $\sigma_{g_c}$ is true — but only because we recognized the identity. Replace $c$ by a numerically indistinguishable expression such as
$$c' = e^{\beta_1} - e^{\beta_2}, \qquad \beta_1,\beta_2 \text{ algebraic},$$
and deciding $\sigma_{g_{c'}}$ is exactly deciding whether $\beta_1 = \beta_2$ *as exponents* — i.e. whether $e^{\beta_1 - \beta_2} = 1$. For $\beta_1 - \beta_2 \ne 0$ algebraic this is Lindemann's theorem, so this instance is decidable. Iterate the construction one level — take $\beta_i$ themselves exponential-algebraic, e.g. $c'' = e^{e^{2}-e\cdot e} - 1$ — and no unconditional theorem tells us how small $|c''|$ can be when nonzero. Schanuel's conjecture supplies the transcendence-degree count that certifies $c'' = 0$ or bounds it away from $0$.

**Conclusion of the example.** The decision procedure has two halves. The geometric half (how many solutions, in which cells) is settled unconditionally by Wilkie plus Khovanskii. The arithmetic half (is this particular exponential constant zero) is the entire open content of Macintyre's conjecture, and $\varepsilon$-close approximation never resolves it: the difference between a tangency and a near-miss is invisible to any finite numerical computation.
