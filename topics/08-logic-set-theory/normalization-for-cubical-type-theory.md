# Normalization for Cubical Type Theory

> **Topic:** Mathematical Logic & Set Theory · **ID:** `08-logic-set-theory/normalization-for-cubical-type-theory` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

**Conjecture (Normalization).** For every cubical type theory $\mathbb{T}$ in the design space of CCHM (De Morgan), Cartesian (ABCFHL), and their extensions by higher inductive types and a cumulative hierarchy of univalent universes, there is a computable function
$$\mathsf{nf} : \{(\Gamma, A, M) \mid \Gamma \vdash M : A\} \longrightarrow \mathsf{Nf}$$
such that (i) *soundness*: $\Gamma \vdash \mathsf{nf}(\Gamma,A,M) = M : A$; (ii) *completeness*: $\Gamma \vdash M = N : A$ implies $\mathsf{nf}(\Gamma,A,M) \equiv \mathsf{nf}(\Gamma,A,N)$ syntactically; (iii) normal forms are closed under the derivable judgemental structure. Consequences: definitional equality is decidable, type checking is decidable, and the theory is consistent with an injectivity-of-type-constructors property.

A complete solution must exhibit $\mathsf{nf}$ (or a normalization proof from which it is extracted) **for the theory as implemented**, i.e. including at minimum $\Pi$, $\Sigma$, path/interval types, `Glue`/`V` types, Kan composition and coercion, a univalent universe hierarchy, and the standard higher inductive types (spheres, pushouts, truncations, quotients). A disproof would exhibit a well-typed term whose conversion problem is undecidable, or an infinite reduction/failure of the normal-form invariant.

**Status.** Solved for Cartesian cubical type theory *without* higher inductive types (Sterling–Angiuli, LICS 2021). Open for CCHM/De Morgan cubical type theory and for any cubical theory with HITs — which is exactly what Cubical Agda and `redtt`/`cooltt` implement.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Interval and cofibrations.** Cubical type theories add a sort $\mathbb{I}$ of *dimensions*. In CCHM, $\mathbb{I}$ is the free De Morgan algebra: $0,1,\ i\wedge j,\ i\vee j,\ 1-i$, with $1-(i\wedge j) = (1-i)\vee(1-j)$. In the Cartesian theory $\mathbb{I}$ is the free structure on $0,1$ and variables only (substitutions are all maps $\{0,1,i_1,\dots\}$, hence "Cartesian cube category" $\square_{\mathrm{cart}}$). A *cofibration* $\varphi$ is a formula in the lattice generated by $(r = s)$ for $r,s : \mathbb{I}$; cofibrations classify a subobject $\Phi \hookrightarrow \Omega$ in the presheaf topos $\widehat{\square}$.

**Kan structure.** Cartesian cubical type theory takes two primitives. *Coercion*:
$$\frac{\Gamma, i:\mathbb{I} \vdash A \quad \Gamma \vdash M : A[r/i]}{\Gamma \vdash \mathsf{coe}^{r\rightsquigarrow s}_{i.A}(M) : A[s/i]}, \qquad \mathsf{coe}^{r\rightsquigarrow r}_{i.A}(M) = M .$$
*Homogeneous composition*: for $\varphi$ a cofibration and a partial tube $\varphi \vdash j.N$ agreeing with $M$ at $j = r$,
$$\Gamma \vdash \mathsf{hcom}^{r\rightsquigarrow s}_{A}[\varphi \mapsto j.N](M) : A, \qquad \mathsf{hcom}^{r\rightsquigarrow s}_A[\varphi\mapsto j.N](M) \;=\; N[s/j] \ \ (\text{when } \varphi),$$
plus $\mathsf{hcom}^{r\rightsquigarrow r} = M$. CCHM instead uses a single heterogeneous $\mathsf{comp}^i_A[\varphi\mapsto u]\,u_0$ derived from connections.

**Univalence.** `Glue` types attach an equivalence along a cofibration:
$$\Gamma \vdash \mathsf{Glue}\,[\varphi \mapsto (T, e)]\,A \ \mathsf{type}, \qquad \mathsf{Glue}[\varphi\mapsto(T,e)]A \;=\; T \ \ (\text{when }\varphi), \quad e : T \simeq A .$$
Univalence follows because the universe is Kan-fibrant via `Glue`.

**Normalization by evaluation, synthetic form.** The proof technique is *synthetic Tait computability* (Sterling). Let $\mathcal{S}$ be the syntactic category (a category with families of the theory) and $\mathcal{R}$ a category of renamings/atomic contexts. Normalization is realized in the *Artin gluing* topos
$$\mathcal{G} \;=\; \widehat{\mathcal{R}} \times_{\widehat{\mathcal{S}}} \mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{op}}\text{-sets} \;\cong\; \mathbf{Gl}\big(\widehat{\mathcal{R}} \xleftarrow{\;\rho^*\;} \mathcal{S}\big),$$
a topos carrying a subterminal $\mathsf{syn} \in \Omega_{\mathcal{G}}$ with open modality $\bigcirc X = X^{\mathsf{syn}}$ (the "syntactic" fragment) and closed modality $\bullet X = X \sqcup_{X\times \mathsf{syn}} \mathsf{syn}$ (the "semantic" fragment). A computability structure on a type $A$ is a $\bigcirc$-connected refinement $\Vdash A \twoheadrightarrow \bigcirc A$ together with reflect/reify maps
$$\mathsf{Ne}(A) \to\ \Vdash A\ \to \mathsf{Nf}(A),$$
and normalization is the statement that the canonical section exists at every type. The key extra axiom in the cubical setting is **boundary separation**: for cofibration $\varphi$, if $M, N : A$ satisfy $\varphi \vdash M = N$ and the "frontier" $\partial$ of the neutral is covered, then $M = N$ — formalized as a *strictness/realignment* axiom
$$\big(\varphi \to A\big) \times_{(\varphi\to B)} B \;\cong\; A' \quad\text{with } A'|_\varphi \equiv A,$$
i.e. the topos supports strict gluing along cofibrations (Orton–Pitts axioms; Licata–Orton–Pitts–Spitters internal universes).

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **2013–2014.** Bezem–Coquand–Huber give the first cubical set model of univalence; Licata–Harper and Angiuli–Harper develop computational (semantic) cubical type theories.
- **2015–2018.** CCHM cubical type theory (Cohen–Coquand–Huber–Mörtberg) publishes a constructive interpretation of univalence with a De Morgan interval. Huber proves **canonicity**: every closed term of type $\mathbb{N}$ evaluates to a numeral (JAR 2019). Canonicity is strictly weaker than normalization — it says nothing about open terms or decidability.
- **2018–2021.** Cartesian cubical type theory (Angiuli–Brunerie–Coquand–Harper–Hou(Favonia)–Licata, MSCS 2021) replaces connections by $\mathsf{coe}$ with arbitrary endpoints. Cavallo–Harper (POPL 2019) give computational HITs. Coquand–Huber–Sattler (LMCS 2022) prove canonicity and homotopy canonicity via sconing.
- **2021 (milestone).** **Sterling–Angiuli, "Normalization for Cubical Type Theory" (LICS 2021)** prove normalization, and hence decidability of conversion and type checking, for Cartesian cubical type theory with $\Pi,\Sigma$, path types, `Glue`/`V`, a univalent universe, booleans/naturals — but **no** higher inductive types beyond the basic ones and no infinite universe hierarchy in the published statement. Sterling's thesis (CMU, 2021) develops the synthetic Tait computability machinery in full.
- **2022–present.** The technique is exported: Sterling–Angiuli–Gratzer normalize XTT (a cubical language for Bishop sets); Gratzer normalizes multimodal type theory (LICS 2022). Implementations (Cubical Agda, `cooltt`) rely on conversion checkers whose termination is *conjectural* for the full feature set.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Theory / fragment | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Cartesian cubical TT, $\Pi,\Sigma,\mathsf{Path}$, `Glue`, one univalent universe $\mathcal{U}$, $\mathbb{B}$, $\mathbb{N}$ | **Normalization**, decidable conversion & type checking | Sterling–Angiuli 2021 |
| XTT (cubical theory of Bishop sets, UIP, no univalence) | Normalization + decidability | Sterling–Angiuli–Gratzer, LMCS 2022 |
| CCHM cubical TT, closed terms of type $\mathbb{N}$ | **Canonicity** only | Huber, JAR 2019 |
| CCHM + HITs, closed terms | Canonicity, homotopy canonicity | Coquand–Huber–Sattler, LMCS 2022 |
| MLTT (no interval) + $\eta$, universes, inductive types | Normalization, decidability, machine-checked | Abel–Öhman–Vezzosi, POPL 2018 |
| MTT (multimodal, any mode theory) | Normalization | Gratzer, LICS 2022 |
| $\mathsf{coe}$/$\mathsf{hcom}$ on strictly positive HITs in *computational* (untyped-semantics) cubical TT | Canonicity via computability, not normalization | Cavallo–Harper, POPL 2019 |

Concretely: for the fragment covered in 2021 the algorithm decides equations such as $\mathsf{ua}(\mathsf{not}) \circ \mathsf{ua}(\mathsf{not}) \stackrel{?}{=} \mathsf{refl}$-transport at $\mathbb{B}$ in finite time; for $S^1$, the torus, or $\|A\|_n$ no such theorem exists.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

1. **Neutrals are unstable under dimension substitution.** In ordinary MLTT, a neutral term stays neutral under any substitution of *term* variables. In cubical type theory, $\mathsf{hcom}^{0\rightsquigarrow 1}_A[\,i=0\mapsto j.N_0,\ i=1\mapsto j.N_1\,](M)$ is neutral in context $i:\mathbb{I}$ but *reduces* under $i \mapsto 0$. So the standard logical-relations recipe "interpret neutrals as computable at every type" breaks: the family of computability predicates is not stable under the base-category maps. Sterling–Angiuli's fix is to index neutrals by the cofibration on which they are *un*defined, using the closed modality $\bullet$ to make "neutral outside $\varphi$" a first-class notion — machinery that has to be re-engineered for every new connective.
2. **De Morgan connections.** With $i \wedge j$, $i\vee j$, $1-i$, the interval has non-trivial equational theory and $\mathbb{I}$-substitution is not a mere renaming. The atomic/renaming category $\mathcal{R}$ used in the gluing has to be replaced by something for which $\rho^*$ is still nice; no such category is known to work for CCHM. This is why the 2021 theorem is Cartesian-only.
3. **Higher inductive types.** For $S^1$, $\mathsf{hcom}$ terms are *constructors*, not eliminable redexes; the normal forms of $S^1$ include unbounded towers $\mathsf{hcom}[\dots \mathsf{hcom}[\dots]]$ whose equality is governed by the (non-confluent-looking) tube equations. Defining a well-founded computability structure on such an inductively-generated-with-Kan-structure type requires an induction principle over "Kan-freely-generated" objects that has not been carried out.
4. **Regularity.** The equation $\mathsf{coe}^{r\rightsquigarrow s}_{i.A}(M) = M$ for $A$ not mentioning $i$ is desirable for normalization (it kills junk normal forms) but is known to be inconsistent with the standard universe Kan structure in the De Morgan setting (Swan's counterexamples on regularity in the CCHM cubical set model); dropping it inflates normal forms.
5. **Universe hierarchy.** Universe-level polymorphism interacts with `Glue` and with the $\bigcirc/\bullet$ modal decomposition; the gluing construction is done in a topos whose internal universe must be strictly aligned along $\mathsf{syn}$, and iterating this for $\mathcal{U}_0 : \mathcal{U}_1 : \cdots$ needs realignment at every level.

## 6. The Gap

The proven statement (§4) is: *Cartesian* interval, *no* HITs, *one* universe. The conjectured statement (§1) is: *any* interval algebra with a cofibration classifier, *all* strictly positive HITs, *cumulative hierarchy*. The three gaps are independent:

- **Gap A (interval).** Produce a category of "atomic" contexts $\mathcal{R}$ for the De Morgan cube category such that (i) $\mathcal{R}\hookrightarrow \mathcal{S}$ has the normal-form-stability property, and (ii) the gluing topos satisfies the boundary-separation and realignment axioms. Equivalently: show De Morgan neutrals admit a *finite* frontier decomposition.
- **Gap B (HITs).** Extend the computability structure from "types whose elements are generated by term constructors" to "types freely generated by constructors *and* $\mathsf{hcom}$", giving a normal-form grammar for $S^1$, pushouts and truncations, and prove it closed under the eliminators' $\beta$/coherence rules.
- **Gap C (hierarchy).** Iterate the strict-realignment universe construction to $\omega$ levels with cumulativity.

Crossing A alone would give decidability for Cubical Agda's core minus HITs; crossing B alone gives it for `cooltt`. Both A and B are needed for the tool most users run.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **CMU / Aarhus / Chalmers–Gothenburg axis.** Sterling, Gratzer, Angiuli and collaborators continue to push synthetic Tait computability as a general normalization technology (multimodal type theory, guarded and modal cubical variants). Extensions to HITs are stated as the principal open target in Sterling's thesis.
- **Stockholm/Chalmers (Mörtberg, Coquand, Vezzosi).** Focus on models (Cavallo–Mörtberg–Swan's unifying framework for cubical models) and on implementation-level conversion algorithms for Cubical Agda; decidability there remains empirical rather than proven.
- **Equivariant / "cartesian-with-symmetries" cubical sets.** Work on equivariant Kan fibrations aimed at giving cubical models that present spaces correctly; whether these variants normalize is untouched. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Machine-checked metatheory.** Efforts to formalize normalization proofs for dependent type theory in a proof assistant (following Abel–Öhman–Vezzosi's Agda formalization of MLTT) have not yet reached the cubical case. *(frontier — verify)*
- Reported progress on normal forms for cubical theories with a single one-dimensional HIT (the circle) via "Kan-normal" grammars is circulating in preprint form. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Future Work

1. **HIT normal forms via free Kan completion.** Present $S^1$ as the free Kan-fibrant object on a point and a loop, and define computability by induction on the free-completion rank; the technical prerequisite is a well-founded rank on $\mathsf{hcom}$-towers modulo the tube equations.
2. **De Morgan via a change of base.** Translate CCHM into a Cartesian theory conservatively (connections as derived operations) and transport normalization along the translation. Conservativity of such a translation is itself open.
3. **Regularity-restoring type theories.** Investigate cubical theories with regular coercion for a large sub-universe (e.g. a universe of Bishop sets, as in XTT) where normalization is already known, and glue them into the univalent theory.
4. **Decidability without normalization.** Prove decidability of conversion directly by a terminating bidirectional algorithm plus a semantic completeness argument, sidestepping normal-form syntax.
5. **Negative results.** Search for a cubical theory with a plausible feature set whose conversion problem is undecidable — this would sharply delimit the design space.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Instability of neutrals under dimension substitution.** Work in Cartesian cubical type theory. Let $a : \mathbb{B}$ be a variable and put, in context $\Gamma = (a:\mathbb{B},\, i:\mathbb{I})$,
$$M \;:=\; \mathsf{hcom}^{0\rightsquigarrow 1}_{\mathbb{B}}\big[\,i=0 \mapsto j.\,a,\ \ i=1 \mapsto j.\,a\,\big](a).$$
The tube is well-formed: on $i=0$ and $i=1$ the two faces are the same term $a$, and at $j=0$ both agree with the cap $a$.

*Step 1 — $M$ is not a redex in $\Gamma$.* The cofibration $\varphi = (i=0)\vee(i=1)$ is not entailed by $\Gamma$ (the dimension variable $i$ is free), and $0 \neq 1$, so neither the tube rule nor the degeneracy rule $\mathsf{hcom}^{r\rightsquigarrow r} = \mathrm{cap}$ applies. Since $\mathbb{B}$ has no regularity rule, $M$ is *not* definitionally equal to $a$. Hence
$$\Gamma \vdash \mathsf{nf}(M) \;\equiv\; \mathsf{hcom}^{0\rightsquigarrow 1}_{\mathbb{B}}[\,i=0\mapsto j.a,\ i=1\mapsto j.a\,](a),$$
a normal form built from a *stuck* $\mathsf{hcom}$.

*Step 2 — restriction destroys neutrality.* Apply the dimension substitution $\sigma = [0/i]$:
$$M\sigma \;=\; \mathsf{hcom}^{0\rightsquigarrow 1}_{\mathbb{B}}[\,0=0\mapsto j.a,\ 0=1\mapsto j.a\,](a) \;=\; (j.a)[1/j] \;=\; a,$$
because the tube rule fires once $\varphi$ holds. So $\mathsf{nf}(M)\sigma \not\equiv \mathsf{nf}(M\sigma)$: the syntactic normal form is *not* stable under $\sigma$. In MLTT the analogous failure cannot happen — a neutral $\mathsf{f}\,x$ remains neutral under any renaming.

*Step 3 — what the fix costs.* Sterling–Angiuli repair this by indexing the computability predicate for $\mathbb{B}$ not by a context but by a pair $(\Gamma,\varphi)$ where $\varphi$ is the *frontier*: the cofibration on which the term ceases to be neutral. Here $\varphi = (i=0)\vee(i=1)$. The predicate becomes
$$\Vdash_{\varphi} \mathbb{B} \;=\; \text{(a $\bullet_\varphi$-modal type)} \quad\text{with}\quad \big(\Vdash_\varphi \mathbb{B}\big)\big|_\varphi \;\equiv\; \Vdash \mathbb{B}\ \text{(already-computed value)},$$
and the strict realignment axiom guarantees this restriction is an *equality*, not an isomorphism. Boundary separation then yields: two elements of $\Vdash_\varphi \mathbb{B}$ agreeing on $\varphi$ and on the neutral part are equal — which is exactly what makes $\mathsf{nf}$ well-defined.

*Step 4 — why HITs break the pattern.* Replace $\mathbb{B}$ by $S^1$ and take
$$M_n := \mathsf{hcom}^{0\rightsquigarrow 1}_{S^1}[\,i=0\mapsto j.\mathsf{base},\ i=1\mapsto j.\mathsf{loop}\,j\,](\mathsf{base}).$$
Here $M_1$ is not stuck-because-of-a-variable; it is a genuine *constructor* of $S^1$'s Kan structure, and $\mathsf{hcom}$ terms nest to arbitrary depth, $M, \mathsf{hcom}[\ldots M \ldots], \dots$, with equalities generated by the tube and cap rules. The frontier trick of Step 3 gives no induction measure on this tower, and no well-founded computability structure for $S^1$ is currently known. That is Gap B of §6 in one term.
