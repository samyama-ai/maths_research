# I3 Cardinals Existence

> **Topic:** Mathematical Logic & Set Theory · **ID:** `08-logic-set-theory/i3-cardinals-existence` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

The axiom **I3** asserts:

> There exist an ordinal $\lambda$ and a nontrivial elementary embedding $j : V_\lambda \prec V_\lambda$.

"Nontrivial" means $j \neq \mathrm{id}$; then $j$ has a least moved ordinal $\kappa = \mathrm{crit}(j) < \lambda$, and $\lambda$ is forced to be $\sup_n \kappa_n$ where $\kappa_0 = \kappa$, $\kappa_{n+1} = j(\kappa_n)$. A cardinal $\kappa$ is an **I3 cardinal** if it is the critical point of such an embedding.

The open problem has three linked components:

1. **Consistency.** Is $\mathrm{ZFC} + \mathrm{I3}$ consistent? No proof of $\mathrm{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC}) \Rightarrow \mathrm{Con}(\mathrm{ZFC}+\mathrm{I3})$ is possible (Gödel), so the question is whether a contradiction can be derived — as Kunen (1971) derived one two ranks higher.
2. **Placement.** Is I3 strictly weaker than I2, I1, I0? Each implication $\mathrm{I0}\Rightarrow\mathrm{I1}\Rightarrow\mathrm{I2}\Rightarrow\mathrm{I3}$ is known; strictness in consistency strength is open at every step except partial separations.
3. **Structure.** What does $V_\lambda$ look like under I3, and which of its consequences (e.g. combinatorial facts about Laver tables) are already theorems of ZFC?

A disproof would be a ZFC derivation of a contradiction from a nontrivial $j:V_\lambda\prec V_\lambda$. A "proof" is impossible in the usual sense; the achievable positive result is a robust inner-model or reflection analysis showing I3 fits the pattern of consistent large-cardinal axioms.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Work in ZFC with the cumulative hierarchy $V_0=\emptyset$, $V_{\alpha+1}=\mathcal P(V_\alpha)$, $V_\delta=\bigcup_{\alpha<\delta}V_\alpha$ for limit $\delta$.

**Elementary embedding.** $j:V_\lambda\prec V_\lambda$ means for every first-order formula $\varphi$ of the language $\{\in\}$ and all $a_1,\dots,a_n\in V_\lambda$,
$$ V_\lambda \models \varphi(a_1,\dots,a_n) \iff V_\lambda \models \varphi(j(a_1),\dots,j(a_n)). $$
Since $j$ is a class of $V_{\lambda+1}$, I3 is a $\Sigma_1$ statement over $V_{\lambda+1}$, expressible in ZFC as a single sentence via $\exists\lambda\,\exists j\subseteq V_\lambda\times V_\lambda\,(\cdots)$.

**Critical sequence.** Let $\kappa_0=\mathrm{crit}(j)$, $\kappa_{n+1}=j(\kappa_n)$. Then $\kappa_n$ is strictly increasing and
$$ \lambda = \sup_{n<\omega}\kappa_n, \qquad \mathrm{cf}(\lambda)=\omega .$$
Indeed if $\lambda>\sup_n\kappa_n$ then $\kappa$ would be a critical point of an embedding $V_{\mu+2}\prec V_{\mu+2}$-style configuration ruled out below; the standard argument shows any $j:V_\lambda\prec V_\lambda$ has $\lambda$ equal to the critical sequence's supremum.

**The rank-into-rank hierarchy** (decreasing strength):
- **I0:** $\exists\lambda\,\exists j:L(V_{\lambda+1})\prec L(V_{\lambda+1})$ with $\mathrm{crit}(j)<\lambda$.
- **I1:** $\exists j:V_{\lambda+1}\prec V_{\lambda+1}$ nontrivial.
- **I2:** $\exists j:V\prec M$ elementary with $V_\lambda\subseteq M$, $\lambda$ the critical sequence supremum.
- **I3:** $\exists j:V_\lambda\prec V_\lambda$ nontrivial.

**Kunen's theorem (1971).** In ZFC there is *no* nontrivial $j:V_{\lambda+2}\prec V_{\lambda+2}$; equivalently no nontrivial $j:V\prec V$. Proof uses a Erdős–Hajnal $\omega$-Jónsson function $f:[\lambda]^\omega\to\lambda$ and the failure of $j$ to fix an $\omega$-club, applied to a set in $V_{\lambda+2}$. So $\lambda+2$ is the exact ceiling: **I3, I1, I0 sit in the two ranks just below the Kunen bound.**

**Self-distributive algebra.** For $j,k:V_\lambda\prec V_\lambda$ define application
$$ j\cdot k \;=\; \bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda} j(k\cap V_\alpha), $$
which is again an elementary embedding $V_\lambda\prec V_\lambda$. This operation satisfies the **left-distributive law**
$$ j\cdot(k\cdot l) = (j\cdot k)\cdot(j\cdot l). $$
The set $\mathcal E_\lambda$ of such embeddings is thus an LD-algebra; $\mathcal A_j\subseteq\mathcal E_\lambda$ denotes the subalgebra generated by one $j$.

**Consequences of I3.** If $\kappa$ is I3 with critical sequence $\langle\kappa_n\rangle$, then each $\kappa_n$ is measurable, $\kappa$ is $n$-huge for every $n$, $\kappa$ is a limit of $n$-huge cardinals, and $V_\lambda\models$ "there is a proper class of supercompacts". By Scott's argument relativized, I3 fails in $L$ and in any $L[A]$ with $A\subseteq V_\lambda$ definable enough.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1967–71.** Reinhardt proposed $j:V\prec V$. **Kunen (1971)** refuted it in ZFC, establishing $V_{\lambda+2}$ as the barrier.
- **1978.** Solovay, Reinhardt and Kanamori, *Strong axioms of infinity and elementary embeddings*, isolated and named I0–I3, proved the implications $\mathrm{I1}\Rightarrow\mathrm{I2}\Rightarrow\mathrm{I3}$, and located $n$-huge cardinals below them.
- **1992–95.** **Laver** used I3 to prove the free LD-algebra on one generator is isomorphic to $\mathcal A_j$, deciding the word problem for left-distributivity — the first "concrete" theorem proved from I3.
- **1994–2000.** **Dehornoy** reproved freeness in ZFC using the braid group $B_\infty$ and its LD-structure, removing the large-cardinal hypothesis. This is the canonical example of an I3 consequence later shown outright.
- **1993.** **Dougherty** proved the Laver-table period function grows faster than any primitive recursive bound, showing the I3 consequence "periods $\to\infty$" is not cheaply provable.
- **2000s–2010s.** **Woodin** developed I0, showing $L(V_{\lambda+1})$ under I0 mirrors $L(\mathbb R)$ under AD, including a $\Theta$-analysis, coding lemma, and failure of choice in $L(V_{\lambda+1})$. **Cramer (2015)** proved inverse-limit reflection theorems for $L(V_{\lambda+1})$.
- **2018.** **Dimonte**'s survey *I0 and rank-into-rank axioms* consolidates the area.
- **2019–.** Bagaria–Koellner–Woodin, *Large cardinals beyond choice*, and Schlutzenberg's ZF-consistency work on $V_{\lambda+2}$-embeddings push above the Kunen line without AC.

No contradiction from I3 has been found in 55 years, and the axiom has generated a coherent structure theory — the standard empirical evidence for consistency.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **Upper bound is exact.** Kunen: $j:V_{\lambda+2}\prec V_{\lambda+2}$ is inconsistent with ZFC. I3 lives at rank $\lambda$, I1 at $\lambda+1$. Goldberg (2021) sharpened the Kunen argument via even ordinals, giving a proof avoiding Jónsson functions.
- **Relative strength chain.** $\mathrm{I0}\Rightarrow\mathrm{I1}\Rightarrow\mathrm{I2}\Rightarrow\mathrm{I3}\Rightarrow n\text{-huge for all }n\Rightarrow$ supercompact $\Rightarrow$ strong $\Rightarrow$ measurable. Laver (1997) showed I3 does *not* imply I2, in the sense that if $\kappa$ is I2 then there is a normal measure concentrating on I3 cardinals that are not I2 — a level-by-level separation of the axioms as *properties of $\kappa$*, though not of consistency strength.
- **Free LD-algebra (fully proved, ZFC).** Laver's theorem "$\mathcal A_j$ is free on one generator, hence the LD word problem is decidable" was proved from I3 in 1992 and re-proved without any large cardinal by Dehornoy via braid orderings by 1994. This case is *closed*.
- **Laver tables.** For each $n$, $A_n$ is the unique LD-structure on $\{1,\dots,2^n\}$ with $a*1 = a+1 \bmod 2^n$. Under I3, the period $\pi(n)$ of the first row satisfies $\pi(n)\to\infty$. In ZFC this remains open; $\pi(n)$ is verified computationally for $n\le 48$ with values $1,1,2,4,4,8,8,8,8,16,\dots$, and the least $n$ with $\pi(n)\ge 32$ exceeds a bound of Ackermann-hierarchy size (Dougherty 1993).
- **Forcing indestructibility.** I3 and I1 survive suitable $\lambda$-directed-closed and Prikry-type forcings; Dimonte–Wu (2016) give a general framework producing models where I1 holds together with, e.g., failure of GCH at $\lambda$ or $\lambda$ singular of cofinality $\omega$ with prescribed behaviour.
- **Non-provability in canonical inner models.** No fine-structural inner model reaching I3 exists; conversely I3 is refutable in $L$, in $L[U]$, and in all current extender models with a "self-similarity" barrier at superstrong-plus.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No inner model theory at this level.** Consistency confidence for large cardinals below a superstrong is anchored by fine-structural models ($L[E]$) with condensation and comparison. Iteration-tree machinery breaks down before I3: the extenders needed overlap, and comparison arguments have no known termination proof. So the usual test — "build a canonical model, check no contradiction" — is unavailable.
- **Kunen's proof is not localizable.** Kunen's contradiction needs the $\omega$-Jónsson function $f:[\lambda]^\omega\to\lambda$ to be an *element* of the domain, i.e. rank $\lambda+2$. Every attempt to push the argument to $V_{\lambda+1}$ or $V_\lambda$ requires coding $f$ by a set of lower rank; no such coding is possible because $j\restriction V_\lambda$ carries no information about $\mathcal P(\mathcal P(V_\lambda))$.
- **AC is essential and its role is opaque.** Kunen's proof uses choice (to well-order $[\lambda]^\omega$). Whether ZF alone refutes $j:V_{\lambda+2}\prec V_{\lambda+2}$ is open, so we cannot even say the barrier is combinatorial rather than choice-theoretic.
- **Reflection gives no independent evidence.** Above supercompacts, the "generic reflection" heuristics (Vopěnka-style, structural reflection) either fail or reproduce I3 without adding confidence.
- **Combinatorial consequences are Ackermann-hard.** The Laver-table statements that I3 implies are true but sit far beyond primitive-recursive verification, so numerical experiment cannot corroborate or refute I3.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: no embedding at rank $\lambda+2$ (Kunen). Unproven: anything at rank $\lambda$, $\lambda+1$, or $L(V_{\lambda+1})$.

The exact gap is the **two-rank window** $[\lambda,\lambda+2)$. To close it, one needs either:

1. A ZFC argument extracting a Jónsson-type contradiction using only $j\restriction V_\lambda$ — which requires a definable-from-$V_\lambda$ substitute for the $\omega$-Jónsson function, or a wholly different combinatorial invariant living at rank $\le\lambda+1$; or
2. A canonical model or a forcing/ultrapower construction relative to a weaker hypothesis that produces a $j:V_\lambda\prec V_\lambda$, giving an equiconsistency downward.

Neither direction has a partial prototype. A secondary gap: the four axioms I0–I3 are known to differ *pointwise* (Laver 1997) but no consistency-strength separation, e.g. $\mathrm{Con}(\mathrm{I3})\not\Rightarrow\mathrm{Con}(\mathrm{I2})$, has been established.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **$L(V_{\lambda+1})$ structure theory (Woodin, Dimonte, Cramer, Shi).** The programme transfers descriptive-set-theoretic results from $L(\mathbb R)+\mathrm{AD}$ to $L(V_{\lambda+1})+\mathrm{I0}$: perfect set property, coding lemma, $\Theta$-analysis, generalized degree theory (Shi, *Axiom I0 and higher degree theory*, JSL 2015). Progress here indirectly supports I3's consistency by showing the region is not pathological.
- **Choiceless cardinals (Bagaria, Koellner, Woodin; Schlutzenberg; Goldberg).** Studying Reinhardt and Berkeley cardinals in ZF probes whether the Kunen bound is a choice artifact. Schlutzenberg's work on ZF-consistency of $j:V_{\lambda+2}\prec V_{\lambda+2}$ is the sharpest current probe *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Inverse-limit and reflection methods (Cramer, Dimonte).** Inverse-limit embeddings give a $j$-generated hierarchy on $V_{\lambda+1}$ and yield reflection principles for I-axioms; several claimed separations of I1-variants rest on these *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Forcing with rank-into-rank (Dimonte, Wu, Hayut, Corazza).** Constructing models of I1 with tailored cardinal arithmetic at $\lambda$; also Corazza's wholeness axiom as a weakened, ZFC-compatible surrogate for $j:V\prec V$.
- **Groups/institutions.** Torino (Dimonte), Beijing Normal (Shi), Berkeley/Harvard (Woodin, Goldberg), Münster (Schlutzenberg), Barcelona (Bagaria).

## 8. Future Work

- Seek a *rank-$\lambda+1$* Kunen-type argument: identify combinatorial objects (generalized Jónsson, square, or partition properties on $[\lambda]^{\omega}$) coded inside $V_{\lambda+1}$ and test whether elementarity contradicts them. Failure patterns here are themselves informative.
- Settle whether ZF refutes $j:V_{\lambda+2}\prec V_{\lambda+2}$. This is the single most cited open question in the area.
- Develop long extenders and iteration theory past superstrong toward an $L[E]$ model with an I3 embedding; even a partial comparison theorem would change the evidential picture.
- Separate I0/I1/I2/I3 in consistency strength via forcing or inner models — currently only pointwise separations exist.
- Continue the ZFC-ification programme begun by Dehornoy: identify further consequences of I3 (e.g. Laver-table periodicity) and prove them outright, testing I3 as a heuristic engine.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**The finite shadow of I3: Laver tables.**

Let $j:V_\lambda\prec V_\lambda$ be I3 with critical sequence $\kappa_0<\kappa_1<\cdots$. The LD-algebra $\mathcal A_j$ generated by $j$ under $\cdot$ projects onto finite quotients by considering critical points modulo $\kappa_n$. These quotients are exactly the **Laver tables** $A_n$: the unique left-distributive operation on $\{1,\dots,2^n\}$ with $a*1=a+1 \pmod{2^n}$ (representative $2^n$ for $0$).

Compute $A_2$ on $\{1,2,3,4\}$. Use $a*1=a+1$ and $a*(b*c)=(a*b)*(a*c)$, with $4$ acting as identity ($4*x=x$):

| $*$ | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | 2 | 4 | 2 | 4 |
| **2** | 3 | 4 | 3 | 4 |
| **3** | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| **4** | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |

Check one entry: $1*2 = 1*(1*1) = (1*1)*(1*1) = 2*2$. And $2*2 = 2*(1*1) = (2*1)*(2*1) = 3*3$; $3*3 = 3*(2*1)=(3*2)*(3*1) = 4*4 = 4$. So $1*2 = 4$. ✓

The first row of $A_2$ is $2,4,2,4$: **period $\pi(2)=2$**. Similarly $\pi(0)=\pi(1)=1$, $\pi(3)=4$, $\pi(4)=4$, $\pi(5)=\dots=\pi(8)=8$, $\pi(9)=16$.

**The I3 content.** Laver proved: if I3 holds, then $\pi(n)\to\infty$. The reason is that in $\mathcal A_j$ the critical points $\mathrm{crit}(j^{*k})$ form a strictly increasing set unbounded in $\lambda$, and the finite quotients cannot stabilize. In ZFC alone, no one can rule out $\pi(n)=16$ for all $n\ge 9$.

**Why computation cannot settle it.** Dougherty showed the least $n$ with $\pi(n)\ge 32$ is larger than $f_9(f_8(f_8(254)))$ in the Ackermann hierarchy — a number with no decimal representation of feasible size. So a single, entirely finitary and elementary arithmetic statement ("the period of row 1 of the Laver table eventually exceeds 16") is currently known only from an axiom asserting an embedding of a rank of the universe into itself, and no finite computation will confirm or refute it. This is the sharpest concrete illustration of I3's status: a hypothesis with real, checkable-in-principle arithmetic consequences, whose own consistency is beyond reach.
