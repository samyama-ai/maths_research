# Decidability of the Real Exponential Field (Tarski's Exponential Function Problem)

> **Topic:** Mathematical Logic & Set Theory · **ID:** `08-logic-set-theory/decidability-of-reals-with-exponential` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let
$$\mathbb{R}_{\exp} = (\mathbb{R}, +, \cdot, -, 0, 1, <, \exp)$$
be the ordered field of real numbers expanded by the unary function $x \mapsto e^x$.

**Tarski's exponential function problem.** Is the first-order theory $\mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{R}_{\exp})$ decidable? That is, is there an algorithm which, given any first-order sentence $\sigma$ in the language $L_{\exp} = \{+,\cdot,-,0,1,<,\exp\}$, halts and outputs `true` iff $\mathbb{R}_{\exp} \models \sigma$?

A positive solution requires exhibiting such an algorithm together with a proof of termination and correctness. A negative solution requires an effective reduction of the halting problem (or any $\Sigma^0_1$-complete set) to $\mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{R}_{\exp})$ — for example, an $L_{\exp}$-definition of $\mathbb{Z}$ in $\mathbb{R}$, which Wilkie's o-minimality theorem rules out.

The status is *conditional*: Macintyre and Wilkie (1996) proved $\mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{R}_{\exp})$ is decidable **if Schanuel's conjecture holds**. The unconditional question is open.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Tarski's base theorem.** $\mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{R}, +, \cdot, <, 0, 1)$ admits quantifier elimination and is decidable (Tarski 1951). Adding $\exp$ destroys quantifier elimination in $L_{\exp}$.

**O-minimality.** A structure $\mathcal{M}$ expanding $(\mathbb{R},<)$ is *o-minimal* if every definable subset of $\mathbb{R}$ is a finite union of points and open intervals.

**Theorem (Wilkie 1996).** $\mathbb{R}_{\exp}$ is model complete and o-minimal. Every $L_{\exp}$-formula $\varphi(\bar x)$ is equivalent in $\mathbb{R}_{\exp}$ to an existential formula
$$\exists \bar y \; f(\bar x, \bar y) = 0,$$
where $f \in \mathbb{Z}[X_1,\dots,X_n, Y_1,\dots,Y_m, e^{X_1},\dots,e^{Y_m}]$ is an *exponential polynomial* — a member of the ring $E[\bar X] $ generated by polynomials and terms $e^{t}$ for $t$ already built.

Consequence: **decidability of the existential theory $\mathrm{Th}_\exists(\mathbb{R}_{\exp})$ implies decidability of the full theory.**

**Schanuel's Conjecture (SC).** For any $z_1,\dots,z_n \in \mathbb{C}$ linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$,
$$\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb{Q}} \, \mathbb{Q}\!\left(z_1,\dots,z_n, e^{z_1},\dots,e^{z_n}\right) \; \ge \; n .$$
Lindemann–Weierstrass ($z_i$ algebraic) and the Hermite–Lindemann theorem are the known special cases.

**Weak Schanuel / effective bound (WS).** There is a computable function $B$ such that for every system
$$f_1(\bar x) = \cdots = f_n(\bar x) = 0, \qquad f_i \in \mathbb{Z}[\bar X, e^{\bar X}],$$
of total degree $\le d$ and coefficient height $\le H$ in $n$ variables, every *isolated* real solution $\bar a$ satisfies: either some $f_i$ vanishes identically on a component, or $|\bar a| \le B(n,d,H)$ and the solutions are separated by $\ge B(n,d,H)^{-1}$. Macintyre–Wilkie show SC $\Rightarrow$ WS $\Rightarrow$ decidability of $\mathrm{Th}_\exists(\mathbb{R}_{\exp})$.

**Khovanskii finiteness.** For a one-variable exponential polynomial $F(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k} p_i(x) e^{\lambda_i x}$ with $\lambda_i\in\mathbb{R}$ distinct and $\deg p_i = d_i$, the number of real zeros counted with multiplicity is at most
$$\sum_{i=1}^{k}(d_i+1) - 1 .$$
Khovanskii's fewnomial theory gives analogous explicit bounds in $n$ variables for nondegenerate systems.

**Constant problem.** $\mathrm{EC}$: given a closed $L_{\exp}$-term $t$ (built from $\mathbb{Z}$, $+,\times,-,\exp$), decide whether $t = 0$ in $\mathbb{R}$. Richardson (1997) gave a semi-algorithm that is a decision procedure under SC.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1930s–1951.** Tarski proves decidability of real closed fields; in the 1951 RAND monograph he explicitly asks about $(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot,\exp)$.
- **1968.** Richardson: $(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot,\exp,\sin)$ has undecidable existential theory — $\mathbb{Z}$ is definable via $\sin(\pi x)=0$. This showed the danger is real and that any positive answer must use a property $\sin$ lacks.
- **1984.** van den Dries reduces Tarski's problem to a "finiteness" statement about exponential-polynomial zero sets, and axiomatizes the *restricted* exponential field.
- **1986–1991.** Khovanskii's fewnomial bounds supply effective finiteness for nondegenerate Pfaffian systems.
- **1991.** Wilkie proves model completeness of $\mathbb{R}_{\exp}$ (published JAMS 1996), yielding o-minimality; hence $\mathbb{Z}$ is *not* definable and the Richardson route to undecidability is blocked.
- **1994.** van den Dries–Macintyre–Marker: $\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\exp}$ is o-minimal and model complete, with an explicit theory $T_{\mathrm{an},\exp}$.
- **1996.** Macintyre–Wilkie, *On the decidability of the real exponential field*: SC $\Rightarrow$ $\mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{R}_{\exp})$ decidable, and a recursive axiomatization is given.
- **1997–2008.** Richardson's zero-recognition algorithm; practical exp-log solvers (Strzeboński; Achatz–McCallum–Weispfenning).
- **2010s–2020s.** Zilber's pseudo-exponentiation and exponential-algebraic closedness; Aschenbrenner–van den Dries–van der Hoeven's model theory of transseries; Binyamini–Novikov's sharp o-minimality supplying effective complexity bounds for restricted exponential structures.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **Full theory conditional on SC** (Macintyre–Wilkie 1996). Also $\mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{R}_{\mathrm{an},\exp})$ is decidable given SC plus decidability of the relevant analytic constants.
- **Restricted exponentiation.** $\mathbb{R}_{\exp\restriction[0,1]}$ — the reals with $\exp$ restricted to $[0,1]$ — is decidable **unconditionally**? No: decidability there still needs constants; but the *theory* is model complete and admits an effective axiomatization modulo SC (van den Dries 1984). The unconditional decidable case is the purely algebraic reduct: Tarski's theorem, with $\mathrm{DEXP}$-time complexity (Collins' CAD, $2^{2^{O(n)}}$; Davenport–Heintz lower bound $2^{2^{\Omega(n)}}$).
- **Dimension 1, single equation.** For $F(x)=\sum_{i\le k} p_i(x)e^{\lambda_i x}$ with *rational* $\lambda_i$ and integer coefficients, real root isolation is algorithmic: the zero count is bounded by $\sum(d_i+1)-1$ and sign evaluation reduces to comparing algebraic numbers with $e^{q}$, decidable by Lindemann–Weierstrass. Hence the existential theory of $(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot,<,e^{qx})$ restricted to one variable with all exponents rational is decidable.
- **Nondegenerate systems in $n$ variables.** Khovanskii's bounds make the count of nondegenerate solutions computable; the decision problem for systems certified nondegenerate (nonvanishing Jacobian) is therefore effectively solvable.
- **Algebraic arguments.** Any sentence whose exponential terms are applied only to numbers algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$ is decidable via Lindemann–Weierstrass — this is exactly the fragment of SC that is proved.
- **Undecidable neighbours.** $(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot,\exp,\sin)$: undecidable (Richardson 1968); Laczkovich (2003) removed $\pi$ from the construction, showing undecidability persists for $\sin$ on a bounded interval with rational data.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Transcendental number theory, not logic, is the bottleneck.** All model-theoretic prerequisites (model completeness, o-minimality, a recursive candidate axiomatization) are in place. The missing input is arithmetic: SC is far beyond current transcendence methods — even the algebraic independence of $e$ and $\pi$ is open, and the strongest general tools (Baker's theorem, Gel'fond–Schneider, Wüstholz's analytic subgroup theorem) give transcendence degree $\ge 1$ or $\ge 2$ in restricted settings, never the linear-in-$n$ bound SC demands.
- **Degenerate systems.** Khovanskii's bounds count only nondegenerate zeros. Deciding whether a given system is degenerate is itself an instance of the constant problem $\mathrm{EC}$, so effective finiteness does not bootstrap to an algorithm.
- **No separation bound.** A decision procedure must know when a computed approximation $|t| < 2^{-N}$ certifies $t=0$. Without an effective lower bound on nonzero closed exp-terms — precisely what WS supplies — numerical evaluation loops forever.
- **O-minimality is non-effective.** Wilkie's proof gives finiteness of definable sets but no computable bound on the number of connected components in terms of formula complexity; the compactness/valuation-theoretic arguments used are not constructive.
- **The negative direction is also blocked.** O-minimality forbids defining $\mathbb{Z}$, killing the only known general technique for proving undecidability of an expansion of $\mathbb{R}$.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: $\mathrm{SC} \Rightarrow \mathrm{WS} \Rightarrow$ decidability. Also proven: model completeness reduces everything to existential sentences $\exists\bar x\, f(\bar x)=0$ with $f$ an exponential polynomial.

Unproven: any *unconditional* effective bound of the form

> there is a computable $B(n,d,H)$ such that every isolated real zero $\bar a$ of a height-$H$, degree-$d$ exponential-polynomial system in $n$ variables has $\|\bar a\| \le B$, and every nonzero closed exp-term of size $s$ satisfies $|t| > B(s)^{-1}$.

The exact step to cross is to replace the appeal to SC by an unconditional transcendence measure for exponential-polynomial systems — or, alternatively, to prove SC itself (for real, and even for rational, arguments). No weakening of SC is currently known that both follows from established transcendence theory and still yields WS.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Effective o-minimality.** Binyamini and Novikov's *sharply o-minimal* structures attach explicit format/degree complexity to definable sets, giving polynomial bounds on component counts for restricted exponential and Pfaffian structures. Extending sharpness from restricted $\exp$ to global $\exp$ would give a large piece of WS. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Zilber's programme.** Exponential fields $\mathbb{B}$ satisfying Schanuel's inequality plus exponential-algebraic closedness; work of Kirby, Bays, Gallinaro and Mantova on the existential closedness conjecture yields unconditional solvability results for systems of exponential equations over $\mathbb{C}$, which feed back into effectivity questions.
- **Transseries and $H$-fields.** Aschenbrenner–van den Dries–van der Hoeven's model completeness of $\mathbb{T}$ supplies a differential-algebraic surrogate for $\mathbb{R}_{\exp}$; recovering effectivity there is an active line.
- **Consequences of SC.** D'Aquino–Macintyre–Terzo and collaborators derive Shapiro's conjecture and related statements from SC, mapping the logical strength of the assumption.
- **Practical solvers.** Exact exp-log root isolation (Strzeboński) and virtual-substitution methods (Weispfenning school) are complete on nondegenerate inputs and used as testbeds.

## 8. Future Work

- Prove SC for the special case $z_i \in \mathbb{R}$ algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}(e)$, or any case giving transcendence degree growing with $n$.
- Establish an unconditional effective separation bound for closed exp-terms of nesting depth $2$ (i.e. terms in $e, e^e, e^{e^e}$) — this would decide a natural fragment outright.
- Push sharp o-minimality from restricted to unrestricted $\exp$, obtaining computable component bounds.
- Determine the complexity of $\mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{R}_{\exp})$ assuming SC: currently only "decidable" is known, with no elementary upper bound.
- Settle whether decidability of $\mathrm{Th}_\exists(\mathbb{R}_{\exp})$ is *equivalent* to a weak Schanuel statement (a reverse-mathematics style characterization).

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**An easy instance.** Decide $\sigma_1 : \exists x\,\big(e^{e^x} = x\big)$.

- If $x \le 0$: $e^x \in (0,1]$, so $e^{e^x} \in (1,e]$, which is $> 0 \ge x$. No solution.
- If $x > 0$: $e^x > x$ and $\exp$ is increasing, so $e^{e^x} > e^x > x$. No solution.

Hence $\mathbb{R}_{\exp} \models \neg\sigma_1$. The whole argument is monotonicity — exactly the kind of case current algorithms handle.

**A hard instance.** Fix $d = 2$ and ask $\sigma_2$: is there a nonzero $P \in \mathbb{Z}[X,Y]$ of total degree $\le 2$ with $P(e, e^e) = 0$? Writing $P = \sum_{i+j\le 2} c_{ij} X^i Y^j$, the statement "$P(e,e^e)=0$ for some integer vector $\bar c \ne 0$ of height $\le H$" is a finite disjunction of $L_{\exp}$-sentences

$$\bigvee_{\bar c} \Big( c_{00} + c_{10}e + c_{01}e^{e} + c_{20}e^{2} + c_{11}e\,e^{e} + c_{02}e^{2e} = 0 \Big),$$

each built from the closed terms $\exp(1)$ and $\exp(\exp(1))$.

*Under SC:* take $z_1 = 1$, $z_2 = e$. Since $e$ is irrational (Euler), $1$ and $e$ are $\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent. SC gives
$$\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}\big(1, e, e^{1}, e^{e}\big) = \operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}\big(e, e^{e}\big) \ \ge\ 2 ,$$
so $e$ and $e^{e}$ are algebraically independent and every disjunct is false; $\sigma_2$ is false, for every degree bound $d$ and height bound $H$.

*Unconditionally:* it is not known whether $e^{e}$ is even irrational, let alone transcendental, so no disjunct can be refuted by proof. A numerical check gives, for example, $e = 2.718281828\ldots$, $e^{e} = 15.15426224\ldots$, and one can verify $|P(e,e^e)| > 10^{-20}$ for all $\bar c$ with $\|\bar c\|_\infty \le 10$ — but without an effective lower bound on $|P(e,e^e)|$ as a function of $\deg P$ and $\|\bar c\|$, no amount of computation certifies non-vanishing. That missing bound is Weak Schanuel, and it is the entire content of the gap in Section 6.
