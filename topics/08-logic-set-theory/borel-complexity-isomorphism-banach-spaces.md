# Borel Complexity of the Isomorphism Relation for Separable Banach Spaces

> **Topic:** Mathematical Logic & Set Theory · **ID:** `08-logic-set-theory/borel-complexity-isomorphism-banach-spaces` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Separable Banach spaces form a standard Borel space $\mathrm{SB}$ (Bossard's coding: the closed linear subspaces of $C(\Delta)$, $\Delta = 2^{\mathbb N}$, with the Effros Borel structure). Linear isomorphism
$$X \cong Y \iff \exists\, T \in \mathcal B(X,Y)\ \text{bijective and bounded}$$
is an analytic ($\boldsymbol\Sigma^1_1$) equivalence relation on $\mathrm{SB}$. The classification program asks for its exact position in the Borel-reducibility order $\le_B$, and the same for natural subclasses and individual classes.

Three components:

1. **Global complexity (solved).** Ferenczi–Louveau–Rosendal (2009): $\cong$ is a *complete* analytic equivalence relation — every analytic equivalence relation on a Polish space is Borel reducible to it. In particular $\cong$ is not Borel and is strictly above isometry.
2. **Subclass complexity (open).** For a Borel or coanalytic class $\mathcal C \subseteq \mathrm{SB}$ (reflexive spaces, spaces with unconditional basis, subspaces of $L_p$, $\mathcal L_\infty$-spaces, separable $C(K)$), locate $\cong\restriction\mathcal C$ in $\le_B$. Is it complete analytic, a universal orbit equivalence relation, essentially countable, or smooth?
3. **Individual classes (open).** Characterize the $X$ whose isomorphism class $\langle X\rangle = \{Y \in \mathrm{SB}: Y \cong X\}$ is Borel. Known Borel: $\ell_p$. Known non-Borel: $c_0$.

A complete resolution of (2)–(3) means: for each listed subclass, a proof of Borel bireducibility with a named benchmark relation, and a structural characterization of the spaces with Borel isomorphism class.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**The coding space.** For a Polish space $Z$, let $F(Z)$ be the set of closed subsets with the **Effros Borel structure**, generated by
$$\{F \in F(Z) : F \cap U \neq \emptyset\},\qquad U \subseteq Z\ \text{open}.$$
$F(Z)$ is standard Borel. Since $C(\Delta)$ is isometrically universal for separable Banach spaces (Banach–Mazur),
$$\mathrm{SB} := \{X \in F(C(\Delta)) : X \text{ is a closed linear subspace}\}$$
is a Borel subset, hence standard Borel. By the Kuratowski–Ryll-Nardzewski selection theorem there are Borel maps $d_n : \mathrm{SB} \to C(\Delta)$ with $\{d_n(X)\}_n$ dense in $X$ for every nonzero $X$; all "elementwise" quantification over $X$ is therefore Borel.

**Reducibility.** For equivalence relations $E$ on $\mathcal X$, $F$ on $\mathcal Y$,
$$E \le_B F \iff \exists\, \text{Borel } f:\mathcal X \to \mathcal Y \ \ \forall x,x'\ (x\,E\,x' \leftrightarrow f(x)\,F\,f(x')).$$
Benchmarks: $\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb R}$ (smooth), $E_0$ (eventual equality on $2^{\mathbb N}$), $E_\infty$ (universal countable), $E_G^{\mathrm{univ}}$ (universal orbit equivalence relation for Polish group actions), and complete analytic $E^{\Sigma^1_1}_{\max}$.

**Why $\cong$ is analytic.** $X\cong Y$ iff there exist $K \ge 1$ and rational sequences coding a linear map on $\{d_n(X)\}$ with
$$K^{-1}\|x\| \le \|Tx\| \le K\|x\|,\qquad \overline{T(X)} = Y,$$
an existential quantifier over a Polish parameter space, so $\Sigma^1_1$. For **fixed** $K$, the Banach–Mazur ball
$$\mathcal B_K(X) := \{Y : d_{BM}(X,Y) \le K\},\qquad d_{BM}(X,Y)=\inf\{\|T\|\|T^{-1}\| : T:X\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }Y\},$$
is analytic and, by compactness of the relevant selection, $\langle X\rangle = \bigcup_{K\in\mathbb N}\mathcal B_K(X)$. Non-Borelness of $\langle X\rangle$ is exactly the failure of a uniform bound on $K$ along Borel-definable families.

**Key theorems relied on.** Gowers' dichotomy (every infinite-dimensional Banach space contains either a hereditarily indecomposable subspace or one with an unconditional basis); Bessaga–Pełczyński isomorphic classification of $C(\alpha)$; the Louveau–Rosendal theory of complete analytic equivalence relations; Szlenk index and ordinal indices as $\Pi^1_1$-ranks.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1993–2002 — Bossard.** Introduced the standard Borel coding and proved the first non-classification results: $\cong$ on $\mathrm{SB}$ is analytic non-Borel, and this persists on the (Borel) set of spaces with a Schauder basis. His method: reduce ill-founded trees to Banach spaces via ordinal indices (Bossard, *Fund. Math.* 172, 2002).
- **2005 — Louveau–Rosendal.** Developed complete analytic equivalence relations, e.g. biembeddability of countable graphs, giving the target for Banach-space reductions (*Trans. AMS* 357).
- **2007 — Melleray.** *Isometry* of separable Banach spaces is Borel bireducible with $E_G^{\mathrm{univ}}$: maximally complicated among orbit equivalence relations, but still an orbit relation.
- **2009 — Ferenczi–Louveau–Rosendal.** $\cong$ is complete analytic. Hence $E_G^{\mathrm{univ}} <_B\ \cong$, and isomorphism is *not* induced by any Polish group action. The same holds for isomorphism restricted to spaces with a Schauder basis, and for bi-embeddability and Lipschitz isomorphism.
- **2010 — Dodos.** Monograph systematizing amalgamation, universality, and $\Pi^1_1$-rank methods (Springer LNM 1993).
- **2016–2018 — Kurka, Godefroy, Godefroy–Saint-Raymond.** Shift of focus to *individual* classes: $\langle c_0 \rangle$ is non-Borel (Kurka); $\langle \ell_p\rangle$ is Borel (Godefroy).

SOTA summary: the global question is closed; the calibration of subclasses and of $\langle X\rangle$ is open.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Class | Complexity of $\cong$ | Source |
|---|---|---|
| All of $\mathrm{SB}$ | complete analytic | Ferenczi–Louveau–Rosendal 2009 |
| Spaces with a Schauder basis | complete analytic | Ferenczi–Louveau–Rosendal 2009 |
| Isometry on $\mathrm{SB}$ | $\sim_B E_G^{\mathrm{univ}}$ | Melleray 2007 |
| $C(K)$, $K$ countable compact | smooth, $\aleph_0$ classes | Bessaga–Pełczyński 1960 |
| $\langle \ell_p\rangle$, $1\le p<\infty$ | Borel | Godefroy |
| $\langle \ell_2\rangle$ | Borel (type 2 + cotype 2, Kwapień) | classical |
| $\langle c_0\rangle$ | analytic non-Borel | Kurka 2016 |

**$C(K)$ detail.** For countable compact $K$ of Cantor–Bendixson rank, $C(K)\cong C(\omega^{\omega^\beta})$ for a unique $\beta < \omega_1$ ($K$ infinite), so the restricted relation has countably many classes and is smooth — yet the *set* of codes of such spaces is $\Pi^1_1$-complete, so smoothness does not lift to a Borel classification of $\mathrm{SB}$.

**Ergodicity (Ferenczi–Rosendal).** $X$ is *ergodic* if $E_0 \le_B\ \cong\restriction \mathrm{SB}(X)$ (subspaces of $X$). Verified for $\ell_p$, $p \ne 2$, for $L_p$, $p\neq 2$, for spaces with a minimal subspace not isomorphic to $\ell_2$, and (Anisca 2010) for weak Hilbert spaces. Every non-ergodic space is, by Gowers-type dichotomies, forced into a very restricted "tight" structural class.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No Borel witness selection.** Membership $Y \in \langle X\rangle$ asserts existence of an isomorphism $T$; the isomorphism constant $K$ is unbounded over the family. Standard $\Sigma^1_1$-boundedness arguments require a $\Pi^1_1$-rank whose values bound $K$; no such rank exists in general because the Banach–Mazur distance is not controlled by any known ordinal index.
- **Complete analyticity collapses information.** Above $E_G^{\mathrm{univ}}$, Borel reducibility stops separating: all complete analytic relations are bireducible. Distinguishing subclasses therefore needs finer notions (Borel reducibility with definability constraints, Wadge-type ranks, or set-theoretic absoluteness arguments) that are undeveloped for Banach spaces.
- **Failure of amalgamation.** The reductions of Bossard and FLR encode trees into spaces via unconditional sums; carrying this into a subclass (reflexive, $\mathcal L_p$, unconditional basis) requires an amalgamation of the subclass that is itself Borel and closed under the construction. Reflexivity and $\mathcal L_\infty$-structure are destroyed by the sums used.
- **Hereditarily indecomposable spaces resist coding.** Gowers–Maurey spaces have few operators ($\lambda I + $ strictly singular), so the very rigidity that makes them interesting kills the freedom needed to encode an arbitrary analytic relation.
- **Positive (Borel) results are isolated isomorphic characterizations.** $\langle\ell_2\rangle$ is Borel only because Kwapień's theorem replaces "$\exists T$" by a quantifier-free-ish condition on finite-dimensional sections. No general mechanism produces such characterizations.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: $\cong$ on all of $\mathrm{SB}$ sits at the top of the analytic hierarchy; a handful of individual classes are decided. The general statement demands, for each natural subclass $\mathcal C$, a *matching pair*: a Borel reduction of a benchmark relation **into** $\cong\restriction\mathcal C$ (lower bound) and a Borel classification map **out of** it (upper bound). The blocking step is the upper bound: converting a $\Sigma^1_1$ statement "$\exists T$" into a Borel invariant. Concretely,

> **Gap statement.** Find a $\Pi^1_1$-rank $\varphi$ on the codes of $\mathcal C$ and a function $g$ with $d_{BM}(X,Y) \le g(\varphi(X),\varphi(Y))$ whenever $X \cong Y$, or prove no such rank exists.

The parallel gap for individual classes: characterize $\{X : \langle X\rangle \text{ Borel}\}$. Present data ($\ell_p$ yes, $c_0$ no) suggest the answer tracks a rigidity/uniqueness-of-structure property rather than any classical isomorphic invariant.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Paris (Ferenczi, Sorbonne/IMJ-PRG; Rosendal, Maryland) — ergodicity program.** Pushing the Ferenczi–Rosendal conjecture ("every separable Banach space not isomorphic to $\ell_2$ is ergodic") through Gowers-type Ramsey dichotomies; de Rancourt's abstract Ramsey theory for non-pigeonhole spaces is the current engine. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Prague (Kurka, Czech Academy of Sciences).** Tsirelson-like constructions used to compute complexity of classes of Banach spaces; source of the $c_0$ non-Borelness proof and of new $\Sigma^1_1$-completeness results for classes defined by ordinal indices.
- **São Paulo (Cuellar Carrera, Ferenczi and collaborators).** Complexity of isomorphism restricted to subspaces of a fixed space and to spaces with unconditional bases. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Athens (Argyros, Dodos and school).** Amalgamation and universality: Borel-definable universal spaces for classes such as reflexive spaces with separable dual, feeding upper-bound arguments.
- **Descriptive-set-theory side (Caltech/UIC/Toronto).** Anti-classification techniques beyond $\le_B$ — Borel-on-Borel reducibility, and generic-ergodicity arguments — as replacements for the collapsed top of the hierarchy.

## 8. Future Work

- Prove or refute: $\cong$ restricted to **reflexive** separable spaces is complete analytic. This is the cleanest test of whether the FLR coding survives a structural restriction.
- Settle $\cong$ on separable $\mathcal L_\infty$-spaces (equivalently, isomorphic classification of $C(K)$ for general metrizable compact $K$) — expected strictly below the top, but no upper bound is known.
- Determine whether $\langle L_p \rangle$, $p \ne 2$, is Borel; the $\mathcal L_p$ characterization theory of Lindenstrauss–Pełczyński is the plausible route.
- Develop a Banach-space $\Pi^1_1$-rank bounding Banach–Mazur distance on a subclass; this is the technical key demanded in §6.
- Resolve the ergodicity conjecture, which would give: for every $X \not\cong \ell_2$, $\cong\restriction\mathrm{SB}(X)$ is non-smooth.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Goal:** show by hand that $\cong$ is smooth on $\{C(\alpha) : \alpha < \omega_1\}$, and contrast with the non-Borelness of $\langle c_0\rangle$.

*Step 1 — the invariant.* For a countable compact ordinal $\alpha$ with $\omega \le \alpha$, Bessaga–Pełczyński give
$$C(\alpha) \cong C(\omega^{\omega^{\beta}}) \iff \omega^{\omega^{\beta}} \le \alpha < \omega^{\omega^{\beta+1}} .$$
So $\beta(\alpha) := $ the unique such $\beta$ is a complete isomorphic invariant. Check the smallest cases:

- $\alpha = \omega$: $\omega^{\omega^0}=\omega^1=\omega \le \omega < \omega^{\omega}$, so $\beta = 0$ and $C(\omega)\cong c_0$.
- $\alpha = \omega^2$: still $\omega \le \omega^2 < \omega^\omega$, so $\beta = 0$ and $C(\omega^2)\cong c_0$. Indeed $C(\omega^2)$ is the $c_0$-sum $\big(\bigoplus_n C(\omega)\big)_{c_0} \cong c_0$.
- $\alpha = \omega^\omega$: now $\beta = 1$, and $C(\omega^\omega)\not\cong c_0$. Separating witness: the **Szlenk index**, $Sz(C(\omega^{\omega^\beta})) = \omega^{\beta+1}$. So $Sz(c_0)=\omega$ while $Sz(C(\omega^\omega))=\omega^2$, and $Sz$ is an isomorphic invariant.

*Step 2 — smoothness.* The map $C(\alpha) \mapsto \beta(\alpha) \in \omega_1$ is a complete invariant taking countably many values on any Borel-parametrized family with uniformly bounded ranks; on each such family the reduction to $\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb N}$ is Borel. Hence $\cong$ restricted to countable-compact $C(K)$ spaces has exactly $\aleph_0$ classes and is smooth.

*Step 3 — the obstruction.* Smoothness here comes from an *ordinal rank* ($Sz$) that is $\Pi^1_1$ and bounded on the family. Move outside: the set $\{X \in \mathrm{SB} : Sz(X) \le \omega\}$ is Borel, but $\langle c_0 \rangle$ is a proper subset of it, and Kurka's theorem says $\langle c_0\rangle$ is $\Sigma^1_1$ non-Borel. So a Borel rank that *separates* the family is not enough: one also needs the rank to bound the Banach–Mazur constant $K$ realizing $Y\cong c_0$. It does not — there are spaces $Y_n$ with $Sz(Y_n)=\omega$, $Y_n \cong c_0$, and $d_{BM}(Y_n,c_0)\to\infty$. That unbounded-$K$ phenomenon is exactly the barrier described in §6, in its smallest concrete instance.
