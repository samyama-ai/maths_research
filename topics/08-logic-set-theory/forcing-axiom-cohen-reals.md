# Forcing Axiom for Cohen Reals

> **Topic:** Mathematical Logic & Set Theory · **ID:** `08-logic-set-theory/forcing-axiom-cohen-reals` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

For a partial order $\mathbb{P}$ and a cardinal $\lambda$, the **forcing axiom** $\mathrm{FA}_\lambda(\mathbb{P})$ says: for every family $\{D_i : i<\lambda\}$ of dense subsets of $\mathbb{P}$ there is a filter $G\subseteq\mathbb{P}$ with $G\cap D_i\neq\emptyset$ for all $i<\lambda$.

Let $\mathrm{Add}(\kappa,1)$ be **$\kappa$-Cohen forcing**: partial functions $p:\kappa\to 2$ with $|\mathrm{dom}(p)|<\kappa$, ordered by reverse inclusion. For $\kappa=\omega$ this is Cohen forcing, and the axiom $\mathrm{FA}_\lambda(\mathrm{Add}(\omega,1))$ — "Martin's Axiom for Cohen reals" — is completely understood: it is equivalent to $\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})>\lambda$.

**The open problem is the uncountable case.**

> **Main question.** For which uncountable regular $\kappa$ is $\mathrm{FA}_{\kappa^{++}}(\mathrm{Add}(\kappa,1))$ consistent with $\mathrm{ZFC}$ (modulo large cardinals)? In particular, is
> $$\mathrm{FA}_{\aleph_3}\big(\mathrm{Add}(\omega_1,1)\big)$$
> consistent? Equivalently, is $\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_{\omega_1})>\omega_2$ consistent?

A complete solution is either (i) a forcing construction, class model, or large-cardinal argument producing a model of $\mathrm{FA}_{\kappa^{++}}(\mathrm{Add}(\kappa,1))$ for some successor $\kappa$, or (ii) a $\mathrm{ZFC}$ proof that $\mathrm{FA}_{\kappa^{++}}(\mathrm{Add}(\kappa,1))$ fails whenever $\kappa$ is a successor cardinal.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Generalized Cantor space.** For $\kappa$ regular with $\kappa^{<\kappa}=\kappa$, let $2^{\kappa}$ carry the *bounded topology*, generated by
$$[s]=\{x\in 2^{\kappa} : x\restriction \mathrm{dom}(s)=s\},\qquad s\in 2^{<\kappa}.$$
A set is **$\kappa$-meager** if it is a union of $\le\kappa$ nowhere dense sets; $\mathcal{M}_\kappa$ denotes the $\kappa$-meager ideal (a $\kappa^+$-complete ideal, since $2^\kappa$ is $\kappa$-Baire). Put
$$\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_\kappa)=\min\{|\mathcal{A}| : \mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_\kappa,\ \bigcup\mathcal{A}=2^{\kappa}\}.$$

**Duality.** $\mathrm{Add}(\kappa,1)$ is a dense subset of the poset of basic open sets of $2^\kappa$, so the generic object is a point avoiding all ground-model $\kappa$-meager sets. The standard translation gives, for $\lambda\ge\kappa$:
$$\mathrm{FA}_{\lambda}\big(\mathrm{Add}(\kappa,1)\big)\iff \mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_\kappa)>\lambda .$$
This is the $\kappa$-analogue of the classical $\mathrm{MA}_\lambda(\text{Cohen})\iff\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})>\lambda$ (Bartoszyński–Judah). The invariant $\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_\kappa)$ is also the *Baire (Novák) number* $\mathfrak{n}(2^\kappa)$ studied by Landver.

**Basic constraints.**

1. *Closure.* $\mathrm{Add}(\kappa,1)$ is $<\kappa$-closed, hence $\mathrm{FA}_{\kappa}(\mathrm{Add}(\kappa,1))$ is a theorem of $\mathrm{ZFC}$; so $\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_\kappa)\ge\kappa^+$.
2. *Diagonalization bound.* $\mathrm{FA}_\lambda(\mathrm{Add}(\kappa,1))\Rightarrow 2^{\kappa}>\lambda$, so $\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_\kappa)\le 2^{\kappa}$.
3. *Chain condition.* $\mathrm{Add}(\kappa,1)$ is $\kappa^+$-cc **iff** $2^{<\kappa}=\kappa$ and, for $\kappa$ a successor $\mu^+$, additionally $2^{\mu}=\kappa$. For $\kappa$ inaccessible the $\Delta$-system lemma applies to $<\kappa$-sized domains because $\lambda^{<\kappa}<\kappa$ for all $\lambda<\kappa$; no hypothesis on $2^\kappa$ is needed.

Point 3 is the fault line the whole problem sits on: at inaccessible $\kappa$ the chain condition is free, at successor $\kappa$ it costs $\mathrm{GCH}$ *below* $\kappa$ and — crucially — the iteration machinery then costs $\mathrm{GCH}$ *at* $\kappa$, which is exactly what the axiom forbids.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1963–1970.** Cohen's forcing and the Solovay–Tennenbaum consistency proof of $\mathrm{MA}+\neg\mathrm{CH}$ settle $\kappa=\omega$: a finite-support ccc iteration of length $\lambda^+$ gives $\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M})=2^{\aleph_0}$ arbitrarily large.
- **1978.** Shelah, *A weak generalization of MA to higher cardinals*, isolates a $\kappa^+$-version of Martin's Axiom for $<\kappa$-closed, well-met, $\kappa^+$-cc posets.
- **1983.** Baumgartner's *Iterated forcing* survey formulates **GMA** (Generalized Martin's Axiom): $\mathrm{FA}_{<2^{\omega_1}}$ for well-met, countably closed, $\aleph_2$-cc posets, consistent with $\mathrm{CH}+2^{\omega_1}=\aleph_3$. Since $\mathrm{Add}(\omega_1,1)$ is well-met, countably closed and (under $\mathrm{CH}$) $\aleph_2$-cc, GMA yields $\mathrm{FA}_{\aleph_2}(\mathrm{Add}(\omega_1,1))$, i.e. $\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_{\omega_1})=\aleph_3$ in that model — but **not** $\aleph_3$-many dense sets.
- **1992.** Landver, *Baire numbers, uncountable Cohen sets and perfect-set forcing*, computes Baire numbers $\mathfrak{n}(2^\kappa)$ and shows the inaccessible case behaves like $\kappa=\omega$.
- **1995.** Cummings–Shelah, *Cardinal invariants above the continuum*, establish independence for $\mathfrak{b}_\kappa,\mathfrak{d}_\kappa$ at inaccessible $\kappa$ and record the successor case as obstructed.
- **2018.** Brendle–Brooke-Taylor–Friedman–Montoya, *Cichoń's diagram for uncountable cardinals*, gives the systematic $\kappa$-analogue of Cichoń's diagram for **inaccessible** $\kappa$, with $\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_\kappa)$ separated from its neighbours; the successor case is explicitly left open.

**SOTA summary.** The gap $\kappa^+\le\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_\kappa)\le 2^\kappa$ is fully exploitable at $\kappa=\omega$ and at inaccessible $\kappa$; at successor $\kappa$ every known model has $\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal{M}_\kappa)=\kappa^+$.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Case | Status |
|---|---|
| $\kappa=\omega$, any $\lambda<2^{\aleph_0}$ | **Solved.** $\mathrm{MA}_\lambda(\text{Cohen})\iff\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal M)>\lambda$; Cohen forcing over a model of $\mathrm{CH}$ with $\lambda^+$ generics gives $\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal M)=2^{\aleph_0}=\lambda^+$. |
| $\lambda=\kappa$, any regular $\kappa$ | **Theorem of ZFC** by $<\kappa$-closure. |
| $\lambda=\kappa^+$, $\kappa=\omega_1$ | **Solved** by GMA (Shelah 1978; Baumgartner 1983) from $\mathrm{CH}+2^{\omega_1}=\aleph_3$: $\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal M_{\omega_1})=\aleph_3>\aleph_2$. |
| $\lambda=\kappa^+$, $\kappa=\mu^+$ with $\mu^{<\mu}=\mu$ | **Solved** by the same $\le\mu$-support GMA template (needs $2^{\mu}=\kappa$). |
| $\kappa$ inaccessible, $\lambda$ any cardinal $<2^\kappa$ | **Solved.** A $\kappa$-support iteration of $\mathrm{Add}(\kappa,1)$ of length $\kappa^{++}$ over $V\models 2^{<\kappa}=\kappa$ is $<\kappa$-closed and $\kappa^+$-cc, giving $\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal M_\kappa)=2^\kappa=\kappa^{++}$ (Landver; Cummings–Shelah; BBFM). |
| $\kappa$ singular | Degenerate: $2^\kappa$ is not $\kappa$-Baire in the usual sense; $\mathrm{Add}(\kappa,1)$ is not $<\kappa$-closed. |
| $\kappa=\omega_1$, $\lambda=\aleph_3$ | **Open.** |
| $\kappa=\omega_2$, $\lambda=\aleph_4$ | **Open.** |

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **The chain-condition/cardinal-arithmetic clash.** $\mathrm{Add}(\omega_1,1)$ is $\aleph_2$-cc only under $\mathrm{CH}$. Preserving $\aleph_2$ through an iteration of length $\ge\omega_3$ requires an $\aleph_2$-cc iteration; the $\Delta$-system lemma for $\aleph_2$-many supports of size $\le\aleph_1$ requires $2^{\aleph_1}=\aleph_2$. But the axiom being forced implies $2^{\aleph_1}\ge\aleph_4$. The hypothesis needed to run the iteration is the negation of its intended conclusion.
- **Support dilemma.** Countable support does not preserve $<\omega_1$-closure-style genericity at limits of cofinality $\omega_1$ (dense sets appearing cofinally are missed); $\le\omega_1$-support restores closure but blows up support size and re-triggers the $\Delta$-system obstruction. There is no analogue of finite support, which is what makes $\kappa=\omega$ easy.
- **No properness at $\omega_2$.** Countable elementary submodels drive proper forcing and PFA. At $\kappa=\omega_1$ one needs internally approachable models of size $\aleph_1$; the corresponding "$\aleph_2$-properness" notions are known to be fragile, and the standard PFA/MM technology does not transfer.
- **Reflection failure.** For inaccessible $\kappa$, $H_\kappa\models\mathrm{ZFC}^-$ and $\kappa$-sized objects reflect; at successors, $\kappa=\mu^+$ carries non-reflecting stationary subsets and $\square$-like structure that generates antichains in iterations.
- **No known ZFC upper bound either.** No argument is known deriving a contradiction from $\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal M_{\omega_1})>\omega_2$ — so the problem is not merely a technique gap; both directions lack a mechanism.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: $\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal M_{\omega_1})\ge\aleph_3$ is consistent (GMA), i.e. $\mathrm{FA}_{\aleph_2}$. Sought: $\mathrm{FA}_{\aleph_3}$, i.e. $\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal M_{\omega_1})\ge\aleph_4$.

The exact step is a **length-$\omega_4$ iteration theorem**: an iteration $\langle\mathbb{P}_\alpha,\dot{\mathbb{Q}}_\alpha\rangle_{\alpha<\omega_4}$ of $\mathrm{Add}(\omega_1,1)$-like posets that is (a) countably closed, (b) $\aleph_2$-preserving, over models where $2^{\aleph_1}$ is already large. Every known preservation theorem for (a)+(b) forces $2^{\aleph_1}=\aleph_2$ in the ground model. Crossing the gap means either a $\Delta$-system-free $\aleph_2$-cc criterion (e.g. a strong $\aleph_2$-linkedness or a matrix/side-condition argument), or a ZFC theorem that any filter meeting $\aleph_3$ dense subsets of $\mathrm{Add}(\omega_1,1)$ decodes a collapse of $\omega_2$.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Generalized descriptive set theory** (Vienna: Friedman school; Helsinki: Hyttinen, Kulikov; Bar-Ilan: Rinot). Focus on $\kappa$-Cichoń diagrams; the successor-$\kappa$ column is the standing open column.
- **Higher forcing axioms** (Neeman, Sinapova, Veličković, Cox). Two-type side-condition forcing gives $\omega_2$-analogues of PFA fragments. Whether any such fragment implies $\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal M_{\omega_1})>\omega_2$ is unresolved. *(frontier — verify)*
- **$\kappa$-Baire-number program** following Landver, computing $\mathfrak{n}(2^\kappa)$ under $\square_\kappa$, weak square, and stationary reflection; partial ZFC lower-bound arguments at successors of singulars. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Large-cardinal transfer.** Attempts to run the inaccessible-$\kappa$ construction and then collapse $\kappa$ to $\omega_2$ while preserving the axiom; all known collapses destroy the meagerness-avoiding filters. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Future Work

1. Isolate a **$\kappa^+$-Knaster/linked** property strong enough for iteration and provably held by $\mathrm{Add}(\omega_1,1)$ without $\mathrm{CH}$ at $\omega_1$.
2. Study the **bounded** version $\mathrm{BFA}_{\aleph_3}(\mathrm{Add}(\omega_1,1))$ (filters meeting $\aleph_3$ maximal antichains of size $\le\aleph_2$), a plausibly easier consistency target and the natural $\Sigma_1$-reflection formulation.
3. Search for a **ZFC refutation** via $\square_{\omega_1}$: does a filter meeting $\aleph_3$ dense sets build a coherent sequence collapsing $\omega_2$?
4. Compute $\mathrm{cov}(\mathcal M_\kappa)$ at $\kappa$ successor of a singular of countable cofinality, where $\mathrm{PCF}$ methods supply extra structure.
5. Determine whether $\mathrm{FA}_{\aleph_3}(\mathrm{Add}(\omega_1,1))$ has consistency strength above $\mathrm{ZFC}$ at all.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take $\kappa=\omega_1$, $\mathbb{P}=\mathrm{Add}(\omega_1,1)$.

**(a) $\mathrm{FA}_{\aleph_1}(\mathbb{P})$ is a ZFC theorem.** Let $\{D_\alpha:\alpha<\omega_1\}$ be dense. Build $p_0\ge p_1\ge\cdots$ of length $\omega_1$: at successor $\alpha+1$ pick $p_{\alpha+1}\le p_\alpha$ in $D_\alpha$; at limit $\delta<\omega_1$, $\delta$ has countable cofinality-index (indeed $\delta$ is countable), so $p_\delta=\bigcup_{\alpha<\delta}p_\alpha$ is a function with countable domain, hence a condition. The filter generated meets every $D_\alpha$. No hypotheses used.

**(b) $\mathrm{FA}_{\aleph_2}(\mathbb{P})\Rightarrow 2^{\aleph_1}\ge\aleph_3$.** Let $\{f_\xi:\xi<\omega_2\}\subseteq 2^{\omega_1}$. For $\xi<\omega_2$ set $D_\xi=\{p:\exists\alpha\in\mathrm{dom}(p),\ p(\alpha)\neq f_\xi(\alpha)\}$; each $D_\xi$ is dense (extend any $p$ by one new coordinate with the wrong value). For $\alpha<\omega_1$ set $E_\alpha=\{p:\alpha\in\mathrm{dom}(p)\}$, dense. A filter $G$ meeting all $\aleph_2+\aleph_1=\aleph_2$ of these sets yields $g=\bigcup G\in 2^{\omega_1}$ with $g\neq f_\xi$ for all $\xi$. So no $\aleph_2$-sized family exhausts $2^{\omega_1}$, i.e. $2^{\aleph_1}>\aleph_2$.

**(c) The clash, explicitly.** Suppose $\mathrm{CH}$ fails, say $2^{\aleph_0}\ge\aleph_2$, and pick distinct $f_\xi\in 2^{\omega}$ for $\xi<\omega_2$. Each $p_\xi=f_\xi$ (a function with domain $\omega\subseteq\omega_1$, countable) is a condition, and for $\xi\neq\eta$ the conditions $p_\xi,p_\eta$ have the same domain and differ somewhere, so they are incompatible. Hence $\{p_\xi:\xi<\omega_2\}$ is an antichain of size $\aleph_2$: **$\mathbb{P}$ is not $\aleph_2$-cc.**

Now combine. By (b), a model of $\mathrm{FA}_{\aleph_3}(\mathbb{P})$ has $2^{\aleph_1}\ge\aleph_4$. The only known way to obtain such a model is a length-$\ge\omega_4$ iteration of $\mathbb{P}$; to preserve $\omega_2$ one wants $\aleph_2$-cc for the iteration, which by the $\Delta$-system route needs $|\alpha|^{\aleph_1}<\aleph_2$ for $\alpha<\omega_2$, i.e. $2^{\aleph_1}=\aleph_2$ — contradicting the target. If instead one keeps $\mathrm{CH}$ and $2^{\aleph_1}=\aleph_2$ in the ground model, part (c) is avoided and the iteration runs, but by (b) it can only deliver $\mathrm{FA}_{\aleph_2}$ — exactly the GMA ceiling of Section 4.

By contrast, if $\kappa$ is inaccessible, a condition has domain of size $<\kappa$ and, given $\kappa^+$ conditions, the $\Delta$-system lemma (valid since $\lambda^{<\kappa}<\kappa$ for $\lambda<\kappa$) yields a root $r$ with $|r|<\kappa$; there are only $2^{|r|}<\kappa$ possible restrictions to $r$, so two of the $\kappa^+$ conditions agree on $r$ and are compatible. The construction in (c) is impossible, $\kappa^+$-cc holds outright, and the iteration to length $\kappa^{++}$ succeeds. The entire open problem is the absence of this one counting step at successor cardinals.
