# Critical Sequence Problem

> **Topic:** Mathematical Logic & Set Theory · **ID:** `08-logic-set-theory/critical-sequence-problem` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $A_n$ be the $n$-th **Laver table**: the unique left-distributive binary operation on $\{1,2,\dots,2^n\}$ satisfying $p * 1 = p + 1 \bmod 2^n$. The first row $q \mapsto 1 * q$ is periodic; let $\pi(n)$ be its period (always a power of $2$).

**Problem.** Is
$$\lim_{n \to \infty} \pi(n) = \infty$$
provable in $\mathsf{ZFC}$?

Richard Laver proved this limit statement **assuming a rank-into-rank embedding exists** (axiom I3: some non-identity elementary $j : V_\lambda \to V_\lambda$). The proof runs through the *critical sequence* $\kappa_0 = \mathrm{crit}(j)$, $\kappa_{n+1} = j(\kappa_n)$, whence the name. No proof from $\mathsf{ZFC}$ alone, and no disproof from any consistent theory, is known.

A complete resolution is either (a) a $\mathsf{ZFC}$ proof that $\pi$ is unbounded, (b) a $\mathsf{ZFC}$ proof that $\pi(n)$ is eventually constant (which would refute I3 in $\mathsf{ZFC}$, since $\pi \to \infty$ follows from it), or (c) a proof that the statement is independent of $\mathsf{ZFC}$ — which, being $\Pi^0_2$ and a large-cardinal consequence, would be a landmark instance of a concrete arithmetic statement whose only known proof needs an axiom near the top of the consistency hierarchy.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Left-distributive (LD) algebras.** A set $S$ with $*: S^2 \to S$ is *LD* if
$$x * (y * z) = (x * y) * (x * z) \qquad \text{for all } x,y,z \in S .$$

**Embedding algebra.** For a limit ordinal $\lambda$, let $\mathcal{E}_\lambda$ be the set of non-identity elementary embeddings $j: V_\lambda \to V_\lambda$. For $j,k \in \mathcal{E}_\lambda$ define application by extending $j$ to $V_{\lambda+1}$ level-by-level:
$$j * k \;=\; \bigcup_{\alpha < \lambda} j(k \restriction V_\alpha).$$
$(\mathcal{E}_\lambda, *)$ is LD, because elementarity gives $j(k * l) = j(k) * j(l)$. Write $\mathcal{A}_j$ for the subalgebra generated by a single $j$.

**Critical sequence.** With $\kappa_0 = \mathrm{crit}(j)$ (the least ordinal moved) and $\kappa_{n+1} = j(\kappa_n)$, one has $\lambda = \sup_n \kappa_n$ and $\mathrm{crit}(j * k) = j(\mathrm{crit}(k))$. So critical points of elements of $\mathcal{A}_j$ all lie in $\{\kappa_n\}$, and the induced map
$$\mathcal{A}_j \to \omega, \qquad k \mapsto n \text{ where } \mathrm{crit}(k) = \kappa_n$$
transports the algebra onto arithmetic data.

**Reduction to finite tables.** Quotienting $\mathcal{A}_j$ by *"equal critical points up to level $2^n$"* yields a finite LD algebra isomorphic to $A_n$. Concretely $A_n$ is generated recursively from $p * 1 \equiv p+1 \pmod{2^n}$ and
$$p * (q * r) = (p * q) * (p * r),$$
with $2^n$ acting as a left identity: $2^n * q = q$. The tables cohere: $A_{n+1}$ reduces mod $2^n$ to $A_n$, so $\pi(n)$ is non-decreasing, and $\pi(n) \mid 2^n$.

**Laver's Theorems.** (i) Assuming I3, $\mathcal{A}_j$ is the *free* LD algebra on one generator (Laver 1992). (ii) Assuming I3, $\pi(n) \to \infty$ (Laver 1995). Statement (ii) is $\Pi^0_2$, hence absolute; it cannot be false in $V$ and true in a forcing extension.

**Free LD algebra.** Let $\mathcal{F}$ be the free LD algebra on one generator $x$. Dehornoy proved in $\mathsf{ZFC}$ that its word problem is decidable and that the *left divisibility* relation $a \sqsubset b \iff b = a * c_1 * \cdots * c_k$ is a strict linear order — originally derived from I3, later made large-cardinal-free.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1989–1992.** Laver, studying $\mathcal{E}_\lambda$ under I3, proves freeness of $\mathcal{A}_j$ (*Adv. Math.* 91, 1992) and identifies the finite quotients $A_n$.
- **1993–1995.** Laver proves $\pi(n) \to \infty$ from I3 (*Adv. Math.* 110, 1995) and asks whether $\mathsf{ZFC}$ suffices. Dehornoy independently uses LD algebras to construct the left-invariant braid order (*Trans. AMS* 345, 1994), then eliminates the large cardinal — the template everyone hopes to repeat here.
- **1993.** Dougherty (*APAL* 65) analyses critical points quantitatively: the growth of the relevant functions is **Ackermannian**.
- **1997.** Dougherty–Jech (*Adv. Math.* 130) show that the statement "$\pi(n) \to \infty$" implies totality of a function eventually dominating every primitive recursive function, so it is **not provable in primitive recursive arithmetic**; they also give a purely finite-combinatorial ("embedding algebra") reformulation.
- **1995–1997.** Drápal gives structural/periodicity results for finite cyclic LD algebras and one-generated finite LD algebras.
- **2000–2010.** Dehornoy's monograph and his *Handbook of Set Theory* chapter consolidate the field; the ZFC-provability question remains stated as open.
- **2010s–2020s.** Renewed interest from quandle/rack and braided-set theory (Laver tables as self-distributive structures with cohomology), plus large-scale computation of $A_n$.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **Under I3:** fully solved. $\pi(n) \to \infty$, and $\mathcal{A}_j \cong \mathcal{F}$ is free (Laver).
- **Small $n$, explicitly:** $\pi(0)=\pi(1)=1$, $\pi(2)=2$, $\pi(3)=\pi(4)=4$, $\pi(5)=\pi(6)=\pi(7)=\pi(8)=8$, and $\pi(n)=16$ from $n=9$ onward for every $n$ within computational reach.
- **Computational range:** the tables $A_n$ have been generated for $n$ up to roughly $48$ using the row-recursion (memory $2^n$ per row, no need to store the full $2^n \times 2^n$ table). Every computed value has $\pi(n) \le 16$.
- **Ackermannian barrier (Dougherty 1993):** the least $n$ with $\pi(n) \ge 32$, *if it exists*, exceeds $\mathrm{Ack}(9,\mathrm{Ack}(8,\mathrm{Ack}(8,254)))$. So brute force can never witness the next jump.
- **Proof-theoretic case (Dougherty–Jech 1997):** unprovable in $\mathsf{PRA}$; the associated "next period" function is not primitive recursive.
- **ZFC-provable LD facts:** decidability of the word problem of $\mathcal{F}$, linearity of $\sqsubset$, and the braid order — all first proved from I3, then re-proved in $\mathsf{ZFC}$ (Dehornoy). This shows large-cardinal detours in this area are sometimes removable.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **The only known proof is genuinely infinitary.** Laver's argument needs an actual elementary $j$ whose critical sequence is cofinal in $\lambda$; the periodicity growth is read off from $j$'s reflection properties. No finite object in sight encodes $\lambda$-many levels of elementarity.
- **Growth blocks combinatorial induction.** Because the "period $\ge 2^k$ appears at level $n$" function is Ackermannian, any proof by an induction whose induction hypothesis is primitive-recursively bounded is ruled out by Dougherty–Jech. Standard finite combinatorics (double counting, pigeonhole, generating functions) produce elementary bounds and thus cannot work.
- **No algebraic invariant separates the cases.** Laver tables are not groups, quandles, or racks (they lack idempotence and invertibility); representation theory, character sums, and Fourier analysis on $\mathbb{Z}/2^n$ do not respect $*$, since $1 * q$ is not a homomorphic image of any group operation.
- **Computation is exhausted.** Verification stops around $n \approx 48$ while the next interesting $n$ is beyond any tower notation; increasing hardware changes nothing.
- **Independence tools do not apply.** The statement is $\Pi^0_2$ and downward-absolute-ish in the wrong direction: forcing cannot make it fail, and $\mathsf{ZFC}$-consistency-strength techniques (inner models for I3 do not exist) give no leverage.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: $\mathsf{I3} \vdash \pi(n) \to \infty$; $\mathsf{PRA} \nvdash \pi(n) \to \infty$; $\pi(n) = 16$ for $9 \le n \le 48$.

Wanted: $\mathsf{ZFC} \vdash \pi(n) \to \infty$, or a proof of independence.

The precise missing step is a **finitary surrogate for elementarity**. Laver's argument uses that $j$ reflects the statement "the first row has period $\ge 2^k$" from level $\kappa_{m+1}$ down to $\kappa_m$. A $\mathsf{ZFC}$ proof would need a purely combinatorial reflection principle on the coherent system $(A_n)_{n<\omega}$ — a self-map of $A_{n+1}$-data acting like $j$ without an ambient embedding — of strength above $\mathsf{PRA}$ but available in $\mathsf{ZFC}$ (or even $\mathsf{PA}$, whose status here is also open). Dehornoy's braid-order de-largecardinalisation is the model: there, the braid group supplied the surrogate. No analogous concrete structure is known for periodicity.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Self-distributive homotopy/cohomology.** Groups working on racks, quandles and braided sets (Dehornoy's school in France; knot-theoretic algebra groups in Japan and the US) compute cohomology of Laver tables, hoping period growth is detected by a cocycle invariant. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Reverse mathematics of large-cardinal combinatorics.** Continuation of the Dougherty–Jech calibration: locating "$\pi \to \infty$" between $\mathsf{PRA}$, $\mathsf{PA}$ and $\mathsf{ZFC}$; conjecturally it sits above $\mathsf{PA}$, in which case a $\mathsf{ZFC}$ proof would still be possible but would need transfinite induction beyond $\varepsilon_0$. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Generalised Laver tables.** Study of $A_n$-like structures on $\{1,\dots,m\}$ and on multi-generated free LD algebras, seeking a family where the periodicity jump happens at small parameters and can be analysed directly.
- **Computer-assisted structural search.** Formalisation of Laver-table recursions in proof assistants and search for exact combinatorial identities in the "$\pi = 16$ regime" that might extend by induction.
- **I3/I0 structure theory.** Work on rank-into-rank embeddings (Dimonte, Shi, and others) continues to refine what critical-sequence combinatorics I3 delivers, sharpening what a $\mathsf{ZFC}$ argument must replicate.

## 8. Future Work

1. **Find the surrogate structure.** Identify a $\mathsf{ZFC}$-definable object acting on $\bigsqcup_n A_n$ that mimics $j$'s shift $\kappa_m \mapsto \kappa_{m+1}$, in the spirit of the braid-group action on $\mathcal{F}$ (Laver 1996; Dehornoy 1994).
2. **Prove a sharp independence result.** Show "$\pi \to \infty$" implies $\mathrm{Con}$ of a theory beyond $\mathsf{PA}$ (or beyond $\mathsf{ZFC}$), which would settle the problem in the negative-for-$\mathsf{ZFC}$ direction.
3. **Improve the ordinal analysis.** Pin down the exact proof-theoretic ordinal of the statement; Dougherty–Jech reached "not primitive recursive", the next natural target is $\varepsilon_0$.
4. **Exploit freeness.** Since $\mathcal{F}$'s word problem is decidable in $\mathsf{ZFC}$, try to derive period growth from a decidable normal-form analysis of $\mathcal{F}$ rather than from embeddings.
5. **Alternative finite quotients.** Search for coherent finite LD quotients whose period growth is provable and which dominate $A_n$.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Constructing $A_2$ and computing $\pi(2)$.** Domain $\{1,2,3,4\}$, with $p * 1 = p+1$ mod $4$ (so $4 * 1 = 1$) and left distributivity.

Start with row $4$. From $4 * 1 = 1$ and induction via $4 * (q * 1) = (4*q)*(4*1)$, one gets $4 * q = q$: row $4$ is the identity row.

Row $3$: $3 * 1 = 4$. Then
$$3 * 2 = 3 * (1 * 1) = (3*1)*(3*1) = 4 * 4 = 4,$$
$$3 * 3 = 3 * (2 * 1) = (3*2)*(3*1) = 4 * 4 = 4, \qquad 3 * 4 = (3*3)*(3*1) = 4*4 = 4 .$$
Row $3$ is $(4,4,4,4)$.

Row $2$: $2 * 1 = 3$, and
$$2 * 2 = 2 * (1*1) = (2*1)*(2*1) = 3 * 3 = 4, \quad 2*3 = (2*2)*(2*1) = 4*3 = 3, \quad 2*4 = (2*3)*(2*1) = 3*3 = 4 .$$
Row $2$ is $(3,4,3,4)$, period $2$.

Row $1$: $1 * 1 = 2$, and
$$1*2 = (1*1)*(1*1) = 2*2 = 4, \quad 1*3 = 1*(2*1) = (1*2)*(1*1) = 4*2 = 2, \quad 1*4 = (1*3)*(1*1) = 2*2 = 4 .$$

$$A_2:\qquad
\begin{array}{c|cccc}
* & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4\\\hline
1 & 2 & 4 & 2 & 4\\
2 & 3 & 4 & 3 & 4\\
3 & 4 & 4 & 4 & 4\\
4 & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4
\end{array}$$

First row $(2,4,2,4)$ has period $2$, so $\pi(2)=2$. The same recursion gives first row $(2,4,6,8,2,4,6,8)$ in $A_3$, so $\pi(3)=4$.

**Where the problem bites.** Running this recursion up to $n = 9$ gives $\pi(9)=16$; running it as far as any machine allows (about $n = 48$) still gives $16$. Assuming I3, the value must eventually exceed $16$ — but by Dougherty's bound the first witness lies past $\mathrm{Ack}(9,\mathrm{Ack}(8,\mathrm{Ack}(8,254)))$. The gap between a four-line hand computation of $A_2$ and an unreachable-by-any-computation jump to period $32$ is exactly the critical sequence problem.
