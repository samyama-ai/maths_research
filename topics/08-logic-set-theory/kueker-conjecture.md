# Kueker's Conjecture

> **Topic:** Mathematical Logic & Set Theory · **ID:** `08-logic-set-theory/kueker-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $T$ be a complete first-order theory in a countable language $L$, with infinite models.

**Kueker's Conjecture.** If every uncountable model of $T$ is $\aleph_0$-saturated, then $T$ is $\aleph_0$-categorical or $T$ is categorical in power (i.e. categorical in every uncountable cardinal).

The two conclusions are exactly the known sources of the hypothesis, so the conjecture asserts a converse:

- If $T$ is $\aleph_0$-categorical, then by Ryll-Nardzewski every model of $T$ realizes every type over every finite set, so every model — countable or not — is $\aleph_0$-saturated.
- If $T$ is uncountably categorical, the unique model of size $\kappa > \aleph_0$ is the saturated model of power $\kappa$ (Morley), hence $\aleph_0$-saturated.

A proof must show that the "smallness in every uncountable cardinal" hypothesis forces one of these two shapes. A disproof requires an explicit countable complete $T$, neither $\aleph_0$-categorical nor uncountably categorical, all of whose uncountable models are $\aleph_0$-saturated. By Hrushovski's theorem (§4) such a $T$ must be unstable, so a counterexample cannot be produced by any of the standard stable constructions.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Work in a monster model $\mathfrak{C} \models T$. For $A \subseteq \mathfrak{C}$, $S_n(A)$ is the set of complete $n$-types over $A$.

**Saturation.** $M \models T$ is $\aleph_0$-saturated if for every finite $A \subseteq M$ and every $p \in S_1(A)$, $p$ is realized in $M$:
$$\forall A \subseteq M,\ |A| < \aleph_0,\ \forall p \in S_1(A)\ \exists a \in M \ \ M \models p(a).$$

**Smallness.** $T$ is *small* if $|S_n(\emptyset)| \le \aleph_0$ for all $n < \omega$; equivalently $|S_n(A)| \le \aleph_0$ for all finite $A$. Small theories have a prime (atomic) model over $\emptyset$.

**$\aleph_0$-categoricity (Ryll-Nardzewski / Engeler / Svenonius).** $T$ is $\aleph_0$-categorical iff $S_n(\emptyset)$ is finite for every $n$, iff every type in $S_n(\emptyset)$ is isolated and there are finitely many, iff $\mathrm{Aut}(M)$ acting on $M^n$ has finitely many orbits for the countable model $M$.

**Uncountable categoricity (Morley; Baldwin–Lachlan).** $T$ is categorical in some uncountable power iff in all, iff
$$T \text{ is } \omega\text{-stable and has no Vaughtian pair},$$
where a Vaughtian pair is a pair $N \prec M$ of models and an $L(N)$-formula $\varphi(x)$ with $\varphi(N) = \varphi(M)$ infinite and $N \ne M$. Under these conditions $T$ has a strongly minimal formula and every model is determined by the dimension of an associated pregeometry $\mathrm{acl}$.

**Stability hierarchy.** $T$ is $\lambda$-stable if $|S_1(A)| \le \lambda$ whenever $|A| \le \lambda$; $\omega$-stable, superstable, and stable are the standard degeneracies, controlled by forking and by the ranks $R^\infty$, $U$, $\mathrm{RM}$.

**Ehrenfeucht–Mostowski models.** For Skolemized $T'$ and any linear order $I$, the EM model $\mathrm{EM}(I)$ generated by an $I$-indexed order-indiscernible sequence realizes only the types in $\bigcup_n \{\mathrm{tp}(\tau(\bar b)) : \tau \text{ an } L\text{-term}, \bar b \text{ increasing}\}$, a countable set. This is the main "type-omitting in large cardinals" engine used against the hypothesis of the conjecture.

**Equivalent forms of the hypothesis.** "Every uncountable model of $T$ is $\aleph_0$-saturated" is equivalent to "every model of $T$ of power $\aleph_1$ is $\aleph_0$-saturated" (upward and downward Löwenheim–Skolem applied to the two-sorted expansion coding an omitted type).

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **Origin.** The problem is due to David W. Kueker, whose 1970s work on infinitary languages and back-and-forth systems (Kueker, *Löwenheim–Skolem and interpolation theorems in infinitary languages*, Bull. AMS 78, 1972) supplied the "all large models look small" viewpoint. The conjecture circulated as a problem through the late 1970s and appears in print as "Kueker's conjecture" in the stability-theoretic literature of the early 1980s.
- **1984.** Steven Buechler, *Kueker's conjecture for superstable theories* (J. Symbolic Logic 49), proves it when $T$ is superstable, using $U$-rank and regular types.
- **1989.** Ehud Hrushovski, *Kueker's conjecture for stable theories* (J. Symbolic Logic 54, 207–220), settles the full stable case: a stable $T$ satisfying the hypothesis is $\omega$-stable and unidimensional, hence $\aleph_0$-categorical or uncountably categorical. This remains the strongest general theorem.
- **Post-1990.** Attention shifts to simple and to unstable-but-tame theories; Ziv Shami, *On Kueker simple theories* (J. Symbolic Logic 70, 2005), gives the analogous analysis for simple theories under supersimplicity/low-rank hypotheses. No progress is known for theories with the strict order property.
- **Status.** Open in general for more than four decades; the stable case is completely closed, the unstable case essentially untouched.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

Cases where the conjecture is a theorem, with the hypothesis "every uncountable model of $T$ is $\aleph_0$-saturated":

1. **$T$ superstable** — Buechler (1984). The hypothesis forces $U$-rank $1$ regular types to be non-orthogonal to everything (unidimensionality), so $T$ is $\omega$-stable and uncountably categorical unless $S_n(\emptyset)$ is finite.
2. **$T$ stable** — Hrushovski (1989). Includes all stable non-superstable theories; the conclusion is that these cannot satisfy the hypothesis at all.
3. **$T$ $\omega$-stable** — immediate from (1): $\omega$-stable $\Rightarrow$ superstable.
4. **$T$ simple**, under supersimplicity plus elimination of hyperimaginaries or low $SU$-rank — Shami (2005) and successors.
5. **Any $T$**: the hypothesis implies $T$ is small (§10), hence $T$ has a prime model, at most $2^{\aleph_0}$ countable models, and no formula with $2^{\aleph_0}$ types over a finite set.
6. **$T$ with finitely many countable models and $T$ small unstable of low rank** — verified in scattered special classes (minimal and quasi-minimal first-order structures; Tanović, *Minimal first-order structures*, APAL 2011, gives the structure theory used for the rank-1 unstable cases).
7. **Explicit families**: strongly minimal theories, theories of vector spaces over a fixed countable field, $\mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{Z},s)$, algebraically closed fields of fixed characteristic (all uncountably categorical); $\mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{Q},<)$, random graph, infinite pure set (all $\aleph_0$-categorical). All satisfy hypothesis and conclusion.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No forking geometry outside stability.** Buechler's and Hrushovski's arguments run on regular types, orthogonality, weight, and dimension. In an unstable $T$ there is no canonical independence relation, so "unidimensional" has no accepted meaning and the Baldwin–Lachlan criterion cannot be invoked.
- **EM models are too coarse.** The one general tool that produces uncountable models omitting types — Ehrenfeucht–Mostowski models over indiscernibles — only omits types over $\emptyset$ (§10). Once $T$ is small, EM models may realize every type over every finite set, and the method gives nothing further. Refining it to omit a type over a *finite* parameter set in some uncountable model is exactly the unsolved construction.
- **Order defeats type-omitting.** With the strict order property, definable orderings let one build large models, but the standard Skolem-hull constructions produce highly homogeneous, hence $\aleph_0$-saturated, models. There is no analogue of the two-cardinal (Vaught) machinery: Vaughtian pairs are handled by Morley's two-cardinal theorem only under $\omega$-stability.
- **No candidate counterexample.** Hrushovski-style ab initio constructions (amalgamation with a predimension) are stable or simple by design, so they fall inside the solved region. Unstable small theories that are not uncountably categorical (e.g. many o-minimal theories) fail the hypothesis for easy reasons, so they give no template for the delicate case.
- **Absoluteness / cardinal arithmetic is not the issue.** Unlike Vaught's conjecture, the statement has no known descriptive-set-theoretic reformulation to attack; forcing and $\Sigma^1_1$ methods provide no leverage.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: stable (and, conditionally, supersimple) $T$ satisfying the hypothesis is $\aleph_0$-categorical or uncountably categorical. Also proven for arbitrary $T$: the hypothesis implies smallness.

Missing: for **unstable** $T$, the hypothesis says nothing usable. The exact step to be crossed:

> Show that if $T$ is small, unstable, and every model of $T$ of power $\aleph_1$ is $\aleph_0$-saturated, then a contradiction follows — i.e. construct $M \models T$ with $|M| = \aleph_1$, a finite $A \subset M$, and $p \in S_1(A)$ omitted in $M$.

Equivalently, one must prove that the hypothesis implies $\omega$-stability. Every known route to $\omega$-stability from a smallness hypothesis (counting types over countable sets, rank computations, order property analysis) requires stability as an input, so the implication "hypothesis $\Rightarrow$ no order property" is the single unbroken link.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Simple and NSOP$_n$ theories.** Extending Shami's supersimple analysis to simple theories without extra hypotheses; the obstruction is elimination of hyperimaginaries. Groups in Israel and the UK (Hebrew University; Leeds; Manchester) maintain this line. *(frontier — verify)*
- **NIP and dp-minimality.** Attempts to prove the conjecture for NIP theories using honest definitions, invariant types, and the Shelah expansion; the expected theorem is that a small NIP theory satisfying the hypothesis is $\aleph_0$-categorical. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Belgrade school (Tanović and collaborators).** Structure theory of minimal and weakly minimal first-order structures, and of small unstable theories with few countable models, aimed at ruling out unstable counterexamples of low rank.
- **Interaction with Vaught's conjecture.** Since the hypothesis implies smallness, and uncountably categorical theories have $1$ or $\aleph_0$ countable models (Baldwin–Lachlan), Kueker's conjecture implies a sharp count of countable models for the class; work relating the two conjectures via topological dynamics on type spaces is ongoing. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Future Work

- Prove **hypothesis $\Rightarrow$ non-existence of the order property** directly, by turning a definable order $\varphi(x,y)$ into an $\aleph_1$-sized model omitting a type over the finitely many parameters naming a cut.
- Develop a cardinal-free "dimension" for small unstable theories, so that the Buechler/Hrushovski unidimensionality argument has a target.
- Settle the conjecture for the concrete tame classes in order of strength: o-minimal and weakly o-minimal, dp-minimal, NIP, NTP$_2$, simple.
- Search deliberately for counterexamples using generic constructions with a definable order (SOP$_n$ hierarchy), where all known positive theorems are silent.
- Clarify whether the hypothesis, restricted to models of power $\aleph_1$, is equivalent to a $\Sigma^1_1$ property of a countable code for $T$, which would open descriptive-set-theoretic attacks.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**(a) The hypothesis implies $T$ is small.** Let $T'$ be a Skolemization of $T$ in a countable language, and let $I$ be a linear order of size $\aleph_1$. Form the EM model $M = \mathrm{EM}(I) \models T'$, so $|M| = \aleph_1$. Every element of $M$ is $\tau(b_1,\dots,b_n)$ for a Skolem term $\tau$ and $b_1 < \dots < b_n$ in $I$. By indiscernibility,
$$\mathrm{tp}\big(\tau(b_1,\dots,b_n)\big) \text{ depends only on } \tau,$$
so $M$ realizes at most countably many $1$-types over $\emptyset$ — one per term. If $M \upharpoonright L$ is $\aleph_0$-saturated it realizes *every* $p \in S_1(\emptyset)$. Hence $|S_1(\emptyset)| \le \aleph_0$, and the same argument in each arity gives $|S_n(\emptyset)| \le \aleph_0$: $T$ is small. So any counterexample to Kueker's conjecture is a small theory, and (Hrushovski) an unstable one.

**(b) A non-small theory, made explicit.** Let $L = \{P_i : i < \omega\}$, unary, and let $T$ say that every finite Boolean combination $\bigcap_{i \in s} P_i \cap \bigcap_{j \in t} \neg P_j$ ($s,t$ finite disjoint) is infinite. $T$ is complete, $\omega$-stable-free of structure but not small: $|S_1(\emptyset)| = 2^{\aleph_0}$, one type per subset $X \subseteq \omega$ via $\{P_i(x) : i \in X\} \cup \{\neg P_j(x) : j \notin X\}$.

Build $M$ of size $\aleph_1$ realizing only the *finite-support* types: take $M = \bigsqcup_{s \in [\omega]^{<\omega}} A_s$ with $|A_s| = \aleph_1$, and set $P_i^M = \bigcup \{A_s : i \in s\}$. Each finite Boolean combination with positive part $s$ contains $A_s$, so $M \models T$ and $|M| = \aleph_1$. But $p(x) = \{P_i(x) : i < \omega\}$ is a complete type over $\emptyset$ omitted in $M$, so $M$ is not $\aleph_0$-saturated. The hypothesis fails, as the conjecture's contrapositive demands: $T$ is neither $\aleph_0$-categorical ($S_1(\emptyset)$ infinite) nor uncountably categorical (models of size $\aleph_1$ differ by which $A_s$ are large).

**(c) The two allowed outcomes.** $T_1 = \mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{Q},<)$: any model is a dense linear order without endpoints, and a $1$-type over a finite $A = \{a_1 < \dots < a_n\}$ names a cut, realized by density and lack of endpoints — every model is $\aleph_0$-saturated, and $T_1$ is $\aleph_0$-categorical. $T_2 = \mathrm{Th}(\mathbb{Z},s)$ with $s$ the successor: models are disjoint unions of $\mathbb{Z}$-chains, and a model of power $\kappa > \aleph_0$ has $\kappa$ chains. A $1$-type over a finite $A$ either fixes a distance $s^k(a)$ or asserts infinite distance from all of $A$; the latter is realized in any chain missing $A$, of which there are $\kappa$ many. So all uncountable models are $\aleph_0$-saturated, and $T_2$ is categorical in power but not $\aleph_0$-categorical (the countable models are $n$ chains, $1 \le n \le \aleph_0$). These are precisely the two conclusions the conjecture claims are exhaustive.
