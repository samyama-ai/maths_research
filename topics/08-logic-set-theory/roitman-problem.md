# Roitman's Problem

> **Topic:** Mathematical Logic & Set Theory · **ID:** `08-logic-set-theory/roitman-problem` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

A superatomic Boolean algebra (sBa) is **thin-very tall** if it has height $\omega_2$ and width $\omega$ — its Cantor–Bendixson sequence is $\langle \omega \rangle_{\omega_2}$, i.e. every level is countably infinite and there are $\omega_2$ levels. Equivalently (via Stone duality) there is a locally compact, scattered, Hausdorff (LCS) space of height $\omega_2$ all of whose Cantor–Bendixson levels are countable.

**Roitman's problem.** Is the existence of a thin-very tall superatomic Boolean algebra consistent with $\mathfrak{d} = \omega_1$?

Stated as an implication, the conjectured answer is negative:

$$\mathfrak{d} = \omega_1 \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{there is no sBa with cardinal sequence } \langle \omega\rangle_{\omega_2}.$$

Here $\mathfrak{d}$ is the **dominating number**: the least cardinality of a family $D \subseteq \omega^\omega$ such that every $g \in \omega^\omega$ is eventually dominated by some $f \in D$.

A complete solution is either (a) a forcing construction (or ZFC theorem from $\mathfrak{d}=\omega_1$ plus $\neg$CH) producing a model of $\mathfrak{d} = \omega_1$ containing a thin-very tall sBa, or (b) a ZFC proof that a thin-very tall sBa yields a dominating family of size $\omega_1$ being impossible — i.e. that its existence implies $\mathfrak{d} \ge \omega_2$.

Note that CH is *not* a counterexample: CH gives $\mathfrak{d}=\omega_1$ and also (Section 10) outright forbids thin-very tall algebras, since a thin LCS space has cardinality at most $\mathfrak{c}$. The problem's content is therefore concentrated in models with $\neg$CH and $\mathfrak{d} = \omega_1$ (e.g. the random real and Sacks models, where $\mathfrak{d}=\omega_1 < \mathfrak{c} = \omega_2$).

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Superatomicity.** A Boolean algebra $B$ is superatomic if every subalgebra (equivalently, every homomorphic image) is atomic. Define the Cantor–Bendixson filtration by $I_0 = \{0\}$, $I_{\alpha+1} = $ the ideal generated by $I_\alpha$ together with all $a$ whose image is an atom of $B / I_\alpha$, and $I_\lambda = \bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda} I_\alpha$ at limits. Then
$$\operatorname{ht}(B) = \min\{\alpha : B/I_\alpha \text{ is trivial or atomless}\},\qquad \operatorname{wd}(B) = \sup_{\alpha<\operatorname{ht}(B)} |\mathrm{At}(B/I_\alpha)| .$$
$B$ is superatomic iff $B / I_\alpha = \{0\}$ for some $\alpha$.

**Topological dual.** $B$ is superatomic iff its Stone space $S(B)$ is scattered (every nonempty subspace has an isolated point). Removing the compactness point, one works with LCS spaces $X$; $X^{(\alpha)}$ is the $\alpha$-th Cantor–Bendixson derivative, the level is $I_\alpha(X) = X^{(\alpha)} \setminus X^{(\alpha+1)}$, and
$$\operatorname{ht}(X)=\min\{\alpha: X^{(\alpha)}=\emptyset\},\qquad \mathrm{SEQ}(X) = \big\langle |I_\alpha(X)| : \alpha < \operatorname{ht}(X)\big\rangle .$$
The **cardinal sequence** $\mathrm{SEQ}(X)$ is the central invariant. $\mathcal{C}(\alpha)$ denotes the class of cardinal sequences of length $\alpha$ realized by some LCS space.

**Names.** $\langle \omega\rangle_{\omega_1}$: *thin-tall*. $\langle\omega\rangle_{\omega_2}$: *thin-very tall*.

**Cardinal characteristics.** For $f,g \in \omega^\omega$, $f \le^* g$ iff $f(n)\le g(n)$ for all but finitely many $n$. Then $\mathfrak{b} = \min\{|F| : F$ unbounded in $(\omega^\omega,\le^*)\}$ and $\mathfrak{d} = \min\{|D| : D$ cofinal in $(\omega^\omega,\le^*)\}$, with $\omega_1 \le \mathfrak{b}\le\mathfrak{d}\le\mathfrak{c}$.

**$\Delta$-functions (Baumgartner–Shelah).** A function $f : [\omega_2]^2 \to [\omega]^{<\omega}$ is a $\Delta$-function if for every uncountable family $\mathcal{A}$ of finite subsets of $\omega_2$ there is an uncountable $\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{A}$ forming a $\Delta$-system with root $r$ such that for all $a\ne b \in \mathcal{B}$ and all $\alpha\in a\setminus r$, $\beta \in b\setminus r$ with $\alpha<\beta$, one has $f(\{\alpha,\beta\}) \subseteq$ a fixed finite set indexed by the enumeration. The point of the definition: a $\Delta$-function makes the natural finite-approximation poset for building $\langle\omega\rangle_{\omega_2}$ ccc.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1976.** Rajagopalan (*A chain compact space which is not strongly scattered*, Israel J. Math. 23) constructs a thin-tall LCS space from CH, the first "tall with countable levels" example.
- **1978.** Juhász and Weiss (*On thin-tall scattered spaces*, Colloq. Math. 40) remove all hypotheses: **in ZFC, for every $\alpha < \omega_2$ there is an LCS space of height $\alpha$ with all levels countable.** So $\langle\omega\rangle_\alpha \in \mathcal{C}(\alpha)$ for all $\alpha<\omega_2$; height $\omega_2$ is exactly the ZFC boundary.
- **1985.** Roitman (*Height and width of superatomic Boolean algebras*, Proc. AMS 94) develops the general height/width theory, including the bound $|B| \le 2^{\operatorname{wd}(B)}$ that forces $\mathfrak{c}\ge\omega_2$ for thin-very tall algebras. The problem in this entry emerges from that circle and is recorded in her Handbook chapter (1989).
- **1985.** Just (*Two consistency results concerning thin-tall Boolean algebras*, Algebra Universalis 20) shows the existence of a thin-very tall algebra does not follow from $\neg$CH: in the model obtained by adding $\omega_2$ Cohen reals to a model of CH there is none.
- **1987.** Baumgartner and Shelah (*Remarks on superatomic Boolean algebras*, APAL 33) prove **Con(ZFC) $\Rightarrow$ Con(there is a thin-very tall sBa)**: first force a $\Delta$-function on $\omega_2$ (via $\sigma$-closed then ccc machinery), then use a ccc finite-approximation poset of size $\omega_2$. The resulting model has $\mathfrak{c} = \mathfrak{d} = \omega_2$.
- **1984 / 1996 / 2001.** Velleman shows a simplified $(\omega_1,1)$-morass with linear limits yields a $\Delta$-function; Rabus (*An $\omega_2$-minimal Boolean algebra*, Trans. AMS 348) and Martínez (*A consistency result on thin-very tall Boolean algebras*, Israel J. Math. 123) refine and extend the construction to longer heights and richer sequences.
- **2004–2011.** Juhász–Soukup–Szentmiklóssy analyze cardinal sequences in Cohen extensions; Martínez–Soukup build superatomic algebras from Koszmider's strongly unbounded functions. All constructions to date live in models where $\mathfrak{d} = \mathfrak{c} \ge \omega_2$, which is precisely what keeps Roitman's problem open.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **Height $<\omega_2$, width $\omega$: settled in ZFC** (Juhász–Weiss 1978). No hypothesis on $\mathfrak{d}$ is needed, so the problem is sharp at height exactly $\omega_2$.
- **CH case: settled positively** (the negative implication holds). CH $\Rightarrow \mathfrak{c}=\omega_1 \Rightarrow$ every thin LCS space has size $\le\omega_1 \Rightarrow$ no height $\omega_2$. See Section 10.
- **More generally $\mathfrak{c} = \omega_1$** is the only cardinal-arithmetic case fully resolved; the constraint is $\mathfrak{c}\ge\omega_2$, not $\mathfrak{d}\ge\omega_2$.
- **Consistency of the object itself: settled** (Baumgartner–Shelah 1987), but only with $\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{c}=\omega_2$.
- **Cohen models: no thin-very tall** for the standard $\omega_2$-Cohen extension of a CH model (Just 1985) — a model with $\mathfrak{d}=\omega_2$ and no example, showing $\mathfrak{d}>\omega_1$ is not sufficient either.
- **Width $\omega_1$, height $\omega_2$** ($\langle\omega_1\rangle_{\omega_2}$) is realizable under much weaker hypotheses (e.g. from GCH-type assumptions, Martínez; Juhász–Soukup–Szentmiklóssy), isolating countable width as the difficulty.
- **Test models where the answer is unknown:** the random real model and the countable support Sacks iteration over a CH model, both of which satisfy $\mathfrak{d} = \omega_1 < \mathfrak{c} = \omega_2$ because the forcings are $\omega^\omega$-bounding.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Every known construction is Cohen-like.** Thin-very tall algebras are built by finite-approximation ccc posets that decide finitely many "level $\alpha$ meets neighbourhood of level $\beta$" constraints. Such posets add Cohen reals; adding $\omega_2$ Cohen reals forces $\mathfrak{d}=\omega_2$. No construction is known that survives an $\omega^\omega$-bounding iteration.
- **$\omega^\omega$-bounding forcings are the wrong tool.** Random and Sacks forcing preserve $\mathfrak{d}=\omega_1$, but they are (in the relevant iterations) proper and add no unbounded reals, and the finitary $\Delta$-system combinatorics that makes the Baumgartner–Shelah poset ccc appears to require exactly the unbounded/Cohen-like genericity these forcings avoid.
- **No ZFC implication is known in the other direction.** There is no proof that a thin-very tall algebra codes a dominating family. The levels are countable, so the natural attempt — reading a function $f_\alpha \in \omega^\omega$ off the neighbourhood trace of a point at level $\alpha$ on a fixed countable level — produces $\omega_2$ functions with no evident unboundedness, since traces are only *almost disjoint*, not $\le^*$-increasing.
- **$\Delta$-functions are not a cardinal characteristic.** Their existence is a purely combinatorial statement on $[\omega_2]^2$ with no known reflection into $(\omega^\omega,\le^*)$, so the standard Cichoń/van Douwen technology does not attach to it.
- **Absoluteness barriers.** The statement "there is a thin-very tall sBa" is $\Sigma^2_1$-like in complexity over $H(\omega_3)$; it is neither preserved nor destroyed by simple forcing operations in any known uniform way, which blocks the usual iteration-preservation arguments.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: (i) thin LCS of height $\alpha$ for all $\alpha<\omega_2$ in ZFC; (ii) $\mathfrak{c}\ge\omega_2$ is necessary for height $\omega_2$; (iii) height $\omega_2$ is consistent, in a model with $\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{c}=\omega_2$.

The general statement asks for the value of $\mathfrak{d}$ to be decoupled from the construction. The exact step to cross is one of:

1. **Construction side:** design a poset (or a ground-model combinatorial object such as a $\Delta$-function usable over a random/Sacks extension) that produces the $\omega_2$ levels while adding no unbounded real, and prove it preserves a ground-model dominating family of size $\omega_1$; or
2. **ZFC side:** extract from any thin LCS space of height $\omega_2$ a $\le^*$-cofinal family of size $\omega_2$-many functions witnessing $\mathfrak{d}\ge\omega_2$ — i.e. convert the almost-disjointness of level traces into unboundedness.

Nobody has produced either half; the gap is exactly between "almost disjoint families of countable traces" and "unbounded/dominating families of functions".

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Barcelona / Budapest axis.** J. C. Martínez (Universitat de Barcelona) and L. Soukup (Rényi Institute) continue the programme of building superatomic algebras from combinatorial functions on $\omega_2$ (strongly unbounded functions à la Koszmider, semimorasses). The programme's stated aim includes weakening the cardinal-characteristic side effects of the forcings used. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Cardinal-sequence classification.** Determining $\mathcal{C}(\alpha)$ for $\alpha\ge\omega_2$ under GCH and its failures remains active (Juhász, Soukup, Szentmiklóssy and coauthors); Roitman's problem is the countable-width fragment of that classification.
- **Preservation-theoretic attacks.** Attempts to run the Baumgartner–Shelah poset inside a countable-support iteration of $\omega^\omega$-bounding forcing, or to make the finite-approximation poset $\mathbb{P}$ "Sacks-indestructible", are the most-discussed strategy. No published success. *(frontier — verify)*
- **PCF / higher-gap analogues.** Whether thin LCS spaces of height $\omega_3$ (width $\omega$) are consistent is a related open question that some groups treat as a testbed for the same machinery.

## 8. Future Work

- Isolate a **new invariant** implied by the existence of a thin-very tall algebra and compare it to $\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{d}$ — the analogue of what $\mathfrak{a}$ and $\mathfrak{s}$ do for almost disjoint and splitting families.
- Determine whether **$\mathfrak{b}=\omega_1$** (weaker than $\mathfrak{d}=\omega_1$) is compatible with a thin-very tall algebra; a positive answer there would immediately reshape the problem.
- Settle the **random real model** concretely: does adding $\omega_2$ random reals to a model of CH (or to a model with a $\Delta$-function) produce a thin-very tall algebra? This is the cleanest single test case.
- Study **$\Delta$-functions under $\omega^\omega$-bounding forcing**: are they preserved by random/Sacks forcing? If yes, one needs only a ccc-in-the-extension version of the Baumgartner–Shelah poset that is itself bounding.
- Explore **$\sigma$-centred or measure-algebra approximations** to the level-construction, replacing finite conditions by conditions of positive measure.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Claim.** If $X$ is an LCS space with all Cantor–Bendixson levels countable, then $|X| \le \mathfrak{c}$; hence CH implies $\operatorname{ht}(X) \le \omega_1$ and there is no thin-very tall superatomic Boolean algebra.

*Proof.*

1. **$X$ is zero-dimensional.** A locally compact Hausdorff scattered space has a base of compact open sets: if $x \in U$ with $\overline{U}$ compact, then $\overline{U}$ is compact Hausdorff scattered, hence totally disconnected, hence zero-dimensional.
2. **Isolated points are dense.** Let $V \ne \emptyset$ be open. As a subspace $V$ is scattered, so it has a point $p$ isolated in $V$; since $V$ is open in $X$, $\{p\}$ is open in $X$. So $p \in I_0(X)$ and $I_0(X)\cap V \ne \emptyset$. Thus $I_0(X)$ is dense in $X$, and $|I_0(X)| \le \omega$ by hypothesis.
3. **Compact open sets are recovered from level 0.** Let $U$ be compact open. $U$ is closed ($X$ Hausdorff). Applying step 2 inside $U$: $U \cap I_0(X)$ is dense in $U$. Hence $U = \overline{U \cap I_0(X)}$. So the map
$$U \;\longmapsto\; U \cap I_0(X) \in \mathcal{P}(I_0(X))$$
is **injective** on compact open sets, giving at most $2^{\omega} = \mathfrak{c}$ compact open subsets of $X$.
4. **Each point is named by one such set.** Let $x \in I_\alpha(X)$. Since $X^{(\alpha+1)}$ is closed, $X \setminus X^{(\alpha+1)}$ is open and contains $x$ as an isolated point of $X^{(\alpha)}\setminus X^{(\alpha+1)}$; by step 1 choose a compact open $U_x \subseteq X\setminus X^{(\alpha+1)}$ with $U_x \cap I_\alpha(X) = \{x\}$. Then $\alpha$ is the largest level meeting $U_x$, and $x$ is its unique point there, so $U_x$ determines $x$: the map $x \mapsto U_x$ is injective.
5. Combining, $|X| \le |\{\text{compact open } U\}| \le \mathfrak{c}$. $\square$

**Numerical instance.** Under CH, $\mathfrak{c}=\aleph_1$, so $|X|\le\aleph_1$. A space of height $\omega_2$ with all levels nonempty has $|X| \ge \aleph_2 > \aleph_1$ — contradiction. So the CH instance of Roitman's problem is *positive*: $\mathfrak{d}=\omega_1$ (as CH gives) together with $\mathfrak{c}=\omega_1$ rules out $\langle\omega\rangle_{\omega_2}$.

**Why this does not settle the problem.** The argument uses $\mathfrak{c}$, not $\mathfrak{d}$. In the random real model over a model of CH one has
$$\mathfrak{d} = \omega_1, \qquad \mathfrak{c} = \omega_2,$$
because random forcing is $\omega^\omega$-bounding (the ground-model reals stay dominating) while the measure algebra adds $\omega_2$ reals. Step 5 then permits $|X| = \omega_2$, so no cardinality obstruction exists, and whether the random extension contains a thin-very tall algebra is exactly the open case. Contrast the Baumgartner–Shelah model, where the example exists but the ccc finite-condition forcing adds Cohen reals and pushes $\mathfrak{d}$ to $\omega_2$.
