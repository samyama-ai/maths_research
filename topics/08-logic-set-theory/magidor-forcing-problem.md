# Magidor Forcing Problem

> **Topic:** Mathematical Logic & Set Theory · **ID:** `08-logic-set-theory/magidor-forcing-problem` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Magidor forcing changes the cofinality of a measurable cardinal $\kappa$ to a prescribed regular uncountable $\delta<\kappa$, adding a closed unbounded subset $C\subseteq\kappa$ of order type $\delta$ without collapsing cardinals. The **Magidor Forcing Problem** is the classification of the generic extension it produces, in two coupled forms.

**(A) Intermediate-model classification.** Let $\vec{U}$ be a coherent sequence of measures on $\kappa$ with $o^{\vec U}(\kappa)=\alpha$, let $\mathbb{M}[\vec U]$ be the associated Magidor–Radin forcing, and let $C$ be $\mathbb{M}[\vec U]$-generic over $V$. Is every model $M$ with
$$V\subseteq M\subseteq V[C],\qquad M\models \mathrm{ZFC},$$
of the form $M=V[C\cap A]$ for some $A\in V[C]$ — i.e. is every intermediate model itself a Magidor-type extension by a *subsequence* of the generic club?

**(B) Genericity criterion.** Is there a Mathias-style, purely combinatorial characterization of which clubs $C\subseteq\kappa$ of order type $\delta$ are $\mathbb{M}[\vec U]$-generic over $V$, valid for arbitrary $\alpha=o^{\vec U}(\kappa)$ up to $\kappa^{++}$?

A complete solution to (A) is a theorem giving, for every coherent $\vec U$, an explicit parametrization of the lattice of intermediate ZFC models; a disproof is a $\vec U$, a generic $C$, and an intermediate $M$ provably not generated by any subsequence of $C$.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Coherent sequences (Mitchell).** $\vec U=\langle U(\beta,\gamma):\beta\le\kappa,\ \gamma<o^{\vec U}(\beta)\rangle$ is *coherent* if each $U(\beta,\gamma)$ is a normal measure on $\beta$ and, with $j_{\beta\gamma}:V\to M_{\beta\gamma}\cong \mathrm{Ult}(V,U(\beta,\gamma))$,
$$j_{\beta\gamma}(\vec U)\restriction(\beta+1)=\vec U\restriction(\beta,\gamma),$$
so the ultrapower sees exactly the measures below index $\gamma$ on $\beta$. The *Mitchell order* $o^{\vec U}(\beta)$ is the number of measures on $\beta$ in $\vec U$.

**Magidor–Radin conditions.** A condition is a finite sequence
$$p=\langle (\beta_1,A_1),\dots,(\beta_n,A_n),(\kappa,A)\rangle,$$
$\beta_1<\dots<\beta_n<\kappa$, where each $A_i$ (resp. $A$) is a *measure-one set* for all measures on $\beta_i$ (resp. all $U(\kappa,\gamma)$, $\gamma<\alpha$): $A\in\bigcap_{\gamma<\alpha}U(\kappa,\gamma)$. Extension $q\le p$ allows (i) shrinking the $A_i$, and (ii) *adding* points $\beta\in A_i$ together with sets $B\subseteq\beta$ of measure one for all measures on $\beta$. The generic object is $C=\bigcup\{\,\mathrm{stem}(p):p\in G\,\}$, a club in $\kappa$.

**Order type.** If $o^{\vec U}(\kappa)=1$ this is Prikry forcing and $\mathrm{ot}(C)=\omega$. In general
$$\mathrm{ot}(C)\ \text{is determined by }\alpha:\quad \alpha=\delta \text{ (indecomposable) } \Rightarrow \mathrm{cf}(\kappa)^{V[C]}=\delta .$$
Magidor's original 1978 forcing uses a $\delta$-increasing sequence of measures on $\kappa$ to make $\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)=\delta$ for uncountable regular $\delta<\kappa$.

**Key structural facts.**
- *Prikry property:* for every statement $\sigma$ and condition $p$ there is a direct (stem-preserving) extension $p^*\le^* p$ deciding $\sigma$.
- $\mathbb{M}[\vec U]$ has the $\kappa^+$-c.c. and $(\mathbb{M},\le^*)$ is $\kappa$-closed; hence **no bounded subsets of $\kappa$ are added** and all cardinals are preserved.
- *Mathias criterion (Prikry case, $\alpha=1$):* $c=\langle \kappa_n:n<\omega\rangle$ is Prikry-generic for $U$ over $V$ **iff** $c$ is almost contained in every $A\in U$:
$$\forall A\in U\ \exists m\ \forall n\ge m\ (\kappa_n\in A).$$

**The classification statement.** For $A\subseteq \mathrm{ot}(C)$ in $V[C]$, $C\cap A$ (the corresponding subsequence) generates an intermediate model $V[C\restriction A]$. Problem (A) asks whether the map $A\mapsto V[C\restriction A]$ is onto the intermediate ZFC models.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1970, Prikry.** Prikry forcing singularizes a measurable to cofinality $\omega$ with no new bounded subsets.
- **1973, Mathias.** Genericity criterion for Prikry sequences; the template all later criteria imitate.
- **1977–78, Magidor.** *On the singular cardinals problem I, II* (Israel J. Math.; Annals of Math.) and *Changing cofinality of cardinals* (Fund. Math.) introduce the forcing: from a coherent sequence one changes $\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)$ to any regular $\delta<\kappa$, and one obtains the first models with $\aleph_\omega$ strong limit and $2^{\aleph_\omega}>\aleph_{\omega+1}$.
- **1982, Radin.** *Adding closed cofinal sequences to large cardinals* — the generalization keeping $\kappa$ regular (even measurable) when $\vec U$ is long enough; Magidor forcing is the "short" case.
- **1980s–2000s, Gitik.** Exact consistency strengths for SCH failure ($o(\kappa)=\kappa^{++}$), extender-based variants, and the *Prikry-type forcings* chapter of the *Handbook of Set Theory* (2010) — the standard reference organizing all of these as one family.
- **2005, Fuchs.** Characterization of generalized Prikry/Magidor sequences via a "Mathias-like" condition for certain iterated and product forms.
- **2021–2022, Benhamou & Gitik.** *Intermediate models of Magidor–Radin forcing*, Parts I and II — the current SOTA on (A): a full classification under bounds on $o^{\vec U}(\kappa)$, and a subsequence-parametrization theorem.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Case | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| $o^{\vec U}(\kappa)=1$ (Prikry) | Every intermediate ZFC model is $V$ or $V[C]$ — the extension is *minimal* | Gitik–Kanovei–Koepke |
| Tree-Prikry / minimal singularizing forcings | Minimality holds; explicit minimal forcing singularizing $\kappa$ | Koepke–Räsch–Schlicht, JSL 2013 |
| $o^{\vec U}(\kappa)<\kappa$, $\vec U$ with each $o^{\vec U}(\beta)$ below the relevant bound | Every intermediate model equals $V[C\restriction A]$ for some $A\subseteq \mathrm{ot}(C)$ in $V[C]$; the lattice of intermediate models is isomorphic to a lattice of "$\vec U$-closed" subsets | Benhamou–Gitik, Part I (Israel J. Math., 2022) |
| Magidor forcing proper, $\mathrm{ot}(C)=\delta$ regular uncountable | Sets of ordinals in $V[C]$ are covered/decomposed: every $X\subseteq V$ in $V[C]$ lies in a $V$-set of small size, giving $V[X]=V[C\restriction A]$ for suitable $A$ | Benhamou–Gitik, *Sets in Prikry and Magidor generic extensions* (APAL, 2021) |
| Genericity criterion, $\alpha$ finite or $\alpha<\kappa$ with mild coherence | Mathias-style criterion available: $C$ generic iff each "block" is almost contained in the corresponding measure-one sets | Fuchs (2005); Benhamou–Gitik, Part II (APAL, 2022) |
| $\kappa$ remains measurable (Radin regime) | Classification results for Radin forcing under $o^{\vec U}(\kappa)$ small; general Radin case open | Benhamou–Gitik, Part II |

Also settled: cardinal and cofinality preservation for all $\delta<\kappa$ regular; $\mathrm{cf}(\kappa)^{V[C]}=\mathrm{cf}(\mathrm{ot}(C))$; no new bounded subsets of $\kappa$; $\kappa^+$-c.c.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Failure of minimality above $o(\kappa)=1$.** The Prikry proof (Gitik–Kanovei–Koepke) uses that a single measure gives an essentially one-dimensional generic object. With $o^{\vec U}(\kappa)\ge 2$ the club $C$ has a nontrivial internal *order structure*: points of $C$ carry Mitchell ranks, and subsequences chosen by rank give genuinely intermediate models. The lattice becomes rich, and no minimality argument survives.
- **Non-definable subsequences.** The candidate models $V[C\restriction A]$ are indexed by $A\in V[C]$, but $V[C]$ itself contains sets built by *diagonal* mixing of $C$ across Mitchell ranks. Showing that such a set is interdefinable with a genuine subsequence requires a covering/decomposition lemma whose known proofs consume the assumption $o^{\vec U}(\kappa)<\kappa$.
- **The Prikry property gives decisions, not canonical names.** $\le^*$-closure lets one decide statements without changing stems, but it yields no *canonical form* for an arbitrary name of a set of ordinals when the measure sequence is long: the "geometric" analysis of names breaks once the direct extension order is no longer $\kappa$-closed relative to the relevant coherence.
- **Long sequences ($\alpha\ge\kappa$).** When $o^{\vec U}(\kappa)\ge\kappa$ one enters the Radin regime; $\kappa$ may stay regular or measurable, the club $C$ is no longer of type $<\kappa$, and iterated-ultrapower comparison arguments — the engine of the Mathias criterion — cease to terminate in the required uniform way.
- **Inner-model dependence.** Optimal criteria appeal to the structure of $\vec U$ inside $L[\vec U]$-like models. Beyond $o(\kappa)=\kappa^{++}$ the fine structure needed (Mitchell–Steel-level) is available but the combinatorial extraction of a genericity criterion from it has no known route.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: classification and Mathias-style criteria for $o^{\vec U}(\kappa)$ **below $\kappa$**, with additional coherence bounds on the measures assigned to points below $\kappa$ (Benhamou–Gitik). Asserted: the same for *all* coherent $\vec U$, including $o^{\vec U}(\kappa)\ge\kappa$, and for extender-based Magidor/Radin variants (Merimovich).

The precise step to cross: produce, for arbitrary $\vec U$, a **decomposition lemma** stating that every $X\subseteq\mathrm{Ord}$ in $V[C]$ satisfies $V[X]=V[C\restriction A]$ for some $A\in V[C]$ — equivalently, that no set in $V[C]$ codes "half" of the information carried by a single point of $C$ of high Mitchell rank. All existing proofs of this lemma use a rank-induction on $o^{\vec U}$ that requires the induction hypothesis at $\kappa$ itself, which is exactly what fails when $o^{\vec U}(\kappa)\ge\kappa$.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Benhamou (Rutgers / Illinois–Chicago) and Gitik (Tel Aviv)** continue the intermediate-model program; extensions to *tree Prikry*, *Prikry with non-normal ultrafilters*, and the interaction with the Galvin property are the active line. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Merimovich (Open University of Israel)** develops *supercompact extender-based Magidor–Radin forcing*, where the analogous classification is wide open; the extender-based setting is where SCH failures at $\aleph_{\omega_1}$ with large gaps are engineered.
- **Ben-Neria, Hayut, Unger** and collaborators study which combinatorial principles (tree property, stationary reflection, Galvin's property) survive Magidor forcing — a proxy for how much structure the generic club carries. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Descriptive/definability angle:** Mathias-style criteria are being recast as "genericity is a $\Sigma^0_2$-type condition over $\vec U$", connecting to the theory of ultrafilters on $\kappa$ and Tukey order. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Future Work

1. **Prove the decomposition lemma at $o^{\vec U}(\kappa)=\kappa^{++}$**, the level where SCH first fails; this is the smallest open case with independent interest.
2. **Extender-based classification.** Determine whether intermediate models of extender-based Magidor forcing are parametrized by subsequences *plus* extender-coordinate subsets — the expected two-parameter answer.
3. **Uniform genericity criterion.** Find a single combinatorial condition, stated in $V$ from $\vec U$ alone, equivalent to genericity for all $\alpha$; Fuchs' and Benhamou–Gitik's criteria should be its specializations.
4. **Radin regime.** Classify intermediate models when $\kappa$ remains measurable in $V[C]$ — here even $V$-definability of $C$'s club filter is delicate.
5. **Lattice theory.** Characterize abstractly which lattices arise as intermediate-model lattices of Magidor extensions; currently only distributive examples are known.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Setting.** Take $o^{\vec U}(\kappa)=1$: a single normal measure $U$ on $\kappa$, so $\mathbb{M}[\vec U]$ is Prikry forcing $\mathbb{P}_U$. Generic object: $c=\langle\kappa_n:n<\omega\rangle$, cofinal in $\kappa$, $\mathrm{ot}(c)=\omega$.

**Step 1 — genericity.** By Mathias, $c$ is generic iff $c\subseteq^* A$ for every $A\in U$. Check the forcing direction: given a dense open $D$ and $p=\langle\kappa_1,\dots,\kappa_n,A\rangle$, the Prikry property gives $A^*\subseteq A$, $A^*\in U$, with $\langle\kappa_1,\dots,\kappa_n,A^*\rangle$ deciding "$\check c\restriction m\in \check D$". Since $c\subseteq^* A^*$, some initial segment of $c$ lands in $D$.

**Step 2 — subsequences give nothing new.** Let $A\subseteq\omega$ be infinite, $A\in V[c]$, and $c\restriction A=\langle\kappa_n:n\in A\rangle$. Claim: $V[c\restriction A]=V[c]$.
*Proof.* $c\restriction A$ is an infinite subsequence of $c$, so for every $B\in U$, $c\subseteq^* B$ gives $c\restriction A\subseteq^* B$. By Mathias, $c\restriction A$ is itself $\mathbb{P}_U$-generic over $V$. Now in $V[c\restriction A]$ recover $c$: $\kappa$ has cofinality $\omega$ there, and $c$ is the unique... more carefully, $V[c\restriction A]\subseteq V[c]$ and by minimality (Gitik–Kanovei–Koepke) $V[c\restriction A]$ is $V$ or $V[c]$; it is not $V$ because $\kappa$ is regular in $V$ and $c\restriction A$ is cofinal in $\kappa$ of type $\omega$. Hence $V[c\restriction A]=V[c]$. $\square$
If $A$ is finite, $c\restriction A\in V$ (a finite set of ordinals) and $V[c\restriction A]=V$.

**Step 3 — the two-model lattice.** So for $o(\kappa)=1$ the intermediate-model lattice is exactly $\{V,\ V[c]\}$: a two-element chain. Every $X\subseteq\mathrm{Ord}$ in $V[c]$ satisfies $V[X]\in\{V,V[c]\}$.

**Step 4 — where it breaks at $o(\kappa)=2$.** Let $U_0\lhd U_1$ with $U_0\in \mathrm{Ult}(V,U_1)$. Now $\mathrm{ot}(C)=\omega^2$ (in the Magidor–Radin form), and $C$ splits into $\omega$ blocks: points of Mitchell rank $1$, each followed by an $\omega$-block of rank-$0$ points. Let
$$A=\{\,\xi<\omega^2:\ \xi \text{ indexes a rank-}1\text{ point of }C\,\}.$$
Then $C\restriction A$ is an $\omega$-sequence of former measurables, generic for the Prikry forcing at $\kappa$ with $U_1$, and
$$V\subsetneq V[C\restriction A]\subsetneq V[C],$$
strictly — $V[C\restriction A]$ does not compute the rank-$0$ blocks, which add subsets of the $\kappa_n$'s. The lattice now has at least three elements, minimality fails, and the classification question becomes nontrivial: are *all* intermediate models of this shape? Benhamou–Gitik answer yes here; the answer for $o^{\vec U}(\kappa)\ge\kappa$ is unknown.
