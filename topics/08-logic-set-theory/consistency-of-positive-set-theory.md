# Consistency of Positive Set Theory

> **Topic:** Mathematical Logic & Set Theory · **ID:** `08-logic-set-theory/consistency-of-positive-set-theory` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Positive set theories replace the separation/replacement machinery of $\mathsf{ZF}$ by a comprehension scheme restricted to *positive* formulas — formulas built without negation. Because $x=x$ is positive, these theories prove that a universal set $V=\{x : x=x\}$ exists, so they are not subtheories of $\mathsf{ZF}$; the Russell class $\{x : x\notin x\}$ is blocked only because $x \notin x$ is not positive.

The problem has three linked components:

1. **Consistency.** Is the strongest standard positive theory $\mathsf{GPK}^+_\infty$ consistent, and relative to what? *(Answered: it is equiconsistent with $\mathsf{MK} + $ "$\mathrm{On}$ is weakly compact"; Esser 1999/2000.)*
2. **Calibration of the weaker theories.** Determine the exact consistency strength of $\mathsf{GPK}_\infty$ (positive theory *without* the closure axiom) and of $\mathsf{GPK}^+$ without infinity. Currently only the interval $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{ZF}) \le \mathsf{GPK}_\infty \le \mathsf{ZFC}+\text{weakly compact}$ is known. **This is the open core.**
3. **Compatibility with choice.** Which choice-like principles are consistent with positive comprehension, given that full $\mathsf{AC}$ is refutable in $\mathsf{GPK}^+_\infty$ (Esser 2000)?

A complete resolution of (2) means: exhibit a model of $\mathsf{GPK}_\infty$ from a hypothesis strictly weaker than a weakly compact cardinal, or prove in $\mathsf{GPK}_\infty$ the existence of an inner model with a weakly compact cardinal.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Work in first-order logic with $\in$ and $=$.

**Positive and generalized positive formulas.** The class $\mathrm{PF}$ of *positive* formulas is generated by atomic $x\in y$, $x=y$ under $\wedge$, $\vee$, $\exists x$, $\forall x$. The class $\mathrm{GPF}$ of *generalized positive* formulas is generated by the same clauses plus **bounded quantification against an arbitrary parameter class**:
$$\varphi \in \mathrm{GPF} \implies (\forall x\,(x\in a \rightarrow \varphi)) \in \mathrm{GPF},$$
where $a$ is a variable. Negation, $\rightarrow$ in general, and unbounded $\forall$ over a negated matrix are excluded.

**Axioms.**
- **(Ext)** $\forall z\,(z\in x \leftrightarrow z\in y) \rightarrow x=y$.
- **(GPK)** For $\varphi \in \mathrm{GPF}$ with free variables among $x,\bar a$: $\ \exists y\ \forall x\,(x\in y \leftrightarrow \varphi(x,\bar a))$.
- **(Closure, the "$+$")** For *every* formula $\psi$ (negation allowed), the class $\{x:\psi(x)\}$ has a least set superset:
$$\exists c\ \Big[\forall x(\psi(x)\rightarrow x\in c)\ \wedge\ \forall d\big(\forall x(\psi(x)\rightarrow x\in d) \rightarrow \forall x (x \in c \rightarrow x\in d)\big)\Big].$$
  Write $c = \mathrm{Cl}(\{x:\psi\})$.
- **($\infty$)** The von Neumann ordinals form a proper class (equivalently, $\mathrm{On}$ is not a set).

$\mathsf{GPK}^+_\infty = \text{(Ext)} + \text{(GPK)} + \text{(Closure)} + (\infty)$. Foundation fails: $V\in V$.

**Topological semantics.** Let $\kappa$ be a regular cardinal. A *$\kappa$-topology* on $X$ is a family of "open" sets closed under arbitrary unions and intersections of $<\kappa$ many members. $X$ is *$\kappa$-compact* if every open cover has a subcover of size $<\kappa$. Let $\mathcal{C}(X)$ be the set of closed subsets with the *exponential ($\kappa$-Vietoris) topology*, generated by
$$U^+ = \{F : F\subseteq U\},\qquad U^- = \{F : F\cap U \ne \emptyset\},\quad U \text{ open}.$$

**Definition (hyperuniverse).** A *$\kappa$-hyperuniverse* is a $\kappa$-compact, $\kappa$-Hausdorff, $\kappa$-topological space $\mathcal{H}$ with a homeomorphism $\mathcal{H} \cong \mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H})$; membership is defined by $x \in y :\Leftrightarrow x \in \iota(y)$.

**Theorem (Forti–Hinnion 1989; Esser 1999).** $\langle \mathcal{H},\in\rangle$ is a model of $\mathsf{GPK}^+_\infty$ iff $\mathcal{H}$ is a $\kappa$-hyperuniverse with $\kappa$ the height of its ordinals; and then $\kappa$ is weakly compact in the ambient theory. Conversely, every weakly compact $\kappa$ yields such an $\mathcal{H}$ (Malitz 1976; Weydert 1989).

**Theorem (Esser).** In $\mathsf{GPK}^+_\infty$ the class $\mathrm{WF}$ of well-founded sets satisfies $\mathsf{ZF}$, and $\mathsf{GPK}^+_\infty$ is equiconsistent with $\mathsf{MK}+$"$\mathrm{On}$ is weakly compact".

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1950s–60s.** Positive comprehension emerges from Gilmore's partial-set and Skolem's positive-formula programmes as an alternative to Quine's $\mathsf{NF}$ for taming the universal set.
- **1976.** R. J. Malitz's UCLA thesis constructs the first candidate models by a transfinite $\kappa$-topological fixed-point construction from a weakly compact $\kappa$, leaving a gap in the verification.
- **1989.** E. Weydert (Bonn) closes the gap; independently Forti and Hinnion give the topological characterisation and prove that any model of $\mathsf{GPK}^+_\infty$ yields a hyperuniverse, so the theory proves $\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{ZF})$ — it is *not* consistent relative to $\mathsf{ZFC}$ alone.
- **1996.** Forti and Honsell give a uniform "general construction of hyperuniverses" (direct limits of $\kappa$-topological approximants), linking the subject to non-well-founded set theory and to final coalgebras of the Vietoris functor in theoretical computer science.
- **1999–2004.** O. Esser (Brussels) settles the strength of $\mathsf{GPK}^+_\infty$ exactly (equiconsistency with $\mathsf{MK}+\mathrm{On}$ weakly compact), refutes $\mathsf{AC}$ in it, and analyses extensionality variants. This is the SOTA for the "$+$" theory.
- **2003–present.** Hinnion and Libert study positive *abstraction* (non-extensional and Frege-style) and Scott-style semantics, where consistency strength collapses to very weak systems — isolating extensionality and closure as the strength-carrying axioms.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **$\mathsf{GPK}^+_\infty$: solved.** Equiconsistent with $\mathsf{MK}+$"$\mathrm{On}$ weakly compact"; models are exactly $\kappa$-hyperuniverses for $\kappa$ weakly compact (Malitz/Weydert/Forti–Hinnion/Esser).
- **Finitary case $\kappa=\omega$.** Dropping $(\infty)$, $\omega$-hyperuniverses are compact metrizable spaces $\mathcal{H}\cong\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H})$ with the Vietoris topology; such spaces (e.g. limits of the sequence $X_0=\{\ast\}$, $X_{n+1}=\mathcal{C}(X_n)$) exist provably in $\mathsf{ZF}$. Hence $\mathsf{GPK}^+$ without infinity is consistent relative to $\mathsf{ZF}$ — no large cardinal needed.
- **Choice.** $\mathsf{GPK}^+_\infty \vdash \neg\mathsf{AC}$ (Esser, JSL 2000). Weak choice for well-founded families survives inside $\mathrm{WF}$.
- **Non-extensional / abstraction fragments.** Positive abstraction with extensionality dropped, and Frege-style positive theories with Scott-style models, are consistent relative to fragments far below $\mathsf{ZF}$ (Hinnion–Libert 2003).
- **Interpretability.** $\mathsf{GPK}^+_\infty$ interprets $\mathsf{MK}$ via $\mathrm{WF}$ and its "hyperclasses"; conversely $\mathsf{MK}+\mathrm{On}$-weakly-compact builds a hyperuniverse of class size.
- **Lower bound for the plain theory.** $\mathsf{GPK}_\infty \vdash \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{ZF})$ is known via the well-founded part; no better lower bound is published.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Loss of the topological dictionary.** Without the closure axiom there is no operator $\mathrm{Cl}$, hence no canonical topology on a model of $\mathsf{GPK}_\infty$. Every known consistency proof is topological: "set" $=$ "closed set", "$\exists$" $=$ "projection", and projections preserve closedness only under compactness. Removing closure removes the only known semantics.
- **Why weak compactness is forced.** A $\kappa$-topology that is $\kappa$-compact and $\kappa$-Hausdorff and non-trivially self-exponential forces $\kappa$ inaccessible plus the tree property — i.e. $\kappa$ weakly compact — because generalized positive formulas admit conjunctions and quantifier alternations of length up to $\kappa$, matching the $L_{\kappa\kappa}$ compactness that defines weak compactness. Any proof that $\mathsf{GPK}_\infty$ needs less strength must produce models that are *not* hyperuniverses, and none are known.
- **Forcing does not apply.** Set forcing over a model of positive set theory does not preserve positive comprehension: generic extensions destroy the fixed point $\mathcal{H}\cong\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H})$, so the standard independence technology is unavailable.
- **No inner model theory.** There is no analogue of $L$ inside a positive universe: $\mathrm{WF}$ is definable but the non-well-founded part is exactly what carries the strength, and it is not stratified by any known hierarchy.
- **Failure of choice.** $\neg\mathsf{AC}$ blocks importing cardinal-arithmetic and combinatorial arguments (e.g. tree constructions requiring well-orderings) from $\mathsf{ZFC}$ practice.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: $\mathsf{GPK}^+_\infty \equiv_{\mathrm{Con}} \mathsf{MK} + \mathrm{On}$ weakly compact. Proven: $\mathsf{GPK}_\infty \vdash \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{ZF})$, and $\mathsf{GPK}_\infty$ is consistent if a weakly compact cardinal exists (drop closure from a hyperuniverse model).

Open: the interval
$$\mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{ZF}) \ \le\ \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{GPK}_\infty)\ \le\ \mathrm{Con}(\mathsf{ZFC} + \exists\,\kappa \text{ weakly compact}).$$
The exact step required: either (a) build a model of generalized positive comprehension $+$ extensionality $+$ infinity that is not a hyperuniverse — a non-topological construction, e.g. by a term-model / consistency-property argument that never needs $\kappa$-compactness — or (b) derive the closure axiom, or the tree property for $\mathrm{On}$, from $\mathsf{GPK}_\infty$ alone. No technique currently spans (a) or (b).

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Brussels school (Hinnion, Libert, successors).** Positive abstraction, non-extensional variants, Scott-style and fixed-point semantics; the programme of isolating which axiom carries the large-cardinal strength. Recent work targets $\mathsf{GPK}_\infty$ with partial closure schemes as intermediate calibration points *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Pisa/Udine (Forti, Honsell, Lenisa).** Coalgebraic reading: hyperuniverses as final coalgebras of the Vietoris functor on $\kappa$-topological spaces; category-theoretic existence criteria that might weaken the compactness requirement *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Boise (M. R. Holmes) and the alternative-set-theory community.** Comparative work with $\mathsf{NF}$/$\mathsf{NFU}$ after the machine-verified consistency of $\mathsf{NF}$ (Holmes–Wilshaw, Lean, 2024); the analogous question is whether positive set theory admits a similarly combinatorial, non-topological model construction.
- **Distinct programme, do not conflate:** the "Hyperuniverse Programme" of S.-D. Friedman and C. Ternullo concerns set-theoretic maximality criteria for $V$, not the hyperuniverses of positive set theory.

## 8. Future Work

- Calibrate $\mathsf{GPK}_\infty$ by proving reflection or a tree-property principle from generalized positive comprehension alone.
- Develop a proof-theoretic treatment: cut-elimination or ordinal analysis for positive comprehension, bypassing topological models entirely.
- Classify the choice principles compatible with $\mathsf{GPK}^+_\infty$ (Esser's refutation of $\mathsf{AC}$ leaves $\mathsf{DC}$, $\mathsf{AC}_{\mathrm{WF}}$, and global well-ordering of $\mathrm{On}$ open in combination).
- Machine-verify the Malitz–Weydert construction; it is long, topological, and has already needed one repair.
- Extend positive comprehension with a positive truth predicate and compare with Cantini-style theories of truth.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Claim.** In the topological semantics, "$a \in x$" defines a set while "$a \notin x$" does not — this is the exact point where Russell's paradox is blocked.

Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a $\kappa$-hyperuniverse, so points of $\mathcal{H}$ are identified with closed subsets of $\mathcal{H}$, and a class $A\subseteq\mathcal{H}$ *is a set of the model* iff $A$ is closed.

*Step 1 — atomic positive formula.* Fix $a\in\mathcal{H}$. Since $\mathcal{H}$ is $\kappa$-Hausdorff, $\{a\}$ is closed, so $U := \mathcal{H}\setminus\{a\}$ is open. In the exponential topology $U^+ = \{F : F\subseteq U\}$ is open by definition. But
$$U^+ = \{F : a\notin F\} = \mathcal{H}\setminus\{x : a\in x\}.$$
Hence $\{x : a \in x\}$ is **closed**, therefore a set. Good: $a\in x$ is positive.

*Step 2 — the negated formula.* $\{x : a\notin x\} = U^+$ is open, and in a non-discrete $\kappa$-topology it is not closed (it is dense but omits, e.g., the point $V=\mathcal{H}$ itself, which is a limit of sets not containing $a$). So it is not a set. Good: $a\notin x$ is not positive.

*Step 3 — Russell.* Suppose $R=\{x : x\notin x\}$ were a set $r$. Then $r\in r \leftrightarrow r\notin r$, contradiction. The topological reading: the diagonal $D=\{x : x\in x\}$ is closed (it is the preimage of the closed evaluation relation), so $D$ *is* a set; its complement $R$ is open and, by the fixed point $\mathcal{H}\cong\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H})$, cannot also be closed. Positive comprehension gives exactly $D$ and refuses $R$.

*Step 4 — where $\kappa$ enters.* Interpret $\exists y\,\varphi(x,y)$ as the projection $\pi_1(\{(x,y):\varphi\})$. Projections of closed sets are closed only when the fibre space is $\kappa$-compact; and generalized positive formulas allow $<\kappa$-length conjunctions, so the topology must be closed under $<\kappa$ intersections and $\kappa$-compact simultaneously. A $\kappa$-compact $\kappa$-Hausdorff self-exponential space forces $\kappa$ inaccessible with the tree property — i.e. $\kappa$ weakly compact. That is the whole reason a large cardinal appears, and Step 1–3 show why no weaker semantics is currently available.

*Step 5 — the finitary contrast.* Take $\kappa=\omega$: $\omega$-compactness is ordinary compactness, and a compact metrizable $\mathcal{H}\cong\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{H})$ exists in plain $\mathsf{ZF}$. So the finitary positive theory costs nothing; all the strength lies in the axiom of infinity together with closure.
