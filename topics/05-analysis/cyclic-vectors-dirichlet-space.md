# Cyclicity in Dirichlet-Type Spaces

> **Topic:** Real & Complex Analysis · **ID:** `05-analysis/cyclic-vectors-dirichlet-space` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $D_\alpha$ be the Dirichlet-type space of analytic functions on the unit disk $\mathbb{D}$ with $\sum_n (n+1)^\alpha |a_n|^2 < \infty$. A function $f \in D_\alpha$ is **cyclic** if the polynomial multiples of $f$ are dense: $[f] := \overline{\{pf : p \in \mathbb{C}[z]\}} = D_\alpha$.

**Brown–Shields conjecture (1984).** For $0 < \alpha \le 1$, $f \in D_\alpha$ is cyclic if and only if

1. $f$ is an outer function, and
2. the boundary zero set $Z(f) = \{\zeta \in \mathbb{T} : \liminf_{z \to \zeta} |f(z)| = 0\}$ has $\alpha$-Riesz capacity zero (logarithmic capacity when $\alpha = 1$).

The "only if" direction is a theorem. **The open problem is sufficiency:** prove that every outer $f \in D_\alpha$ whose boundary zero set is of capacity zero is cyclic, or produce an outer $f$ with $c_\alpha(Z(f)) = 0$ and $[f] \subsetneq D_\alpha$. A complete solution must handle $f$ with no continuity up to $\mathbb{T}$ and zero sets that are uncountable capacity-zero compacta.

Two companion problems are catalogued here: the characterization of cyclic vectors in the Bergman range $\alpha < 0$ (open, and equivalent in strength to major invariant-subspace questions), and cyclicity of polynomials in Dirichlet-type spaces of the bidisk $\mathbb{D}^2$ (solved for polynomials, open in general).

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

For $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}$ define
$$
D_\alpha = \Big\{ f(z) = \sum_{n \ge 0} a_n z^n \text{ analytic on } \mathbb{D} \ :\ \|f\|_\alpha^2 = \sum_{n\ge 0} (n+1)^\alpha |a_n|^2 < \infty \Big\}.
$$
This is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space; $\alpha = -1$ gives the Bergman space $A^2$, $\alpha = 0$ the Hardy space $H^2$, $\alpha = 1$ the classical Dirichlet space $D$, with the equivalent Douglas formula
$$
\|f\|_{D}^2 \asymp |f(0)|^2 + \int_{\mathbb{D}} |f'(z)|^2\, dA(z) = |f(0)|^2 + \operatorname{Area}\big(f(\mathbb{D})\big) \ \ (\text{counted with multiplicity}).
$$
The scale is monotone: $D_\beta \subset D_\alpha$ for $\alpha \le \beta$, and $D_\alpha \subset A(\overline{\mathbb{D}})$ is a Banach algebra for $\alpha > 1$.

**Capacity.** For $0 < \alpha < 1$ let $k_\alpha(t) = |t|^{\alpha - 1}$, and $k_1(t) = \log(e/|t|)$. For a probability measure $\mu$ on a compact $E \subset \mathbb{T}$, the energy is $I_\alpha(\mu) = \iint k_\alpha(|e^{is} - e^{it}|)\, d\mu(s)\, d\mu(t)$, and $c_\alpha(E) = \big(\inf_\mu I_\alpha(\mu)\big)^{-1}$. Countable sets have $c_\alpha = 0$; sets of positive Hausdorff dimension may or may not.

**Outer functions.** $f \in H^2$ is outer if $f(z) = \lambda \exp\!\big(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}} \frac{e^{it}+z}{e^{it}-z} \log|f(e^{it})|\, dt\big)$. By Beurling's theorem, $f \in H^2$ is cyclic iff $f$ is outer — the $\alpha = 0$ case of the conjecture, where capacity imposes no constraint.

**Optimal polynomial approximants (OPAs).** For $f \in D_\alpha$ with $f(0) \ne 0$, let $p_n$ minimize $\|pf - 1\|_\alpha$ over $\deg p \le n$. Then
$$
f \text{ is cyclic} \iff \|p_n f - 1\|_\alpha \to 0,
$$
so cyclicity is a quantitative linear-algebra question: $p_n$ solves a Gram system with entries $\langle z^j f, z^k f\rangle_\alpha$.

**Richter–Sundberg local Dirichlet integral.** For $\zeta\in\mathbb{T}$,
$$
D_\zeta(f) = \int_{\mathbb{T}} \frac{|f(e^{it}) - f(\zeta)|^2}{|e^{it}-\zeta|^2}\,\frac{dt}{2\pi}, \qquad \|f\|_D^2 \asymp \|f\|_{H^2}^2 + \int_{\mathbb{T}} D_\zeta(f)\,\frac{|d\zeta|}{2\pi},
$$
the key tool for the structure of $D$-invariant subspaces.

## 3. History & State of the Art

- **1964–66.** H. S. Shapiro raised weak invertibility (his term for cyclicity) in weighted spaces and proved first sufficient conditions.
- **1984.** L. Brown and A. Shields, *Cyclic vectors in the Dirichlet space*, established necessity of the two conditions, proved cyclicity when $1/f \in H^\infty$, and posed the conjecture.
- **1985.** L. Brown and W. Cohn showed the conjecture is not vacuous in its hard regime: for every closed $E \subset \mathbb{T}$ of logarithmic capacity zero there is a cyclic $f \in D \cap A(\overline{\mathbb{D}})$ with $Z(f) = E$.
- **1990–93.** Hedenmalm–Shields, Richter–Sundberg and Aleman developed the invariant-subspace theory of $D$ (index-one property, local Dirichlet integrals), yielding $[f] = \{g \in D : g/f \in N^+\}$-type descriptions for special $f$.
- **1996.** Aleman–Richter–Sundberg proved Beurling's theorem for the Bergman space (invariant subspaces are generated by their wandering subspaces), but this does **not** characterize cyclic vectors.
- **2006, 2009.** El-Fallah, Kellay and Ransford introduced *Bergman–Smirnov exceptional sets* and proved the conjecture for large families of zero sets, including all countable closed sets and explicit Cantor-type sets.
- **2015–2019.** Bénéteau, Condori, Khavinson, Liaw, Seco, Simanek and Sola developed the OPA program: closed-form extremal polynomials, sharp decay rates, zero location of $p_n$, and a complete solution of the polynomial case in $\mathbb{D}^2$.
- **2014.** El-Fallah, Kellay, Mashreghi and Ransford, *A Primer on the Dirichlet Space* (CUP), remains the standard reference.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Regime / class | Status |
|---|---|
| $\alpha \le 0$, i.e. $H^2$ and larger | $\alpha=0$: solved (Beurling — cyclic $\iff$ outer). $\alpha<0$ (Bergman scale): **open**, no capacity criterion known |
| $\alpha > 1$ | Solved: $D_\alpha$ is a Banach algebra inside $A(\overline{\mathbb{D}})$; $f$ cyclic $\iff$ $f$ has no zeros in $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$ |
| $0<\alpha\le 1$, $1/f \in H^\infty$ | Cyclic (Brown–Shields 1984) |
| $0<\alpha\le1$, $f$ a polynomial | Solved: $p$ is cyclic in $D_\alpha(\mathbb{D})$ iff $p$ has no zeros in $\mathbb{D}$ (its finitely many boundary zeros have capacity $0$) |
| $f \in D \cap A(\overline{\mathbb{D}})$ outer, $Z(f)$ countable | Cyclic (El-Fallah–Kellay–Ransford 2009) |
| $Z(f)$ inside a Bergman–Smirnov exceptional set | Cyclic; such sets include countable closed sets and certain Cantor sets of capacity zero |
| $f$ outer with $\log|f| \in$ suitable Besov/Dirichlet class | Cyclic (Hedenmalm–Shields-type criteria) |
| $\mathbb{D}^2$, polynomials, isotropic $D_\alpha(\mathbb{D}^2)$ | Solved: $2 - z_1 - z_2$ and $1 - z_1z_2$ are cyclic exactly for $\alpha \le 1/2$ (Bénéteau–Khavinson–Liaw–Seco–Sola 2016) |
| $\mathbb{D}^2$, anisotropic weights $(n+1)^{\alpha_1}(m+1)^{\alpha_2}$ | $1 - z_1z_2$ cyclic iff $\alpha_1 + \alpha_2 \le 1$ (Bénéteau–Knese–Kosiński–Liaw–Seco–Sola 2019) |

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No factorization theory.** In $H^2$, cyclicity is decided by inner–outer factorization. $D_\alpha$ for $\alpha > 0$ has no analogue: the outer part of a $D$-function need not lie in $D$-controlled classes, and $f = f_{\text{in}} f_{\text{out}}$ does not respect $\|\cdot\|_\alpha$. All Beurling-type arguments break at this step.
- **Capacity is not multiplicative or local enough.** Capacity zero of $Z(f)$ is a purely metric condition on the boundary, while cyclicity is an approximation-theoretic condition on the whole disk. Bridging them requires constructing $p_n$ with $p_n f \to 1$ at a rate keyed to the equilibrium measure of $Z(f)$; no general construction exists for uncountable $Z(f)$ with a wild Cantor structure.
- **Quantitative decay is logarithmically slow.** Even for $f = 1-z$ in $D$, $\|p_nf-1\|_D^2 \asymp 1/\log n$ (Section 10). Any perturbation argument must survive an error term that decays slower than any power, so standard Corona/$\bar\partial$ estimates and functional-calculus perturbations lose control.
- **The Bergman range is genuinely harder.** For $\alpha < 0$, functions with essentially arbitrary boundary growth belong to the space; Korenblum's premeasure theory gives a partial description of zero sets but no cyclicity test, and a characterization would bear directly on the invariant subspace problem for the Bergman shift, whose lattice has invariant subspaces of every index $1 \le n \le \infty$.
- **No known monotonicity in $\alpha$ for individual functions** beyond the trivial inclusion; a function can fail to be cyclic in $D_\beta$ while being cyclic in $D_\alpha$, $\alpha<\beta$, and the threshold value is not computable by any known formula except in special cases.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: the two Brown–Shields conditions are necessary, and they are sufficient when the zero set sits inside a Bergman–Smirnov exceptional set — which requires *exhibiting* an auxiliary cyclic function $\varphi \in D \cap A(\overline{\mathbb{D}})$ vanishing on $E$ with quantitative control on $1/\varphi$. Missing: a proof that **every** compact $E \subset \mathbb{T}$ with $c_\alpha(E) = 0$ is Bergman–Smirnov exceptional, plus a mechanism to pass from $f \in D \cap A(\overline{\mathbb{D}})$ to arbitrary outer $f \in D_\alpha$ with no boundary continuity. The precise step to cross: convert the vanishing of the $\alpha$-energy of $E$ into an explicit sequence of polynomials $p_n$ with $\|p_n f - 1\|_\alpha \to 0$, uniformly over the (uncountable, non-self-similar) capacity-zero compacta.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **OPA school** (Bénéteau–Khavinson–Liaw–Seco–Simanek–Sola; South Florida, Baylor, UAM Madrid, Stockholm): explicit extremal polynomials, sharp rates, and the theorem that zeros of optimal polynomial approximants lie outside $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$, with Jentzsch-type limit distributions (Rev. Mat. Iberoam. 2019). Ongoing: extending zero-location bounds to non-polynomial $f$ and to weighted/anisotropic scales.
- **Potential-theoretic school** (El-Fallah, Kellay, Ransford, Mashreghi; Rabat, Bordeaux, Laval): enlarging the class of Bergman–Smirnov exceptional sets; capacity of Cantor sets under general gauge functions.
- **Several-variable programme** (Knese, Kosiński, Sola): cyclicity of rational and polynomial functions on $\mathbb{D}^n$, contact order of zero varieties with the distinguished boundary, and the role of stable polynomials. *(frontier — verify)* Reports of thresholds for cyclicity of $\prod(1-z_i)$-type polynomials on $\mathbb{D}^n$ in terms of $\sum_i \alpha_i$.
- **Operator-theoretic** (Richter–Sundberg lineage; Luo, Aleman): weak products $D \odot D$, corona and Toeplitz-type criteria for membership in $[f]$.
- *(frontier — verify)* Numerical work computing $\|p_nf-1\|_\alpha$ for engineered $f$ with fat capacity-zero zero sets, intended to search for a counterexample; no counterexample has been reported.

## 8. Future Work

- Prove or refute: every compact $E\subset\mathbb{T}$ with $c_1(E)=0$ is Bergman–Smirnov exceptional. This alone would close the conjecture for $f \in D\cap A(\overline{\mathbb{D}})$.
- Develop a substitute for inner–outer factorization on the $D_\alpha$ scale — e.g. a "Dirichlet-outer" factor with controlled local Dirichlet integrals.
- Obtain two-sided rate estimates $\|p_nf-1\|_\alpha \asymp \Phi(n, c_\alpha)$ linking decay to the equilibrium potential of $Z(f)$; a matching lower bound is the natural route to a counterexample.
- Settle the polydisk case for non-polynomial $f$, where even necessary conditions are incomplete.
- For $\alpha<0$, decide whether cyclicity in $A^2$ admits any capacity/premeasure characterization (Korenblum's programme).

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take $f(z) = 1 - z$, which vanishes at the single boundary point $\zeta=1$ (capacity zero) and is outer. Compute the optimal polynomial approximants exactly in $D_\alpha$.

Let $p(z) = \sum_{k=0}^n a_k z^k$ and set $c = (c_0,\dots,c_{n+1})$ to be the coefficients of $pf - 1$:
$$
c_0 = a_0 - 1,\qquad c_k = a_k - a_{k-1}\ (1\le k \le n), \qquad c_{n+1} = -a_n .
$$
Summing telescopes: $\sum_{k=0}^{n+1} c_k = -1$, and conversely any $c$ with $\sum_k c_k = -1$ arises from a unique $p$. So the problem is
$$
\min \Big\{ \sum_{k=0}^{n+1} (k+1)^\alpha |c_k|^2 \ :\ \sum_{k=0}^{n+1} c_k = -1 \Big\}.
$$
Cauchy–Schwarz with weights $w_k = (k+1)^\alpha$ gives $1 = |\sum c_k|^2 \le \big(\sum w_k |c_k|^2\big)\big(\sum w_k^{-1}\big)$, with equality for $c_k = -w_k^{-1}/\sum_j w_j^{-1}$. Hence
$$
\boxed{\ \|p_n f - 1\|_\alpha^2 = \Big(\sum_{k=0}^{n+1} (k+1)^{-\alpha}\Big)^{-1}. }
$$

Reading off the asymptotics:

- $\alpha < 1$: $\sum_{k\le n}(k+1)^{-\alpha} \asymp n^{1-\alpha} \to \infty$, so $\|p_nf-1\|_\alpha^2 \asymp n^{\alpha-1} \to 0$ — $1-z$ is cyclic, with polynomial rate.
- $\alpha = 1$: $\sum_{k \le n} (k+1)^{-1} = \log n + O(1)$, so $\|p_nf-1\|_D^2 \sim 1/\log n \to 0$ — cyclic in the Dirichlet space, but only logarithmically fast.
- $\alpha > 1$: $\sum_k (k+1)^{-\alpha} \to \zeta(\alpha) < \infty$, so $\operatorname{dist}(1,[f])^2 = 1/\zeta(\alpha) > 0$ — **not** cyclic, matching the Banach-algebra criterion (a zero on $\overline{\mathbb{D}}$ kills cyclicity once $\alpha>1$).

This single example pins the exponent $\alpha=1$ as the exact endpoint of the conjectured regime and shows the decay rate any general proof must tolerate: slower than any power of $n$. Contrast $\mathbb{D}^2$: for $f(z_1,z_2)=2-z_1-z_2$, the analogous extremal computation yields $\|p_nf-1\|_\alpha \to 0$ iff $\alpha \le 1/2$, so the zero variety's contact with the distinguished boundary $\mathbb{T}^2$ — not just its size — controls cyclicity in several variables.
