# Beurling-Lax Theorem Generalizations

> **Topic:** Real & Complex Analysis · **ID:** `05-analysis/beurling-lax-theorem-generalizations` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Beurling's theorem (1949) classifies the closed subspaces of the Hardy space $H^2(\mathbb{D})$ invariant under multiplication by $z$: each is $\theta H^2(\mathbb{D})$ for an inner function $\theta$, unique up to a unimodular constant. Lax (1959) and Halmos (1961) extended this to vector-valued and abstract-shift settings: every subspace of $H^2(\mathbb{D})\otimes\mathcal{E}$ invariant under the shift is $\Theta\,(H^2(\mathbb{D})\otimes\mathcal{E}_*)$ for an inner operator-valued multiplier $\Theta$.

**The open problem.** Determine for which reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces, multivariable domains, and operator tuples a Beurling–Lax classification survives — and in what form. Three concrete open questions carry the weight:

1. **Wandering subspace problem.** For an operator $T$ on $\mathcal{H}$ that is a *concave* or *expansive* left-invertible operator (equivalently, for the Bergman-type shifts $M_z$ on the weighted Dirichlet spaces $D_\alpha$, $\alpha>1$, and on Bergman spaces of $\mathbb{B}_d$, $d\ge 2$), is every $T$-invariant subspace $\mathcal{M}$ generated by its wandering subspace, i.e. $\mathcal{M}=\bigvee_{n\ge0}T^n(\mathcal{M}\ominus T\mathcal{M})$?
2. **Polydisc classification.** Is there any usable parametrization of the lattice $\mathrm{Lat}(M_{z_1},\dots,M_{z_n})$ of joint invariant subspaces of $H^2(\mathbb{D}^n)$ for $n\ge2$?
3. **Beyond complete Nevanlinna–Pick kernels.** For which kernels $k$ does every invariant subspace of $\mathcal{H}(k)\otimes\mathcal{E}$ equal $M_\Theta(\mathcal{H}(k)\otimes\mathcal{E}_*)$ with $\Theta$ a partially isometric multiplier?

A resolution means either a representation theorem with an explicit uniqueness statement, or a counterexample subspace provably not of the proposed form.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Let $\mathcal{H}(k)$ be the reproducing kernel Hilbert space on a domain $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^d$ with kernel $k:\Omega\times\Omega\to\mathbb{C}$, $\langle f,k_w\rangle=f(w)$. A function $\varphi$ is a **multiplier** if $M_\varphi f=\varphi f$ maps $\mathcal{H}(k)$ into itself; the multiplier algebra is $\mathrm{Mult}(\mathcal{H}(k))$.

**Classical case.** $H^2(\mathbb{D})=\{f=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^n:\ \|f\|^2=\sum|a_n|^2<\infty\}$, with Szegő kernel $k(z,w)=(1-z\bar w)^{-1}$. Beurling's theorem:
$$\mathcal{M}\subseteq H^2(\mathbb{D}),\ z\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{M},\ \mathcal{M}\neq\{0\}\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathcal{M}=\theta H^2(\mathbb{D}),\quad |\theta^*(e^{i\vartheta})|=1\ \text{a.e.}$$

**Wold decomposition.** If $V$ is an isometry on $\mathcal{H}$ with wandering subspace $\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{H}\ominus V\mathcal{H}$, then
$$\mathcal{H}=\Big(\bigoplus_{n\ge0}V^n\mathcal{W}\Big)\oplus\bigcap_{n\ge0}V^n\mathcal{H}.$$
Beurling–Lax is exactly Wold applied to $V=M_z|_{\mathcal{M}}$: pure isometry plus $\dim\mathcal{W}$ as multiplicity gives $\mathcal{M}\cong H^2\otimes\mathcal{W}$, and the unitary intertwiner is $M_\Theta$.

**Weighted shifts.** On $D_\alpha=\{f=\sum a_nz^n:\ \sum (n+1)^{1-\alpha}|a_n|^2<\infty\}$ one has $\alpha=0$ (Dirichlet), $\alpha=1$ ($H^2$), $\alpha=2$ (Bergman $L^2_a(\mathbb{D})$). For $\alpha>1$, $M_z$ is expansive but not isometric: $\|M_z f\|\ge\|f\|$ with $M_z^*M_z\neq I$, so Wold fails verbatim.

**Concavity / Shimorin condition.** $T$ is *concave* if $\|T^2x\|^2+\|x\|^2\le 2\|Tx\|^2$. Shimorin's sufficient condition for the wandering subspace property is the operator inequality
$$\|Tx+y\|^2\le 2\big(\|x\|^2+\|Ty\|^2\big)\qquad (x,y\in\mathcal{H}),$$
plus left-invertibility, giving a Wold-type decomposition for the *Cauchy dual* $T'=T(T^*T)^{-1}$.

**Drury–Arveson space.** $H^2_d$ on the ball $\mathbb{B}_d$ has kernel $k(z,w)=(1-\langle z,w\rangle)^{-1}$ and is the universal complete Nevanlinna–Pick (CNP) space: $k$ is CNP iff $1-1/k$ is positive semidefinite. The tuple $M_z=(M_{z_1},\dots,M_{z_d})$ is a **row contraction**: $\sum_i M_{z_i}M_{z_i}^*\le I$.

**Multiplier form of the target statement.** $\Theta\in\mathrm{Mult}(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{E}_*,\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{E})$ is *inner* (partially isometric) if $M_\Theta$ is a partial isometry. The desired conclusion:
$$\mathcal{M}=M_\Theta\big(\mathcal{H}(k)\otimes\mathcal{E}_*\big).$$

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1949.** Beurling, *On two problems concerning linear transformations in Hilbert space*, Acta Math. 81 — the inner-function classification, motivated by the invariant subspace problem.
- **1958.** Helson–Lowdenslager extend prediction theory to $\mathbb{T}^2$ with lexicographic half-plane orders, showing the several-variable picture is genuinely different.
- **1959–1961.** Lax (Acta Math. 102) treats finite multiplicity; Halmos (J. reine angew. Math. 208) gives the Wold/wandering-subspace proof valid for arbitrary multiplicity. Sz.-Nagy–Foiaș embed this in dilation theory, where $\Theta$ becomes the characteristic function of a contraction.
- **1969.** Rudin, *Function Theory in Polydiscs*: exhibits invariant subspaces of $H^2(\mathbb{D}^2)$ that are not finitely generated, and subspaces whose generator counts are arbitrary. This kills any naive Beurling analogue in $n\ge2$ variables.
- **1988.** Mandrekar: an invariant subspace of $H^2(\mathbb{D}^2)$ is of Beurling type $\Theta H^2(\mathbb{D}^2)$ **iff** $(M_{z_1}|_\mathcal{M},M_{z_2}|_\mathcal{M})$ is doubly commuting.
- **1996.** Aleman–Richter–Sundberg (Acta Math. 177): every invariant subspace of the Bergman space $L^2_a(\mathbb{D})$ satisfies the wandering subspace property, $\mathcal{M}=[\mathcal{M}\ominus z\mathcal{M}]$. The index $\dim(\mathcal{M}\ominus z\mathcal{M})$ can be any value in $\{1,2,\dots,\infty\}$ — so the analogue of "one inner function" fails, but generation survives.
- **1998–2000.** Arveson (Acta Math. 181) builds the $H^2_d$ model; McCullough–Trent (J. Funct. Anal. 178, 2000) prove the Beurling–Lax theorem for all CNP kernels.
- **2001.** Shimorin (J. reine angew. Math. 531) proves the wandering subspace property for a broad class of "operators close to isometries", covering $D_\alpha$ for $0\le\alpha\le 2$ under his inequality.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Setting | Status |
|---|---|
| $H^2(\mathbb{D})\otimes\mathcal{E}$, $\dim\mathcal{E}\le\infty$ | **Solved** (Beurling/Lax/Halmos): $\mathcal{M}=\Theta(H^2\otimes\mathcal{E}_*)$, $\Theta$ inner, unique up to right unitary. |
| Any pure isometry on a Hilbert space | **Solved** via Wold. |
| $L^2_a(\mathbb{D})$ (Bergman, $\alpha=2$) | **Solved** for the wandering property (ARS 1996); no single-generator form — index runs over $\{1,\dots,\infty\}$. |
| $D_\alpha(\mathbb{D})$, $0\le\alpha\le 2$ | **Solved** by Shimorin's inequality; standard weighted Bergman $A^2_\beta(\mathbb{D})$, $-1<\beta\le0$, included. |
| CNP spaces: $H^2_d$, $D(\mathbb{D})$, and all $\mathcal{H}(k)$ with $1-1/k\succeq0$ | **Solved** (McCullough–Trent 2000; Greene–Richter–Sundberg, J. Funct. Anal. 194, 2002): $\mathcal{M}=M_\Theta(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{E}_*)$ with $\Theta$ a partially isometric multiplier; $\Theta$ non-unique. |
| Full Fock space / free semigroup algebra $\mathcal{L}_n$ | **Solved** noncommutatively (Popescu, Math. Ann. 303, 1995; Davidson–Pitts, Proc. LMS 78, 1999): invariant subspaces are ranges of inner multi-analytic operators. |
| $H^2(\mathbb{D}^n)$, doubly commuting $\mathcal{M}$ | **Solved** (Mandrekar 1988): exactly the Beurling-type subspaces. |
| Finite-codimension subspaces of $H^2(\mathbb{D}^n)$ | **Solved** (Ahern–Clark 1970): determined by zero sets with multiplicity. |
| $H^2(\mathbb{D}^n)$, general $\mathcal{M}$, $n\ge2$ | **Open**; Rudin's examples rule out finite generation. |
| $D_\alpha(\mathbb{D})$, $\alpha>2$; Bergman space of $\mathbb{B}_d$, $d\ge2$ | **Open** (wandering subspace problem). |

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Loss of the Wold decomposition.** For $\alpha>1$ the shift is expansive, not isometric; $\mathcal{H}\ominus T\mathcal{H}$ need not be wandering ($T^n\mathcal{W}$ are not mutually orthogonal), so the direct-sum bookkeeping that drives Beurling–Lax has no substitute. Shimorin's route through the Cauchy dual $T'=T(T^*T)^{-1}$ requires $T'$ to be a contraction plus a concavity-type inequality, and both fail for large $\alpha$ and for the ball with $d\ge2$.
- **No inner–outer factorization in several variables.** In $H^2(\mathbb{D}^n)$, $n\ge2$, boundary values live on the distinguished boundary $\mathbb{T}^n$, which has positive-codimension geometry inside $\partial\mathbb{D}^n$; Nevanlinna factorization and the Szegő/entropy identity $\log|f(0)|\le\int\log|f^*|$ do not yield a multiplicative decomposition. Zero sets are analytic varieties, not discrete, so Blaschke products have no analogue.
- **The multiplier algebra is too small.** $\mathrm{Mult}(H^2(\mathbb{D}^n))=H^\infty(\mathbb{D}^n)$ but the corresponding operator-valued "inner" class is not rich enough to hit every invariant subspace; von Neumann's inequality already fails for $n\ge3$, breaking the dilation-theoretic proof.
- **Rudin's obstruction.** Some $\mathcal{M}\subseteq H^2(\mathbb{D}^2)$ have $\dim(\mathcal{M}\ominus(z_1\mathcal{M}+z_2\mathcal{M}))=\infty$ while $\mathcal{M}$ has finite "size" in every other reasonable sense — invariants such as the core operator, fringe operators, and the Samuel/Hilbert–Samuel multiplicity give inequivalent classifications, none complete.
- **Non-CNP kernels.** The McCullough–Trent proof uses the CNP factorization $1-1/k\succeq0$ to build a contractive multiplier realization. Bergman kernels $(1-z\bar w)^{-2}$ are not CNP, and no replacement positivity certificate is known.
- **Entanglement with the invariant subspace problem.** Any fully general lattice classification would settle whether nontrivial invariant subspaces always exist for the relevant model operators — a known-hard barrier.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: classification whenever the restricted operator is an isometry (Wold), or whenever an explicit positivity certificate exists (CNP kernels), or whenever a concavity inequality holds ($D_\alpha$, $\alpha\le2$).

Missing: a mechanism producing an *orthogonal* multiplicity decomposition when the shift is neither isometric nor CNP-modelled. Concretely, three steps:

1. Prove or refute: $T$ expansive and left-invertible $\Rightarrow$ $\mathcal{M}=[\mathcal{M}\ominus T\mathcal{M}]$ for all invariant $\mathcal{M}$. Shimorin's inequality is sufficient, not necessary; no necessary-and-sufficient condition is known.
2. In several variables, replace "inner function" by an invariant complete for the lattice. Candidates (core operator, curvature, defect operators, Hilbert module resolutions) each separate some pairs of subspaces but none is known complete even for $H^2(\mathbb{D}^2)$.
3. Identify the exact kernel-theoretic hypothesis — weaker than CNP — under which partially isometric multipliers exhaust the invariant subspace lattice.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Wandering subspaces beyond Shimorin.** Groups around Eschmeier (Saarbrücken) and Sarkar (ISI Bangalore) study analytic models for $\mathbb{B}_d$-Bergman shifts and Dirichlet-type spaces on the ball; partial results cover invariant subspaces of finite index and those generated by polynomials. *(frontier — verify)* claims of the wandering property for $D_\alpha(\mathbb{B}_d)$ in restricted ranges of $\alpha$ appear periodically and should be checked against Shimorin's inequality directly.
- **Hilbert modules and free resolutions.** Douglas–Yang–Misra program: treat $\mathcal{M}\subseteq H^2(\mathbb{D}^n)$ as a Hilbert module, classify by resolutions and by the core operator $C=\sum_{|\alpha|\ge0}(-1)^{|\alpha|}\dots$; similarity invariants for quotient modules are the current output.
- **Noncommutative Hardy algebras.** Muhly–Solel's $H^\infty(E)$ over $W^*$-correspondences and Blecher–Labuschagne's Beurling theorems in noncommutative $L^p$ give clean Beurling-type theorems where classical function theory is absent, suggesting the right hypothesis is algebraic (finite maximal subdiagonality) rather than geometric.
- **Free/CNP interpolation.** Ball–Bolotnikov, Jury, Martin, Shalit continue extending the CNP Beurling–Lax to weighted Fock spaces and to the "column-extreme" setting.

## 8. Future Work

- Find a necessary and sufficient operator inequality for the wandering subspace property, replacing Shimorin's sufficient condition.
- Test the Bergman space of $\mathbb{B}_2$: either construct an invariant subspace with $\mathcal{M}\supsetneq[\mathcal{M}\ominus M_z\mathcal{M}]$, or push ARS's dominant-function/Green-function argument (which uses one-variable biharmonic Green function estimates) to the ball.
- Extend McCullough–Trent past CNP by finding a positivity certificate for kernels of the form $(1-\langle z,w\rangle)^{-m}$, $m\ge2$.
- Classify $\mathrm{Lat}(H^2(\mathbb{D}^2))$ modulo unitary equivalence for subspaces of finite rank in the fringe/core sense — a plausible first complete sublattice.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**(a) One variable — the theorem works.** Let $\mathcal{M}=\{f\in H^2(\mathbb{D}): f(\tfrac12)=0\}$. This is $z$-invariant and has codimension 1. Beurling predicts $\mathcal{M}=\theta H^2$ with
$$\theta(z)=\frac{z-\frac12}{1-\frac12 z}.$$
Check: $|\theta(e^{i\vartheta})|=1$ since $|e^{i\vartheta}-\tfrac12|=|1-\tfrac12 e^{i\vartheta}|$, so $M_\theta$ is an isometry; $\theta H^2\subseteq\mathcal{M}$ is clear, and $\mathcal{M}\ominus z\mathcal{M}$ is one-dimensional, spanned by $\theta$, because $\mathcal{M}^\perp=\mathbb{C}k_{1/2}$ with $k_{1/2}(z)=(1-z/2)^{-1}$ and $\dim(H^2\ominus\mathcal{M})=1$ forces index $1$. Wold then gives $\mathcal{M}=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}z^n\mathbb{C}\theta=\theta H^2$.

**(b) Two variables — the theorem breaks.** In $H^2(\mathbb{D}^2)$ take
$$\mathcal{M}=\{f:\ f(0,0)=0\}=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{z_1^jz_2^k:\ (j,k)\neq(0,0)\},$$
which is invariant under both $M_{z_1},M_{z_2}$ and has codimension 1. Compute the wandering space:
$$z_1\mathcal{M}+z_2\mathcal{M}=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{z_1^jz_2^k:\ j+k\ge2\},\qquad \mathcal{M}\ominus(z_1\mathcal{M}+z_2\mathcal{M})=\mathrm{span}\{z_1,z_2\},$$
of dimension 2. So $\mathcal{M}$ needs two generators and cannot be $\Theta H^2(\mathbb{D}^2)$ for a scalar inner $\Theta$: a single inner $\Theta$ would give $\mathcal{M}\ominus(z_1\mathcal{M}+z_2\mathcal{M})=\mathbb{C}\Theta$. Equivalently, if $\mathcal{M}=\Theta H^2(\mathbb{D}^2)$ then $\Theta$ has codimension-1 range, forcing $\Theta$ constant, contradiction.

Failure of double commutativity is the diagnostic. Write $R_i=M_{z_i}|_{\mathcal{M}}$. Then $R_1^*R_2 z_1z_2 = R_1^*z_1^2z_2\cdot 0 + \dots$; directly, $R_2^*R_1 (z_1)=R_2^*(z_1^2)=0$ while $R_1R_2^*(z_1)=0$ too, but on $z_2$: $R_2^*R_1(z_2)=R_2^*(z_1z_2)=z_1$, whereas $R_1R_2^*(z_2)=R_1(0)=0$ since $z_2/z_2=1\notin\mathcal{M}$. So $R_2^*R_1\neq R_1R_2^*$ and Mandrekar's criterion is violated — exactly as it must be.

**(c) Bergman — generation without a single generator.** In $L^2_a(\mathbb{D})$ let $\mathcal{M}=\{f: f(0)=f'(0)=\dots=0\ \text{on a suitable zero set}\}$; ARS guarantee $\mathcal{M}=[\mathcal{M}\ominus z\mathcal{M}]$, but $\dim(\mathcal{M}\ominus z\mathcal{M})$ can be made any $n$ (or $\infty$) by taking $\mathcal{M}$ to be the invariant subspace generated by $n$ suitably chosen functions with disjoint zero behaviour. This is the precise sense in which the Beurling conclusion splits into two independent statements — *generation* (still true) and *multiplicity one* (false) — and it is the generation half whose validity is open for $D_\alpha$, $\alpha>2$, and for Bergman spaces of $\mathbb{B}_d$, $d\ge 2$.
