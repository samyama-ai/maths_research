# Morrey's Conjecture

> **Topic:** Real & Complex Analysis · **ID:** `05-analysis/morreys-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $f:\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\to\mathbb{R}$ be continuous. Two convexity notions govern the vectorial calculus of variations:

- $f$ is **quasiconvex** (Morrey, 1952) if for every $X\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$, every bounded open $\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ and every $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^m)$,
$$\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_\Omega f\big(X+D\varphi(x)\big)\,dx \;\ge\; f(X).$$
- $f$ is **rank-one convex** if $t\mapsto f(X+t\,a\otimes b)$ is convex on $\mathbb{R}$ for all $X$, $a\in\mathbb{R}^m$, $b\in\mathbb{R}^n$.

Quasiconvexity always implies rank-one convexity. **Morrey conjectured that the converse fails** whenever $m,n\ge 2$.

Šverák (1992) proved the conjecture for $m\ge 3$, $n\ge 2$. The surviving open case — now what "Morrey's conjecture / Morrey's problem" usually denotes — is:

> **Open problem.** Does rank-one convexity imply quasiconvexity for $f:\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\to\mathbb{R}$?

A resolution means either (a) an explicit rank-one convex $f$ on $\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ together with $X$ and $\varphi$ violating the Jensen inequality above, or (b) a proof that every rank-one convex $f$ on $\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ satisfies it for all admissible $\varphi$. Opinion is split on which way it goes: Morrey expected (a); the Burkholder/Iwaniec circle of conjectures is consistent with (b).

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Convexity hierarchy.** For $f:\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}\to\mathbb{R}$,
$$\text{convex}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{polyconvex}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{quasiconvex}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{rank-one convex},$$
where $f$ is **polyconvex** if $f(X)=g(M(X))$ with $g$ convex and $M(X)$ the vector of all minors of $X$ (for $m=n=2$: $M(X)=(X,\det X)\in\mathbb{R}^5$). All implications are strict except the last, which is strict for $m\ge3$ and open for $m=n=2$ (Dacorogna, *Direct Methods*, Ch. 5).

**Why quasiconvexity matters.** Morrey's theorem: for $f$ with $0\le f(X)\le C(1+|X|^p)$, $1<p<\infty$, the functional $u\mapsto\int_\Omega f(Du)$ is sequentially weakly lower semicontinuous on $W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^m)$ **iff** $f$ is quasiconvex. Quasiconvexity is therefore the exact condition for the direct method to produce minimizers; rank-one convexity is the only condition that is *locally checkable* (it is a pointwise inequality on $D^2f$, the Legendre–Hadamard condition $\sum \partial^2_{X^i_\alpha X^j_\beta} f\, a_ia_j b_\alpha b_\beta\ge0$ for smooth $f$).

**Measure-theoretic form.** $f$ is quasiconvex iff Jensen's inequality $f(\bar\nu)\le\langle\nu,f\rangle$ holds for every homogeneous **gradient Young measure** $\nu$; rank-one convexity is equivalent to the same inequality for **laminates** — the sub-class generated by iterated rank-one splittings. Morrey's problem asks whether these two classes of probability measures on $\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ enforce the same inequalities.

**Burkholder's function.** For $p\ge2$ and $X\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ with singular values $s_1\ge s_2\ge0$, set (up to a positive normalizing constant)
$$\mathbf{B}_p(X)=\big(s_1-(p-1)s_2\big)\,(s_1+s_2)^{p-1}.$$
$\mathbf{B}_p$ is rank-one concave; equivalently $-\mathbf{B}_p$ is rank-one convex and is **not** polyconvex. Whether $-\mathbf{B}_p$ is quasiconvex is the sharpest concrete instance of Morrey's problem, and a positive answer implies Iwaniec's conjecture $\|S\|_{L^p\to L^p}=p^*-1$ for the Beurling–Ahlfors transform $S$ (Astala–Iwaniec–Prause–Saksman, JAMS 2012).

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1938–39.** Terpstra: for quadratic $f$, rank-one convexity $\Leftrightarrow$ quasiconvexity $\Leftrightarrow$ convexity along the Fourier-transformed cone (van Hove independently, 1947).
- **1952.** Morrey introduces quasiconvexity, proves the lower-semicontinuity theorem, and conjectures rank-one convexity is strictly weaker in all vectorial cases $m,n\ge2$.
- **1977.** Ball introduces polyconvexity and existence theory for nonlinear elasticity, making the hierarchy central to continuum mechanics.
- **1992.** Šverák constructs a rank-one convex, non-quasiconvex $f$ on $\mathbb{R}^{3\times2}$; by adding dummy rows/columns the result extends to all $m\ge3$, $n\ge2$. The case $m=2$ (any $n$) is untouched by his method.
- **1999.** Müller: rank-one convexity implies quasiconvexity on **diagonal** $2\times2$ matrices. Kristensen: quasiconvexity is not a local condition — no finite-order pointwise condition can characterize it.
- **2008.** Faraco–Székelyhidi settle Tartar's conjecture on the structure of quasiconvex hulls in $\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$, giving strong rigidity in the 2D case.
- **2012.** Astala–Iwaniec–Prause–Saksman link Morrey's problem in $\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ to sharp quasiconformal and martingale estimates via $\mathbf{B}_p$.
- **2016–2019.** Kirchheim–Kristensen (1-homogeneous rank-one convex functions), Harris–Kirchheim–Lin (upper triangular case), Guerra (extremal rank-one convex integrands).
- **2018–2022.** Grabovsky produces new families of rank-one convex non-quasiconvex functions from composite-material formulas (higher-dimensional spaces); Voss et al. develop systematic numerical quasiconvexity tests aimed directly at $2\times2$ candidates.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Setting | Result |
|---|---|
| $m\ge3$, $n\ge2$ | **Conjecture true**: Šverák's counterexample (1992) |
| $m=1$ or $n=1$ | All four notions coincide with convexity |
| $f$ quadratic, any $m,n$ | Rank-one convex $\Rightarrow$ quasiconvex (Terpstra 1938) |
| $f$ on diagonal $2\times2$ matrices | Rank-one convex $\Rightarrow$ quasiconvex (Müller, IMRN 1999) |
| $f$ on upper-triangular $2\times2$ matrices | Rank-one convex $\Rightarrow$ quasiconvex (Harris–Kirchheim–Lin, Calc. Var. PDE 2018) |
| Positively $1$-homogeneous rank-one convex $f$ | Convexity at rank-$\le1$ matrices; strong structure results (Kirchheim–Kristensen, ARMA 2016) |
| $-\mathbf{B}_p$, $p\ge2$, on restricted classes (radial stretchings, maps of small distortion, $\det Df\ge0$ subclasses) | Burkholder inequality verified (AIPS, JAMS 2012) |
| $\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$, general $f$ | **Open** |

Numerics: extensive finite-element and neural-network relaxation searches over Burkholder-type and Šverák-type integrands on $\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ have produced **no** violation of the quasiconvexity inequality (Voss–Martin–Sander–Kumar–Kochmann–Neff, J. Nonlinear Sci. 2022).

## 5. Principal Obstacles

1. **Šverák's mechanism is unavailable in $2\times2$.** His counterexample lives on a $3$-dimensional linear subspace $L\subset\mathbb{R}^{3\times2}$ containing **no** rank-one matrix; on such an $L$ *every* function is trivially rank-one convex, and a suitably chosen $f$ (a cubic) then fails Jensen along an explicit periodic map. In $\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ no such subspace exists — see §10 — so the "free lunch" of automatic rank-one convexity is gone.
2. **Quasiconvexity is non-local and undecidable-looking.** Kristensen (1999) showed quasiconvexity cannot be detected by any local condition, so no pointwise differential inequality can ever separate the two classes; any proof must be global.
3. **Testing requires all test functions.** Verifying quasiconvexity means checking a Jensen inequality against an infinite-dimensional space of periodic gradient fields. There is no known finite reduction, and no algorithm decides quasiconvexity even for polynomials.
4. **Laminates are hard to exhaust.** To disprove quasiconvexity one usually exhibits a gradient Young measure that is not a laminate; in $\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ the rigidity results (Faraco–Székelyhidi; Šverák's compactness results for $2\times2$) push in the opposite direction, and no non-laminate gradient Young measure with the required extremality has been produced.
5. **Fourier/null-Lagrangian methods stall.** The quadratic case is fully solved by Plancherel because the cone condition transfers exactly; for degree $\ge3$ integrands the Fourier symbol no longer controls the sign, and the only null Lagrangian available in $2\times2$ is $\det$, giving a one-parameter family of corrections — far too little to certify or break quasiconvexity.

## 6. The Gap

Proven (§4) covers *linear slices* (diagonal, triangular, quadratic) and *specific integrands on restricted map classes*. The general statement quantifies over **all** continuous rank-one convex $f$ on the full $4$-dimensional space $\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ and **all** Lipschitz periodic $\varphi$.

The precise barrier is:

> **Is every homogeneous gradient Young measure on $\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ (equivalently, every $\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$-valued gradient distribution satisfying the minors relations) a laminate — i.e. reachable by iterated rank-one convex combinations?**

If yes, rank-one convexity implies quasiconvexity in $2\times2$. If no, the gap is a single non-laminate gradient Young measure $\nu$ whose barycenter violates Jensen for some rank-one convex $f$. Both directions need a genuinely new construction: an approximation theorem building arbitrary $2\times2$ gradient measures from laminates, or a "$T_5$-style" configuration in $\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ that is quasiconvexly but not rank-one convexly nontrivial.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Oxford / ETH school (Kristensen, Guerra, Kirchheim).** Structure of *extremal* rank-one convex integrands: Guerra's classification (Calc. Var. PDE 2019) reduces Šverák's conjecture on extremals in $2\times2$ to a rigidity statement about $1$-homogeneous functions. Ongoing work couples this to compensated compactness and Hardy-space integrability of minors.
- **Helsinki / Aalto (Astala, Prause, Saksman) and Syracuse (Iwaniec).** The Burkholder route: verify $\int_{\mathbb{T}^2}\mathbf{B}_p(Df)\le \mathbf{B}_p(\bar{Df})$ for wider classes of quasiregular maps; each extension of the admissible distortion range narrows the possible counterexample. *(frontier — verify)* Extensions to non-negative-Jacobian $W^{1,p}$ classes are reported in recent preprints.
- **Duisburg-Essen / Dortmund (Neff, Martin, Voss) with Kochmann (ETH).** Computational quasiconvexity envelopes for isotropic energies in planar elasticity; the "Morrey conjecture testbed" of candidate integrands remains unbroken numerically.
- **Temple (Grabovsky).** Composite-material microstructure-independent formulas as a factory for rank-one convex non-quasiconvex functions; the open question is whether the construction can be compressed into $\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Machine-learning searches.** Parameterized neural integrands trained to maximize the Jensen defect subject to rank-one convexity constraints; so far only rediscoveries of known extremals. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Future Work

- Prove or refute **laminate density**: is every homogeneous gradient Young measure on $\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ a weak-$*$ limit of laminates? This is the cleanest equivalent reformulation.
- Settle quasiconvexity of $-\mathbf{B}_p$ for a single $p>2$; a positive answer yields Iwaniec's conjecture, a negative answer disproves the $2\times2$ implication outright.
- Extend Müller's and Harris–Kirchheim–Lin's slice theorems from $2$-dimensional linear subspaces of $\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ to $3$-dimensional ones — the last step before the full space.
- Build a $T_N$-type configuration in $\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ whose quasiconvex hull strictly exceeds its rank-one convex hull; Faraco–Székelyhidi's localization theorem constrains where such a set could live.
- Develop quantitative, certified numerics (interval-arithmetic relaxation bounds) so a computed Jensen defect can be turned into a proof.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Why $m\ge3$ is easy and $m=n=2$ is not.**

*Šverák's subspace.* In $\mathbb{R}^{3\times2}$ take
$$L=\left\{X(r,s,t)=\begin{pmatrix} r&0\\ 0&s\\ t&t\end{pmatrix}: r,s,t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.$$
A nonzero $a\otimes b$ in $L$ would need all $2\times2$ minors to vanish; the minors of $X(r,s,t)$ include $rs$, $rt$, $-st$, and vanishing of all of them forces at most one coordinate nonzero — but then the third row structure $(t,t)$ conflicts with the first two rows unless $X=0$. Hence $L\cap\{\mathrm{rank}\,1\}=\{0\}$: **no rank-one line lies in $L$**. Define $g(X(r,s,t))=-rst$ on $L$, extend by the orthogonal projection $P:\mathbb{R}^{3\times2}\to L$, and set
$$f_\varepsilon(X)=g(PX)+\varepsilon\big(|X|^2+|X|^4\big).$$
For small $\varepsilon>0$ this is rank-one convex (the quadratic term dominates on rank-one directions, since $g\circ P$ has no rank-one direction of pure concavity available). But testing with the periodic gradient
$$\varphi(x_1,x_2)=\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\big(\sin 2\pi x_1,\;\sin 2\pi x_2,\;\sin 2\pi(x_1+x_2)\big),$$
whose gradient traces out $X(\cos2\pi x_1,\cos2\pi x_2,\cos2\pi(x_1+x_2))\in L$, gives
$$\int_{[0,1]^2} g(D\varphi)\,dx=-\int_{[0,1]^2}\cos2\pi x_1\cos2\pi x_2\cos2\pi(x_1+x_2)\,dx=-\tfrac14<0=g(0),$$
using $\cos A\cos B\cos(A+B)$ averaging to $\tfrac14$ over the torus. For $\varepsilon$ small the perturbation $\varepsilon\int(|D\varphi|^2+|D\varphi|^4)$ is $O(\varepsilon)$ and cannot restore the inequality. So $f_\varepsilon$ is rank-one convex and not quasiconvex.

*The $2\times2$ obstruction.* Write $X=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}$ and change coordinates
$$u=\tfrac{a+d}{2},\quad v=\tfrac{a-d}{2},\quad s=\tfrac{b+c}{2},\quad t=\tfrac{b-c}{2}.$$
Then $ad=u^2-v^2$, $bc=s^2-t^2$, so
$$\det X = u^2 - v^2 - s^2 + t^2,$$
a nondegenerate quadratic form of **signature $(2,2)$** on $\mathbb{R}^4$, whose null cone is exactly the rank-one matrices. A maximal positive-definite (or negative-definite) subspace has dimension $2$. Hence the restriction of $\det$ to any $3$-dimensional subspace $V\subset\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ is either degenerate — and a kernel vector is null — or indefinite of signature $(2,1)$ or $(1,2)$ — and an indefinite form on $\mathbb{R}^3$ has nonzero null vectors. Either way $V$ contains a nonzero rank-one matrix.

**Conclusion.** Every subspace of $\mathbb{R}^{2\times2}$ of dimension $\ge3$ meets the rank-one cone, so rank-one convexity is never a vacuous constraint there, and Šverák's construction has no analogue. Two-dimensional rank-one-free subspaces do exist (e.g. $\mathrm{span}\{I,J\}$ with $J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$, on which $\det=u^2+t^2>0$), but a function of two variables alone cannot produce the Jensen defect above: this is exactly the dimensional deficit at the heart of the open case.
