# Schanuel Conjecture

> **Topic:** Real & Complex Analysis · **ID:** `05-analysis/schanuel-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

**Conjecture (Schanuel, c. 1960).** Let $z_1,\dots,z_n \in \mathbb{C}$ be linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$. Then

$$\operatorname{trdeg}_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}\bigl(z_1,\dots,z_n,\;e^{z_1},\dots,e^{z_n}\bigr) \;\ge\; n .$$

Equivalently: among the $2n$ numbers $z_i, e^{z_i}$ there are at least $n$ that are algebraically independent over $\mathbb{Q}$. The bound is sharp — take $z_i$ algebraic, or $z_1=1$, $z_2=e$, giving $\operatorname{trdeg}=2$ for $n=2$.

A complete proof must establish this for every $n$ and every $\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent tuple. A disproof requires exhibiting a single tuple with $\operatorname{trdeg} \le n-1$: an *unexpected* polynomial relation $P(z_1,\dots,z_n,e^{z_1},\dots,e^{z_n})=0$ not forced by linear dependence. The conjecture is the assertion that the only algebraic relations between numbers and their exponentials are those generated by the functional equation $e^{a+b}=e^ae^b$ and by $\mathbb{Q}$-linear dependencies among the arguments.

Status: **open for every $n\ge 2$** in the general complex setting.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Exponential fields.** An *exponential field* is a pair $(K,E)$ with $K$ a field of characteristic $0$ and $E:(K,+)\to(K^\times,\cdot)$ a group homomorphism: $E(x+y)=E(x)E(y)$. The motivating example is $\mathbb{C}_{\exp}=(\mathbb{C},\exp)$, with $\ker \exp = 2\pi i\mathbb{Z}$.

**Predimension.** For a finite tuple $\bar z=(z_1,\dots,z_n)$ in an exponential field over a subfield $F$, define Zilber's predimension

$$\delta(\bar z / F) \;=\; \operatorname{trdeg}_F F(\bar z, E(\bar z)) \;-\; \operatorname{ldim}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\bar z / F),$$

where $\operatorname{ldim}_\mathbb{Q}$ is linear dimension over $\mathbb{Q}$. Schanuel's conjecture is exactly the statement

$$\delta(\bar z/\mathbb{Q}) \ \ge\ 0 \qquad \text{for all finite } \bar z \subset \mathbb{C},$$

i.e. $(\mathbb{C},\exp)$ carries a Hrushovski-style predimension with no negative-dimensional configurations. The associated *Schanuel dimension* $d(\bar z)=\min\{\delta(\bar y): \bar z\subseteq \langle\bar y\rangle_\mathbb{Q}\}$ is then a pregeometry.

**Underlying classical theorems.**

- *Hermite–Lindemann:* $z\ne0$ algebraic $\Rightarrow$ $e^z$ transcendental. This is $n=1$.
- *Lindemann–Weierstrass:* if $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n$ are $\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent algebraic numbers, then $e^{\alpha_1},\dots,e^{\alpha_n}$ are algebraically independent over $\mathbb{Q}$. This is the case $\bar z\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$.
- *Baker (1966):* if $\log\alpha_1,\dots,\log\alpha_n$ are $\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent logarithms of algebraic numbers, they are linearly independent over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ — i.e. $\operatorname{trdeg}\ge1$ where Schanuel predicts $n$.
- *Gel'fond–Schneider:* $\alpha^\beta$ transcendental for $\alpha\ne0,1$ algebraic and $\beta$ algebraic irrational.

**Geometric reformulation.** Let $V\subseteq \mathbb{G}_a^n\times\mathbb{G}_m^n$ be an irreducible variety over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ of dimension $<n$. Schanuel says the graph $\Gamma=\{(\bar z,e^{\bar z})\}$ meets $V$ only in points whose coordinates $z_i$ satisfy a $\mathbb{Q}$-linear relation. Varieties $V$ containing no translate of a proper subgroup $\mathbb{G}_a^k\times\mathbb{G}_m^k$ are called *rotund*/*free*; Schanuel is an upper-bound statement about $\Gamma\cap V$, dual to Zilber's *exponential-algebraic closedness*, which asserts $\Gamma\cap V\ne\emptyset$ for rotund $V$ of dimension $n$.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

Stephen Schanuel formulated the conjecture around 1960 in a course of Serge Lang's at Columbia; Lang published it in *Introduction to Transcendental Numbers* (1966), noting that it "contains all known transcendence results as special cases" and would settle essentially every classical open transcendence question at one stroke.

Milestones:

- **1971 — Ax.** James Ax proved the differential-field/formal-power-series analogue (*Annals of Mathematics* 93): the "Ax–Schanuel theorem". This is the deepest evidence for the conjecture and made clear that the obstruction is arithmetic, not formal.
- **1989 — Diaz.** Best general quantitative lower bounds on transcendence degree for exponential families: $\operatorname{trdeg}\ge\lceil (d+1)/2\rceil$ in Gel'fond's problem, against Schanuel's predicted $d-1$.
- **1996 — Nesterenko.** Algebraic independence of $\pi,\,e^{\pi},\,\Gamma(1/4)$ via modular functions — a genuine transcendence-degree-$3$ result, though not directly an instance of Schanuel.
- **1996 — Macintyre & Wilkie.** Schanuel's conjecture implies the first-order theory of $\mathbb{R}_{\exp}$ is decidable, building on Wilkie's o-minimality theorem for $\mathbb{R}_{\exp}$ (JAMS 1996). Schanuel thus became a logic problem as well as a number-theory one.
- **2005 — Zilber.** Construction of *pseudo-exponential fields* $B_\kappa$: algebraically closed exponential fields of each uncountable cardinality, categorical, satisfying Schanuel's conjecture *by construction* plus exponential-algebraic closedness. Zilber's conjecture: $\mathbb{C}_{\exp}\cong B_{2^{\aleph_0}}$, which would imply Schanuel.
- **2006–2010 — Kirby, Marker, Bays.** Uniform versions, exponential algebraicity, and reduction of Zilber's conjecture to (i) Schanuel and (ii) exponential-algebraic closedness of $\mathbb{C}$.
- **2018–2024.** Substantial progress on the *second* half (closedness) by Bays–Kirby, Aslanyan–Kirby–Mantova, D'Aquino–Terzo, and Gallinaro. Schanuel itself has not moved.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

Proven cases and near-misses:

1. **$n=1$:** complete. $z\ne0$ $\Rightarrow$ $\operatorname{trdeg}\mathbb{Q}(z,e^z)\ge1$ (Hermite–Lindemann).
2. **All $\bar z\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, any $n$:** complete (Lindemann–Weierstrass). Hence $e,\ e^{\sqrt2},\ e^{i}$ etc. behave as predicted.
3. **Function-field / differential case, all $n$ (Ax 1971).** If $x_i,y_i$ lie in a differential field of characteristic $0$ with derivations $D$, $Dy_i=y_i\,Dx_i$, and the $x_i$ are $\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent modulo constants $C$, then
   $$\operatorname{trdeg}_C C(x_1,\dots,x_n,y_1,\dots,y_n)\ \ge\ n+\operatorname{rank}(Dx_i).$$
   In particular Schanuel holds for formal power series in $t\mathbb{C}[[t]]$.
4. **Semiabelian and elliptic analogues.** Kirby (Selecta Math. 2009) for exponential differential equations of semiabelian varieties; Bertrand for non-isoconstant elliptic curves over function fields.
5. **Zilber's fields $B_\kappa$:** Schanuel holds outright in these; so the conjecture is consistent with strong closure axioms.
6. **Quantitative fragments.** For $\beta$ algebraic of degree $d$, Schanuel predicts $\alpha^{\beta},\alpha^{\beta^2},\dots,\alpha^{\beta^{d-1}}$ have transcendence degree $d-1$; Gel'fond gave $\ge1$, Chudnovsky $\gg\log d$, Diaz (1989) $\ge\lceil(d+1)/2\rceil$. Roughly **half** the predicted degree, for all $d$.
7. **$n$ logarithms of algebraic numbers:** Schanuel predicts full algebraic independence; Baker's theorem gives $\operatorname{trdeg}\ge1$ only. No unconditional proof that $\log2$ and $\log3$ are algebraically independent.

Open even for $n=2$: algebraic independence of $e$ and $\pi$ (take $z_1=1$, $z_2=i\pi$); transcendence of $e+\pi$, $e\pi$, $\pi^e$, $2^e$, $e^e$.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Transcendence machinery caps out at low degree.** All unconditional results come from auxiliary-function constructions (Siegel's lemma, Schneider–Lang, zero estimates on group varieties). To get transcendence degree $t$ one needs a *multiplicity/zero estimate* on a variety of dimension $t$ together with an elimination step; the known "algebraic independence criteria" (Philippon, Jabbouri, Nesterenko) lose roughly a factor $2$ at each level, which is precisely the source of Diaz's $(d+1)/2$ against the expected $d-1$. Pushing to full degree requires arithmetic information about ideals in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ of every dimension, which no current method supplies.
- **No arithmetic input distinguishes $\exp$ from formal exponentiation.** Ax's proof is differential-algebraic: it uses the derivation, which exists in a power-series or function-field setting but has no analogue over $\mathbb{C}$ with $\mathbb{Q}$ as the constant field. There is no derivation on $\mathbb{C}$ compatible with $\exp$ and trivial on $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, so Ax–Schanuel cannot be specialized.
- **The kernel obstruction.** $\ker\exp=2\pi i\mathbb{Z}$ is cyclic and its generator $2\pi i$ is entangled with the very transcendence questions at issue; the model-theoretic construction of $B_\kappa$ makes the kernel a free rank-1 module by fiat, which is exactly what one cannot verify in $\mathbb{C}$.
- **No "descent" or induction on $n$.** A relation among $n$ numbers does not decompose into relations among fewer; $\delta$ is submodular but the minimum can be attained only at the top, so a putative counterexample cannot be localized.
- **Analytic methods see only growth.** Nevanlinna theory, o-minimality, and Pila–Wilkie counting control *how many* algebraic points lie on transcendental sets, but Schanuel concerns a single point's field of definition — a statement invisible to counting.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: (a) all cases where the arguments are algebraic; (b) all cases in the presence of a derivation; (c) transcendence degree $\gtrsim n/2$ in structured exponential families. Conjectured: transcendence degree exactly $\ge n$ for arbitrary complex arguments.

The gap is one precise step: **converting a differential-algebraic independence statement into an arithmetic one without a derivation.** In Ax's theorem the hypothesis "$\bar x$ linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$ modulo constants" carries the whole proof; over $\mathbb{C}$ every element is a constant. Equivalently, in transcendence-method language: given a variety $V\subset\mathbb{G}_a^n\times\mathbb{G}_m^n$ of dimension $n-1$ defined over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ and a point of $\Gamma\cap V$ with $\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent $z_i$, one must derive a contradiction from a height/degree estimate on $V$. Current zero estimates yield the contradiction only when $\dim V \lesssim n/2$. Closing the interval $n/2 < \dim V < n$ is the entire problem.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Zilber programme (Oxford, UEA, Leeds, Napoli).** Split Zilber's conjecture into Schanuel + exponential-algebraic closedness. Bays–Kirby (2018) reduced quasiminimality of $\mathbb{C}_{\exp}$ to closedness statements. Aslanyan–Kirby–Mantova (IMRN 2023) proved exponential-algebraic closedness for large classes of rotund varieties, including those with dominant projection to $\mathbb{G}_m^n$. Gallinaro has extended this to "exponential sums" and raising-to-powers systems via tropical geometry *(frontier — verify)*. Net effect: the *existence* half is close to done; Schanuel remains untouched.
- **Ax–Schanuel for other functions.** A large industry (Pila–Tsimerman for the $j$-function, Mok–Pila–Tsimerman for Shimura varieties, Aslanyan for Painlevé equations) proves functional analogues in ever more settings, feeding unlikely-intersection results. These confirm the *shape* of Schanuel but do not approach the arithmetic case.
- **Algebraic independence via modular/E-functions.** Nesterenko-style methods (Moscow, Paris) remain the only route to transcendence degree $\ge3$; extending them past Ramanujan functions is active.
- **Conditional consequences.** Continued cataloguing of what Schanuel implies (decidability of $\mathbb{R}_{\exp}$, Shapiro's conjecture on exponential polynomials — D'Aquino–Macintyre–Terzo 2014; algebraic independence of exponential towers).

## 8. Future Work

- Find a *non-differential* substitute for Ax's argument: a "generic derivation" or an arithmetic analogue of the Kolchin–Ax rank hypothesis. Suggested repeatedly by Bertrand and Kirby.
- Improve algebraic independence criteria so the loss per elimination step is $o(1)$ rather than a factor $2$; Waldschmidt lists this as the key technical target for the Gel'fond–Schneider family.
- Prove *any* new instance with $n=2$: e.g. $\operatorname{trdeg}\mathbb{Q}(e,\pi)=2$, or algebraic independence of $\log2,\log3$. Even one would be a landmark.
- Establish the *uniform* Schanuel conjecture (Kirby–Zilber 2006) — bounds depending only on degree and $n$ — which is formally weaker in appearance and may be attackable by compactness.
- Complete exponential-algebraic closedness of $\mathbb{C}$, isolating Schanuel as the single remaining obstruction to Zilber's isomorphism conjecture.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Goal:** derive the algebraic independence of $e$ and $\pi$ from Schanuel with $n=2$, then show why the argument cannot be run unconditionally.

Take $z_1=1$, $z_2=i\pi$. These are $\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent: a relation $q_1\cdot 1+q_2\cdot i\pi=0$ with $q_i\in\mathbb{Q}$ forces $q_1=q_2=0$ since $i\pi\notin\mathbb{R}$. Then $e^{z_1}=e$ and $e^{z_2}=e^{i\pi}=-1$. Schanuel gives

$$\operatorname{trdeg}_\mathbb{Q}\,\mathbb{Q}(1,\;i\pi,\;e,\;-1)\ \ge\ 2 .$$

The generators $1$ and $-1$ are algebraic, so the field is $\mathbb{Q}(i\pi,e)$ and $\operatorname{trdeg}_\mathbb{Q}\mathbb{Q}(i\pi,e)\ge2$: the numbers $i\pi$ and $e$ are algebraically independent. Since $\mathbb{Q}(i\pi)$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\pi)$ have the same transcendence degree ($i$ algebraic), $\pi$ and $e$ are algebraically independent. Immediate corollaries: $e+\pi$, $e\pi$, $e-\pi$, $e/\pi$ are all transcendental — none of which is known unconditionally.

**Where the unconditional proof stops.** Suppose, for contradiction, $\operatorname{trdeg}\mathbb{Q}(i\pi,e)=1$. Then there is a nonzero $P\in\mathbb{Q}[X,Y]$ with $P(i\pi,e)=0$. Standard machinery would attack this by building an auxiliary function
$$F(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{J}\sum_{k=0}^{K} a_{jk}\,z^{j}e^{kz},\qquad a_{jk}\in\mathbb{Z},$$
vanishing to high order at points of the lattice $\{m+ n\,i\pi\}$, then deriving a contradiction from "a nonzero algebraic integer of small house is $0$". The Siegel-lemma count works when the number of unknowns $(J{+}1)(K{+}1)$ beats the number of vanishing conditions — which it does for *linear* independence (Baker) and for degree-$1$ transcendence (Hermite–Lindemann, $n=1$). For $n=2$ one must instead show a $1$-dimensional ideal $I\subset\overline{\mathbb{Q}}[X,Y]$ containing $P$ cannot have small height *and* small degree simultaneously. The available algebraic independence criteria (Philippon, Nesterenko) require a multiplicity estimate on the $1$-dimensional variety $V(P)$ inside $\mathbb{G}_a\times\mathbb{G}_m$; the known zero estimates on $\mathbb{G}_a^n\times\mathbb{G}_m^n$ (Masser–Wüstholz, Philippon) give the needed bound only when $\dim V \le n/2$, i.e. $\dim V=0$ here. The chain therefore breaks at exactly the point identified in Section 6.

For contrast, the $n=1$ case *does* close: $z=1$ gives $\operatorname{trdeg}\mathbb{Q}(1,e)\ge1$, i.e. $e$ transcendental (Hermite, 1873); $z=i\pi$ would give $\operatorname{trdeg}\mathbb{Q}(i\pi,-1)\ge1$, i.e. $\pi$ transcendental (Lindemann, 1882). Schanuel's content is entirely in the passage from one number to several at once.
