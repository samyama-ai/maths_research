# Zeros of the Riemann Xi Function and de Branges Spaces

> **Topic:** Real & Complex Analysis · **ID:** `05-analysis/de-branges-space-approach-to-rh` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let
$$\xi(s) = \tfrac12 s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{s}{2}\right)\zeta(s), \qquad \Xi(z) = \xi\!\left(\tfrac12 + iz\right).$$
$\Xi$ is entire of order $1$, even, and real on $\mathbb{R}$. The Riemann Hypothesis (RH) is the statement that all zeros of $\Xi$ are real.

**The problem.** Decide whether $\Xi$ arises as the "imaginary part" of a Hermite–Biehler function, i.e. whether there exists an entire $E$ with $|E(\bar z)| < |E(z)|$ for $\operatorname{Im} z > 0$ and $E = A - iB$ with $A,B$ real entire and $B = c\,\Xi$ for some $c \neq 0$. Equivalently: does $\Xi$ lie in the Laguerre–Pólya class, and can a de Branges space $\mathcal{H}(E)$ be constructed — from the arithmetic of $\zeta$ rather than assumed — whose structure forces this?

A complete solution is either (a) an explicit construction of such an $E$ (or of a chain of de Branges spaces with a self-adjoint-extension/positivity argument) yielding RH, or (b) a proof that no de Branges-space realization of $\Xi$ with the required positivity can exist, closing the programme. Partial credit — identifying which positivity hypotheses hold — is the current currency.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Hermite–Biehler class.** For entire $E$ set $E^*(z) = \overline{E(\bar z)}$. $E$ is *Hermite–Biehler* ($E \in \mathbb{HB}$) if $|E^*(z)| < |E(z)|$ for all $z$ in the open upper half-plane $\mathbb{C}^+$. Writing $A = \tfrac12(E + E^*)$, $B = \tfrac{i}{2}(E - E^*)$, so $E = A - iB$, both $A,B$ are real entire, all their zeros are real, and the zeros of $A$ and $B$ strictly interlace.

**de Branges space.** For $E \in \mathbb{HB}$,
$$\mathcal{H}(E) = \Big\{ F \text{ entire} : \ \|F\|_E^2 = \int_{\mathbb{R}} \Big|\frac{F(t)}{E(t)}\Big|^2 dt < \infty,\ \ F/E,\ F^*/E \text{ of bounded type and nonpositive mean type in } \mathbb{C}^+ \Big\}.$$
$\mathcal{H}(E)$ is a reproducing-kernel Hilbert space with
$$K(w,z) = \frac{B(z)\overline{A(w)} - A(z)\overline{B(w)}}{\pi (z - \bar w)} = \frac{\overline{E(w)}E(z) - \overline{E^*(w)}E^*(z)}{2\pi i(\bar w - z)} ,$$
and $K(w,w) > 0$ for all $w$. The *Paley–Wiener space* $PW_a$ is the case $E_a(z) = e^{-iaz}$, $A = \cos az$, $B = \sin az$.

**de Branges' structure theory** (1968 monograph): the subspaces of a given $\mathcal{H}(E)$ that are themselves de Branges spaces are totally ordered by inclusion, forming a *chain* $\{\mathcal{H}(E_t)\}$; the chain is generated by a canonical system
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\begin{pmatrix} A(t,z) & B(t,z)\end{pmatrix} = z \begin{pmatrix} A(t,z) & B(t,z)\end{pmatrix} J\,H(t), \qquad J = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ 1 & 0\end{pmatrix},$$
with $H(t) \succeq 0$ a $2\times2$ Hamiltonian. Zeros of $B(t,\cdot)$ are then eigenvalues of a self-adjoint operator, hence real — this is the mechanism the programme wants to attach to $\Xi$.

**Laguerre–Pólya reformulation.** $B$ real entire of genus $\le 1$ is the $B$-part of some $E \in \mathbb{HB}$ iff $B \in \mathcal{LP}$, i.e.
$$B(z) = c z^m e^{\alpha z - \beta z^2}\prod_n \left(1 - \frac{z}{a_n}\right)e^{z/a_n}, \quad \beta \ge 0,\ a_n \in \mathbb{R},\ \textstyle\sum a_n^{-2} < \infty.$$
Hence **RH $\iff \Xi \in \mathcal{LP}$**.

**Pólya's Fourier representation.** $\Xi(z) = 2\int_0^\infty \Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du$ with
$$\Phi(u) = \sum_{n\ge1}\left(2\pi^2 n^4 e^{9u/2} - 3\pi n^2 e^{5u/2}\right)e^{-\pi n^2 e^{2u}} > 0,$$
even and rapidly decaying. Heat flow on the Fourier side gives $\Xi_\lambda(z) = \int_0^\infty e^{\lambda u^2}\Phi(u)\cos(zu)\,du$ and the **de Bruijn–Newman constant** $\Lambda = \inf\{\lambda : \Xi_\lambda \in \mathcal{LP}\}$; RH $\iff \Lambda \le 0$.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1926–1927.** Pólya links reality of zeros of Fourier transforms of positive kernels to RH; the $\mathcal{LP}$ class is codified (Pólya–Schur).
- **1950–1968.** Louis de Branges develops Hilbert spaces of entire functions, culminating in *Hilbert Spaces of Entire Functions* (Prentice-Hall, 1968) — the structure, ordering, and subspace theorems.
- **1986.** de Branges, *The Riemann hypothesis for Hilbert spaces of entire functions* (Bull. AMS 15, 1–17): a criterion deducing reality of zeros from a positivity hypothesis on an associated kernel.
- **1976.** Dym–McKean's *Gaussian Processes, Function Theory, and the Inverse Spectral Problem* recasts the chain theory as inverse spectral theory for strings — the bridge to Krein.
- **1998–2000.** Conrey and Li show the strong positivity hypotheses invoked in de Branges' zeta-specific arguments **fail** for $\zeta$ and Dirichlet $L$-functions (IMRN 2000, no. 18, 929–940). This is the decisive negative result on the direct route.
- **2002.** Burnol constructs the "Sonine spaces" of de Branges concretely for the Fourier transform, giving the first fully verified nontrivial arithmetic-adjacent examples.
- **2006.** Lagarias' survey *Hilbert spaces of entire functions and Dirichlet L-functions* gives the cleanest modern statement of what the programme would need.
- **2018–2020.** Rodgers–Tao prove $\Lambda \ge 0$ (*Ann. of Math.* 194? — published in **Duke Math. J. 169 (2020), 2135–2196**); Polymath15 proves $\Lambda \le 0.22$. So RH $\iff \Lambda = 0$: RH is now *exactly* the borderline case, meaning no "slack" is available in any $\mathcal{LP}$-deformation argument.
- **Computation.** Zeros verified real up to height $3\times10^{12}$ (Platt–Trudgian, 2021); Gourdon (2004) reported the first $10^{13}$ zeros.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **Paley–Wiener / trigonometric cases.** $E_a = e^{-iaz}$: the full structure theory is explicit, $\mathcal{H}(E_a) = PW_a$, zeros of $\sin az$ real and interlacing $\cos az$. Every finite-rank or exponential-type toy model is settled.
- **Sonine spaces.** Burnol (CRAS 334, 2002) determined the de Branges spaces of the Fourier/Hankel transform restricted to functions vanishing on $[-a,a]$ together with their transforms; their structure functions and reproducing kernels are known in closed form.
- **Canonical systems.** Remling (*J. Funct. Anal.* 196, 2002) and Kaltenbäck–Woracek gave complete de Branges realizations for Schrödinger operators with $L^1_{loc}$ potentials and for Pontryagin-space (indefinite) analogues: any real, discrete, suitably-counted spectrum *is* realized by some chain. Existence of *a* Hamiltonian is never the obstacle.
- **Degenerate / partial Euler products.** Truncated Dirichlet polynomials $\sum_{n\le N} n^{-s}$ and the completed $L$-functions of the "degree 0/1" part of the Selberg class admit direct verification; RH holds for the gamma-factor alone (no zeros).
- **$\Lambda$-range.** $0 \le \Lambda \le 0.22$; $\Xi_\lambda \in \mathcal{LP}$ for all $\lambda \ge 0.22$ unconditionally.
- **Weil positivity.** The explicit-formula quadratic functional is positive on many concrete test-function cones (Bombieri, 2000), and Li's criterion ($\lambda_n \ge 0$ for all $n\ge1$, *J. Number Theory* 65, 1997) is verified numerically for $n$ in the low thousands.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Positivity fails where it is needed.** Conrey–Li show the specific kernel positivity de Branges used is false for $\zeta$. Any repair must weaken positivity without losing the self-adjointness that produces real spectrum — no such weakening is known.
- **Existence of a chain is vacuous without arithmetic.** Given RH, a Hamiltonian $H(t)$ realizing the zeros exists trivially; the programme needs $H(t)$ produced *from* the Euler product or the explicit formula. The inverse spectral map (Krein's string / Gelfand–Levitan) is not effective enough to be run from the Dirichlet series.
- **$\Xi$ has order 1, maximal type 0 but infinite-order-like density.** $N(T) \sim \frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}$ grows faster than the linear density of $PW_a$, so $\Xi$ is not in any Paley–Wiener-type space; the standard Beurling–Malliavin machinery (density theorem for completeness radii) does not directly apply.
- **No slack at $\Lambda = 0$.** Because $\Lambda \ge 0$, any argument that would tolerate an $\varepsilon$-perturbation of the kernel proves nothing: the perturbed statement is already known and the unperturbed one is exactly RH.
- **Bounded type is fragile.** The membership conditions in $\mathcal{H}(E)$ ($F/E$ of bounded type, nonpositive mean type) are not stable under the multiplicative decompositions the Euler product suggests; the local factors $(1-p^{-s})^{-1}$ have essential singularities on the boundary at $\Re s = 0$.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: $\Xi$ is real, even, order 1, genus 1, with $\sum \rho^{-2}$ convergent — every $\mathcal{LP}$ prerequisite **except** reality of the zeros. Also proven: if a suitable $E$ exists then RH follows, and conversely RH gives $E$ (e.g. $E = A - i\Xi$ for a suitable interlacing $A$). The gap is therefore the *non-circular construction*: exhibit $A$ real entire with $A - i\Xi \in \mathbb{HB}$, or exhibit the Hamiltonian $H(t)$, using only the functional equation, the Euler product, and the explicit formula. Concretely, the missing step is a positivity statement — a replacement for the Conrey–Li-refuted hypothesis — strong enough to force $K(w,w) > 0$ off the real axis for the candidate kernel built from $\Xi$.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Canonical-systems / spectral-theory school** (Woracek, Vienna; Remling, Oklahoma; Baranov, St. Petersburg; Eichinger): asymptotics of Hamiltonians from spectral data, oscillation theory, and "Krein strings with $\log$-density" that could match $\frac{T}{2\pi}\log T$. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Extremal / Beurling–Selberg school** (Carneiro, ICTP; Chirre; Littmann, NDSU): de Branges spaces used as the natural setting for extremal majorants, giving the best current conditional bounds on $S(t)$, gaps between zeros, and pair-correlation-type sums. This branch produces theorems, not RH.
- **Burnol-style explicit constructions**: Sonine/co-Poisson spaces and their conductor-indexed chains; attempts to attach adelic Poisson summation to a de Branges chain.
- **Hilbert–Pólya adjacent operator constructions** (Connes' trace formula, Berry–Keating $xp$ quantizations, Bender–Brody–Müller): these produce candidate self-adjoint models whose de Branges realization is the open question. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Numerics on $\Lambda$**: sharpening the Polymath15 upper bound $0.22$ remains an active target; no bound below $0.2$ is confirmed.

## 8. Future Work

1. Identify the *weakest* positivity hypothesis in de Branges' 1986 criterion that survives Conrey–Li and is still sufficient — Lagarias explicitly poses this as the programme's decision point.
2. Determine the asymptotic Hamiltonian $H(t)$ that a de Branges chain for $\Xi$ *must* have, assuming RH, and test whether that shape is derivable from the explicit formula alone.
3. Connect Weil positivity of the explicit-formula quadratic form to reproducing-kernel positivity in $\mathcal{H}(E)$; both are positivity of a Hermitian form on a space of test functions, but no dictionary exists.
4. Extend Burnol's Sonine-space computations from $\mathrm{GL}_1$ over $\mathbb{R}$ to the full adelic setting, where the Euler product is a product of local data.
5. Prove unconditional structural facts about $\mathcal{LP}$-membership under Fourier transform of positive kernels — a genuine converse to Pólya's 1926 theorem.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**The model that works: $E_a(z) = e^{-iaz}$, $a>0$.**

For $z = x+iy$, $|E_a(z)| = e^{ay}$ and $|E_a^*(z)| = |e^{i a z}| = e^{-ay}$. For $y > 0$, $e^{-ay} < e^{ay}$, so $E_a \in \mathbb{HB}$. Decompose:
$$A(z) = \tfrac12(e^{-iaz} + e^{iaz}) = \cos az, \qquad B(z) = \tfrac{i}{2}(e^{-iaz} - e^{iaz}) = \sin az.$$
Zeros: $A$ vanishes at $z = \frac{\pi}{2a} + \frac{k\pi}{a}$, $B$ at $z = \frac{k\pi}{a}$ — all real, strictly interlacing, spacing $\pi/a$. The kernel is
$$K(w,z) = \frac{\sin az \cos a\bar w - \cos az \sin a \bar w}{\pi(z-\bar w)} = \frac{\sin\!\big(a(z-\bar w)\big)}{\pi(z-\bar w)},$$
and $K(w,w) = \frac{\sinh(2ay)}{2\pi y} > 0$ for $w = x+iy$, $y\neq 0$; $K(x,x) = a/\pi$. This strict positivity **off the real axis** is exactly what forces $B = \sin az$ to have only real zeros: if $B(w_0)=0$ with $\operatorname{Im} w_0 \ne 0$, the reproducing identity applied to $K(w_0,\cdot)$ contradicts $K(w_0,w_0)>0$.

**Why the same argument stalls for $\Xi$.** Suppose we posit $B(z) = c\,\Xi(z)$ and seek $A$. Counting: $PW_a$ has zero-density $a/\pi$ per unit length, constant; $\Xi$ has density $\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\frac{|z|}{2\pi}$, unbounded. So no fixed $a$ works and one must use a *chain* $\{\mathcal{H}(E_t)\}$ with $H(t)$ engineered so that the eigenvalue density grows logarithmically. Numerically the first three would-be zeros of $B$ are $t_1 = 14.134725142$, $t_2 = 21.022039639$, $t_3 = 25.010857580$, with gaps $6.887$ and $3.989$; an interlacing companion $A$ must have exactly one zero in each of $(t_1,t_2)$ and $(t_2,t_3)$, and its own zeros must be real. Constructing $A$ from $\Xi$ requires knowing that $\Xi$ has no complex zeros — the assumption. Breaking that circle by producing $H(t)$ from the explicit formula
$$\sum_\rho h(\gamma_\rho) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}} h(r)\,\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}\!\left(\tfrac14 + \tfrac{ir}{2}\right)dr + h\!\left(\tfrac{i}{2}\right)+h\!\left(-\tfrac{i}{2}\right) - 2\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\,\hat h(\log n)$$
is precisely the unsolved step of Section 6.
