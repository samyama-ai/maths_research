# KPZ Equation Analysis

> **Topic:** Real & Complex Analysis · **ID:** `05-analysis/kpz-equation-analysis` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

The Kardar–Parisi–Zhang (KPZ) equation is the singular stochastic PDE
$$\partial_t h(t,x) \;=\; \nu\,\partial_x^2 h \;+\; \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\,(\partial_x h)^2 \;+\; \sqrt{D}\,\xi(t,x),$$
with $\xi$ space–time white noise. The nonlinearity is classically meaningless: solutions are Hölder-$\tfrac12^-$ in $x$, so $\partial_x h$ is a distribution and $(\partial_x h)^2$ is undefined.

Three linked problems, in decreasing order of resolution:

1. **Well-posedness (1D — solved).** Give an intrinsic solution theory in which $(\partial_x h)^2$ is defined by renormalization, solutions are unique, and lattice/mollifier approximations converge to the same limit.
2. **Weak universality (open in general).** Prove that every microscopic model in the conjectured class — driven interfaces, directed polymers, interacting particle systems, randomly forced Burgers — converges under weakly asymmetric scaling to the KPZ equation, and under $1{:}2{:}3$ scaling $(h,x,t)\mapsto(\epsilon^{b}h,\epsilon^{-1}x,\epsilon^{-3/2}t)$ to the **KPZ fixed point**, with universal exponents $\chi=1/3$, $z=3/2$ and Tracy–Widom limit laws. Currently proved only for special models.
3. **Dimensions $d\ge 2$ (open).** In $d\ge2$ the equation is not subcritical; no solution theory exists at the physical coupling. Establish (or refute) a nontrivial strong-coupling fixed point in $d=2$, and determine the critical dimension and roughening transition in $d\ge3$.

A complete resolution of (2) means: a checkable structural hypothesis on a microscopic model implying convergence, not a model-by-model computation. A resolution of (3) means a construction of a nontrivial $d=2$ or $d=3$ limit object at strong coupling with identified exponents.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Scaling.** Under $h_\epsilon(t,x)=\epsilon^{b}h(\epsilon^{-z}t,\epsilon^{-1}x)$ the noise scales with weight $-\tfrac{d}{2}-\tfrac{z}{2}$. Taking $z=2$, the equation is *subcritical* (nonlinearity irrelevant at small scales) iff $d<2$; $d=2$ is critical, $d\ge3$ supercritical.

**Regularity.** For $d=1$, $h\in\mathcal C^{\alpha}$ for every $\alpha<\tfrac12$ locally in $x$, and $\mathcal C^{1/4^-}$ in $t$. Hence $\partial_x h\in\mathcal C^{-1/2^-}$ and the square lies outside Bony's paraproduct range: $\mathcal C^{\alpha}\times\mathcal C^{\beta}\to\mathcal C^{\alpha\wedge\beta}$ requires $\alpha+\beta>0$, and here $\alpha+\beta=-1<0$.

**Cole–Hopf.** Set $Z=\exp\!\big(\tfrac{\lambda}{2\nu}h\big)$. Formally $Z$ solves the multiplicative stochastic heat equation (SHE)
$$\partial_t Z=\nu\,\partial_x^2 Z+\tfrac{\lambda\sqrt D}{2\nu}\,Z\,\xi,$$
interpreted in the Itô/Skorokhod sense. For $d=1$ the SHE is well-posed by Walsh integration and, by the Mueller comparison principle, $Z>0$ for a.e. $t>0$ if $Z_0\ge0$, $Z_0\not\equiv0$. Then $h_{\rm CH}:=\tfrac{2\nu}{\lambda}\log Z$ is the **Cole–Hopf solution**.

**Renormalization.** Mollify $\xi_\epsilon=\xi*\rho_\epsilon$ and solve
$$\partial_t h_\epsilon=\nu\partial_x^2h_\epsilon+\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\big[(\partial_x h_\epsilon)^2-C_\epsilon\big]+\sqrt D\,\xi_\epsilon,\qquad C_\epsilon=\frac{c_\rho}{\epsilon}\;(+\,c_0),$$
Then $h_\epsilon\to h_{\rm CH}$ in probability, locally uniformly (Bertini–Giacomin 1997).

**Intrinsic theories.** Hairer's *regularity structures* replaces the Taylor polynomial model by a graded algebra $(\mathcal T,\mathcal G)$ generated by noise symbols $\Xi$, integration $\mathcal I$, and products; a *model* $(\Pi,\Gamma)$ gives analytic meaning to symbols such as $\mathcal I'(\Xi)^2$ via a BPHZ-type renormalization group, and the reconstruction theorem produces a unique distribution matching the local expansion. *Paracontrolled distributions* (Gubinelli–Imkeller–Perkowski) instead posit the ansatz $\partial_x h=\partial_x X\prec \Phi+h^\sharp$ with $h^\sharp$ of positive regularity, reducing the ill-defined product to a resonance term controlled by a commutator estimate.

**Energy solutions.** For the stationary stochastic Burgers equation $u=\partial_x h$, $\partial_t u=\partial_x^2u+\partial_x u^2+\partial_x\xi$, one requires: (i) $u(t)$ is spatial white noise for each $t$; (ii) the nonlinear drift $\int_0^t\partial_x u^2$ exists as a limit of $\int_0^t \partial_x (u*\rho_\epsilon)^2$ in $L^2$ with the Itô-trick bound; (iii) time reversal $u(t,x)\mapsto -u(T-t,x)$ preserves the law.

**Limit objects.** The **KPZ fixed point** is the Markov process on upper semicontinuous functions with transition kernels given by Fredholm determinants of a hit-operator kernel; the **directed landscape** $\mathcal L(x,s;y,t)$ is the four-parameter random field with the metric composition law $\mathcal L(x,s;y,t)=\max_z[\mathcal L(x,s;z,r)+\mathcal L(z,r;y,t)]$ and Airy-sheet marginals.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1986.** Kardar, Parisi and Zhang propose the equation, predict $\chi=1/3$, $z=3/2$ in $d=1$ by a one-loop dynamic RG with an exact fluctuation–dissipation identity; they also predict a strong-coupling/roughening transition for $d>2$.
- **1997.** Bertini–Giacomin: weakly asymmetric simple exclusion converges to the Cole–Hopf solution — the first rigorous "KPZ equation from particle systems" theorem, and the source of the $C_\epsilon$ subtraction.
- **2009–2010.** Tracy–Widom compute ASEP transition probabilities; Amir–Corwin–Quastel and (non-rigorously) Sasamoto–Spohn, Calabrese–Le Doussal–Rosso obtain the exact one-point distribution of narrow-wedge KPZ, a Fredholm determinant crossing from Gaussian to GUE Tracy–Widom.
- **2013–2014.** Hairer solves the KPZ equation intrinsically (Annals of Math. 2013) and then builds regularity structures (Invent. Math. 2014). Gubinelli–Imkeller–Perkowski give the paracontrolled alternative (2015).
- **2014–2018.** Gonçalves–Jara introduce energy solutions; Gubinelli–Perkowski prove their uniqueness (JAMS 2018), unlocking convergence proofs for many non-integrable particle systems.
- **2018–2022.** Hairer–Quastel: weak universality for a class of growth models with general polynomial nonlinearity. Matetski–Quastel–Remenik construct the KPZ fixed point (Acta Math. 2021); Dauvergne–Ortmann–Virág construct the directed landscape (Acta Math. 2022).
- **2020–2023.** Caravenna–Sun–Zygouras settle the entire subcritical regime in $d=2$ and construct the **critical 2D stochastic heat flow** (Invent. Math. 2023) — a genuinely non-Gaussian critical object, but for the SHE, not a $d=2$ KPZ height function.
- **2023–2024.** Quastel–Sarkar prove convergence of a class of exclusion processes *and* the KPZ equation to the KPZ fixed point (JAMS 2023); Virág and Wu establish convergence of the KPZ equation / directed polymers to the directed landscape.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **$d=1$, well-posedness:** complete. Existence, uniqueness, and approximation-independence for $h_0\in\mathcal C^{\alpha}$, $\alpha>0$, and for narrow wedge $h_0=\delta_0$-type data; solutions global in time (no blow-up), on $\mathbb T$ and on $\mathbb R$.
- **Exact one-point law:** for narrow wedge, $\mathbb P(h(t,0)+\tfrac{t}{24}\le s)$ has a closed Fredholm determinant; as $t\to\infty$, $t^{-1/3}(h(t,0)+\tfrac{t}{24})\Rightarrow \mathrm{TW}_{\rm GUE}$ up to constants. Flat and stationary initial data give $\mathrm{TW}_{\rm GOE}$ and Baik–Rains $F_0$ respectively.
- **Stationarity:** two-sided Brownian motion (with drift) is invariant for stochastic Burgers; superdiffusive variance $\mathrm{Var}\,h(t,0)\asymp t^{2/3}$.
- **Tail bounds:** Corwin–Ghosal establish upper tail $\exp(-\tfrac{4}{3}s^{3/2})$ and lower tail $\exp(-\tfrac{1}{12}s^3)$ exponents for narrow-wedge KPZ at all $t$.
- **Weak universality (proved instances):** weakly asymmetric exclusion (ASEP, and multi-species/non-simple variants), directed polymers in the intermediate disorder regime $\beta=\hat\beta n^{-1/4}$, Ginzburg–Landau interface models, and the Hairer–Quastel class $\partial_t h=\partial_x^2h+\sqrt\epsilon\,F(\partial_x h)+\xi$ with polynomial $F$, where only $\int F''$-type constants enter the limiting coefficient.
- **$d=2$:** for the SHE with coupling $\beta_\epsilon=\hat\beta\sqrt{2\pi/\log(1/\epsilon)}$, the entire subcritical phase $\hat\beta<1$ gives Edwards–Wilkinson (Gaussian) fluctuations; at $\hat\beta=1$ the stochastic heat flow exists and is not Gaussian.
- **$d\ge3$:** for small coupling $\beta<\beta_c$, the mollified KPZ height function has Gaussian (Edwards–Wilkinson) fluctuations after centering — proved by Magnen–Unterberger and by Dunlap–Gu–Ryzhik–Zeitouni; Chatterjee–Dunlap construct a $(2+1)$-dimensional limit in a weak-coupling regime.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Failure of harmonic analysis.** Products $\mathcal C^{\alpha}\times\mathcal C^{\beta}$ need $\alpha+\beta>0$; KPZ sits at $-1$. No paraproduct, Bessel-potential or Besov machinery repairs this without additional stochastic input; the definition of $(\partial_x h)^2$ is not analytic but probabilistic (Wick renormalization + Itô isometry in Wiener chaos).
- **Non-criticality in $d\ge2$.** Subcriticality is the hypothesis for regularity structures and paracontrolled calculus alike: the number of divergent subdiagrams must be finite. In $d=2$ the counting is logarithmic and infinitely many terms diverge; the whole local-expansion framework collapses. There is no known substitute.
- **Integrability is fragile.** The exact distributions come from Bethe-ansatz-solvable models (ASEP, $q$-TASEP, log-gamma polymer, Macdonald processes). Perturbing the jump rates destroys the Yang–Baxter structure, so universality cannot be obtained by transporting formulas.
- **Non-Gaussian noise / non-stationary starts.** Energy-solution uniqueness needs stationarity; Cole–Hopf needs the exponential to linearize; regularity structures need Gaussian-type stochastic estimates. No single method covers general initial data *and* general microscopic noise.
- **Long-time control.** Even in $d=1$, convergence of the KPZ equation to the KPZ fixed point requires uniform-in-$t$ tail and regularity estimates that current fixed-point arguments obtain only via integrable formulas or via Brownian-Gibbs resampling on Airy line ensembles — again model-specific.

## 6. The Gap

Precisely:

- Section 4 gives a solution theory in $d=1$ and exact limit laws for a *finite list* of integrable models. Section 1(2) asks for a **structural criterion**. The missing step is a robust proof that the $1{:}2{:}3$ rescaled height function of a model with only assumed properties (local Markov, non-degenerate curvature of the flux $\ell''(\rho)\ne0$, exponential mixing, a stationary measure with Brownian scaling) converges — without integrable formulas or Brownian-Gibbs.
- For $d\ge2$: the gap is the absence of *any* nontrivial construction of KPZ at fixed coupling. The critical 2D stochastic heat flow is a genuine limit object, but $\log$ of it is not known to define a height function; the transfer from SHE to KPZ requires strict positivity and moment control that are unavailable at criticality.
- For $d\ge3$: whether a phase transition in $\beta$ separating the proven Gaussian phase from a rough strong-coupling phase actually exists is open; numerics support $\chi\approx0.18$ in $d=3$, but no rigorous lower bound on roughness exceeding the Edwards–Wilkinson prediction is known.

## 8. Future Work

- Prove weak universality for a non-integrable interacting particle system with general (non-Brownian) stationary measure, via energy solutions extended beyond stationarity.
- Build a coupling-based, formula-free proof of convergence to the KPZ fixed point, replacing Fredholm determinants by comparison and Brownian-resampling arguments.
- Identify $\log Z$ for the critical 2D stochastic heat flow, or prove that no height-function limit exists at $\hat\beta=1$.
- Rigorously exhibit a roughening transition in $d=3$: show that for $\beta$ large the interface fluctuation exponent strictly exceeds the Edwards–Wilkinson value $0$.
- Extend to KPZ with multiplicative or non-Gaussian noise, and to systems of coupled KPZ equations, where the renormalization constants are matrix-valued.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Why $C_\epsilon\sim c/\epsilon$: an exact computation.** Take $\nu=D=1$ and drop the nonlinearity, i.e. the Edwards–Wilkinson equation on $\mathbb R$ with mollified noise
$$\partial_t X_\epsilon=\partial_x^2X_\epsilon+\xi_\epsilon,\qquad \xi_\epsilon=\xi*\rho_\epsilon,\quad \hat\rho_\epsilon(k)=\hat\rho(\epsilon k),$$
with $\hat\rho$ smooth, $\hat\rho(0)=1$, even, rapidly decaying. In Fourier modes,
$$d\hat X_\epsilon(k)=-k^2\hat X_\epsilon(k)\,dt+\hat\rho(\epsilon k)\,d\hat W(k).$$
This is an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process per mode, with stationary variance
$$\mathbb E|\hat X_\epsilon(k)|^2=\frac{\hat\rho(\epsilon k)^2}{2k^2}.$$
Hence
$$\mathbb E\big[(\partial_x X_\epsilon(t,0))^2\big]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}k^2\,\frac{\hat\rho(\epsilon k)^2}{2k^2}\,dk=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}\hat\rho(\epsilon k)^2\,dk=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\cdot\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}\hat\rho(u)^2\,du.$$
So $c_\rho=\tfrac{1}{4\pi}\|\hat\rho\|_{L^2}^2$ and the divergence is exactly linear in $\epsilon^{-1}$: the constant depends on the mollifier, the *rate* does not. Removing $C_\epsilon=c_\rho/\epsilon$ leaves the Wick square $(\partial_xX_\epsilon)^{\diamond2}$, which converges in $\mathcal C^{-1-\kappa}$ for every $\kappa>0$. This single subtraction suffices for KPZ in $d=1$; the same computation in $d=2$ gives $\int_{\mathbb R^2}\hat\rho(\epsilon k)^2\,dk/k^0$-type integrals that diverge logarithmically *and* produce infinitely many further divergent subdiagrams — the concrete arithmetic reason the theory stops at $d=1$.

**Consistency check via Cole–Hopf.** With $\nu=1,\lambda=2,D=1$, narrow-wedge data $Z_0=\delta_0$, the SHE solution has $\mathbb E Z(t,x)=p_t(x)$ (heat kernel) and
$$\mathbb E\big[Z(t,x)^2\big]=p_t(x)^2\Big(1+\int_0^t\!\!\int p\text{-terms}\Big)\;\Longrightarrow\;\mathbb E[Z^2]<\infty\ \ \forall t>0,$$
so all moments are finite in $d=1$ and $Z>0$ a.s. by Mueller's comparison theorem, making $h=\log Z$ well-defined. In $d=2$ the analogous second-moment series is exactly the one that diverges unless $\beta_\epsilon\sim\sqrt{2\pi/\log(1/\epsilon)}$ — the scaling that defines the subcritical regime and the critical point $\hat\beta=1$.
