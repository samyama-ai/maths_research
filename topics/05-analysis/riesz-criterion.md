# Riesz Criterion

> **Topic:** Real & Complex Analysis · **ID:** `05-analysis/riesz-criterion` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Define the **Riesz function**
$$R(x)\;=\;\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}x^{k}}{(k-1)!\,\zeta(2k)},\qquad x>0,$$
an entire function of $x$ (the coefficients decay faster than $1/(k-1)!\,$, since $\zeta(2k)\to 1$).

**Riesz criterion (1916).** The Riemann Hypothesis (RH) holds if and only if
$$R(x)=O\!\left(x^{1/4+\varepsilon}\right)\quad\text{as }x\to\infty,\ \text{for every }\varepsilon>0 .$$

The open problem is to prove — or refute — the growth bound directly, by real/complex-analytic estimation of $R$, without assuming RH. A complete solution is either (i) a proof that $R(x)\ll_\varepsilon x^{1/4+\varepsilon}$, which proves RH, or (ii) a proof that $\limsup_{x\to\infty} R(x)x^{-1/4-\varepsilon}=\infty$ for some $\varepsilon>0$, which refutes it. The exponent $1/4$ is sharp: unconditionally $R(x)=\Omega_\pm(x^{1/4})$, because $\zeta$ has zeros on $\Re s=\tfrac12$.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Dirichlet-series form.** Using $1/\zeta(2k)=\sum_{n\ge1}\mu(n)n^{-2k}$ for $k\ge1$ and $\sum_{k\ge1}(-1)^{k+1}y^{k}/(k-1)!=y e^{-y}$,
$$R(x)\;=\;x\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{2}}\,e^{-x/n^{2}} ,$$
where $\mu$ is the Möbius function. So $R$ is a Möbius-weighted, exponentially smoothed counterpart of the Mertens function $M(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\mu(n)$.

**Mellin transform.** With $\int_0^\infty x^{-s}e^{-x/n^{2}}\,dx=n^{2(1-s)}\Gamma(1-s)$ for $\Re s<1$,
$$\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{R(x)}{x^{s+1}}\,dx\;=\;\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{\zeta(2s)},\qquad \tfrac12<\Re s<1,$$
and by Mellin inversion, for any $c\in(\tfrac12,1)$,
$$R(x)\;=\;\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\frac{\Gamma(1-s)}{\zeta(2s)}\,x^{s}\,ds .$$

**Why $1/4$.** The integrand's singularities left of $\Re s=\frac12$ are the poles of $1/\zeta(2s)$ at $s=\rho/2$ ($\rho$ a nontrivial zero) and at $s=-k$ (trivial zeros $\zeta(-2k)=0$, $k\ge1$). Set $\Theta=\sup\{\Re\rho\}$. Shifting the contour to $\Re s=\Theta/2+\varepsilon$ — legitimate because on RH $1/\zeta(\sigma+it)\ll_\varepsilon |t|^{\varepsilon}$ for $\sigma>\frac12$, while $|\Gamma(1-s)|$ decays like $e^{-\pi|t|/2}$ — gives $R(x)\ll x^{\Theta/2+\varepsilon}$. Conversely, if $R(x)\ll x^{\alpha+\varepsilon}$ then the Mellin integral converges for $\Re s>\alpha$, so $\Gamma(1-s)/\zeta(2s)$ is holomorphic there and $\zeta$ has no zeros with $\Re s>2\alpha$. Hence
$$R(x)=O(x^{\Theta/2+\varepsilon})\ \text{and}\ R(x)\neq O(x^{\Theta/2-\varepsilon}),$$
so the criterion is exactly the statement $\Theta=\tfrac12$, i.e. RH.

**Formal residue expansion.** Collecting residues,
$$R(x)\;\sim\;\sum_{\rho}\frac{\Gamma\!\left(1-\tfrac{\rho}{2}\right)}{2\,\zeta'(\rho)}\;x^{\rho/2}\;+\;\sum_{k\ge1}\frac{\Gamma(1+k)}{2\,\zeta'(-2k)}x^{-k},$$
valid under simplicity of the zeros and subject to convergence of the sum over $\rho$ (see §5).

**Cousins.** Hardy–Littlewood (1916): RH $\iff \sum_{n\ge1}\frac{(-x)^{n}}{n!\,\zeta(2n+1)}=O(x^{-1/4})$. Báez-Duarte (2005), sequential form: with
$$c_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-1)^{j}\binom{k}{j}\frac{1}{\zeta(2j+2)},$$
RH $\iff c_{k}=O(k^{-3/4+\varepsilon})$.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1916.** Marcel Riesz, *Sur l'hypothèse de Riemann* (Acta Math. 40), introduces $R(x)$ and proves the equivalence. In the same volume-year Hardy and Littlewood (Acta Math. 41) give their $\zeta(2n+1)$ variant, developed from Ramanujan's correspondence.
- **1930s–1986.** The criterion is canonised in Titchmarsh's monograph (§14.32), which contains the Mellin-transform proof in the form above.
- **2000.** Báez-Duarte, Balazard, Landreau and Saias sharpen the Nyman–Beurling criterion (*Notes sur la fonction $\zeta$ de Riemann, 3*, Adv. Math. 149), reviving the "criterion" programme with quantitative $L^2$ methods.
- **2003–2005.** Báez-Duarte's strengthened Nyman–Beurling criterion and then his *sequential Riesz-like criterion* replace the continuous variable $x$ by the binomial-transform sequence $c_k$, which is numerically far more tractable.
- **2006–2008.** Maślanka analyses $c_k$ by Rice (Nörlund–Rice) integrals; Wolf computes $c_k$ to large $k$ in high precision and reports agreement with $c_k\approx C k^{-3/4}$ modulated by an oscillation whose frequencies match $\gamma/(2\pi)\log k$ for low zeros $\rho=\tfrac12+i\gamma$; Cisło and Wolf compare the Riesz and Báez-Duarte criteria directly.
- **2013–2016.** Dixit, Roy and Zaharescu (*Riesz-type criteria and theta transformation analogues*, J. Number Theory 160) embed the Riesz and Hardy–Littlewood criteria in a one-parameter family generated by theta-transformation and Koshliakov-kernel identities, producing new equivalents of RH with the same $x^{1/4}$ threshold.

Best unconditional exponent: $\tfrac12$. No unconditional bound of the form $R(x)\ll x^{1/2-\delta}$ with fixed $\delta>0$ is known, mirroring the absence of any zero-density-free improvement on $\Theta<1$.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **Unconditional upper bound.** $R(x)=O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})$, since $\zeta(2s)\neq0$ for $\Re s>\tfrac12$ (there $\Re(2s)>1$). Pushing the contour marginally left along the Vinogradov–Korobov zero-free region upgrades this to $R(x)\ll x^{1/2}\exp\!\big(-c(\log x)^{3/5}(\log\log x)^{-1/5}\big)$ — the same quality of saving as in the prime number theorem *(derived by the standard contour shift)*.
- **Unconditional lower bound.** $R(x)=\Omega_{\pm}(x^{1/4})$; the exponent $1/4$ cannot be lowered.
- **Conditional equivalences (fully proven).** RH $\Rightarrow R(x)\ll_\varepsilon x^{1/4+\varepsilon}$, and the converse; both directions are theorems of Riesz. Likewise for the Hardy–Littlewood function and for the Báez-Duarte sequence $c_k$ with exponent $-3/4$.
- **Partial-zero information.** If it is only known that $\zeta$ has no zeros with $\Re\rho>\theta_0$ for some $\theta_0<1$, then $R(x)\ll x^{\theta_0/2+\varepsilon}$ follows immediately. So every quasi-RH regime is covered; only $\theta_0=\tfrac12$ is missing.
- **Families.** The Dixit–Roy–Zaharescu framework proves the $O(x^{1/4+\varepsilon})\iff$ RH equivalence for a continuum of Riesz-type functions built from $\sum_n \mu(n)n^{-2}\varphi(x/n^{2})$ with kernels $\varphi$ that are self-reciprocal under the relevant theta/Koshliakov transform, e.g. $\varphi(y)=ye^{-y}$ (Riesz) and $\varphi(y)=e^{-y}$-type variants (Hardy–Littlewood).
- **Numerics.** Direct evaluation from the power series suffers cancellation of size $e^{x}$, i.e. about $0.434x$ decimal digits of loss, so unaided high-precision computation of $R(x)$ is confined to modest $x$; the smoothed form $x\sum\mu(n)n^{-2}e^{-x/n^{2}}$ needs $n$ up to $\gg\sqrt{x}$. The sequence $c_k$ has been tabulated to $k$ of order $10^{5}$ in several hundred digits and is consistent with $c_k=O(k^{-3/4})$ *(empirical)*.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **The criterion is an equivalence, not a reduction.** Any proof of $R(x)\ll x^{1/4+\varepsilon}$ must contain a proof of RH; the Mellin argument converts one into the other with no loss, so no genuinely weaker analytic input suffices.
- **Cancellation in $\mu$.** $R(x)=x\sum\mu(n)n^{-2}e^{-x/n^{2}}$ is dominated by $n\asymp\sqrt{x}$, where the exponential is $\Theta(1)$: the required bound is square-root cancellation in Möbius sums of length $\sqrt{x}$, exactly the Mertens-type barrier ($M(y)\ll y^{1/2+\varepsilon}$). Sieve and elementary methods give at best $M(y)\ll y\exp(-c(\log y)^{3/5-\varepsilon})$.
- **The explicit-formula route stalls on $1/\zeta'(\rho)$.** In the residue expansion the gamma factor $|\Gamma(1-\rho/2)|\asymp e^{-\pi\gamma/4}$ decays fast, but no unconditional bound rules out $|\zeta'(\rho)|$ being extremely small; convergence of $\sum_\rho 1/|\zeta'(\rho)|$ is unknown and is entangled with simplicity of zeros and with the (unproven) Montgomery-type gap hypotheses.
- **Positivity is unavailable.** Unlike Li's criterion or the Nyman–Beurling $L^2$-distance, the Riesz criterion is a size bound on an oscillating, sign-changing entire function with no known positivity or variational structure to exploit.
- **Numerical evidence is weak evidence.** Because $R$ measures $\Theta$ only in the limit, and any hypothetical zero off the line would sit at large height, computations at accessible $x$ cannot distinguish $\Theta=\tfrac12$ from $\Theta=\tfrac12+10^{-3}$.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: $R(x)\ll x^{\Theta/2+\varepsilon}$ with $\Theta\le 1$ (plus a sub-polynomial saving). Wanted: the same with $\Theta=\tfrac12$. The gap is the entire interval of exponents $(\tfrac14,\tfrac12]$, and closing any part of it — proving $R(x)\ll x^{1/2-\delta}$ for a single fixed $\delta>0$ — would already give a zero-free half-plane $\Re s>1-2\delta$, a result far beyond present technology. Equivalently: analytic control of $1/\zeta(2s)$ on vertical lines to the left of $\Re s=\tfrac12$ is precisely the missing step, and the Riesz function supplies no independent handle on it.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Riesz-type criteria from integral-transform identities.** The Indian school around Dixit (IIT Gandhinagar) and coauthors continues to generate new $x^{1/4}$-threshold equivalents from Koshliakov kernels, generalized Lambert series and Ramanujan-type transformation formulas; the aim is a variant whose kernel makes the Möbius cancellation more accessible *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Sequential/discretised forms.** Work descended from Báez-Duarte and Maślanka on the asymptotics of $c_k$ via Nörlund–Rice integrals, seeking an unconditional $c_k=o(k^{-1/2})$-type bound as an intermediate target *(frontier — verify)*.
- **High-precision computation.** Multiprecision studies of $c_k$ and of $R(x)$ in the smoothed representation, used to test the conjectured oscillatory law $c_k\sim k^{-3/4}\sum_\rho A_\rho\cos(\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\log k+\phi_\rho)$.
- **Cross-criterion comparison.** Ongoing effort to quantify implications among the Riesz, Hardy–Littlewood, Báez-Duarte, Nyman–Beurling and Li criteria, so that a partial result in one transfers to another.

## 8. Future Work

- Prove an unconditional bound $R(x)\ll x^{1/2}/(\log x)^{A}$ for large $A$ by an exponential-sum treatment of $\sum_n\mu(n)n^{-2}e^{-x/n^{2}}$ around $n\asymp\sqrt x$ — a modest but currently open goal.
- Obtain rigorous, unconditional truncated versions of the residue expansion, with explicit error terms, using averages of $1/|\zeta'(\rho)|$ over zeros rather than pointwise bounds.
- Seek a kernel $\varphi$ within the Dixit–Roy–Zaharescu family for which the associated function admits a positivity or spectral interpretation, joining the Riesz picture to the Nyman–Beurling/de Branges circle.
- Establish the conjectured precise asymptotics of $c_k$; a proof of $c_k\sim Ck^{-3/4}(1+o(1))$-type behaviour would be RH-strength, but a proof of the oscillation's *frequency* structure conditionally on RH would test the model.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Evaluate $R(1)$ from the defining series.** Using $\zeta(2)=\pi^{2}/6$, $\zeta(4)=\pi^{4}/90$, $\zeta(6)=\pi^{6}/945$, $\zeta(8)=\pi^{8}/9450$:
$$R(x)=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}x-\frac{90}{\pi^{4}}x^{2}+\frac{945}{2\pi^{6}}x^{3}-\frac{9450}{6\pi^{8}}x^{4}+\frac{x^{5}}{24\,\zeta(10)}-\cdots$$
Numerically the coefficients are $0.60793,\,-0.92393,\,+0.49147,\,-0.16599,\,+0.041625,\,-0.008331,\,+0.0013889,\,-0.00019841,\dots$ At $x=1$ the partial sums are
$$0.60793,\;-0.31600,\;0.17547,\;0.00948,\;0.05110,\;0.04277,\;0.04416,\;0.04397,\;0.04399,$$
converging to $R(1)\approx 0.043987$. Note the cancellation: individual terms reach $0.92$ while the sum is $0.044$. At $x=20$ the largest term is of size $\sim e^{20}/\sqrt{2\pi\cdot20}\approx 4\times10^{7}$ against a target of order $x^{1/4}\approx 2.1$ — the loss is about $0.434x$ digits, which is exactly why the naive series is useless for large $x$ and why the criterion resists numerical probing.

**The same value from the Möbius form.** $R(1)=\sum_{n\ge1}\mu(n)n^{-2}e^{-1/n^{2}}$. The first terms are $e^{-1}=0.367879$, $-\tfrac14e^{-1/4}=-0.194700$, $-\tfrac19e^{-1/9}=-0.099427$, $-\tfrac1{25}e^{-1/25}=-0.038432$, $+\tfrac1{36}e^{-1/36}=+0.027017$, $-\tfrac1{49}e^{-1/49}=-0.019996,\dots$; the tail behaves like $\sum\mu(n)/n^{2}$ and converges to the same $0.0440$, but only with Mertens-strength cancellation — the analytic heart of the problem visible in a single line.

**What the criterion asserts here.** The claim under test is that the quantity
$$\frac{R(x)}{x^{1/4}}=x^{3/4}\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\mu(n)}{n^{2}}e^{-x/n^{2}}$$
stays bounded by $x^{\varepsilon}$ for all $\varepsilon>0$. If a single zero $\rho_{0}=\sigma_{0}+i\gamma_{0}$ with $\sigma_{0}=0.6$ existed, the residue term $\tfrac{\Gamma(1-\rho_{0}/2)}{2\zeta'(\rho_{0})}x^{\rho_{0}/2}$ would force $|R(x)|$ up to size $x^{0.3}$ infinitely often — undetectable in the computable range, yet fatal to the bound.
