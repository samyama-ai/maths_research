# Ore's Conjecture on Commutators in Infinite Simple Groups

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/commutator-width-of-simple-groups` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Ore's conjecture (1951) asserts that in a non-abelian **finite** simple group $G$, every element is a single commutator: for all $g \in G$ there exist $x,y \in G$ with $g = [x,y] = x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$. That statement was proved in full by Liebeck, O'Brien, Shalev and Tiep (2010).

The open problem catalogued here is the infinite-group analogue, in the sharpened form of **commutator width**. For a perfect group $G$ define

$$\mathrm{cw}(G) \;=\; \sup_{g \in G}\ \min\{\, n : g = [x_1,y_1]\cdots[x_n,y_n] \text{ for some } x_i,y_i \in G \,\} \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}.$$

Ore's conjecture for finite simple groups says $\mathrm{cw}(G) = 1$. For infinite simple groups the literal generalisation is **false**: Muranov (2010) constructed finitely generated infinite simple groups with $\mathrm{cw}(G) = \infty$. The live questions are therefore:

1. **(Q1)** For which natural classes of infinite simple groups is $\mathrm{cw}(G) = 1$?
2. **(Q2)** Does every **finitely presented** infinite simple group have $\mathrm{cw}(G) < \infty$? Is $\mathrm{cw}(G)=1$?
3. **(Q3)** For simple groups $G(K)$ of Lie type over an arbitrary (possibly infinite, non-algebraically-closed, non-commutative-base) field or ring, is $\mathrm{cw} = 1$ uniformly in the rank and the base?

A complete resolution of (Q2) means either a proof that finite presentability (or finite generation plus some finiteness of a homological invariant) forces bounded commutator width, or an explicit finitely presented simple group of unbounded width.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Let $G$ be a group, $[x,y] = x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$, and $K(G) = \{[x,y] : x,y \in G\}$ the set of commutators. The derived subgroup $G' = \langle K(G)\rangle$; $G$ is **perfect** if $G'=G$, and every non-abelian simple group is perfect. Then

$$G = G' \iff G = \bigcup_{n \ge 1} K(G)^n, \qquad \mathrm{cw}(G) = \min\{ n : K(G)^n = G\} \ (\text{or } \infty).$$

$G$ is **uniformly perfect** iff $\mathrm{cw}(G) < \infty$. Ore's conjecture is the statement $K(G) = G$.

**Stable commutator length.** For $g \in G'$ let $c\ell(g)$ be the least $n$ with $g$ a product of $n$ commutators, and

$$\mathrm{scl}(g) \;=\; \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{c\ell(g^n)}{n} \;=\; \inf_{n\ge 1}\frac{c\ell(g^n)}{n}.$$

**Bavard duality** (Bavard, 1991) identifies scl with homogeneous quasimorphisms: for $g$ of infinite order in $G'$,

$$\mathrm{scl}(g) \;=\; \sup_{\phi} \frac{|\phi(g)|}{2 D(\phi)},$$

the supremum over homogeneous quasimorphisms $\phi: G \to \mathbb{R}$ with defect $D(\phi) = \sup_{x,y}|\phi(xy)-\phi(x)-\phi(y)| > 0$. Hence $\mathrm{scl} \equiv 0$ on $G$ iff $H^2_b(G;\mathbb{R}) \to H^2(G;\mathbb{R})$ is injective, i.e. every homogeneous quasimorphism is a homomorphism.

**Key asymmetry.** $\mathrm{cw}(G) < \infty \Rightarrow \mathrm{scl} \equiv 0$, but the converse fails; Muranov's groups have $\mathrm{scl}\equiv 0$ and $\mathrm{cw} = \infty$. So bounded cohomology obstructions detect only the "stable" half of the problem.

**Word maps.** Writing $w = [x,y]$, Ore's statement is surjectivity of the word map $w: G\times G \to G$. For $G$ a connected semisimple algebraic group over an algebraically closed field, $w$ is dominant, and Ree's theorem upgrades dominance to surjectivity on $k$-points.

**Chevalley groups.** For a reduced irreducible root system $\Phi$ of rank $\ge 1$ and a commutative ring $R$, the elementary Chevalley group $E(\Phi,R)$ is generated by root elements $x_\alpha(t)$, $\alpha\in\Phi$, $t\in R$, subject to the Chevalley commutator formula

$$[x_\alpha(s), x_\beta(t)] = \prod_{i\alpha+j\beta \in \Phi} x_{i\alpha+j\beta}\!\left(N_{\alpha\beta ij}\, s^i t^j\right).$$

Over a field $K$, $E(\Phi,K)/Z$ is simple except for the small exceptions $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{F}_2)$, $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{F}_3)$.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1936.** Shoda: every element of $\mathrm{SL}_n(K)$, $K$ algebraically closed, is a commutator.
- **1951.** Ore, *Some remarks on commutators*: proves $K(G)=G$ for $G = A_n$ ($n\ge 5$) and, notably, for the **infinite** symmetric group $\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)$ on any infinite set. He conjectures the finite simple case.
- **1961.** R. C. Thompson: commutators in $\mathrm{SL}_n(K)$ and $\mathrm{GL}_n(K)$ over arbitrary fields; every non-central element of $\mathrm{SL}_n(K)$ is a commutator with small explicit exceptions.
- **1964.** Ree: every element of a connected semisimple algebraic group over an algebraically closed field is a commutator.
- **1998.** Ellers–Gordeev: Ore's conjecture for all quasi-simple Chevalley groups over fields $K$ with $|K| > 8$ — in particular for **all infinite fields**, uniformly in type and rank.
- **2010.** Liebeck–O'Brien–Shalev–Tiep, *The Ore conjecture* (JEMS): the finite case, closing the residual small-field and sporadic cases with Deligne–Lusztig character estimates plus computer verification.
- **2010.** Muranov: finitely generated infinite simple groups of infinite commutator width and vanishing scl — the general infinite conjecture is false.
- **2000s–2020s.** Verbal-width programme: Nikolov (2004) on commutator width of perfect groups, Nikolov–Segal on width in profinite groups, Segal's monograph; Hazrat–Vavilov–Zhang on commutator calculus in Chevalley groups over rings.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Class | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| All finite non-abelian simple groups | $\mathrm{cw}=1$ | Liebeck–O'Brien–Shalev–Tiep 2010 |
| $\mathrm{Sym}(\Omega)$, $\mathrm{Alt}(\Omega)$, $\Omega$ infinite | $\mathrm{cw}=1$ | Ore 1951 |
| Quasi-simple Chevalley groups over fields with $\lvert K\rvert>8$ (all infinite fields, all types, all ranks) | $\mathrm{cw}=1$ | Ellers–Gordeev 1998 |
| Connected semisimple algebraic groups over $\bar{K}$ | $\mathrm{cw}=1$ | Ree 1964; Shoda 1936 for $\mathrm{SL}_n$ |
| $E(\Phi,R)$, $\mathrm{rank}\,\Phi \ge 2$, $R$ of finite Bass–Serre dimension | $\mathrm{cw}$ bounded by an absolute constant | Hazrat–Vavilov–Zhang 2013 |
| Compactly supported homeomorphism groups of manifolds (Anderson-method groups) | simple and $\mathrm{cw}=1$ | Anderson 1958 |
| Thompson groups $T$, $V$ and Higman–Thompson $V_{n,r}$ | uniformly perfect, $\mathrm{cw}<\infty$ | Gal–Kędra 2011 |
| First Grigorchuk group (not simple, but width-model case) | $\mathrm{cw}=2$ | Bartholdi–Groth–Lysenok 2022 |
| Some f.g. infinite simple groups | $\mathrm{cw}=\infty$, $\mathrm{scl}\equiv 0$ | Muranov 2010 |

Negative-direction constraints: groups acting acylindrically on hyperbolic spaces carry infinite-dimensional spaces of quasimorphisms (Bestvina–Fujiwara 2002), so $\mathrm{scl}\not\equiv 0$ and $\mathrm{cw}=\infty$ — but such groups are never simple, and simple quotients kill the quasimorphisms.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Character theory does not survive.** The finite proof rests on Frobenius's formula: $g \in K(G)$ iff $\sum_{\chi \in \mathrm{Irr}(G)} \chi(g)/\chi(1) \neq 0$. For infinite $G$ there is no finite dual, no averaging, and no Deligne–Lusztig bound $|\chi(g)| \le C\,\chi(1)^{1-\delta}$ to exploit.
- **No algebraic geometry off the algebraic groups.** Ree's argument uses irreducibility and dimension counts on $G\times G \to G$. Thompson-like, Burger–Mozes, and Muranov's groups have no variety structure.
- **Bounded cohomology is blind at width $\ge 2$.** Vanishing of $\mathrm{scl}$ certifies only $c\ell(g^n) = o(n)$; it gives no bound on $c\ell(g)$ itself. Muranov's construction is exactly the counterexample showing this gap is real, so any proof of (Q2) must be non-stable.
- **Small-cancellation constructions are elastic.** Iterated graded small-cancellation quotients can impose arbitrarily large width on chosen elements while retaining simplicity, so no purely combinatorial "simplicity implies width" argument can hold.
- **Ring-theoretic obstructions.** For $\mathrm{SL}_2$ over rings, or $\mathrm{rank}\,1$ groups over non-closed fields, commutators are constrained by $K_1$-type invariants and Whitehead groups; the rank $\ge 2$ Chevalley commutator formula, which powers the bounded-width results, has no rank-1 analogue.

## 6. The Gap

Proved: $\mathrm{cw}=1$ for finite simple groups, for finitary/full symmetric groups, and for Chevalley-type simple groups over fields with $|K|>8$. Disproved: $\mathrm{cw}<\infty$ for arbitrary f.g. infinite simple groups.

The gap is the middle band: **finitely presented** infinite simple groups (Thompson–Higman family, Burger–Mozes lattices, Röver–Nekrashevych groups, twisted Brin–Thompson groups), and rank-1 or ring-based simple groups. Muranov's groups are finitely generated but not finitely presented; every known finitely presented infinite simple group has finite width. The precise unresolved step: **does finite presentability (equivalently, some finiteness property $F_n$ or $FP_2$) force $K(G)^n = G$ for some $n$?** No mechanism is known linking a finiteness property of $G$ to boundedness of a verbal width, and no candidate finitely presented counterexample exists.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Word maps on infinite groups.** Continuation of the Shalev–Larsen–Tiep programme to algebraic and adelic settings: surjectivity and fibre geometry of $w:G^2 \to G$ for $G$ simple algebraic over non-closed fields; Kunyavskii–Plotkin–Gordeev on word maps with constants.
- **Chevalley groups over rings.** Vavilov's St. Petersburg school and Hazrat's group: explicit absolute bounds on commutator width of $E(\Phi,R)$, extension to exceptional types and to Bak's unitary groups.
- **Big mapping class groups and homeomorphism groups.** Whether the simple groups appearing in Mann–Rafi / Calegari–Chen classifications are uniformly perfect; several are known to admit unbounded conjugation-invariant norms *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Twisted Brin–Thompson groups.** Belk–Zaremsky's simple groups $SV_G$ give a machine turning any f.g. group into a simple group with prescribed embeddings; computing $\mathrm{cw}(SV_G)$ in terms of $G$ is an active target and the most plausible route to a finitely presented counterexample *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Boundedness/bounded cohomology.** Monod–Nariman-style work on boundedness of transformation groups; vanishing of $H^2_b$ as a first filter before attacking width.

## 8. Future Work

- Decide (Q2) for the Higman–Thompson family: prove $\mathrm{cw}(V_{n,r}) = 1$ by an explicit rearrangement/Anderson-type infinite-swindle argument, or exhibit an element requiring two commutators.
- Extend Ellers–Gordeev to simple groups of rank 1 over arbitrary skew fields and to Kac–Moody groups over infinite fields.
- Build a graded small-cancellation quotient that is finitely presented *and* has an element of commutator length $\ge 2$ provably, using a non-stable invariant (a "bounded" analogue of scl, e.g. width-sensitive $\ell^\infty$-filling norms).
- Develop an invariant strictly between $\mathrm{cw}$ and $\mathrm{scl}$ — a lower bound on $c\ell(g)$ that does not stabilise — since quasimorphisms provably cannot detect the counterexamples.
- Determine whether $\mathrm{cw}(G) \in \{1,2\}$ for all uniformly perfect simple groups arising from geometric actions; no example with $2 < \mathrm{cw} < \infty$ among simple groups is known.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Unipotent elements of $\mathrm{SL}_2(K)$ are commutators when $|K|>3$.**

Take $K$ any field with an element $t$ satisfying $t^2 \neq 1$ and $t\neq 0$ (possible iff $|K| > 3$). Set

$$x = \begin{pmatrix} t & 0 \\ 0 & t^{-1}\end{pmatrix}, \qquad y = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & s \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}, \quad s \in K.$$

Then $x y x^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & t^{2} s \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}$, so

$$[x^{-1},y^{-1}] = x y x^{-1} y^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & t^{2}s \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -s \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & s(t^{2}-1) \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}.$$

Given any target $u = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & c \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}$ with $c \in K$, choose $s = c/(t^2-1)$, legal since $t^2-1 \neq 0$. So every unipotent element is a single commutator. Conjugating, every element of $\mathrm{SL}_2(K)$ with a repeated eigenvalue $1$ is a commutator; Thompson's theorem handles the semisimple and non-split classes, giving $\mathrm{cw}(\mathrm{PSL}_2(K)) = 1$ for all infinite $K$ — a special case of Ellers–Gordeev. The excluded fields $\mathbb{F}_2,\mathbb{F}_3$ are exactly those where $\mathrm{PSL}_2(K)$ is not simple.

**Contrast: the free group.** In $F_2 = \langle a,b\rangle$, Culler's identity

$$[a,b]^{3} = [\,a b a^{-1},\, b^{-1} a b a^{-2}\,]\,[\,b^{-1} a b,\, b^{2}\,]$$

shows $c\ell([a,b]^3) = 2$, hence $\mathrm{scl}([a,b]) \le 2/3 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}$; the sharp value is $\mathrm{scl}([a,b]) = 1/2$, certified by Bavara duality against the Rhemtulla/Brooks quasimorphisms. Here width grows without bound — $c\ell([a,b]^n) = \lfloor n/2\rfloor + 1$ — precisely because $F_2$ is far from simple. The open problem is that in Muranov's simple groups the *stable* growth disappears ($\mathrm{scl}\equiv 0$) while the *unstable* width still blows up, and no current invariant sees that.
