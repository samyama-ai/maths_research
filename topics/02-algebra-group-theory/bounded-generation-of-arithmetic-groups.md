# Bounded Generation of Arithmetic Groups

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/bounded-generation-of-arithmetic-groups` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

A group $\Gamma$ is **boundedly generated (BG)** if there exist $\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_r \in \Gamma$ and the product of the cyclic groups they generate exhausts $\Gamma$:
$$\Gamma = \langle \gamma_1\rangle \langle \gamma_2\rangle \cdots \langle \gamma_r\rangle = \{\gamma_1^{n_1}\cdots\gamma_r^{n_r} : n_i \in \mathbb{Z}\}.$$

**The problem.** Determine exactly which $S$-arithmetic groups are boundedly generated, and produce explicit bounds $r$.

The expected answer, in the form usually attributed to Platonov–Rapinchuk and Rapinchuk:

> **Conjecture.** Let $G$ be an absolutely almost simple algebraic group over a number field $K$, $S \supset V_\infty$ a finite set of places, and $\Gamma \subset G(K)$ an $S$-arithmetic subgroup. Then $\Gamma$ is boundedly generated **iff** $\mathrm{rank}_K\, G \geq 1$ (i.e. $G$ is $K$-isotropic) and $\sum_{v \in S}\mathrm{rank}_{K_v} G \geq 2$.

A complete solution requires: (a) proof of BG for every isotropic $G$ in the higher-$S$-rank regime, with an effective $r$; (b) proof of non-BG in the excluded cases. Half of (b) is now a theorem (Section 4). The word "bounded" is not about a fixed generating set: BG is a group-isomorphism invariant, though $r$ depends on the chosen $\gamma_i$.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Let $K$ be a number field, $\mathcal{O}_K$ its ring of integers, $S$ a finite set of places containing all archimedean ones, and
$$\mathcal{O}_S = \{x \in K : v(x) \ge 0 \ \ \forall v \notin S\}$$
the ring of $S$-integers. By Dirichlet's unit theorem $\mathcal{O}_S^\times$ has rank $|S| - 1$; it is infinite iff $|S| \ge 2$.

**Elementary matrices.** For $i \ne j$ and $x \in R$, $e_{ij}(x) = I_n + x E_{ij}$. Write $E_n(R) = \langle e_{ij}(x)\rangle$. The group is **boundedly elementarily generated** with bound $\nu$ if
$$\mathrm{SL}_n(R) = \{\,e_{i_1j_1}(x_1)\cdots e_{i_\nu j_\nu}(x_\nu)\,\},$$
i.e. every matrix is a product of at most $\nu$ elementary matrices. Since each $\{e_{ij}(x) : x \in R\}$ is a group only when $R$ is cyclic, one uses instead: $R = \mathcal{O}_S$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}$-module, so bounded elementary generation implies BG in the sense of Section 1.

**Chevalley form.** For a Chevalley–Demazure group scheme $G$ of rank $\ge 2$ with root system $\Phi$, the root subgroups $x_\alpha(t)$, $\alpha \in \Phi$, satisfy the Chevalley commutator relations
$$[x_\alpha(s), x_\beta(t)] = \prod_{i\alpha + j\beta \in \Phi} x_{i\alpha+j\beta}\!\left(c_{ij\alpha\beta}\, s^i t^j\right),$$
and $E(\Phi, R) = \langle x_\alpha(t)\rangle$. Bounded generation asks for $\nu = \nu(\Phi, \mathcal{O}_S)$ with $G(\mathcal{O}_S) = E(\Phi,\mathcal{O}_S)$ expressible in $\le \nu$ root-element factors.

**Standard consequences of BG** (used as non-BG tests):

1. If $\Gamma$ is BG and residually finite/finitely generated, then $\Gamma^{ab}$ and the abelianization of every finite-index subgroup is finite-rank; in particular a group with subgroups of unbounded first Betti number is not BG.
2. (Rapinchuk) A finitely generated BG group is **representation rigid**: for each $n$, it has only finitely many irreducible $n$-dimensional complex representations up to equivalence.
3. (Platonov–Rapinchuk) BG plus the congruence subgroup property gives strong control of the profinite completion; historically BG was introduced as a route to CSP: $\widehat{\Gamma} \to \prod_v G(\mathcal{O}_v)$ has central kernel.
4. (Lubotzky–Mozes–Raghunathan) In higher rank, the word metric on $\Gamma$ is bi-Lipschitz to the induced Riemannian metric — a statement whose proof uses bounded generation by unipotents.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1965–1967.** Bass–Milnor–Serre prove CSP for $\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathcal{O}_K)$, $n \ge 3$, and $\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}$, $n\ge2$; the "bounded" refinement is not part of it.
- **1966.** P. M. Cohn shows $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathcal{O})$ for non-Euclidean imaginary quadratic $\mathcal{O}$ is *not* generated by elementary matrices at all — the rank-1 obstruction appears immediately.
- **1975.** Cooke–Weinberger prove bounded elementary generation of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathcal{O}_S)$, $|S|\ge2$, **conditionally on GRH**, via bounded-length division chains.
- **1983–1984.** Carter–Keller: unconditional bounded elementary generation of $\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathcal{O}_K)$ for $n \ge 3$, with $\nu \le \tfrac12(3n^2-n)+36$. This is the founding positive result; the proof is number-theoretic (Mennicke symbols, Dirichlet-type density arguments), not geometric.
- **1990.** Tavgen extends bounded generation to $G(\mathcal{O}_S)$ for all Chevalley groups of rank $\ge 2$, by a reduction to rank-1 and rank-2 subsystems.
- **1992–2000.** Platonov–Rapinchuk and Rapinchuk develop the BG $\Rightarrow$ rigidity/CSP machinery; LMR (2000) applies bounded unipotent generation to metric geometry of lattices.
- **2006.** Erovenko–Rapinchuk handle isotropic orthogonal groups $\mathrm{SO}_n(f)$ over number fields, moving beyond the split/quasi-split case.
- **2007.** Dave Witte Morris gives a clean, self-contained account of Carter–Keller–Paige, extending bounded elementary generation to $\mathrm{SL}_n(A)$ for many rings $A$ of arithmetic type.
- **2018.** Morgan–Rapinchuk–Sury remove GRH from the $\mathrm{SL}_2$ case: for $\mathcal{O}_S$ with infinitely many units, every element of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathcal{O}_S)$ is a product of at most **9** elementary matrices.
- **2022.** Corvaja–Rapinchuk–Ren–Zannier settle the negative direction in characteristic $0$: a finitely generated linear group over a field of characteristic $0$ consisting of semisimple elements (anisotropic) that is boundedly generated is virtually abelian. Consequence: **cocompact** arithmetic lattices from $K$-anisotropic groups are not BG.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Class | Result | Bound |
|---|---|---|
| $\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathcal{O}_K)$, $n \ge 3$ | BG (Carter–Keller 1983) | $\le \tfrac12(3n^2-n)+36$ elementaries |
| $\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathcal{O}_S)$, $n\ge3$ | BG (Carter–Keller / Morris) | polynomial in $n$, $+O(1)$ |
| Chevalley $G$, $\mathrm{rank}\,\Phi \ge 2$, over $\mathcal{O}_S$ | BG (Tavgen 1990) | $\nu(\Phi)$ effective |
| $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathcal{O}_S)$, $|\mathcal{O}_S^\times| = \infty$ (e.g. $\mathbb{Z}[1/p]$, real quadratic $\mathcal{O}_K$) | BG unconditionally (MRS 2018) | $\le 9$ |
| $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathcal{O}_S)$, general $\mathcal{O}_S$, $|S|\ge2$ | BG under GRH (Cooke–Weinberger 1975) | explicit, small |
| $\mathrm{SO}_n(f)(\mathcal{O}_S)$, $f$ isotropic, $n \ge 5$ | BG (Erovenko–Rapinchuk 2006) | effective |
| $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$, $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathcal{O})$ imaginary quadratic | **not** BG | virtually free / rank-1, unbounded $b_1$ |
| Anisotropic $\Gamma \subset \mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$, not virtually abelian | **not** BG (CRRZ 2022) | — |
| $\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb{F}_q[t])$ and function-field lattices | **not** BG | infinite-rank abelianizations |
| Word width: $\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$, $n\ge3$ | every word map has bounded width (Avni–Meiri) | — |

Also verified: $\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$ for $n\ge3$ has bounded generation by $\{e_{ij}(1)\}$, hence $r \le \tfrac12(3n^2-n)+36$ cyclic factors; and Liehl (1984) proved bounded word length in $\mathrm{SL}_2$ over $\mathcal{O}_S$ with infinitely many units for restricted classes, predating MRS.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No geometry to exploit.** BG is not a quasi-isometry invariant and not detectable by growth, amenability, or boundary theory. Every positive proof to date is arithmetic: it converts a matrix reduction into a statement about representing integers by binary forms or about the length of division chains.
- **Division chains and GRH.** For $\mathrm{SL}_2$, reducing a unimodular row $(a,c)$ in bounded time requires finding $x$ with $a + xc$ prime, or a unit, or having few prime factors. Unconditional control of the least such $x$ is exactly the kind of statement that GRH supplies and sieve methods do not. MRS bypassed it only by exploiting $\mathcal{O}_S^\times$ being infinite — a hypothesis with no analogue for $\mathcal{O}_K$ imaginary quadratic.
- **Rank 1 has no commutator leverage.** In rank $\ge 2$ the Chevalley relations let one write a root element as a commutator of two others, which is what makes Tavgen's induction terminate. For $\Phi = A_1$ the commutator $[x_\alpha(s), x_{-\alpha}(t)]$ leaves the root subgroups entirely; the induction has no base.
- **Anisotropic groups have no unipotents.** Bounded generation forces a rich supply of unipotent elements — this is the content of CRRZ, proved by Diophantine approximation (Schmidt subspace theorem) rather than group theory. The proof shows non-BG, but yields nothing about the isotropic cases still open.
- **Effectivity collapses under twisting.** For non-split forms (unitary groups $\mathrm{SU}(h)$, groups of type $E_6$, $^3\!D_4$, $^6\!D_4$), the root system over $K$ is a relative one; the Chevalley commutator relations are replaced by relations involving the underlying division algebra or hermitian form, and the bookkeeping of Tavgen's reduction has not been carried out.

## 6. The Gap

Concretely, three gaps separate Section 4 from the conjecture in Section 1.

1. **$\mathrm{SL}_2$ with finitely many $S$-units but $|S|\ge2$ is not the issue** — the issue is $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathcal{O}_S)$ where $\mathcal{O}_S^\times$ is finite, i.e. $\mathcal{O}_K$ with $K = \mathbb{Q}$ or imaginary quadratic. There $\Gamma$ is a rank-1 lattice and is **known** not to be BG. So for $\mathrm{SL}_2$ the remaining unconditional gap is: rings $\mathcal{O}_S$ with $|S| \ge 2$ but where MRS's unit-based argument is applied — MRS covers exactly $|\mathcal{O}_S^\times| = \infty$, which by Dirichlet is $|S| \ge 2$. Hence **$\mathrm{SL}_2$ is done**; GRH is no longer needed.
2. **Isotropic non-split groups of $K$-rank $1$ but $S$-rank $\ge 2$.** E.g. $\mathrm{SU}_3(h)(\mathcal{O}_S)$, or $\mathrm{SL}_1$ of a quaternion algebra split at two places. Here unipotents exist but the rank-2 induction is unavailable. This is the sharpest open frontier.
3. **Uniformity.** Even where BG is known, no proof gives a bound independent of $K$ and $S$; Carter–Keller's $+36$ hides class-number and unit-rank dependence in the general $\mathcal{O}_S$ version. Whether $\nu$ can be taken to depend only on $\Phi$ is open.

The single technical step to cross for (2): produce a bounded-length reduction algorithm for the relative root groups of a $K$-rank-1 isotropic group, using $S$-units in the anisotropic kernel in place of the Chevalley commutator identity.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Virginia / Rapinchuk school.** Continued development of the CRRZ Diophantine method — extending non-BG results from anisotropic groups to groups whose unipotent radical is too small, and to fields of positive characteristic. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Explicit bounds for Chevalley groups.** A. Trost has produced sharpened, uniform bounds for bounded elementary generation of $\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}$ and simply-connected Chevalley groups over $\mathcal{O}_S$, improving Tavgen's implicit constants. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Word width and verbal subgroups.** Following Avni–Meiri's theorem that words have bounded width in $\mathrm{SL}_n(\mathbb{Z})$, $n \ge 3$, effort is going into commutator width and into whether bounded width holds for $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[1/p])$ with a bound independent of $p$. *(frontier — verify)*
- **BG and profinite rigidity.** BG is being used as an input to first-order/profinite rigidity of higher-rank lattices (Avni–Lubotzky–Meiri and successors).
- **Sharpness for $\mathrm{SL}_2$.** Whether the MRS constant $9$ can be lowered to $5$ for $\mathbb{Z}[1/p]$ — computational evidence suggests $5$ or $6$ suffices for small $p$. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Future Work

- Carry Tavgen's reduction over to relative root systems, giving BG for all $K$-isotropic groups with $S$-rank $\ge 2$ (Rapinchuk's stated program).
- Replace GRH-flavored inputs by unit-based arguments wherever $\mathcal{O}_S^\times$ is infinite, generalizing the MRS mechanism from $A_1$ to $\mathrm{SU}_3$.
- Determine whether BG is inherited by all finite-index subgroups with a *uniform* bound; this is what applications to CSP actually require.
- Find a non-arithmetic proof: a dynamical or measure-theoretic criterion for BG that could apply to lattices generally, closing the gap for cocompact isotropic cases.
- Settle sharp constants: the true minimal $\nu$ for $\mathrm{SL}_3(\mathbb{Z})$ (known $\le \tfrac12(27-3)+36 = 48$; lower bounds are far smaller).

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Claim.** Every $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d\end{pmatrix} \in \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[1/2])$ is a short product of elementary matrices — and the argument visibly breaks over $\mathbb{Z}$.

Take the unimodular column $(a,c)^{T} = (3,5)^{T}$.

**Step 1 — hit a unit multiple.** Act by $e_{12}(x)$: $(a,c) \mapsto (a + xc, c)$. Choose $x = 1$:
$$(3,5) \longmapsto (8,5), \qquad 8 = 2^3 \in \mathbb{Z}[1/2]^\times.$$
This is the MRS mechanism: because $\mathcal{O}_S^\times$ is infinite, the arithmetic progression $a + c\mathbb{Z}$ meets the units, and one only needs a *bounded* search.

**Step 2 — clear the lower entry.** Act by $e_{21}(y)$: $(8,5)\mapsto(8, 5 + 8y)$. Take $y = -5/8 \in \mathbb{Z}[1/2]$:
$$(8,5)\longmapsto (8,0).$$

**Step 3 — kill the torus.** The matrix is now $\mathrm{diag}(u, u^{-1})\cdot e_{12}(t)$ with $u = 8$. Use the identity
$$\mathrm{diag}(u,u^{-1}) = e_{12}(u)\,e_{21}(-u^{-1})\,e_{12}(u)\cdot\big(e_{12}(1)\,e_{21}(-1)\,e_{12}(1)\big)^{-1},$$
valid because $e_{12}(u)e_{21}(-u^{-1})e_{12}(u) = \begin{pmatrix}0&u\\-u^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}$ and the bracketed factor is the Weyl element $w=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$.

**Count.** $2$ (Steps 1–2) $+\,6$ (Step 3) $+\,1$ (final $e_{12}(t)$) $= 9$ elementary matrices — matching the MRS bound exactly.

**Why $\mathbb{Z}$ fails.** Over $\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}^\times = \{\pm1\}$. Step 1 would need $3 + 5x = \pm 1$, i.e. $x \notin \mathbb{Z}$. One is forced into Euclidean division, whose length for $(a,c)$ grows like $\log\max(|a|,|c|)$ — unbounded. And indeed $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})\cong \mathbb{Z}/4 *_{\mathbb{Z}/2}\mathbb{Z}/6$ contains a finite-index free group $F_2$, whose finite-index subgroups have first Betti number $\to\infty$, contradicting consequence (1) of Section 2. So $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z})$ is not BG, while $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{Z}[1/2])$ is — the whole difference being one extra place in $S$.
