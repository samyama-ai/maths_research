# Fröberg Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/froberg-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $0$ and $R = k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ the polynomial ring with the standard grading. Fix integers $r \ge 1$ and degrees $d_1,\dots,d_r \ge 1$. Choose forms $f_i \in R_{d_i}$ *generically*, i.e. with coefficient vectors in a suitable nonempty Zariski-open subset of the affine space of coefficients, and set $A = R/(f_1,\dots,f_r)$.

**Conjecture (Fröberg, 1985).** The Hilbert series of $A$ is

$$
\mathrm{HS}_A(t) \;=\; \left[\; \frac{\prod_{i=1}^{r}\bigl(1-t^{d_i}\bigr)}{(1-t)^{n}} \;\right]_{+},
$$

where $[\,\cdot\,]_+$ truncates the power series at the first index whose coefficient is non-positive: if $\sum_{j\ge 0} a_j t^j$ and $m = \min\{ j : a_j \le 0\}$, then $\left[\sum_j a_j t^j\right]_+ = \sum_{j<m} a_j t^j$.

A proof must establish, for generic $f_i$, that every graded piece has the smallest dimension permitted by the obvious relations. A disproof requires one explicit tuple $(n; d_1,\dots,d_r)$ in characteristic $0$ where the generic Hilbert function strictly exceeds the truncated series in some degree. The conjecture is *not* asserted in positive characteristic without restriction; computations there are more delicate.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Graded setup.** $R = \bigoplus_{j\ge0} R_j$ with $\dim_k R_j = \binom{n+j-1}{n-1}$, so $\mathrm{HS}_R(t) = (1-t)^{-n}$. For a homogeneous ideal $I$, $\mathrm{HS}_{R/I}(t) = \sum_j \dim_k (R/I)_j\, t^j$.

**Genericity.** A degree-$d$ form is a point of $\mathbb{A}^{N_d}$, $N_d = \binom{n+d-1}{n-1}$. The function $(f_1,\dots,f_r) \mapsto \dim_k (R/(f_1,\dots,f_r))_j$ is upper semicontinuous on $\prod_i \mathbb{A}^{N_{d_i}}$ for each $j$. Hence there is a nonempty open $U$ on which the whole Hilbert series is constant and coefficientwise **minimal**. This series, $\mathrm{HS}^{\mathrm{gen}}_{n;d_1,\dots,d_r}(t)$, is what the conjecture computes.

**Fröberg's inequality (theorem, not conjecture).** For *any* forms of these degrees,

$$
\mathrm{HS}_{R/(f_1,\dots,f_r)}(t) \;\succeq\; \left[\frac{\prod_i (1-t^{d_i})}{(1-t)^n}\right]_+ ,
$$

coefficientwise. So only the reverse inequality for generic forms is open; equivalently, the truncated series is attained.

**Maximal-rank reformulation.** Write $A_j = R/(f_1,\dots,f_j)$. The conjecture is equivalent to: for generic $f_1,\dots,f_r$ and every $j < r$ and every $m$, the multiplication map

$$
\times f_{j+1} \colon (A_j)_m \longrightarrow (A_j)_{m+d_{j+1}}
$$

has **maximal rank** (injective or surjective). Such a sequence is called *semi-regular*. For $d_{j+1}=1$ this is exactly the Weak Lefschetz Property (WLP) of $A_j$; for general $d_{j+1}$ with $f_{j+1}=\ell^{\,d}$ it is the Strong Lefschetz Property (SLP).

**Regular-sequence boundary.** If $r \le n$, generic forms are a regular sequence (Koszul complex exact), so
$\mathrm{HS}_A(t) = \prod_i (1-t^{d_i}) / (1-t)^n$ with no truncation needed. All content lies in $r > n$, where $A$ is Artinian and the numerator series eventually goes negative.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1978–1980.** Stanley, *Hilbert functions of graded algebras* (Adv. Math. 28) and *Weyl groups, the hard Lefschetz theorem, and the Sperner property* (SIAM J. Alg. Disc. Meth. 1), proves that monomial complete intersections $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]/(x_1^{d_1},\dots,x_n^{d_n})$ satisfy the SLP in characteristic $0$, using the hard Lefschetz theorem for a product of weighted projective spaces. Watanabe (1987) gave a representation-theoretic proof via $\mathfrak{sl}_2$. This settles $r = n+1$.
- **1985.** Fröberg, *An inequality for Hilbert series of graded algebras* (Math. Scand. 56, 117–144), proves the inequality, states the conjecture, and settles $n \le 2$.
- **1986.** Anick, *Thin algebras of embedding dimension three* (J. Algebra 100), settles $n = 3$ in characteristic $0$ by constructing explicit "thin" algebras and an inductive deformation argument.
- **1987.** Hochster–Laksov, *The linear syzygies of generic forms* (Comm. Algebra 15), prove the conjecture in the first nontrivial degree: for equal degrees $d$, $\dim_k (R_1\cdot\langle f_1,\dots,f_r\rangle)$ is maximal, i.e. the coefficient in degree $d+1$ is correct for all $n,r$.
- **1994.** Fröberg–Hollman, *Hilbert series for ideals generated by generic forms* (J. Symbolic Comput. 17), verify the conjecture by computer over large ranges of $(n; d_1,\dots,d_r)$ using random forms over finite fields.
- **2003.** Migliore–Miró-Roig connect the problem to WLP and to minimal free resolutions of $n+1$ general forms; they also show how frequently WLP is the exact obstruction.
- **2010.** Pardue, *Generic sequences of polynomials* (J. Algebra 324), shows Fröberg's conjecture follows from the Moreno-Socías conjecture (that the generic initial ideal in revlex is weakly reverse-lexicographic) in the equal-degree/complete-intersection settings.
- **2017.** Nenashev extends Hochster–Laksov one degree further for equal degrees.

Status: **open** for $n \ge 4$ and $r \ge n+2$ in general; **empirically overwhelming** in every computed range.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Case | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| $r \le n$, any $d_i$ | True (Koszul regular sequence) | Macaulay; classical |
| $n \le 2$ | True, all $r$, all degrees | Fröberg 1985 |
| $n = 3$, char $0$ | True, all $r$, all degrees | Anick 1986 |
| $r = n+1$, any $n$, char $0$ | True (SLP of monomial CI + semicontinuity) | Stanley 1980; Watanabe 1987 |
| $d_1=\dots=d_r=d$, coefficient of $t^{d+1}$ | True, all $n,r$ | Hochster–Laksov 1987 |
| Equal degrees, coefficient of $t^{d+2}$ | True | Nenashev 2017 |
| $d_i \in \{1,2\}$ with $r$ small relative to $\binom{n+1}{2}$ | True (quadrics; Koszul-homology methods) | Fröberg–Löfwall 2002 |
| Many $(n;d_1,\dots,d_r)$ with $n \le 11$, $r \le 20$, $d_i \le 5$ | Verified by machine over $\mathbb{F}_p$, $p$ large | Fröberg–Hollman 1994; Macaulay2/Singular reruns |
| $r = n+2$, special degree patterns in $n = 4$ | True | Migliore–Miró-Roig 2003 |

Note that a computer check over a large finite field with random forms proves an *upper* bound on the generic Hilbert function (a witness), which combined with Fröberg's inequality is a genuine proof for that tuple in that characteristic — but only for the specific tuple, and lifting to characteristic $0$ needs a standard but nontrivial semicontinuity argument.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No induction that survives truncation.** Adding $f_{j+1}$ changes the algebra by a maximal-rank map; but once truncation occurs (Hilbert function hits $0$), the inductive hypothesis loses the numerical slack that makes the next step provable. The known proofs ($n \le 3$) build explicit algebras degree by degree, and the combinatorics blows up for $n \ge 4$.
- **Lefschetz properties are themselves open.** The maximal-rank reformulation shows the conjecture is a vast family of WLP/SLP statements for Artinian algebras. WLP is known to *fail* for many Artinian complete intersections in $n \ge 4$ variables and in positive characteristic, so no uniform Lefschetz theorem can be invoked. The characteristic-$0$ proofs for monomial CIs rest on hard Lefschetz for a smooth projective variety — a geometric input with no analogue for the algebras arising after $r \ge n+2$ steps, which are not Gorenstein.
- **Genericity is not constructible.** There is no known family of *explicit* forms achieving the conjectured series for general $(n,r,d_i)$. Monomial or lex-segment substitutes give Hilbert functions that are strictly larger (Macaulay's bound is attained by lex ideals, the wrong extreme).
- **Positive characteristic breaks the statement.** Frobenius-induced degeneracies mean the generic Hilbert function can be larger in char $p$, so any proof must use char $0$ essentially, ruling out reduction-mod-$p$ and $F$-singularity toolkits.
- **Resolution-theoretic routes stall.** The Minimal Resolution Conjecture-style approach (predict the whole Betti table, then read off the Hilbert function) is strictly harder: the expected resolution of generic forms is not even conjecturally consistent in all cases, since ghost terms can cancel.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: $n \le 3$ (all $r$); $r \le n+1$ (all $n$); the first two nontrivial graded pieces for equal degrees; finitely many verified tuples.

Conjectured: all $n \ge 4$ with $r \ge n+2$, in every degree beyond the second nontrivial one.

The precise missing step: given generic $f_1,\dots,f_j$ in $n \ge 4$ variables with $j \ge n+1$, so that $A_j = R/(f_1,\dots,f_j)$ is Artinian, non-Gorenstein, and has no known structure theory, prove that a generic form $f_{j+1}$ of degree $d$ induces a maximal-rank map $(A_j)_m \to (A_j)_{m+d}$ for every $m$. Even the case $j = n+2$, $d_i = 2$ for all $i$, $n = 4$ has no proof independent of computation. Equivalently, one needs a construction — algebraic, geometric, or representation-theoretic — that certifies non-degeneracy of a single determinantal condition on a variety whose defining equations are not understood.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Lefschetz-property school** (Migliore, Miró-Roig, Nagel, Harima, Watanabe, and successors in Notre Dame / Barcelona / Kentucky): systematically classifying which Artinian algebras have WLP/SLP, since each new class feeds directly into a Fröberg step.
- **Stockholm school** (Fröberg, Löfwall, Lundqvist, Nicklasson): Koszul-homology and Lie-algebra methods for generic algebras, plus classification of degree tuples where the truncated series is short enough to be verified structurally.
- **Moreno-Socías route** (Pardue, Richert, and follow-ups): proving the generic initial ideal is weakly reverse-lexicographic would imply Fröberg's conjecture; the reduction is established, the hypothesis is not. *(frontier — verify: recent claims of Moreno-Socías in four variables for restricted degree sequences.)*
- **Powers of linear forms / Fröberg–Iarrobino variant**: when $f_i = \ell_i^{d}$ for generic linear $\ell_i$, apolarity converts the question into fat-point interpolation; here genuine exceptions exist (Iarrobino), which sharpens what "generic" must mean in the original conjecture.
- **Computational fronts**: large-scale Macaulay2/Singular/msolve verification over $\mathbb{F}_{32003}$ pushing $n \le 12$ and mixed degrees; and numerical-algebraic-geometry rank certificates. *(frontier — verify: SAT/Gröbner-hybrid certificates for $n=5$, equal quadrics.)*

## 8. Future Work

- Prove SLP for the class of Artinian algebras arising as $R/(\text{generic forms})$ directly, bypassing the complete-intersection restriction.
- Settle $n = 4$ completely; this is the first genuinely open dimension and would likely reveal whether a new inductive mechanism exists or whether the truth is case-by-case.
- Find an explicit (non-generic) witness family: monomial-plus-power ideals whose Hilbert function equals the truncated series for all $n,r,d$. Such a family would prove the conjecture by semicontinuity in one stroke.
- Determine the exact characteristic-$p$ statement: for which $p$ and which $(n;d_i)$ does the generic Hilbert function agree with the char-$0$ one?
- Prove Moreno-Socías, or find the minimal fragment of it that implies Fröberg.
- Extend Hochster–Laksov/Nenashev to the coefficient of $t^{d+3}$ and beyond, ideally with a uniform method in the degree index.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take $n = 3$, $r = 4$, all $d_i = 2$: four generic quadrics in $k[x,y,z]$, $\operatorname{char} k = 0$. This is the case $r = n+1$.

**Predicted series.** Since $1 - t^2 = (1-t)(1+t)$,

$$
\frac{(1-t^2)^4}{(1-t)^3} = (1-t)(1+t)^4 = (1+4t+6t^2+4t^3+t^4) - (t+4t^2+6t^3+4t^4+t^5)
$$
$$
= 1 + 3t + 2t^2 - 2t^3 - 3t^4 - t^5 .
$$

The first non-positive coefficient occurs at $t^3$, so the truncation gives

$$
\mathrm{HS}_A(t) = 1 + 3t + 2t^2, \qquad \dim_k A = 6 .
$$

**Direct check of the prediction.** $\dim_k R_2 = 6$; four generic quadrics are linearly independent, so $\dim_k A_2 = 6-4 = 2$ ✓. In degree $3$, $\dim_k R_3 = 10$ and $I_3 = R_1 \cdot \langle f_1,\dots,f_4\rangle$ has at most $3\cdot 4 = 12$ spanning elements; the conjecture asserts these span all of $R_3$, i.e. $\dim_k A_3 = 0$.

**Proof for this case via SLP.** Specialize to $f_1 = x^2$, $f_2 = y^2$, $f_3 = z^2$, $f_4 = \ell^2$ with $\ell = x+y+z$. Let $B = k[x,y,z]/(x^2,y^2,z^2)$, a monomial complete intersection with Hilbert function $(1,3,3,1)$. By Stanley's theorem $B$ has the SLP: $\times \ell^{\,2} : B_m \to B_{m+2}$ has maximal rank for all $m$.

- $m=0$: $B_0 \to B_2$, ranks $1 \to 3$, injective, rank $1$. So $\dim A_2 = 3 - 1 = 2$ ✓.
- $m=1$: $B_1 \to B_3$, ranks $3 \to 1$, surjective, rank $1$. So $\dim A_3 = 1 - 1 = 0$ ✓.

Hence this *specific* choice already realizes $1+3t+2t^2$. By upper semicontinuity, the generic Hilbert function is coefficientwise $\le$ this one; by Fröberg's inequality it is $\ge$ the truncated series. The two bounds coincide, so the conjecture holds for $(n;d)=(3;2,2,2,2)$.

**Where the method dies.** Push to $r = 5$ generic quadrics in $3$ variables: the predicted series is $\left[(1-t)^2(1+t)^5\right]_+ = 1+3t+t^2$, still fine. But take $n=5$, $r=8$, $d_i=2$: now the algebra $R/(f_1,\dots,f_6)$ after six steps is Artinian, non-Gorenstein, and there is no monomial specialization known to have the required maximal-rank property — the SLP input that carried the example above simply has no counterpart. That is exactly the gap of Section 6.
