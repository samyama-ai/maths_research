# Harbourne-Hirschowitz Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/harbourne-hirschowitz-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Fix $n$ general points $p_1,\dots,p_n \in \mathbb{P}^2$ over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$, integers $d \ge 0$ and $m_1,\dots,m_n \ge 0$. Let

$$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_d(m_1,\dots,m_n)$$

be the linear system of plane curves of degree $d$ having multiplicity at least $m_i$ at $p_i$. Each condition "multiplicity $\ge m_i$" imposes at most $\binom{m_i+1}{2}$ linear conditions on the $\binom{d+2}{2}$ coefficients, so the **virtual dimension** and **expected dimension** are

$$v(\mathcal{L}) = \frac{d(d+3)}{2} - \sum_{i=1}^n \frac{m_i(m_i+1)}{2}, \qquad e(\mathcal{L}) = \max\{v(\mathcal{L}), -1\}.$$

Always $\dim \mathcal{L} \ge e(\mathcal{L})$. The system is **special** when the inequality is strict.

**Conjecture (Harbourne 1986, Hirschowitz 1989).** $\mathcal{L}_d(m_1,\dots,m_n)$ is special if and only if its base locus contains a $(-1)$-curve of multiplicity at least $2$; equivalently, on the blow-up $X$ of $\mathbb{P}^2$ at the $n$ points there exist $(-1)$-curves $C_1,\dots,C_k$ with $N_j := -\mathcal{L}\cdot C_j \ge 2$ such that the residual system $\mathcal{L} - \sum_j N_j C_j$ is non-special of non-negative virtual dimension, and

$$h^1(X,\mathcal{L}) = \sum_{j=1}^k \binom{N_j}{2}.$$

A complete proof must establish this for all $(d;m_1,\dots,m_n)$ with $n$ arbitrary; a disproof needs one system that is special with no multiple $(-1)$-curve in its base locus. The conjecture is equivalent to the older Segre and Gimigliano formulations and is usually called **SHGH** (Segre–Harbourne–Gimigliano–Hirschowitz).

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Let $\pi: X \to \mathbb{P}^2$ be the blow-up at $p_1,\dots,p_n$, with $H = \pi^*\mathcal{O}(1)$ and exceptional divisors $E_1,\dots,E_n$. Then $\operatorname{Pic}(X) = \mathbb{Z}H \oplus \bigoplus_i \mathbb{Z}E_i$ with intersection form $H^2 = 1$, $E_i^2 = -1$, $H \cdot E_i = E_i \cdot E_j = 0$ $(i \ne j)$, and canonical class $K_X = -3H + \sum_i E_i$. Set

$$L = dH - \sum_{i=1}^n m_i E_i, \qquad \mathcal{L}_d(m_1,\dots,m_n) = |L|,\quad \dim|L| = h^0(X,L) - 1 .$$

Riemann–Roch on the rational surface $X$ gives

$$h^0(L) - h^1(L) + h^2(L) = \chi(\mathcal{O}_X) + \frac{L\cdot(L-K_X)}{2} = 1 + v(\mathcal{L}),$$

and $h^2(L) = h^0(K_X - L) = 0$ whenever $d \ge 0$ and $L$ is effective-ish (in all relevant ranges). Hence

$$\dim|L| = v(\mathcal{L}) + h^1(X,L),$$

so **speciality is exactly the non-vanishing of $h^1$** for a system with $h^0 > 0$.

A **$(-1)$-curve** is an irreducible curve $C \subset X$ with

$$C^2 = -1, \qquad C \cdot K_X = -1 \quad(\text{equivalently } p_a(C) = 0).$$

For $n \le 8$ these are the finitely many classes in the $E_n$ root-lattice orbit of $E_1$: $E_i$, $H - E_i - E_j$ (lines through $2$ points), $2H - E_{i_1} - \cdots - E_{i_5}$ (conics through $5$), $3H - 2E_{i_1} - E_{i_2} - \cdots - E_{i_7}$, and so on. For $n \ge 9$ there are infinitely many, generated from the $E_i$ by the **Cremona action** — the Weyl group $W_n$ generated by permutations of the $E_i$ and the standard quadratic transformation

$$\sigma: (d; m_1,m_2,m_3,\dots) \mapsto (d + k;\, m_1+k,\, m_2+k,\, m_3+k,\, m_4,\dots), \quad k = d - m_1 - m_2 - m_3 .$$

$\sigma$ preserves $\dim|L|$, $v(\mathcal{L})$ and speciality; a system is **standard** if $d \ge m_1+m_2+m_3$ after sorting $m_1 \ge m_2 \ge \cdots$. If $C$ is a $(-1)$-curve with $L\cdot C = -N \le -2$, then $C$ splits off $N$ times, $h^0(L) = h^0(L - NC)$, and the conditions imposed by the fat points drop by exactly $\binom{N}{2}$ — this is the *only* source of speciality the conjecture allows.

The conjecture implies **Nagata's conjecture**: for $n \ge 10$ general points and $m \ge 1$, $\mathcal{L}_d(m^n) = \emptyset$ whenever $d \le m\sqrt{n}$.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1961 — Segre.** Beniamino Segre states the first modern form: a special system in "standard" position must have a multiple component in its base locus (*Alcune questioni su insiemi finiti di punti in geometria algebrica*, Atti Convegno Torino, 1961).
- **1959–1960 — Nagata.** In solving Hilbert's 14th problem, Nagata proves the homogeneous non-speciality statement for $n = k^2 \ge 16$ points and formulates the Nagata conjecture, showing the problem's arithmetic depth.
- **1986 — Harbourne.** *The geometry of rational surfaces and Hilbert functions of points in the plane* (CMS Conf. Proc. 6) recasts the question cohomologically on anticanonical rational surfaces and proves the $n \le 9$ case.
- **1987 — Gimigliano.** Thesis (Queen's University) gives the base-locus formulation independently.
- **1989 — Hirschowitz.** *Une conjecture pour la cohomologie des diviseurs sur les surfaces rationnelles génériques* (Crelle 397) states the $(-1)$-curve criterion in the form used today and proves it for $m_i \le 3$ via the **Horace method** (specialization of points onto a line plus induction).
- **1995 — Alexander–Hirschowitz.** Full classification of double-point systems in every $\mathbb{P}^N$: in $\mathbb{P}^2$ the only special $\mathcal{L}_d(2^n)$ are $\mathcal{L}_2(2^2)$, $\mathcal{L}_4(2^5)$.
- **1998–2011 — Ciliberto–Miranda.** Degeneration of the plane into unions of surfaces (the "Ciliberto–Miranda degeneration") settles all uniform-multiplicity systems for $m \le 12$, later $m \le 20$, and the full homogeneous case for exactly $10$ points.
- **2000s — algorithmic era.** Mignon ($m_i\le 4$), Yang ($m_i \le 7$), Dumnicki–Jarnicki ($m_i \le 11$) push the multiplicity bound by combinatorial/reduction algorithms.

No counterexample is known; extensive computation supports the statement, so its empirical status is strong while the general case is open.

Additionally, SHGH is known for systems whose Cremona reduction reaches a standard class with $d \ge m_1+m_2+m_3$ and $v \ge -1$ under extra numerical hypotheses (Ciliberto–Miranda's "standard classes" theorem), and for all systems with $\sum m_i \le 3d$ small relative to $d$.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Infinitely many $(-1)$-curves for $n \ge 9$.** Once $-K_X$ is not effective ($n \ge 10$), the effective cone of $X$ is not rationally polyhedral and its boundary is unknown; one cannot enumerate the potential base-locus contributors. This is precisely the same wall as Nagata's conjecture, which SHGH implies — so no SHGH proof can be easier than Nagata's still-open case (all $n \ge 10$ non-squares).
- **Cremona reduction stalls.** The $W_n$-action reduces every class with $n\le 9$ to a normal form, but for $n \ge 10$ the Weyl group is of infinite hyperbolic type and orbits of standard classes need not terminate in a recognizable form.
- **Degeneration methods lose control.** Ciliberto–Miranda's degeneration of $\mathbb{P}^2$ into unions of planes/Hirzebruch surfaces requires solving a combinatorial matching of multiplicities across the components; the number of admissible configurations grows super-exponentially in $m$, which is exactly why the method caps out near $m = 20$.
- **Horace / specialization is lossy.** Specializing points onto a line gives an exact sequence with a residual system in lower degree, but the induction hypothesis is destroyed whenever the residual is itself potentially special — a circularity that only closes for small multiplicities.
- **No characteristic-free or valuation-theoretic invariant.** The conjectured $h^1 = \sum \binom{N_j}{2}$ has no known interpretation as a Euler characteristic of a natural complex, so no vanishing theorem (Kawamata–Viehweg, Nadel) applies: $L$ is typically *not* nef, and multiplier-ideal arguments give only asymptotic (Seshadri-constant) bounds, never exact $h^1$.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: all systems with small multiplicity ($m_i \le 11$), all systems on $\le 9$ points (equivalently, where $-K_X$ is effective and the effective cone is finitely generated), and the homogeneous case with $m \le 20$ or $n = 10$.

Missing: every regime where both $n \ge 11$ and $\max_i m_i \ge 12$, with $d$ near the "critical ratio" $d/m \approx \sqrt{n}$. The exact barrier: one must prove that a standard class ($d \ge m_1+m_2+m_3$, $m_1\ge\cdots\ge m_n$) with $v(\mathcal{L}) \ge -1$ is non-special. This "standard classes are non-special" statement is *equivalent* to SHGH (Ciliberto–Miranda), and crossing it requires a new source of upper bounds on $h^0$ — i.e., an unconditional degree bound of Nagata type $d > m\sqrt{n} \Rightarrow$ non-special — which no current method supplies for non-square $n \ge 10$.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Degeneration and limit linear systems.** Ciliberto, Miranda, Roé and Dedieu continue refining degenerations of $\mathbb{P}^2$ and "limit linear systems" on tree-like degenerate surfaces, aiming to make the combinatorics uniform in $m$ rather than case-by-case *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Cones of divisors on blow-ups.** Work on the Mori dream space / effective cone side (Laface, Ugaglia, Postinghel, Dumnicki, Küronya) studies $\mathrm{Bs}|L|$ via Zariski decompositions and Seshadri constants; the bounded negativity conjecture and Nagata's conjecture are treated as companions.
- **Higher-dimensional analogues.** SHGH-type statements for fat points in $\mathbb{P}^3$ and on toric/Hirzebruch surfaces (Laface–Ugaglia, Brambilla–Dumitrescu–Postinghel) test which features of the plane statement are essential.
- **Algorithmic verification.** Dumnicki's reduction/cutting algorithms and SAT/LP-based certificates continue to verify individual large systems and could in principle raise the multiplicity bound above $11$ with more compute.
- **Positive characteristic and arithmetic.** Whether SHGH can fail in characteristic $p$ (where "general" points behave differently and $(-1)$-curves can be non-reduced in families) remains a live probe of the statement's true content.

Institutions with sustained activity: Roma Tor Vergata (Ciliberto), Michigan State (Miranda, emeritus-active), Universitat de Barcelona (Roé), Jagiellonian Kraków (Dumnicki, Szemberg, Tutaj-Gasińska), Nebraska–Lincoln (Harbourne).

## 8. Future Work

- Prove the **standard-class reformulation** directly: show any standard $L$ with $v \ge -1$ has $h^1 = 0$, using a degeneration whose combinatorics is indexed by a bounded object independent of $m$.
- Establish Nagata's conjecture for a single non-square $n \ge 10$ by a new method; Ciliberto–Harbourne–Miranda–Roé argue this is the decisive test case.
- Develop a cohomological interpretation of $\sum \binom{N_j}{2}$ — e.g. as the length of a Tor module of a monomial-like degeneration — which would make the conjecture an equality of two computable invariants.
- Search systematically for counterexamples in the window $n \in [11, 40]$, $m \in [12, 30]$ with $d$ within $1$ of $\lceil m\sqrt{n}\rceil$, where any failure would first appear.
- Transfer the statement to $\mathbb{P}^N$ and to blow-ups of other rational surfaces to isolate which step is genuinely two-dimensional.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_4(2,2,2,2,2)$: quartic plane curves with five general double points.

**Step 1 — expected dimension.** Quartics form a $\binom{6}{2} = 15$-dimensional vector space, so $\dim |\mathcal{O}(4)| = 14$. Each double point imposes at most $\binom{3}{2} = 3$ conditions:

$$v(\mathcal{L}) = 14 - 5\cdot 3 = -1, \qquad e(\mathcal{L}) = -1 .$$

Expected: no such quartic exists.

**Step 2 — actual dimension.** Through $5$ general points passes exactly one smooth conic $Q$. The quartic $2Q$ has degree $4$ and multiplicity $2$ at each $p_i$. It is the only member (any member must contain $Q$ by Bézout: a quartic meeting $Q$ in $\ge 2\cdot 5 = 10 > 8 = 4\cdot 2$ points contains it; the residual conic through the $5$ points is $Q$ again). So

$$h^0(\mathcal{L}) = 1, \qquad \dim \mathcal{L} = 0 > -1 = e(\mathcal{L}).$$

The system is special, with $h^1 = \dim\mathcal{L} - v(\mathcal{L}) = 0 - (-1) = 1$.

**Step 3 — the $(-1)$-curve certificate.** On the blow-up $X$ at the five points, the proper transform of $Q$ is $C = 2H - E_1 - \cdots - E_5$:

$$C^2 = 4 - 5 = -1, \qquad C\cdot K_X = (2H - \textstyle\sum E_i)\cdot(-3H + \sum E_i) = -6 + 5 = -1 .$$

So $C$ is a $(-1)$-curve. With $L = 4H - 2\sum_{i=1}^5 E_i$,

$$L \cdot C = 8 - 2\cdot 5 = -2 =: -N, \qquad N = 2 \ge 2 .$$

The conjectured formula gives $h^1(L) = \binom{N}{2} = \binom{2}{2} = 1$, matching Step 2 exactly. The residual $L - NC = 4H - 2\sum E_i - 2(2H - \sum E_i) = 0$ is the trivial class, non-special with $h^0 = 1$, $v = 0$.

This is the smallest non-trivial confirmation: speciality is caused entirely by the conic splitting off twice, and the five "wasted" conditions are accounted for by the single $\binom{2}{2}$ term. The conjecture asserts that *every* special plane system, for any $n$ and any multiplicities, decomposes in exactly this way — and it is this universality, not any individual case, that remains unproved.
