# Bogomolov Multiplier Vanishing Problem

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/bogomolov-multiplier-problem` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

For a finite group $G$, the **Bogomolov multiplier** $\mathrm{B}_0(G)$ is the subgroup of the Schur multiplier $H^2(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})$ consisting of classes that restrict to zero on every abelian subgroup. Bogomolov proved that $\mathrm{B}_0(G)$ is exactly the unramified Brauer group $\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{nr}}(\mathbb{C}(V)^G)$ of the quotient of a faithful linear representation $V$, so $\mathrm{B}_0(G)\neq 0$ forces a negative answer to **Noether's problem** for $G$: the field $\mathbb{C}(V)^G$ is not rational, not even stably rational, and not retract rational.

The problem has three linked parts, all open:

1. **Classification.** Give a group-theoretic characterization of the finite groups (already open for $p$-groups) with $\mathrm{B}_0(G)=0$. A complete solution means a criterion checkable from the presentation, or a classification of the nonvanishing cases by order/class/isoclinism family.
2. **Converse to Saltman.** For a $p$-group $G$, does $\mathrm{B}_0(G)=0$ imply that $\mathbb{C}(V)^G$ is retract rational? Saltman proved the forward direction; the converse is open.
3. **Structural bounds.** Bound $|\mathrm{B}_0(G)|$, its exponent, and the minimal order of a $p$-group with $\mathrm{B}_0(G)\neq 0$, in terms of the order, nilpotency class, and derived length of $G$.

A disproof of (2) would be an explicit $p$-group with vanishing multiplier and non-retract-rational invariant field; this requires an obstruction finer than $\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{nr}}$.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Definition (Bogomolov, 1987).** For a finite group $G$,
$$\mathrm{B}_0(G)\;=\;\bigcap_{A\leq G,\ A\ \text{abelian}}\ \ker\!\big(\mathrm{res}^G_A:H^2(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\to H^2(A,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})\big).$$
Bogomolov showed the intersection may be taken over **bicyclic** subgroups $A=\langle x\rangle\langle y\rangle$ with $[x,y]=1$; this makes $\mathrm{B}_0$ finitely checkable.

**Theorem (Bogomolov, 1987).** If $V$ is a faithful $\mathbb{C}$-linear representation of $G$, then
$$\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{nr}}\big(\mathbb{C}(V)^G\big)\;\cong\;\mathrm{B}_0(G),$$
where $\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{nr}}(K)=\bigcap_{\nu}\ker\big(\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{O}_\nu)\to\mathrm{Br}(K)\big)^{\!\vee}$ ranges over divisorial valuations of $K/\mathbb{C}$. The group is independent of $V$ (no-name lemma) and is a stable birational invariant, vanishing for rational and even retract rational fields.

**Homological model (Moravec, 2012).** Let $G\wedge G$ be the nonabelian exterior square, generated by symbols $x\wedge y$ subject to
$$xy\wedge z=({}^{x}y\wedge{}^{x}z)(x\wedge z),\qquad x\wedge yz=(x\wedge y)({}^{y}x\wedge{}^{y}z),\qquad x\wedge x=1 .$$
The commutator map $G\wedge G\to[G,G]$, $x\wedge y\mapsto[x,y]$, has kernel the Schur multiplier $M(G)=H_2(G,\mathbb{Z})$. Put
$$M_0(G)=\big\langle\, x\wedge y \;:\; x,y\in G,\ [x,y]=1 \,\big\rangle\ \le\ M(G).$$
Then $\mathrm{B}_0(G)$ is the Pontryagin dual of
$$\widetilde{\mathrm{B}}_0(G)\;=\;M(G)/M_0(G)\;=\;H_2(G,\mathbb{Z})/\langle\text{commuting wedges}\rangle .$$
Equivalently, $\widetilde{\mathrm{B}}_0(G)$ measures relations among commutators in $G$ that are **not** consequences of the universal commutator relations plus $[x,y]=1$ for commuting pairs. This description is purely combinatorial and drives all modern computation.

**Isoclinism invariance (Moravec; Bogomolov–Böhning).** If $G_1$ and $G_2$ are isoclinic — there are isomorphisms $\alpha:G_1/Z(G_1)\to G_2/Z(G_2)$, $\beta:[G_1,G_1]\to[G_2,G_2]$ compatible with commutators — then $\mathrm{B}_0(G_1)\cong \mathrm{B}_0(G_2)$. Hence $\mathrm{B}_0$ is constant on James's isoclinism families $\Phi_k$, reducing classification of $p$-groups of a given order to finitely many families.

**Saltman's implication.** $\mathbb{C}(V)^G$ retract rational $\Rightarrow \mathrm{B}_0(G)=0$.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1918.** Noether asks whether $k(x_g:g\in G)^G$ is rational, motivated by the inverse Galois problem.
- **1969–1974.** Swan and Voskresenskii give negative answers for cyclic $G$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ (e.g. $\mathbb{Z}/47$), but these obstructions are arithmetic and vanish over $\mathbb{C}$.
- **1984.** Saltman (*Invent. Math.* 77) produces the first groups with $\mathbb{C}(V)^G$ non-rational: $p$-groups of order $p^9$, detected by an unramified Brauer class. This also refuted the expectation that $\mathbb{C}(V)^G$ is always rational.
- **1987.** Bogomolov computes $\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{nr}}$ purely group-theoretically as $\mathrm{B}_0(G)$, reduces to bicyclic subgroups, and gives examples of order $p^6$. He proves $\mathrm{B}_0(G)=0$ for $|G|\le p^4$ and for groups with cyclic derived subgroup (hence metacyclic groups).
- **1989.** Colliot-Thélène–Ojanguren place these examples in the general framework of unramified cohomology, extending Artin–Mumford.
- **2008–2010.** Chu–Hu–Kang–Prokhorov: Noether's problem is affirmative for all groups of order 32. Chu–Hu–Kang–Kunyavskii: among the 267 groups of order 64, exactly the **9 groups of the isoclinism family $\Phi_{16}$** have $\mathrm{B}_0(G)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2$; all others have $\mathrm{B}_0=0$.
- **2010.** Kunyavskii: $\mathrm{B}_0(G)=0$ for every finite **simple** group. (Bogomolov had conjectured this.)
- **2012.** Moravec introduces $M(G)/M_0(G)$, corrects Bogomolov's claim that $\mathrm{B}_0=0$ for all groups of order $p^5$ by exhibiting three groups of order $3^5=243$ with $\mathrm{B}_0\cong\mathbb{Z}/3$.
- **2013.** Hoshi–Kang–Kunyavskii: for odd $p$ and $|G|=p^5$, $\mathrm{B}_0(G)\neq 0$ **iff** $G$ lies in the isoclinism family $\Phi_{10}$, and then $\mathrm{B}_0(G)\cong\mathbb{Z}/p$. This is the sharpest complete-order classification known.
- **2014–2015.** Jezernik–Moravec compute $\mathrm{B}_0$ for all groups of order 128 and prove commuting-probability criteria forcing $\mathrm{B}_0(G)=0$.
- **2020.** Hoshi–Kang–Yamasaki push to degree-three unramified cohomology $H^3_{\mathrm{nr}}$ for groups of order 243, detecting non-rationality beyond the Brauer group.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

**Vanishing ($\mathrm{B}_0(G)=0$) is proved for:**
- all abelian groups; all groups with cyclic derived subgroup $[G,G]$ (Bogomolov), covering metacyclic and dihedral/quaternion/semidihedral $2$-groups;
- all $p$-groups of order $\le p^4$ (Bogomolov 1987), and all groups of order $32$ (Chu–Hu–Kang–Prokhorov, with $\mathbb{C}(V)^G$ actually rational);
- all finite simple groups (Kunyavskii 2010) and, more generally, all quasi-simple groups;
- groups of order $p^5$ outside $\Phi_{10}$, $p$ odd; groups of order $64$ outside $\Phi_{16}$;
- groups $G$ with commuting probability $\mathrm{cp}(G)=|\{(x,y):[x,y]=1\}|/|G|^2$ above an explicit threshold (Jezernik–Moravec 2015);
- symmetric groups $S_n$, and $\mathbb{Z}/p\wr\mathbb{Z}/p$ (Bogomolov–Böhning).

**Nonvanishing is established for:**
- Saltman's groups of order $p^9$ and Bogomolov's of order $p^6$;
- the 9 groups of order 64 in $\Phi_{16}$: $\mathrm{B}_0\cong\mathbb{Z}/2$;
- for each odd $p$, the groups of order $p^5$ in $\Phi_{10}$ (three of them when $p=3$): $\mathrm{B}_0\cong\mathbb{Z}/p$;
- a complete machine-verified list for $|G|=128$ (Jezernik–Moravec, *Exp. Math.* 2014), realized as a GAP computation over all 2328 groups.

**Retract rationality converse verified:** for $|G|\le 64$ and $|G|=p^5$ ($p$ odd), $\mathrm{B}_0(G)=0$ has in each checked case been matched by an explicit proof of retract rationality (often full rationality) of $\mathbb{C}(V)^G$.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Cohomology does not localize well.** $\mathrm{B}_0$ is an intersection of kernels over *all* abelian subgroups; it is not a functor with Mayer–Vietoris or a spectral sequence adapted to it. Standard tools (LHS spectral sequence, transfer/corestriction) compute $H^2(G,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})$ but do not see the abelian-restriction condition, so vanishing statements never follow formally.
- **Non-monotonicity.** $\mathrm{B}_0$ is neither subgroup- nor quotient-monotone: subgroups and quotients of groups with $\mathrm{B}_0=0$ can have $\mathrm{B}_0\neq0$ and conversely. Every finite group embeds in some $S_n$ with $\mathrm{B}_0(S_n)=0$, so no induction on subgroup lattices works.
- **Combinatorial explosion.** The exterior-square presentation gives a finite algorithm, but $|G\wedge G|$ grows with $|G|^2$-many relators; complete enumeration stalls past order $2^7$ and $3^5$. The number of $p$-groups of order $p^6$, $p^7$ is already astronomically large, and isoclinism only cuts it by a bounded factor.
- **Class-2 groups are the hard core.** Most known nonvanishing examples have nilpotency class 2 or 3, exactly the regime where the exterior square is largest relative to the group and where no invariant-theoretic normal form exists.
- **No finer invariant matched to retract rationality.** Retract rationality is characterized by lifting properties of $G$-torsors; $\mathrm{B}_0$ captures only degree-2 unramified data. Higher unramified cohomology $H^i_{\mathrm{nr}}$ is computable only in isolated cases, so the converse question has no systematic attack.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: complete answers for $|G|\in\{p^{\le4}\}\cup\{32,64,128\}$ and $|G|=p^5$ ($p$ odd), plus structural vanishing theorems for cyclic-derived, simple, and high-commuting-probability groups. Unproven: everything of order $p^6$ and above for general $p$ — precisely the order at which Bogomolov's first nonvanishing examples appear, so no order is fully settled once nonvanishing becomes generic.

The concrete gap is a **local-to-global step**: one needs a mechanism that converts information about restrictions to bicyclic subgroups into a bound on $M(G)/M_0(G)$ valid uniformly in $|G|$. Every existing proof either (i) exploits a special structure ($[G,G]$ cyclic, $G$ simple), or (ii) exhausts an isoclinism list. Neither scales. For part (2) of Section 1, the gap is the absence of any candidate invariant strictly between $\mathrm{B}_0$ and retract rationality.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Ljubljana school (Moravec, Jezernik).** Commutativity-preserving extensions, "$\mathrm{B}_0$-defect" of a presentation, and GAP implementations; recent work relates $\mathrm{B}_0$ to the Bogomolov multiplier of the associated graded Lie ring, giving vanishing for groups of exponent $p$ and class $<p$ *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Taipei/Tokyo (Kang, Hoshi, Yamasaki).** Systematic computation of $H^3_{\mathrm{nr}}$ for $p$-groups of order $p^5$–$p^6$; the aim is examples with $\mathrm{B}_0=0$ but $H^3_{\mathrm{nr}}\neq0$, which would refute the naive converse to Saltman.
- **Bogomolov, Böhning, and collaborators.** Stable cohomology of wreath products and of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{F}_p)$-related groups; "stable cohomology" as a refinement carrying more than $\mathrm{B}_0$.
- **Lie-theoretic transfer.** Bogomolov multipliers of Lie algebras (a $\mathrm{B}_0$ for $\mathfrak{g}$ defined via the exterior square modulo commuting pairs) and Lazard correspondence transfer for $p$-groups of small class *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Computational classification.** Ongoing attempts to enumerate $\mathrm{B}_0$ for all groups of order $3^6=729$ and $2^8=256$ using nilpotent quotient algorithms; partial data exists for restricted isoclinism families.

## 8. Future Work

- Prove or disprove: for a $p$-group of nilpotency class 2, $\mathrm{B}_0(G)$ is determined by the alternating form $G/Z(G)\times G/Z(G)\to[G,G]$ and its isotropic subspaces — a linear-algebra criterion would settle a large family at once.
- Find an obstruction functor $\mathcal{O}$ with $\mathcal{O}(G)=0\iff\mathbb{C}(V)^G$ retract rational, refining $\mathrm{B}_0$; candidates come from $H^3_{\mathrm{nr}}$ and from motivic/decomposition-of-the-diagonal methods.
- Bound $\exp(\mathrm{B}_0(G))$ by $\exp([G,G])$, and determine which finite abelian groups occur as $\mathrm{B}_0(G)$.
- Determine the minimal order of a $p$-group with $\mathrm{B}_0\neq0$ for $p=2$ (known: $64$) and prove the analogue $p^5$ for all odd $p$ is sharp in the family sense.
- Extend to positive characteristic and to non-closed base fields, where $\mathrm{B}_0$ interacts with Galois descent.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Claim.** For $p$ odd, the Heisenberg group $G=\mathrm{UT}_3(\mathbb{F}_p)$ of order $p^3$ and exponent $p$ has $\mathrm{B}_0(G)=0$.

Present $G=\langle x,y \mid x^p=y^p=z^p=1,\ z=[x,y],\ [x,z]=[y,z]=1\rangle$, so $[G,G]=Z(G)=\langle z\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}/p$ and $G/Z(G)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p)^2$.

*Step 1 — the Schur multiplier.* $M(G)=H_2(G,\mathbb{Z})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p)^2$ (standard for the extraspecial group of order $p^3$ and exponent $p$, $p$ odd).

*Step 2 — the exterior square.* $G\wedge G$ is a central extension
$$1\to M(G)\to G\wedge G \xrightarrow{\ x\wedge y\,\mapsto\,[x,y]\ } [G,G]\to 1,$$
so $|G\wedge G| = |M(G)|\cdot|[G,G]| = p^2\cdot p = p^3$. Generators are $x\wedge y$, $x\wedge z$, $y\wedge z$; since $z$ is central and $G$ has class 2, all three have order dividing $p$ and commute, and $M(G)=\langle x\wedge z,\ y\wedge z\rangle$ (these map to $[x,z]=[y,z]=1$).

*Step 3 — the commuting subgroup.* $z\in Z(G)$, so $[x,z]=1$ and $[y,z]=1$. By definition $x\wedge z$ and $y\wedge z$ are wedges of **commuting** pairs, hence lie in $M_0(G)$. Therefore
$$M_0(G)\supseteq\langle x\wedge z,\ y\wedge z\rangle = M(G)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \widetilde{\mathrm{B}}_0(G)=M(G)/M_0(G)=0 .$$
Dualizing, $\mathrm{B}_0(G)=0$, and consequently $\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{nr}}(\mathbb{C}(V)^G)=0$; indeed $\mathbb{C}(V)^G$ is known to be rational here.

*Contrast.* The mechanism above fails as soon as $M(G)$ contains a class not expressible by commuting wedges. For the order-$3^5$ groups in the family $\Phi_{10}$ (Moravec 2012), $M(G)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/3)^2$ but $M_0(G)$ has index 3, giving $\mathrm{B}_0(G)\cong\mathbb{Z}/3$ and non-rationality of $\mathbb{C}(V)^G$. The whole difficulty of the problem is predicting, from a presentation, whether the commuting wedges exhaust $M(G)$.
