# Baum-Connes Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/baum-connes-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

The Baum-Connes Conjecture proposes a fundamental isomorphism between two distinct mathematical realms associated with a group $G$: the topological K-homology of the classifying space of the group, and the analytical K-theory of its reduced C*-algebra. 

Specifically, for a locally compact, second-countable topological group $G$ (which includes all countable discrete groups), the conjecture states that the assembly map $\mu$ is an isomorphism:
$$ \mu: K_*^G(\underline{E}G) \xrightarrow{\cong} K_*(C^*_r(G)) $$
where the left side is the $G$-equivariant K-homology of the universal classifying space for proper $G$-actions $\underline{E}G$, and the right side is the topological K-theory of the reduced group C*-algebra $C^*_r(G)$. 

Proving this conjecture would provide an algorithmic or topological way to compute the K-theory of group C*-algebras and would resolve several other major open problems, including the Novikov Conjecture (on the homotopy invariance of higher signatures) and the Kadison-Kaplansky Conjecture (that the reduced C*-algebra of a torsion-free discrete group contains no non-trivial idempotents).

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

The conjecture bridges algebraic topology, differential geometry, and operator algebras. The necessary foundations are:

1. **Locally Compact Group $G$:** A topological group that admits a left-invariant Haar measure, allowing for integration.
2. **Universal Space for Proper Actions $\underline{E}G$:** A locally compact Hausdorff space equipped with a proper $G$-action, such that for any other space $X$ with a proper $G$-action, there exists a unique (up to $G$-homotopy) $G$-equivariant map from $X$ to $\underline{E}G$.
3. **Equivariant K-homology $K_*^G(\underline{E}G)$:** Formulated rigorously using Kasparov's bivariant KK-theory, $K_*^G(\underline{E}G)$ is defined as the direct limit of $KK^G(C_0(X), \mathbb{C})$ over all $G$-compact subsets $X \subset \underline{E}G$. It captures topological and geometric invariants associated with the group's actions.
4. **Reduced Group C*-algebra $C^*_r(G)$:** Let $L^2(G)$ be the Hilbert space of square-integrable functions on $G$. The group acts on $L^2(G)$ by left translation, providing the left regular representation $\lambda: G \to \mathcal{B}(L^2(G))$. $C^*_r(G)$ is the norm closure of the algebra generated by $\{\lambda(f) \mid f \in L^1(G)\}$ in the C*-algebra of bounded operators on $L^2(G)$.
5. **K-theory of a C*-algebra $K_*(A)$:** The K-theory groups $K_0(A)$ and $K_1(A)$ characterize the stably free modules over $A$. 
6. **The Assembly Map $\mu$:** An index map defined via elliptic differential operators (in the context of index theory) or through Kasparov's KK-intersection product. It maps geometric cycles (e.g., an equivariant Dirac operator on a manifold $X$) to an index lying in the K-theory of the group C*-algebra.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

The conjecture was formulated by Paul Baum and Alain Connes in 1982. It emerged from their efforts to extend the Atiyah-Singer Index Theorem to non-compact manifolds and foliations. 

Over the decades, the conjecture has catalyzed the development of non-commutative geometry. While the general conjecture remains open, spectacular progress has been made for vast classes of groups. 
- **1997–2001:** Nigel Higson and Gennadi Kasparov proved the conjecture for a-T-menable groups (groups with the Haagerup property).
- **2002:** Vincent Lafforgue achieved a breakthrough by proving the conjecture for many groups with Kazhdan's Property (T), including cocompact lattices in $SL(3, \mathbb{R})$ and word-hyperbolic groups, by developing bivariant K-theory for Banach algebras.
- **2003:** Mikhail Gromov demonstrated that the Baum-Connes conjecture *with coefficients* (a generalized version involving twisted K-theory) is false for certain random groups that contain expander graphs in their Cayley graphs. However, Gromov's counterexamples did not falsify the classical (un-twisted) Baum-Connes conjecture.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

The Baum-Connes conjecture has been strictly verified for several broad classes of groups:

- **Amenable Groups:** E.g., abelian groups, nilpotent groups, and solvable groups (by Higson-Kasparov).
- **a-T-menable Groups (Haagerup Property):** Groups that admit a proper affine isometric action on a Hilbert space, including free groups and Coxeter groups.
- **Hyperbolic Groups:** Word-hyperbolic groups in the sense of Gromov, verified using Lafforgue's Banach KK-theory.
- **Lie Groups:** Connected Lie groups, and more broadly, almost connected locally compact groups (Chabert, Echterhoff, and Nest).
- **Linear Algebraic Groups:** Many reductive algebraic groups over $p$-adic fields and their discrete cocompact subgroups.
- **One-Relator Groups:** All groups given by a single relation satisfy the conjecture.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

The main bottleneck for the full conjecture involves groups that exhibit extreme forms of rigidity, particularly groups related to **expander graphs** and **Strong Property (T)**.

1. **The Dirac-Dual Dirac Method Fails:** The standard proof machinery (the Dirac-dual Dirac method introduced by Kasparov) constructs a $\gamma$-element in $KK_G(\mathbb{C}, \mathbb{C})$. For the assembly map to be an isomorphism, one typically needs $\gamma = 1$. However, for groups with Property (T), the $\gamma$-element does not equal the identity. Lafforgue circumvented this using Banach algebras, but his method is limited by structural constraints.
2. **Expanders and Ghosts:** Gromov showed that certain finitely generated random groups contain expander graphs uniformly embedded in their Cayley graphs. These spaces do not admit uniform embeddings into Hilbert spaces. The existence of "ghost projections" (operators that are locally invisible but globally non-trivial) in the C*-algebras of these groups breaks the standard exactness sequences used in K-theory, shattering the Coarse Baum-Connes conjecture and the BC conjecture with coefficients.
3. **Absence of Analytic Methods for Pathological Groups:** For Gromov's "monster" groups, no current analytic tools (neither KK-theory, E-theory, nor Banach K-theory) are powerful enough to evaluate K-theory of their C*-algebras directly.

## 6. The Gap

The exact mathematical gap lies in determining the status of the assembly map for **non-exact discrete groups**, particularly Gromov's random groups with embedded expanders. 
What remains unproven is whether the failure of the conjecture *with coefficients* trickles down to the classical, coefficient-free version. The boundary of current knowledge separates groups for which we can construct a well-behaved compactification or boundary (like hyperbolic groups) from pathological groups that lack any form of geometric amenability.

To close the gap, mathematics requires either:
1. The construction of a completely novel counterexample for the classical Baum-Connes conjecture (likely involving a sophisticated spectral analysis of the regular representation of an expander-based random group).
2. The invention of a new homology theory bridging non-exact C*-algebras with topology, successfully generalizing the assembly map beyond KK-theory.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

Current active research falls largely into two camps:
- **Exotic Crossed Products / Modified Baum-Connes:** Researchers (like Baum, Guentner, and Willett) are exploring whether the conjecture is inherently false for $C^*_r(G)$ but true for an intermediate "exotic" C*-algebra $C^*_{exotic}(G)$ that sits between the universal and reduced C*-algebras. The formulation of the modified Baum-Connes conjecture is a highly active area.
- **Group Actions on Expanders:** Determining the precise index-theoretic properties of group elements acting on expanders. 
- *(frontier — verify)*: Recent preprints have claimed that certain constructions of groups with "robustly Strong Property (T)" (building on Osajda's construction) yield explicit counterexamples to the classical Baum-Connes conjecture. These claims involve showing that $K_0(C^*_r(G))$ contains torsion elements not present in the classifying space, but they remain under intense peer review.

## 8. Future Work

Leading researchers recommend the following strategic directions:
- **Analyze Gromov's Random Groups:** Fully compute the topological K-theory of $C^*_r(G)$ for a specific Gromov random group containing an expander.
- **The Ideal Structure of $C^*_r(G)$:** Better understand the ideal structure of the reduced C*-algebras for non-exact groups to see if "ghost" elements generate non-trivial K-theory classes.
- **Higher Rank Lattices:** Simplify the proofs for higher-rank lattices (e.g., $SL(n, \mathbb{Z})$ for $n \ge 3$). While Lafforgue's work covers many cases, a purely Hilbert-space theoretic proof (bypassing Banach algebras) would provide monumental new tools.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Consider the simplest non-trivial case where $G = \mathbb{Z}$ (the integers, an amenable discrete group). We will verify the Baum-Connes conjecture for this group.

1. **Left Side (Geometric):** 
The classifying space for proper $\mathbb{Z}$-actions is the real line, $\underline{E}\mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{R}$, where $\mathbb{Z}$ acts freely and properly by translation ($x \mapsto x + n$). 
The equivariant K-homology $K_*^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{R})$ is isomorphic to the ordinary topological K-homology of the quotient space $\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z} = S^1$ (the circle).
The topological K-homology of the circle is well known:
$$ K_0(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}, \quad K_1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z} $$
(generated by the Dirac operator on $S^1$ and the trivial representation).

2. **Right Side (Analytic):**
The reduced C*-algebra $C^*_r(\mathbb{Z})$ is the completion of the group ring $\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}]$. By Pontryagin duality and the Fourier transform, $C^*_r(\mathbb{Z})$ is isomorphic to the algebra of continuous functions on the Pontryagin dual of $\mathbb{Z}$, which is $S^1$.
Thus, $C^*_r(\mathbb{Z}) \cong C(S^1)$.
The topological K-theory of a commutative C*-algebra $C(X)$ is naturally isomorphic to the topological K-theory of the space $X$. Thus:
$$ K_0(C(S^1)) = K^0(S^1) = \mathbb{Z}, \quad K_1(C(S^1)) = K^1(S^1) = \mathbb{Z} $$

3. **The Assembly Map:**
The assembly map $\mu: K_*^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{R}) \to K_*(C^*_r(\mathbb{Z}))$ maps the K-homology generators of $S^1$ directly to the K-theory generators of $C(S^1)$ via the abstract Fourier transform mapping symbols of pseudodifferential operators to vector bundles. This map is clearly an isomorphism ($\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}$ for both dimensions). 

This verifies the conjecture for $G = \mathbb{Z}$. It successfully translates the geometric problem of the K-homology of $S^1$ into the purely algebraic realm of the C*-algebra generated by the group.
