# Han's Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/hans-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $k$ be a field and $A$ a finite-dimensional associative unital $k$-algebra. Write $HH_n(A)$ for the $n$-th Hochschild homology of $A$ with coefficients in $A$.

**Han's Conjecture (Y. Han, 2006).** If $HH_n(A) = 0$ for all $n \gg 0$, then $A$ has finite global dimension.

Equivalently, in contrapositive form: if $\operatorname{gl.dim} A = \infty$, then $HH_n(A) \neq 0$ for infinitely many $n$. Hochschild homology should *detect singularity*.

The converse implication is a theorem, subject to a separability hypothesis: if $\operatorname{gl.dim} A < \infty$ and $A/\operatorname{rad} A$ is separable over $k$ (automatic when $k$ is perfect), then $\operatorname{pd}_{A^e} A < \infty$ and hence $HH_n(A) = 0$ for $n \gg 0$ (Happel, 1989). So under separability the conjecture asserts the equivalence

$$\operatorname{gl.dim} A < \infty \iff HH_n(A) = 0 \text{ for } n \gg 0 .$$

A complete proof must handle all finite-dimensional $A$ over an arbitrary field; a disproof requires an explicit $A$ with $\operatorname{gl.dim} A = \infty$ and $HH_n(A) = 0$ for all large $n$.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Let $A^e = A \otimes_k A^{\mathrm{op}}$ be the enveloping algebra, so $A$-bimodules $=$ left $A^e$-modules. The Hochschild chain complex is

$$C_\bullet(A): \quad \cdots \to A^{\otimes (n+1)} \xrightarrow{\ b_n\ } A^{\otimes n} \to \cdots \to A\otimes A \xrightarrow{\ b_1\ } A ,$$

$$b_n(a_0\otimes\cdots\otimes a_n) = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}(-1)^i\, a_0\otimes\cdots\otimes a_ia_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_n \; + \; (-1)^n\, a_na_0\otimes a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n-1},$$

and $HH_n(A) = H_n(C_\bullet(A)) \cong \operatorname{Tor}^{A^e}_n(A,A)$. Dually $HH^n(A) = \operatorname{Ext}^n_{A^e}(A,A)$. One has $HH_0(A) = A/[A,A]$ and $HH^0(A) = Z(A)$.

Define the **Hochschild homology dimension**

$$\operatorname{hhd}(A) = \sup\{\, n : HH_n(A) \neq 0 \,\} \in \mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\},$$

and analogously $\operatorname{hcd}(A)$ for cohomology. Both are bounded above by $\operatorname{pd}_{A^e} A$, the **Hochschild dimension**. Han's conjecture reads: $\operatorname{hhd}(A) < \infty \Rightarrow \operatorname{gl.dim} A < \infty$.

Two structural facts frame the problem.

- **Derived invariance.** $HH_\bullet$ is invariant under derived equivalence of algebras (Keller, 1998), and $\operatorname{gl.dim} A<\infty$ is equivalent to vanishing of the singularity category $D_{sg}(A) = D^b(\operatorname{mod} A)/\operatorname{perf}(A)$, itself derived invariant. Hence the conjecture is a statement about the derived category, stable under tilting.
- **Separability is essential.** If $k$ is imperfect of characteristic $p$ and $K = k(t^{1/p}) \supset k$ is purely inseparable, then $\operatorname{gl.dim} K = 0$ but $K\otimes_k K$ is a non-reduced local ring and $HH_n(K)\neq 0$ for all $n$. So the "easy" direction genuinely needs $A/\operatorname{rad}A$ separable; Han's direction does not.

For a monomial algebra $A = kQ/I$ with $I$ generated by paths, minimal bimodule resolutions are given by Bardzell's combinatorial resolution, and $HH_\bullet(A)$ is computable from cycles in $Q$ — the source of the first proved cases.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

The problem grew out of **Happel's question** (1989): does $HH^n(A) = 0$ for $n\gg 0$ imply $\operatorname{gl.dim} A < \infty$? This was answered **negatively** by Buchweitz, Green, Madsen and Solberg (2005): for $q\in k^\times$ not a root of unity, the four-dimensional quantum complete intersection

$$\Lambda_q = k\langle x,y\rangle/(x^2,\;y^2,\;xy+qyx)$$

has $\operatorname{gl.dim}\Lambda_q = \infty$ but $HH^n(\Lambda_q) = 0$ for all $n\ge 3$.

Yang Han (2006) observed that the *homological* analogue survives this counterexample: $HH_n(\Lambda_q) \neq 0$ for all $n\ge 0$. He proposed the conjecture now bearing his name and proved it for monomial algebras and for graded local algebras. Bergh–Erdmann (2008) computed homology and cohomology of general quantum complete intersections, confirming the conjecture there; Bergh–Madsen (2009) extended it to positively graded algebras with semisimple degree-zero part, covering Koszul algebras. In the commutative setting the statement was already known: Avramov–Vigué-Poirrier (1992) showed that for a commutative algebra essentially of finite type over a field of characteristic zero, a gap in $HH_\bullet$ forces smoothness.

Since 2019 the most systematic progress comes from the Cibils–Lanzilotta–Marcos–Solotar (CLMS) programme: rather than computing $HH_\bullet$, they prove that the class $\mathcal{H}$ of algebras satisfying Han's conjecture is closed under well-behaved ring extensions, using a *Jacobi–Zariski long nearly exact sequence* in Hochschild homology.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

The conjecture is a theorem in the following classes.

- **Monomial algebras** $kQ/I$, $I$ generated by paths (Han 2006), building on Sköldberg's computation of $HH_\bullet$ for truncated and quadratic monomial algebras (1999). Includes all radical-square-zero algebras $kQ/\operatorname{rad}^2$.
- **Graded local algebras**, i.e. $A = \bigoplus_{i\ge 0}A_i$ with $A_0 = k$ (Han 2006).
- **Positively graded algebras with $A_0$ semisimple**, in particular **Koszul algebras** (Bergh–Madsen 2009).
- **Quantum complete intersections** $k\langle x_1,\dots,x_c\rangle/(x_i^{a_i},\, x_jx_i - q_{ij}x_ix_j)$ for all $c\ge 1$, $a_i\ge 2$ and all quantisation parameters $q_{ij}$ (Bergh–Erdmann 2008; Bergh–Madsen 2009) — including the BGMS counterexamples to Happel's question.
- **Commutative finite-dimensional algebras** over a perfect field: such an $A$ has $\operatorname{gl.dim}A<\infty$ iff $A$ is a product of fields, and Avramov–Vigué-Poirrier's smoothness criterion (char $0$; Avramov–Iyengar 2005 for cohomology gaps) settles the case.
- **Cellular / one-point extensions and arrow deletions.** If $A = kQ/I$ and $a$ is an arrow such that $A$ and the algebra with $a$ deleted are related by a *split bounded extension*, then one satisfies Han's conjecture iff the other does (CLMS, *Proc. AMS* 2020).
- **Bounded extensions.** If $B\subseteq A$ is a bounded extension of finite-dimensional algebras (the $B$-bimodule $A/B$ is projective and the associated filtration terminates), then $A\in\mathcal{H} \iff B\in\mathcal{H}$ (CLMS, *J. Algebra* 2022). Corollaries: triangular matrix algebras $\begin{pmatrix} B & M \\ 0 & C\end{pmatrix}$ with $B,C\in\mathcal{H}$; null-square projective algebras; new proofs for monomial algebras.
- **Algebras with $\operatorname{hhd}(A)\le 1$**: trivially $HH_1(A)=0$ with $A$ elementary forces the quiver to have no loops, connecting to Igusa's no-loops theorem (1990).

No counterexample is known in any dimension; small-dimensional exhaustive checks (algebras of dimension $\le 6$ over small fields, via quiver-with-relations enumeration) are consistent with the conjecture.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No general computation of $HH_\bullet$.** Minimal bimodule resolutions of $A$ are known only for structured families (monomial, Koszul, self-injective of finite type). For a general $kQ/I$ with non-homogeneous relations, no combinatorial model for $b_\bullet$ exists, so direct verification is out of reach.
- **Cohomology fails, so the proof must use homology-specific structure.** The BGMS example shows any argument that only uses the $A^e$-module structure of $A$ symmetrically (as $\operatorname{Ext}$ and $\operatorname{Tor}$ both do) cannot work: $\operatorname{Tor}^{A^e}_n(A,A)$ vanishes iff... it does not, while $\operatorname{Ext}^n$ does. Techniques must exploit the extra structure carried by $HH_\bullet$ — the Connes operator $B$, cyclic homology, and the trace map $A^{\otimes n}\to k$ — not merely dimension shifting.
- **Support-variety methods break down.** For self-injective algebras of finite representation type, support varieties control $\operatorname{Ext}$-vanishing, but $A$ arbitrary need not satisfy the finiteness condition (Fg) of Erdmann–Holloway–Snashall–Solberg–Taillefer, and homology has no comparable variety theory.
- **Characteristic and separability interference.** Over imperfect fields the "easy" direction fails, so any uniform argument must separate the contribution of $A/\operatorname{rad}A$ from that of the radical; the Jacobi–Zariski sequence used by CLMS is only *nearly* exact for that reason.
- **Reduction to a normal form is unavailable.** Global dimension is not invariant under Morita-type constructions that simplify $HH_\bullet$, and there is no known "minimal" singular algebra to which the general case reduces.

## 6. The Gap

Everything proved so far falls into two buckets: (i) classes with an explicit minimal bimodule resolution (monomial, graded, quantum complete intersections), and (ii) closure results propagating the conjecture along extensions $B\subseteq A$ that are *bounded*. The gap is precisely the algebras reachable by neither route: a general $A = kQ/I$ with non-homogeneous, non-monomial relations that is not a bounded extension of any algebra already in $\mathcal{H}$.

Concretely, the missing step is a **lower bound on $\operatorname{hhd}$ from singularity**: produce, from the non-vanishing of the singularity category $D_{sg}(A)$, an infinite family of degrees $n$ with a nonzero Hochschild homology class. No mechanism currently converts "$A$ has a module of infinite projective dimension" into "$HH_n(A)\neq 0$ for infinitely many $n$". A plausible intermediate target is the associated graded algebra: show $\operatorname{gr} A \in \mathcal{H}$ implies $A \in \mathcal{H}$, which would immediately settle all algebras whose radical filtration has monomial associated graded — a very large class.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **CLMS extension programme** (Cibils, Montpellier; Lanzilotta, Montevideo; Marcos, São Paulo; Solotar, Buenos Aires). Continued development of Jacobi–Zariski nearly exact sequences to widen the class of extensions preserving $\mathcal{H}$, and to relative/Hochschild-homology of $B$-$A$ pairs. Recent work targets extensions where $A/B$ is only *filtered* projective *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Singularity-category reformulations.** Attempts (Trondheim: Bergh; Bielefeld/Stuttgart schools) to phrase the conjecture as the non-vanishing of a stable or Tate version of Hochschild homology, using Keller's singular Hochschild cohomology and its $B_\infty$-structure.
- **Quantum and Nakayama families.** Explicit computations for Brauer graph algebras, Nakayama algebras with arbitrary relations, and skew group algebras, each confirming the conjecture in new cases.
- **Cyclic homology approach.** Since $\operatorname{gl.dim}A<\infty$ forces $HC_n(A)\cong HC_n(A/\operatorname{rad}A)$ in high degrees, some groups study whether the failure of this isomorphism can be forced directly, bypassing $HH_\bullet$ *(frontier — verify)*.

## 8. Future Work

- Prove the graded-degeneration step: $\operatorname{gr}A\in\mathcal H \Rightarrow A\in\mathcal H$, via a spectral sequence for the radical filtration on $C_\bullet(A)$.
- Settle self-injective algebras in general; the periodicity of many such algebras suggests $HH_n$ should be eventually periodic and nonzero.
- Establish the conjecture for algebras satisfying (Fg), where support varieties are available, as a large test class.
- Develop a Tate–Hochschild homology $\widehat{HH}_\bullet$ whose non-vanishing is equivalent to $D_{sg}(A)\neq 0$ by construction, then compare with $HH_\bullet$ in positive degrees.
- Machine-assisted search for a counterexample among algebras of dimension $\le 10$ with non-monomial relations, computing $HH_n$ up to $n\approx 20$.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take $A = k[x]/(x^2)$ with $\operatorname{char} k \neq 2$. Then $\operatorname{gl.dim}A = \infty$: the module $k = A/(x)$ has the infinite minimal resolution $\cdots \to A \xrightarrow{x} A \xrightarrow{x} A \to k \to 0$.

$A^e = A\otimes_k A \cong k[x,y]/(x^2,y^2)$, and $A$ admits the $2$-periodic minimal bimodule resolution

$$\cdots \xrightarrow{\ v\ } A^e \xrightarrow{\ u\ } A^e \xrightarrow{\ v\ } A^e \xrightarrow{\ \mu\ } A \to 0, \qquad u = x\otimes 1 - 1\otimes x, \quad v = x\otimes 1 + 1\otimes x,$$

with $\mu$ the multiplication map. Applying $A\otimes_{A^e} -$ replaces each $A^e$ by $A$, and the maps become multiplication by $x - x = 0$ and by $x + x = 2x$ alternately:

$$\cdots \xrightarrow{\ 0\ } A \xrightarrow{\ 2x\ } A \xrightarrow{\ 0\ } A \xrightarrow{\ 2x\ } A .$$

Since $2\in k^\times$: for $n$ odd, $HH_n(A) = \ker(0)/\operatorname{im}(2x) = A/xA \cong k$; for $n$ even $\ge 2$, $HH_n(A) = \ker(2x)/\operatorname{im}(0) = \operatorname{ann}_A(x) = xA \cong k$. Also $HH_0(A) = A$. So

$$\dim_k HH_n(A) = \begin{cases} 2, & n = 0,\\ 1, & n \ge 1,\end{cases}$$

never zero: $\operatorname{hhd}(A) = \infty$, exactly as Han's conjecture requires. (In characteristic $2$ both maps vanish and $HH_n(A) = A$ for all $n$, dimension $2$.)

Contrast this with the BGMS algebra $\Lambda_q = k\langle x,y\rangle/(x^2,y^2,xy+qyx)$, $q$ not a root of unity. Here $\dim_k \Lambda_q = 4$ with basis $\{1,x,y,xy\}$, and $\operatorname{gl.dim}\Lambda_q = \infty$ (the simple module has infinite projective dimension). Its Hochschild *cohomology* is $HH^n(\Lambda_q)=0$ for $n\ge3$ — the failure of Happel's question — because the relevant $\operatorname{Ext}$-cocycles are killed by the $q$-commutation, $q^m \neq 1$ for all $m\ge1$. But the corresponding $\operatorname{Tor}$-cycles survive: $HH_n(\Lambda_q)\neq 0$ for every $n\ge 0$ (Han 2006; Bergh–Erdmann 2008). This pair — cohomology blind, homology sharp — is the whole motivation for stating the conjecture in homology.
