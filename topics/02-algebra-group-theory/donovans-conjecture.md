# Donovan's Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/donovans-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Donovan's Conjecture is a central finiteness postulate in the modular representation theory of finite groups. It asserts that the structural complexity of block algebras of finite groups is universally bounded by the size and structure of their local defect groups.

**Donovan's Conjecture:** Let $p$ be a prime, $k$ an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$, and $D$ a finite $p$-group. There exist only finitely many Morita equivalence classes of block algebras $B$ of finite group algebras $kG$ (where $G$ ranges over all finite groups) such that the defect group of $B$ is isomorphic to $D$.

A corollary to this, often called the **Weak Donovan Conjecture**, states: 
For a given finite $p$-group $D$, there exists an integer $c(D)$ such that for any finite group $G$ and any block $B$ of $kG$ with defect group isomorphic to $D$, the entries of the Cartan matrix $C_B$ are bounded by $c(D)$. 

Because two Morita equivalent finite-dimensional algebras possess identical Cartan matrices (up to permutation of the simple modules), the full Donovan's Conjecture immediately implies the Weak Donovan Conjecture. A complete proof requires establishing this finiteness without arbitrary restrictions on the global structure of $G$.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

The conjecture sits at the intersection of finite group theory, representation theory, and homological algebra. Let $p$ be a prime. We work over a $p$-modular system $(K, \mathcal{O}, k)$ where $\mathcal{O}$ is a complete discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal $J(\mathcal{O})$, fraction field $K$ of characteristic zero, and algebraically closed residue field $k = \mathcal{O} / J(\mathcal{O})$ of characteristic $p$.

For a finite group $G$, the group algebra $kG$ decomposes into a direct sum of indecomposable two-sided ideals, known as **blocks** or block algebras:
$$ kG = B_1 \oplus B_2 \oplus \dots \oplus B_n $$
Each block $B_i$ is generated by a primitive central idempotent $e_i \in Z(kG)$. 

The **defect group** of a block $B = kG e$ is defined as the minimal subgroup $D \le G$ such that the projection $kG \to B$ factors through the relative trace map:
$$ \text{Tr}_D^G : (kG)^D \to (kG)^G, \quad \text{Tr}_D^G(x) = \sum_{g \in G/D} g x g^{-1} $$
where $(kG)^D$ denotes the fixed points under conjugation. By Rosenberg's Lemma, $D$ is a $p$-subgroup of $G$ and is unique up to $G$-conjugacy. The order $|D| = p^d$ determines the defect $d$ of the block.

Two block algebras $B$ and $B'$ (possibly belonging to different groups $G$ and $G'$) are **Morita equivalent** if their categories of finitely generated left modules $\text{Mod}(B)$ and $\text{Mod}(B')$ are $k$-linearly equivalent. Equivalently, $B' \cong \text{End}_B(P)^{\text{op}}$ for some progenerator $P \in \text{Mod}(B)$. For finite-dimensional $k$-algebras, Morita equivalence implies the algebras share the same basic algebra $\mathcal{B}$, defined as $\text{End}_B(\bigoplus_{i=1}^l P_i)^{\text{op}}$ where the $P_i$ are representatives of the isomorphism classes of indecomposable projective $B$-modules.

The **Cartan matrix** $C_B = (c_{ij})$ of $B$ is an $l \times l$ integer matrix where $c_{ij}$ is the multiplicity of the simple module $S_i$ as a composition factor of the projective cover $P_j$ of $S_j$. Since Morita equivalence preserves the basic algebra, it preserves the Cartan matrix, limiting the entries $c_{ij}$ to a finite set of possibilities if the Morita classes are finite.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

Donovan's Conjecture emerged in the late 1970s, formulated by Peter Donovan during an era when representation theorists were discovering profound connections between the local structure of a group (the normalizers of its $p$-subgroups) and the global representation theory of $G$. The conjecture was first widely popularized by J. L. Alperin. 

Historically, the conjecture provided a framework to bound the number of irreducible Brauer characters $l(B)$ and ordinary characters $k(B)$. Brauer's Problem 21 (1954) asked whether $k(B) \le |D|$. Since the number of simple modules $l(B)$ is an invariant of the Morita equivalence class, Donovan's Conjecture guarantees that for a fixed $D$, $l(B)$ is universally bounded.

The state of the art relies heavily on the Classification of Finite Simple Groups (CFSG). Using CFSG, deep reduction theorems by Külshammer, Puig, Kessar, and Linckelmann have reduced the general conjecture to verifying the finiteness of Morita classes for blocks of quasi-simple groups, combined with structural control over central extensions and twisted group algebras. As of 2026, the conjecture is fully solved for specific structural classes of defect groups (cyclic, tame, abelian $2$-groups) but remains open for general non-abelian $p$-groups and odd primes.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

The conjecture has been rigorously proven in the following regimes:

- **Cyclic Defect Groups:** Proven by Linckelmann (1996), building on work by Dade and Michler. If $D \cong C_{p^n}$, the basic algebra of the block is a Brauer tree algebra. The number of edges in the Brauer tree divides $p-1$, and the multiplicity of the exceptional vertex is bounded by $p^n$. Hence, there are strictly finitely many Morita equivalence classes.
- **Tame Representation Type:** For $p=2$, when $D$ is dihedral, semidihedral, or generalized quaternion, Erdmann (1990) classified the blocks up to Morita equivalence using quiver presentations and relations, effectively proving Donovan's Conjecture for these groups.
- **Symmetric and Alternating Groups:** Proven by Scopes (1991). By deploying translation functors (now known as Scopes equivalences), she showed that for a given $p$-weight $w$ (which strictly determines the defect group), there are only finitely many Morita equivalence classes of blocks across all symmetric groups $S_n$.
- **General Linear Groups:** Proven by Bonnafé and Rouquier (2003) for $GL_n(q)$ in non-defining characteristic using properties of Deligne-Lusztig varieties to establish Morita equivalences.
- **Abelian $2$-groups:** Proven unconditionally by Eaton, Kessar, Linckelmann, and Robinson (2014) for any block whose defect group is an abelian $2$-group.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

The conjecture remains open due to three principal bottlenecks:

**1. Wild Representation Type:** For defect groups of $p$-rank $\ge 2$ ($p$ odd) or $p$-rank $\ge 3$ ($p=2$), block algebras possess wild representation type. This implies that their module categories contain the problem of classifying pairs of non-commuting matrices up to simultaneous similarity. Because one cannot classify the indecomposable projective modules explicitly, constructing the basic algebra via quiver relations to verify finiteness is impossible. Cohomological bounds are required, but general bounds invariant under Morita equivalence (like higher Hochschild cohomology dimensions) are currently too loose.

**2. Clifford Theory over Crossed Products:** The reduction of the conjecture to simple groups requires analyzing a block $b$ of a normal subgroup $N \lhd G$ covered by a block $B$ of $G$. Clifford theory yields that $B$ is Morita equivalent to a twisted group algebra $k_{\alpha} [G_b/N]$ where $G_b$ is the inertia group and $\alpha \in H^2(G_b/N, k^\times)$ is a Külshammer-Puig cocycle. Bounding the invariants of this crossed product requires universally bounding the orbits of the outer automorphism groups of finite simple groups on their blocks, which introduces severe combinatorial explosions.

**3. Cross-Characteristic Lie Type Groups:** For groups of Lie type defined over a field of characteristic $r \neq p$, blocks can have extremely complex defect groups. Bounding the Morita classes requires bridging the geometric cohomology of Deligne-Lusztig varieties with the purely algebraic invariants of the block algebras, which currently lacks a unified functorial framework outside of the general linear group.

## 6. The Gap

The precise mathematical barrier lies in extending the established Morita equivalence bounds from **isolated blocks of quasi-simple groups** to **arbitrary group extensions**. 

To cross this gap, one must prove the conjecture for blocks of exceptional groups of Lie type ($E_6, E_7, E_8, F_4$) in cross-characteristic, and subsequently prove a strong lifting theorem. The lifting theorem must show that if Donovan's conjecture holds for a block $b$ of a quasi-simple group $N$, then it strictly holds for any block $B$ of a group $G \triangleright N$ covering $b$. Currently, the Külshammer-Puig classes $\alpha$ obstruct this lift because the dimensions of the basic algebras can inflate unboundedly if the $p$-part of the outer automorphism group is not strictly constrained by the defect group $D$ of $B$.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

Active research primarily targets abelian defect groups for odd primes and the utilization of higher categorical structures. 

- **Perverse Equivalences:** Researchers at City St George's (University of London) and the University of Oxford are using Chuang and Rouquier's theory of perverse equivalences to systematically construct derived equivalences between blocks of cross-characteristic Lie type groups, aiming to bound the Morita classes by stratifying the derived categories.
- **Local-Global Connections:** A major school of thought aims to solve Donovan's Conjecture by proving Broué's Abelian Defect Group Conjecture functorially. If a block $B$ is derived equivalent to its Brauer correspondent $b$ in $N_G(D)$ via a splendid tilting complex that is equivariant under automorphisms, Donovan's conjecture for abelian defect groups would reduce purely to local block algebras, which are much easier to bound. 
- *(frontier — verify)*: Recent preprints suggest that higher categorical actions ($SL_2$-categorifications) on the module categories of exceptional groups of Lie type may constrain the Cartan matrices directly, potentially yielding a proof of the Weak Donovan Conjecture for abelian defect groups without requiring a full Morita classification.

## 8. Future Work

Leading modular representation theorists suggest the following immediate pathways:
1. Complete the proof of Donovan's Conjecture for all defect groups of the form $C_p \times C_p$ for odd $p$.
2. Develop new numerical invariants for wild block algebras (such as bounding the dimensions of Hochschild cohomology groups $HH^n(B)$ solely in terms of $|D|$) to cleanly separate infinite families of wild algebras.
3. Establish a generalized reduction theorem that absorbs the Külshammer-Puig cocycles, proving that Donovan's Conjecture holds if and only if it holds for the principal blocks of finite simple groups.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Consider Donovan's conjecture for the symmetric groups $S_n$ at the prime $p=2$, specifically for the defect group $D = C_2$ (the cyclic group of order 2).

In the representation theory of symmetric groups, the defect group of a block is determined by its $p$-weight $w$. A block of weight $w=1$ for $p=2$ has defect group $C_2$. The blocks are indexed by a $2$-core partition $\kappa$. The symmetric group containing a block with 2-core $\kappa$ and weight 1 is $S_n$ where $n = 2(1) + |\kappa|$.

Let us look at the first two 2-cores: $\kappa_1 = \emptyset$ (size 0) and $\kappa_2 = (1)$ (size 1).

1. **For $\kappa_1 = \emptyset$:** The group is $S_{2(1)+0} = S_2$. The principal block $B_0(S_2)$ has weight 1. The simple modules over a field $k$ of characteristic 2 correspond to 2-regular partitions. For $S_2$, there is only one 2-regular partition: $(2)$, corresponding to the trivial module $k$. The projective cover $P_k$ is the regular module $kS_2$, which has dimension 2. The basic algebra is isomorphic to $k[X]/(X^2)$, and the Cartan matrix is the $1 \times 1$ matrix $C = (2)$.

2. **For $\kappa_2 = (1)$:** The group is $S_{2(1)+1} = S_3$. The block $B_1(S_3)$ corresponding to the core $(1)$ has weight 1. The irreducible ordinary characters in this block correspond to partitions $(3)$ and $(1,1,1)$. In characteristic 2, both reduce to the trivial module. Thus, $B_1(S_3)$ also contains exactly one simple module. Its projective cover has dimension 2, yielding a basic algebra $k[X]/(X^2)$ and Cartan matrix $C = (2)$.

By Scopes' equivalence, the block $B_0(S_2)$ and the block $B_1(S_3)$ are strictly Morita equivalent. In fact, Scopes proved that *all* blocks of *all* symmetric groups with $p=2$ and weight $w=1$ form a single Morita equivalence class. 

Therefore, for the defect group $D = C_2$, the number of Morita equivalence classes of blocks of symmetric groups is exactly **1**. Since 1 is a finite number, Donovan's Conjecture holds concretely in this regime, bounding the Cartan invariant universally at $c(C_2) = 2$.
