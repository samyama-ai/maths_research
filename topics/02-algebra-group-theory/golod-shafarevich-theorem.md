# Golod-Shafarevich Theorem

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/golod-shafarevich-theorem` · **Status:** solved-recently

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

The **Golod–Shafarevich theorem** (1964) is a counting inequality: an associative algebra, or a pro-$p$ group, presented with too few relations relative to its number of generators cannot be finite-dimensional (resp. finite).

**Algebra form.** Let $K$ be a field, $A = K\langle x_1,\dots,x_d\rangle$ the free associative algebra, and $I \subseteq A$ a two-sided ideal generated by homogeneous elements, $r_i$ of them in degree $i$, with $r_1 = 0$. If the power series $1 - dt + \sum_{i\ge 2} r_i t^i$ takes a negative value at some $t_0 \in (0,1)$, then $B = A/I$ is infinite-dimensional over $K$.

**Group form.** Let $G$ be a finite $p$-group, $d = \dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} H^1(G,\mathbb{F}_p)$ its minimal number of generators, $r = \dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} H^2(G,\mathbb{F}_p)$ its minimal number of relations as a pro-$p$ group. Then
$$ r > \tfrac{d^2}{4}. $$
Contrapositive: a pro-$p$ group with $r \le d^2/4$ is infinite.

The theorem itself is proved and not in dispute. What remains open is the cluster of questions it opened, in particular:

1. **Sharpness of the constant $1/4$.** What is the exact minimum of $r$ over finite $p$-groups with $d$ generators? The gap between the Golod–Shafarevich lower bound $d^2/4$ and the best known constructions is not closed.
2. **Non-amenability.** Is every Golod–Shafarevich group non-amenable? Open (Zelmanov's question).
3. **Golod–Shafarevich towers.** Which number fields have infinite $p$-class towers, and what is the true infimum of root discriminants of infinite towers?

A complete resolution of (1) means an exact formula or matching asymptotics for $\min\{r : \exists$ finite $p$-group with invariants $(d,r)\}$; of (2), either a proof of non-amenability or an amenable Golod–Shafarevich group.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Hilbert series.** For a graded algebra $B = \bigoplus_{n\ge 0} B_n$ with $\dim B_n = b_n < \infty$, set $H_B(t) = \sum_{n\ge 0} b_n t^n$. Write $f \succeq g$ for two series when every coefficient of $f - g$ is $\ge 0$.

**Golod–Shafarevich inequality (graded form).** With $A$, $I$, $B$ as above and $R(t) = \sum_{i\ge 2} r_i t^i$,
$$ H_B(t)\,\bigl(1 - dt + R(t)\bigr) \;\succeq\; 1 . $$
Proof sketch: filter $I$ by the degrees of the generating relations and compare dimensions degree by degree using the free resolution $A^{r} \to A^{d} \to A \to B \to 0$ in low degrees; the inequality is the resulting Euler-characteristic estimate.

**Deduction of infinite-dimensionality.** If $\dim_K B < \infty$ then $H_B(t)$ is a polynomial with nonnegative coefficients, so $H_B(t_0) > 0$ for $t_0 \in (0,1)$. Evaluating at any $t_0 \in (0,1)$ with $1 - dt_0 + R(t_0) < 0$ gives $H_B(t_0)(1-dt_0+R(t_0)) < 0$, contradicting $\succeq 1$. Hence $B$ is infinite-dimensional.

**The $d^2/4$ corollary.** If all relations are quadratic, $R(t) = rt^2$, then $\min_{t} (1 - dt + rt^2) = 1 - d^2/(4r)$, attained at $t = d/(2r)$, which lies in $(0,1)$ when $r > d/2$. So $r < d^2/4$ forces $B$ infinite-dimensional.

**Group-theoretic transfer.** For a pro-$p$ group $G$ with minimal presentation $1 \to R \to F \to G \to 1$ ($F$ free pro-$p$ of rank $d$), the completed group algebra $\mathbb{F}_p[[G]]$ has an augmentation ideal $\mathfrak{m}$; the graded algebra $\mathrm{gr}\,\mathbb{F}_p[[G]] = \bigoplus \mathfrak{m}^n/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}$ is a quotient of $\mathbb{F}_p\langle x_1,\dots,x_d\rangle$ by an ideal generated by $r = \dim H^2(G,\mathbb{F}_p)$ homogeneous relations of degree $\ge 2$ (the initial forms of the relators, whose degrees are their **weights**). $G$ finite $\iff$ $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} \mathbb{F}_p[[G]] < \infty$. Hence the algebra statement yields the group statement.

**Weighted refinement (Vinberg 1965; Ershov–Jaikin-Zapirain).** If relator $f_j$ has weight $w_j \ge 2$, $G$ is infinite whenever
$$ \exists\, t_0 \in (0,1): \quad 1 - dt_0 + \sum_{j=1}^{r} t_0^{\,w_j} \;<\; 0 . $$
A group admitting such a presentation for some prime $p$ is called a **Golod–Shafarevich group** (with respect to $p$); the definition extends to abstract groups via the Zassenhaus/lower $p$-central filtration.

**Class-field-tower criterion.** For a number field $k$ with $r_1$ real and $r_2$ complex places, the Galois group of the maximal unramified $p$-extension $G = \mathrm{Gal}(k_\infty^{(p)}/k)$ satisfies $d = \dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} \mathrm{Cl}(k)/p$ and $r \le d + r_1 + r_2$ (Shafarevich). Combined with the inequality, the $p$-class tower is infinite if
$$ d \;\ge\; 2 + 2\sqrt{\,r_1 + r_2 + 1\,}. $$

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1964.** E. S. Golod and I. R. Shafarevich, *On the class field tower*, prove the inequality and settle the class field tower problem negatively: there exist number fields with infinite class field tower, so not every ideal becomes principal in a finite unramified extension.
- **1964.** Golod, *On nil-algebras and finitely approximable $p$-groups*, uses the same counting to build a finitely generated infinite-dimensional nil algebra — a counterexample to the **Kurosh problem** — and from its adjoint group a finitely generated infinite periodic group, a counterexample to the **general Burnside problem**.
- **1965.** E. B. Vinberg gives a shorter proof and the weighted form; Roquette's exposition in Cassels–Fröhlich (1967) makes the tower application standard.
- **1978.** J. Martinet uses Golod–Shafarevich towers to produce infinite towers of root discriminant $\approx 92.368$, giving upper bounds for the limit infimum of root discriminants.
- **2000.** E. Zelmanov proves every Golod–Shafarevich **pro-$p$** group contains a nonabelian free pro-$p$ subgroup — the strongest structural theorem in the pro-$p$ setting.
- **2001.** F. Hajir and C. Maire use tamely ramified towers to lower the record root discriminant to $\approx 82.2$.
- **2008–2013.** M. Ershov shows Golod–Shafarevich groups can have Kazhdan's property (T), so they need not be *large* (no finite-index subgroup surjects onto $F_2$), answering a question of Zelmanov. Ershov–Jaikin-Zapirain develop the *positive weighted deficiency* framework, yielding golden-ratio-type torsion and residual finiteness results.
- **2012.** Ershov's survey consolidates the abstract-group theory and lists non-amenability as the central open question.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **$d = 2$: the bound is sharp.** $r > d^2/4 = 1$ forces $r \ge 2$, and $Q_8$ (quaternion group of order 8) has $d = 2$, $r = 2$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$. So the minimum is attained.
- **$d = 3$:** $r > 9/4$ gives $r \ge 3$; finite $3$-generator $p$-groups with $r = 3$ exist (e.g. elementary abelian rank-3 groups have $r = 6$, but smaller-$r$ examples occur among groups of maximal class), so the bound is attained or near-attained in low rank.
- **Asymptotics.** Constructions of finite $p$-groups with $r = \tfrac{d^2}{4}(1+o(1))$ are known (Wisliceny; Ershov–Jaikin-Zapirain via weighted deficiency), so the constant $1/4$ is asymptotically correct; the second-order term is not pinned down.
- **Pro-$p$ case fully resolved structurally** (Zelmanov 2000): free pro-$p$ subgroups always exist.
- **Torsion groups.** Golod's construction gives, for each $d \ge 3$ and each prime $p$, a $d$-generated infinite residually finite $p$-group with every $\le (d-2)$-generated subgroup finite.
- **Number fields.** Infinite $p$-class towers verified for explicit fields, e.g. $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-30030})$ at $p=2$ (six ramified primes, $2$-rank $5 \ge 2+2\sqrt{2}$); infinite towers known for infinitely many imaginary quadratic fields and for all quadratic fields with sufficiently many ramified primes.
- **Property (T) examples** (Ershov 2008): explicit Golod–Shafarevich groups inside Kac–Moody lattices with (T), so non-largeness is settled.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **The inequality is one-sided and lossy.** $H_B(t)(1-dt+R(t)) \succeq 1$ discards all information about *which* relations are used; it only counts them. Any construction of small-$r$ finite $p$-groups must exploit cancellation the inequality cannot see, so improving the constant from below requires a genuinely new invariant, not a sharper power-series estimate.
- **No cohomological obstruction beyond degree 2.** The method sees $H^1$ and $H^2$ only. Finite $p$-groups near the boundary have complicated $H^3$, and no usable Euler-characteristic bound incorporating $H^3$ is known.
- **Amenability is not visible to the filtration.** Golod–Shafarevich is a statement about $\mathrm{gr}\,\mathbb{F}_p[[G]]$, an associated graded object; amenability is a property of the group's Følner structure, invisible after passing to the graded algebra. Zelmanov's free pro-$p$ subgroup does not lift to an abstract free subgroup, and property (T) examples rule out proving non-amenability by exhibiting largeness.
- **Characteristic $p$ rigidity.** The nil algebras Golod constructs are not finitely presented and have no nice basis; Gröbner-basis or Anick-resolution methods degenerate because the ideals are not generated in bounded degree.
- **Tower discriminants.** Lowering root-discriminant records needs towers with more ramification but controlled $r$; the Shafarevich bound $r \le d + r_1 + r_2$ is nearly tight, leaving little slack.

## 6. The Gap

Precisely:

- **Constant.** Proven: $r > d^2/4$ for all finite $p$-groups; constructions give $r = d^2/4 + O(d)$. Unproven: the exact value of $\min r$ as a function of $d$, i.e. the correct linear and lower-order correction terms. The barrier is that both the lower bound (power series) and the upper bound (explicit presentations) are optimal within their own methods.
- **Amenability.** Proven: pro-$p$ Golod–Shafarevich groups contain free pro-$p$ subgroups; abstract ones may have (T). Unproven: that abstract Golod–Shafarevich groups are non-amenable, or even that they have exponential subgroup growth in every finite-index subgroup. The missing step is a transfer from the graded/pro-$p$ world back to the abstract group — every known bridge (largeness, free subgroups, uniform exponential growth) has been shown either false or unattainable by current tools.
- **Towers.** Proven: infinite towers with root discriminant $< 82.3$. Unproven: whether the Golod–Shafarevich mechanism can reach the Odlyzko-type analytic lower bounds ($\approx 44.7$ under GRH for totally imaginary fields), leaving a factor-$\approx 1.8$ gap.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Weighted deficiency and generalized Golod–Shafarevich algebras** — Ershov (Virginia) and Jaikin-Zapirain (ICMAT/UAM Madrid) continue the program relating positive weighted deficiency to residual properties, subgroup growth, and $\ell^2$-invariants of Golod–Shafarevich groups.
- **Non-amenability via cost and $\ell^2$-Betti numbers** — attempts to show $\beta_1^{(2)}(G) > 0$ or positive rank gradient for Golod–Shafarevich groups; property (T) examples force $\beta_1^{(2)} = 0$, so the approach must be restricted to sub-classes. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Nil algebra structure** — Bell, Small, Smoktunowicz and collaborators study growth of Golod-type nil algebras, including whether finitely generated nil algebras of subquadratic Gelfand–Kirillov dimension exist.
- **Galois-theoretic side** — Hajir, Maire, and collaborators combine tame ramification with Golod–Shafarevich to push asymptotically good towers; interaction with the Fontaine–Mazur conjecture and Boston's conjectures on $p$-adic analytic Galois groups is active. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Computational** — systematic $p$-group generation (ANUPQ / GAP, Magma) to search for finite $p$-groups with record-small $r/d^2$ at $d \le 8$.

## 8. Future Work

- Find a degree-3 correction to the Golod–Shafarevich inequality using $H^3(G,\mathbb{F}_p)$ or the Massey-product structure on $H^*(G,\mathbb{F}_p)$, which would raise the constant $1/4$.
- Decide non-amenability for the special case of Golod's original torsion groups, where the presentation is explicit.
- Determine whether every Golod–Shafarevich group has a finite-index subgroup with infinite abelianization (weaker than largeness, not excluded by property (T)).
- Construct infinite $p$-class towers with root discriminant below 80, or prove a Golod–Shafarevich-specific lower bound.
- Settle whether Golod–Shafarevich groups can be finitely presented as abstract groups with the same $(d,r)$ data.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Setting.** $d = 3$ generators, $r = 2$ quadratic relations, $p$ arbitrary. Take $B = \mathbb{F}_p\langle x,y,z\rangle / I$ with $I$ generated by two homogeneous degree-2 elements, say $f_1 = xy - yx$ and $f_2 = z^2$.

**Step 1 — the test polynomial.**
$$ P(t) = 1 - dt + rt^2 = 1 - 3t + 2t^2 . $$

**Step 2 — minimize on $(0,1)$.** $P'(t) = -3 + 4t = 0$ at $t_0 = 3/4 \in (0,1)$, and
$$ P(3/4) = 1 - \tfrac94 + 2\cdot\tfrac{9}{16} = 1 - 2.25 + 1.125 = -0.125 < 0 . $$
Equivalently $1 - d^2/(4r) = 1 - 9/8 = -1/8$.

**Step 3 — contradiction.** Suppose $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} B = N < \infty$. Then $H_B(t) = \sum_{n=0}^{m} b_n t^n$ with all $b_n \ge 0$ and $b_0 = 1$, so $H_B(3/4) \ge 1 > 0$. But the Golod–Shafarevich inequality gives
$$ H_B(3/4)\cdot P(3/4) \;\ge\; 1 , $$
while the left side is $\le -0.125 \cdot 1 < 0$. Contradiction. Hence $B$ is infinite-dimensional, and any pro-$p$ group with $3$ generators and $2$ relations is infinite.

**Step 4 — the bound is not wasteful.** Compare $d = 2$, $r = 2$: $P(t) = 1 - 2t + 2t^2$, with minimum $P(1/2) = 1/2 > 0$. No contradiction arises — consistent with the existence of the finite group $Q_8 = \langle a,b \mid a^4, a^2b^{-2}, b^{-1}aba \rangle$, which as a pro-$2$ group has $d = \dim H^1(Q_8,\mathbb{F}_2) = 2$ and $r = \dim H^2(Q_8,\mathbb{F}_2) = 2$, exactly saturating $r > d^2/4 = 1$.

**Step 5 — number field instance.** Let $k = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-30030})$, $-30030 = -2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11\cdot13$. Genus theory gives $2$-rank of $\mathrm{Cl}(k)$ equal to $t-1 = 5$ where $t = 6$ is the number of ramified primes. Since $r_1 = 0$, $r_2 = 1$, the criterion requires $d \ge 2 + 2\sqrt{2} \approx 4.83$; here $d = 5$. Therefore the $2$-class tower of $k$ is infinite: $k$ has no finite unramified extension in which every ideal becomes principal.
