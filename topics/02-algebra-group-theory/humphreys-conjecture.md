# Humphreys' Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/humphreys-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $G$ be a connected simply connected semisimple algebraic group over an algebraically closed field $k$ of characteristic $p > 0$, with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, Coxeter number $h$, and first Frobenius kernel $G_1$. For each dominant weight $\lambda \in X_+$ let $T(\lambda)$ be the indecomposable tilting module of highest weight $\lambda$, and let
$$V_{G_1}(T(\lambda)) \subseteq \mathcal N = \{x \in \mathfrak g : x \text{ nilpotent}\}$$
be its $G_1$-support variety.

**Conjecture (Humphreys, 1995/97).** Assume $p \ge h$. Then $V_{G_1}(T(\lambda))$ is the Zariski closure of a single nilpotent $G$-orbit, and that orbit is the one attached by Lusztig's bijection to the two-sided Kazhdan–Lusztig cell of the affine Weyl group $W_{\mathrm{aff}}$ containing the alcove of $\lambda$:
$$V_{G_1}(T(\lambda)) \;=\; \overline{\mathcal O_{\mathbf c(\lambda)}} .$$

A complete proof requires establishing this equality for all $\lambda \in X_+$ and all $p \ge h$ in every type; a disproof requires one pair $(\lambda, p)$ with $p \ge h$ where the two closed conical $G$-stable subvarieties differ. Both inclusions are open in general: neither $\subseteq$ nor $\supseteq$ is known by a uniform argument.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Cohomology and support.** By Friedlander–Parshall (1986) and Andersen–Jantzen (1984), for $p \ge h$,
$$H^{\bullet}(G_1, k) \;\cong\; k[\mathcal N] \quad\text{(even degrees, } \deg = 2\text{)},$$
so $\operatorname{Spec} H^{2\bullet}(G_1,k) = \mathcal N$. For a finite-dimensional $G_1$-module $M$,
$$V_{G_1}(M) \;=\; \mathrm{supp}_{\mathcal N}\big(\operatorname{Ext}^\bullet_{G_1}(M,M)\big) \;=\; Z\big(\ker(H^{2\bullet}(G_1,k) \to \operatorname{Ext}^{\bullet}_{G_1}(M,M))\big).$$
Equivalently (rank-variety description, Friedlander–Parshall; Suslin–Friedlander–Bendel 1997 for general infinitesimal group schemes):
$$V_{G_1}(M) = \{0\} \cup \{x \in \mathcal N \setminus \{0\} : M|_{k[x]/(x^p)} \text{ is not free}\}.$$
Basic properties: $V_{G_1}(M) = \{0\}$ iff $M$ is $G_1$-projective; $V_{G_1}(M\otimes N)=V_{G_1}(M)\cap V_{G_1}(N)$; if $M$ is a $G$-module then $V_{G_1}(M)$ is closed, conical and $G$-stable, hence a union of nilpotent orbit closures.

**Tilting modules.** $T(\lambda)$ is the unique indecomposable module with a $\Delta$-filtration (Weyl modules $V(\mu)$) and a $\nabla$-filtration (induced modules $H^0(\mu)=\nabla(\mu)$) and highest weight $\lambda$ (Ringel, Donkin 1993). Tilting modules are closed under $\otimes$ and under Frobenius twist tensoring; $T((p-1)\rho)=\mathrm{St}$ is the Steinberg module, which is $G_1$-projective.

**Cells and orbits.** Let $W_{\mathrm{aff}} = W \ltimes p\mathbb Z\Phi^\vee$ act on alcoves; write $\lambda \in \widehat{A}$ for its alcove under the dot action. Lusztig (*Cells in affine Weyl groups IV*, 1989) constructed a bijection
$$\{\text{two-sided cells of } W_{\mathrm{aff}}\} \;\longleftrightarrow\; \{\text{nilpotent } G^\vee\text{-orbits}\},$$
with the lowest cell (containing the fundamental alcove) $\mapsto$ the regular orbit and the highest cell $\{e\}$-type cell $\mapsto \{0\}$, and with the $a$-function satisfying $a(\mathbf c) = \dim \mathcal B_{x}$, the dimension of the Springer fibre for $x \in \mathcal O_{\mathbf c}$. So the conjecture predicts
$$\dim V_{G_1}(T(\lambda)) = \dim \mathcal N - 2\,a(\mathbf c(\lambda)).$$

**Comparison data.** Nakano–Parshall–Vella (2002) computed the analogous invariants for costandard modules: for $p \ge h$ and $\lambda$ with stabiliser generated by $J \subseteq \Delta$,
$$V_{G_1}(H^0(\lambda)) = G \cdot \mathfrak u_J,$$
the Richardson orbit closure of the parabolic $P_J$. Tilting modules interpolate between these, and their supports are not additive in filtrations — only $V_{G_1}(T(\lambda)) \subseteq \bigcup_\mu V_{G_1}(\nabla(\mu))$ over the filtration subquotients.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1984–1997.** The support-variety machinery is built: Andersen–Jantzen and Friedlander–Parshall identify $H^\bullet(G_1,k)$ with $k[\mathcal N]$ for $p$ large; Suslin–Friedlander–Bendel give the general $\pi$-point/rank-variety theory.
- **1995/97.** J. E. Humphreys, in *Comparing modular representations of semisimple groups and their Lie algebras* (Modular Interfaces, Riverside 1995; AMS/IP Stud. Adv. Math. 4, 1997), states the conjecture after computing examples for $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ and $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ and noticing the match with Lusztig's cell–orbit correspondence. He restates it in his 1998 Bulletin AMS survey.
- **1998.** Ostrik formulates and partially proves the quantum-group analogue at an $\ell$-th root of unity, replacing $G_1$-cohomology by the cohomology of Lusztig's small quantum group $u_\zeta(\mathfrak g)$, whose spectrum is again $\mathcal N$ (Ginzburg–Kumar 1993).
- **2006.** Bezrukavnikov proves Ostrik's conjecture for $\ell > h$ (*Cohomology of tilting modules over quantum groups and t-structures on derived categories of coherent sheaves*, Invent. Math. 166). The proof runs through the exotic t-structure on $D^b\mathrm{Coh}(\widetilde{\mathcal N})$ and the affine braid group action — i.e. it is characteristic-zero geometry, and does not transfer to $G_1$.
- **2010.** Cooper computes support varieties of tilting modules in type $A$ by explicit Weyl-module filtration bookkeeping, confirming the conjecture in further small cases.
- **2018–2019.** Hardesty (Adv. Math. 329) computes $V_{G_1}(T(\lambda))$ in type $A$ for distinguished families of weights. Achar–Hardesty–Riche (*On the Humphreys conjecture on support varieties of tilting modules*, Transform. Groups 24, 2019) give the modern geometric treatment and argue that the correct general statement replaces Lusztig cells by **antispherical $p$-cells** defined via the $p$-canonical basis (Jensen–Williamson; Riche–Williamson, Astérisque 397, 2018). Their companion paper *Conjectures on tilting modules and antispherical $p$-cells* formulates this refinement.

**Status.** Open for $p \ge h$ in general. The quantum analogue is a theorem. For $p < h$ the statement is not even well-posed in its original form, and the $p$-cell version is the current working conjecture.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **$G = SL_2$, all $p \ge 2$:** complete. Two two-sided cells, two orbits; the calculation is in §10.
- **$G = SL_3$ ($p \ge 3$) and $SL_4$ ($p\ge 4$):** verified by Humphreys' original computations together with Cooper's type-$A$ analysis (J. Algebra 324, 2010).
- **Quantum group $u_\zeta(\mathfrak g)$, $\ell > h$, all types:** proved by Bezrukavnikov (2006).
- **Extreme cells, all types, $p \ge h$:** if $\lambda$ lies in the lowest $p$-alcove then $T(\lambda) = L(\lambda) = \nabla(\lambda)$ and $V_{G_1}(T(\lambda)) = \mathcal N$ (regular orbit closure), as predicted. If $\lambda \in \mathrm{St} + X_+$, i.e. $\lambda \ge (p-1)\rho$ within the Donkin region, $T(\lambda)$ is $G_1$-projective and $V_{G_1}(T(\lambda))=\{0\}$, matching the zero orbit.
- **Costandard/standard modules, $p \ge h$:** the exact answer $V_{G_1}(\nabla(\lambda)) = \overline{G\cdot \mathfrak u_J}$ (Nakano–Parshall–Vella 2002) supplies the sharp upper bound used in every verification.
- **Type $A$, subregular and near-extremal cells:** Hardesty (2018) computes $V_{G_1}(T(\lambda))$ for these families for $SL_n$ with $p \ge n$, agreeing with the conjecture.
- **$p$-cell version:** Achar–Hardesty–Riche verify their reformulation in low rank and show it agrees with the Lusztig-cell version when $p$ is large relative to the root system *(frontier — verify the precise bound in each type)*.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

1. **No character formula input.** Computing $V_{G_1}(T(\lambda))$ from a $\nabla$-filtration requires knowing which $\nabla(\mu)$ occur *and* how the extensions glue; support varieties are not additive over filtration steps, so multiplicities alone are insufficient. Tilting characters for $p < 2h-2$ are governed by the $p$-canonical basis, which is not combinatorially known.
2. **Lusztig's conjecture is false.** Williamson's counterexamples (2017) destroyed the assumption that the characteristic-zero KL combinatorics controls modular characters for $p \ge h$, removing the mechanism by which one hoped to import Bezrukavnikov's theorem.
3. **Geometric localisation degenerates.** Bezrukavnikov's proof uses the exotic t-structure on $D^b\mathrm{Coh}^{G\times \mathbb G_m}(\widetilde{\mathcal N})$ and Bezrukavnikov–Mirković–Rumynin localisation. In characteristic $p$ the relevant equivalence involves $\mathcal D$-modules on the Frobenius twist, and the tilting objects transported to the coherent side are not known to be the same as $T(\lambda)$ — the comparison map is only an equivalence after inverting factors that vanish when $p$ is small.
4. **Cohomology of $G_1$ is unknown for $p < h$.** The isomorphism $H^\bullet(G_1,k)\cong k[\mathcal N]$ fails; the support scheme can be non-reduced and need not be a union of orbit closures, so the conjecture's very statement must be replaced.
5. **Lower bounds are hard.** Showing $x \in V_{G_1}(T(\lambda))$ needs a non-free restriction to $k[x]/(x^p)$, i.e. control of Jordan types of nilpotent elements on $T(\lambda)$ — a problem with no known uniform technique beyond rank-one reductions.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: the quantum analogue in all types ($\ell > h$); the modular case for the two extreme cells; type $A$ in low rank and for special cell families; the exact answer for standard/costandard modules.

Missing: a characteristic-$p$ mechanism connecting the tilting module $T(\lambda)$ to the orbit $\mathcal O_{\mathbf c(\lambda)}$ that does not pass through Lusztig's character conjecture. Concretely, two steps remain:

- **Upper bound.** Show $V_{G_1}(T(\lambda)) \subseteq \overline{\mathcal O_{\mathbf c(\lambda)}}$, i.e. that $T(\lambda)$ becomes free on $k[x]/(x^p)$ for all $x$ outside the orbit closure. This should follow from a suitable "cell-filtration" of the tilting category, which exists conjecturally but has been constructed only in special cases.
- **Lower bound.** Show $\overline{\mathcal O_{\mathbf c(\lambda)}} \subseteq V_{G_1}(T(\lambda))$, which requires exhibiting non-projectivity along a specific orbit — currently only accessible via explicit Jordan-type computations.

Additionally, it is unresolved whether the correct combinatorial index is Lusztig's cell or the antispherical $p$-cell; the two agree in the known cases, and separating them for a specific $(\lambda,p)$ with $p \ge h$ would itself be a decisive result.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Geometric representation theory (Riche, Achar, Williamson, and collaborators; Clermont-Ferrand, LSU, Sydney/Bonn).** The programme replaces cells by $p$-cells, using the $p$-canonical basis and the modular Springer correspondence; the goal is a filtration of $\mathrm{Tilt}(G)$ by $p$-cell ideals whose associated graded is controlled by coherent sheaves on nilpotent orbit closures.
- **Exotic t-structures modulo $p$.** Work extending Bezrukavnikov–Riche's affine braid group action to $\mathrm{char}\, k = p$, aiming to identify $T(\lambda)$ with an exotic tilting sheaf on $\widetilde{\mathcal N}_k$ *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Support theory for Frobenius kernels beyond $G_1$.** Drupieski–Nakano and others study $G_r$-support and $\pi$-point supports, testing whether the orbit prediction has a height-$r$ analogue.
- **Computational verification.** Explicit tilting-character computations in types $A_3$–$A_5$, $B_2$, $G_2$ for small $p$, using $p$-canonical basis software (Jensen–Williamson's `IHecke`/related packages), continue to supply test data.

## 8. Future Work

- Construct the conjectural **cell filtration** of the tilting category directly and prove that its subquotients have support in the expected orbit closure — the route Humphreys himself advocated.
- Prove the **upper bound** independently via Nakano–Parshall–Vella-type parabolic induction: bound $V_{G_1}(T(\lambda))$ by intersecting the supports of the $\nabla(\mu)$ in *any* filtration, then show the intersection realises the cell orbit.
- Settle the **$p$-cell vs. Lusztig cell** question: find the smallest $(\text{type}, p)$ where antispherical $p$-cells differ from Lusztig cells with $p \ge h$, and compute the corresponding tilting support.
- Transfer Bezrukavnikov's theorem by **reduction mod $p$** of the quantum answer, controlling the failure of the comparison functor.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take $G = SL_2$, $\mathfrak g = \mathfrak{sl}_2$, $p \ge 2$, $h=2$. Here $\mathcal N = \{x \in \mathfrak{sl}_2 : \det x = 0,\ \operatorname{tr} x=0\}$ is the quadric cone $\{ \begin{psmallmatrix} a & b \\ c & -a\end{psmallmatrix} : a^2+bc=0\}$, of dimension $2$, with exactly two orbits: $\{0\}$ and the regular orbit $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{reg}}$, $\overline{\mathcal O_{\mathrm{reg}}} = \mathcal N$.

The affine Weyl group is $\widetilde A_1$, with two two-sided cells: the lowest cell (the fundamental alcove, weights $0 \le \lambda \le p-2$) and the complementary cell. Lusztig's bijection sends the lowest cell to $\mathcal O_{\mathrm{reg}}$ and the other cell to $\{0\}$. So the conjecture predicts
$$V_{G_1}(T(\lambda)) = \begin{cases} \mathcal N, & 0 \le \lambda \le p-2,\\[2pt] \{0\}, & \lambda \ge p-1 .\end{cases}$$

**Case $0 \le \lambda \le p-2$.** The weight lies in the lowest alcove, so $T(\lambda)=V(\lambda)=L(\lambda)$, the $(\lambda+1)$-dimensional module $S^\lambda(k^2)$. Let $e = \begin{psmallmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{psmallmatrix}$, a regular nilpotent. On the basis $x^\lambda, x^{\lambda-1}y,\dots,y^\lambda$, $e$ acts as $x\,\partial_y$, a single nilpotent Jordan block of size $\lambda+1 \le p-1$. A module over $k[e]/(e^p)$ is free iff all Jordan blocks have size exactly $p$; here the single block has size $<p$, so $L(\lambda)$ is not free. Hence $e \in V_{G_1}(L(\lambda))$, and since the support is closed, conical and $G$-stable, $V_{G_1}(T(\lambda)) = \overline{G\cdot e} = \mathcal N$. ✓

**Case $\lambda = p-1$.** $T(p-1) = L(p-1) = \mathrm{St}$, dimension $p$, on which $e$ acts as one Jordan block of size $p$ — free. So $\mathrm{St}$ is $G_1$-projective and $V_{G_1}(\mathrm{St})=\{0\}$. ✓

**Case $p \le \lambda \le 2p-2$.** By Donkin, $T(\lambda)$ is the $G_1$-projective cover $Q_1(2p-2-\lambda)$ of $L(2p-2-\lambda)$, so its support is $\{0\}$. ✓ For general $\lambda \ge p-1$, write $\lambda = \lambda_0 + p\lambda_1$ with $\lambda_0 \ge p-1$ forced in the Donkin region; then $T(\lambda) \cong T(\lambda_0) \otimes T(\lambda_1)^{[1]}$, and the Frobenius twist is $G_1$-trivial, so $V_{G_1}(T(\lambda)) = V_{G_1}(T(\lambda_0)) \cap \mathcal N = \{0\}$. ✓

Every case matches, so the conjecture holds for $SL_2$. The point of failure of this argument in higher rank is visible already at $SL_3$: there $T(\lambda)$ for $\lambda$ in the second alcove is a non-simple, non-projective self-dual module whose Jordan type at a subregular nilpotent cannot be read off from its Weyl filtration multiplicities, and the prediction $V_{G_1}(T(\lambda)) = \overline{\mathcal O_{\mathrm{subreg}}}$ (dimension $4$ inside $\dim\mathcal N = 6$) requires an explicit extension computation.
