# Herzog-Huneke-Srinivasan Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/herzog-huneke-srinivasan-conjecture` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Also called the **Multiplicity Conjecture**. Let $S = k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ with the standard grading, $k$ a field, and let $I \subset S$ be a homogeneous ideal of codimension $c = \operatorname{ht}(I)$. Write the minimal graded free resolution of $S/I$ as

$$0 \to F_p \to \cdots \to F_1 \to F_0 = S \to S/I \to 0, \qquad F_i = \bigoplus_{j} S(-j)^{\beta_{i,j}},$$

and set $m_i = \min\{ j : \beta_{i,j} \neq 0\}$, $M_i = \max\{ j : \beta_{i,j} \neq 0\}$ for $1 \le i \le c$. Let $e(S/I)$ be the multiplicity (degree) of $S/I$.

**Upper bound conjecture.** For every homogeneous $I$,
$$e(S/I) \;\le\; \frac{1}{c!}\prod_{i=1}^{c} M_i .$$

**Lower bound conjecture.** If in addition $S/I$ is Cohen–Macaulay (so $p = c$),
$$\frac{1}{c!}\prod_{i=1}^{c} m_i \;\le\; e(S/I) .$$

A complete resolution requires either a proof for all homogeneous $I$ over all fields, or an explicit ideal violating one of the inequalities. The lower bound is **known to fail** without the Cohen–Macaulay hypothesis (Section 10), so part of the problem is to identify the correct non-Cohen–Macaulay substitute. Secondary claims: equality in either bound should force the resolution to be *pure* ($\beta_{i,j}\neq 0$ for at most one $j$ per $i$), and analogous bounds should hold over ambient rings other than $S$.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Multiplicity.** For a graded module $M$ over $S$ with $\dim M = d$, the Hilbert polynomial satisfies $P_M(t) = \frac{e(M)}{(d-1)!}t^{d-1} + O(t^{d-2})$; $e(M) \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ is the multiplicity. Equivalently, writing the Hilbert series as $H_M(t) = K_M(t)/(1-t)^n$ with $K_M(t)=\sum_{i,j}(-1)^i \beta_{i,j}t^j$ the $K$-polynomial, one has $K_M^{(\ell)}(1)=0$ for $\ell < c$ and
$$e(S/I) = \frac{K_{S/I}^{(c)}(1)}{c!} = \frac{1}{c!}\sum_{i,j}(-1)^i \beta_{i,j}\, j^{\,\underline{c}},$$
so **multiplicity is a linear functional on the Betti table** $\beta(S/I) = (\beta_{i,j})$. This linearity is the pivot of the modern proof.

**Pure resolutions.** A resolution is pure of type $\mathbf{d} = (d_0 < d_1 < \cdots < d_c)$ if $F_i = S(-d_i)^{\beta_i}$. Huneke–Miller (1985): if $S/I$ is Cohen–Macaulay with pure resolution of type $\mathbf{d}$ with $d_0=0$, then
$$e(S/I) = \frac{1}{c!}\prod_{i=1}^{c} d_i ,$$
so both conjectured bounds are equalities. The conjecture asserts that the pure case is extremal among all Betti tables with the same $m_i, M_i$.

**Boij–Söderberg cone.** Let $B_{\mathbb{Q}}$ be the $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space of tables $(\beta_{i,j})$. For a strictly increasing degree sequence $\mathbf{d}$, the normalized pure diagram $\pi(\mathbf{d})$ has entries
$$\pi(\mathbf{d})_{i,d_i} = \prod_{j \neq i} \frac{1}{|d_j - d_i|},$$
and $\pi(\mathbf{d})_{i,j}=0$ otherwise. **Theorem (Eisenbud–Schreyer 2009).** The Betti table of any graded Cohen–Macaulay $S$-module lies in the rational cone generated by $\{\pi(\mathbf{d})\}$; concretely
$$\beta(M) = \sum_{t=1}^{r} c_t\, \pi(\mathbf{d}^{(t)}), \qquad c_t \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0},$$
with $\mathbf{d}^{(1)} < \cdots < \mathbf{d}^{(r)}$ a chain in the termwise partial order. Existence of modules realizing each $\pi(\mathbf{d})$ is Eisenbud–Fløystad–Weyman (char $0$, equivariant) and Eisenbud–Schreyer (all characteristics, via the pushforward of line bundles on $\mathbb{P}^1$ under the Beilinson-type "$\mathbb{P}^1$ trick"). The upper facet inequalities of the cone are supplied by the duality pairing with cohomology tables of vector bundles on $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$.

**Why decomposition implies the conjecture.** Normalize $\sum_t c_t \beta_0(\pi(\mathbf{d}^{(t)})) = 1$. Since $e$ is linear and equals $\frac{1}{c!}\prod_i d_i$ on each normalized pure summand, $e(S/I)$ is a convex combination of the numbers $\frac{1}{c!}\prod_i d^{(t)}_i$. Each $\mathbf{d}^{(t)}$ satisfies $m_i \le d_i^{(t)} \le M_i$ because the chain's minimum and maximum are bounded by the actual degrees occurring, giving both inequalities at once.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1985.** Huneke and Miller compute $e$ exactly for Cohen–Macaulay algebras with pure resolutions (*Canad. J. Math.* 37), establishing the equality case that the conjecture generalizes.
- **1998.** Herzog and Srinivasan, *Bounds for multiplicities* (*Trans. AMS* 350), state the conjecture in print and prove the first families. The attribution to Huneke reflects the conjecture's origin in Huneke–Srinivasan discussions during the 1990s; the name "Herzog–Huneke–Srinivasan conjecture" is standard in the subsequent literature.
- **2000–2007.** A large case-by-case industry: codimension 2, Gorenstein codimension 3, stable and squarefree strongly stable monomial ideals, componentwise linear ideals, edge ideals, some Stanley–Reisner rings of triangulated manifolds. Surveyed by Francisco–Srinivasan (2007).
- **2006.** Boij and Söderberg propose the conjecture that the cone of Cohen–Macaulay Betti tables is simplicial-fan-generated by pure diagrams, and show it implies the multiplicity conjecture (*J. LMS* 78, 2008).
- **2009.** Eisenbud and Schreyer prove the Boij–Söderberg conjectures (*JAMS* 22), settling the multiplicity conjecture for all Cohen–Macaulay graded modules in all characteristics.
- **2012.** Boij and Söderberg extend the decomposition to arbitrary graded modules and deduce the **upper** bound in general (*Algebra & Number Theory* 6).

State of the art: the two displayed inequalities of Section 1 are theorems. What remains open is everything around them — non-Cohen–Macaulay lower bounds, non-polynomial ambient rings, non-standard gradings, and the local (non-graded) analogue.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

Chronologically, before the general proof:

- **Pure resolutions, CM:** equality, all $c$ (Huneke–Miller 1985).
- **Complete intersections; quasi-pure resolutions** ($m_i \ge M_{i-1}$ for all $i$), including all CM ideals of codimension $\le 2$ and Gorenstein codimension $3$ (Herzog–Srinivasan 1998).
- **Stable, squarefree strongly stable, and $p$-Borel monomial ideals**; codimension-$2$ and codimension-$3$ Gorenstein cases without the CM hypothesis where meaningful (Herzog–Srinivasan 1998; Migliore–Nagel–Römer, *Math. Res. Lett.* 12 (2005): upper bound for all ideals of codimension $\le 2$ and for codimension-3 Gorenstein).
- **Componentwise linear ideals** and ideals with linear resolutions (Römer, *JPAA* 195 (2005)).
- **Equality forces purity:** Herzog–Zheng (*Collect. Math.* 57 (2006)) show that equality in either bound for CM $S/I$ implies the resolution is pure.
- **Computational verification:** exhaustive Macaulay2 checks over families of monomial ideals in $n \le 6$ variables and over random points in $\mathbb{P}^3$ (Francisco, *J. Algebra* 299 (2006)).

After 2009: **all graded Cohen–Macaulay modules over $S$, any $c \le n$, any field** (Eisenbud–Schreyer). After 2012: **the upper bound for all graded modules**, Cohen–Macaulay or not (Boij–Söderberg).

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **The lower bound is simply false in the non-CM case,** and no repaired statement is known. Depth deficiency lets $e$ drop while $m_i$ stays large: components of lower dimension contribute syzygies but nothing to the degree. Any fix must be sensitive to $\operatorname{depth}(S/I)$ and to the unmixed part of $I$, quantities the Betti table does not determine.
- **The Boij–Söderberg machinery is specific to $S$.** The proof needs (i) existence of pure modules for every degree sequence and (ii) a supply of dual functionals from cohomology tables of coherent sheaves on $\mathbb{P}^{n-1}$. Over a hypersurface ring, a complete intersection, or a general graded quotient, resolutions are infinite, "pure" needs redefinition, and the cone is no longer finitely generated by known pure objects. Berkesch–Burke–Erman–Gibbons (2012) resolve only embedding dimension $\le 2$ hypersurfaces.
- **Non-standard and multigradings break linearity of $e$ in $\beta$.** With $\deg x_i = a_i$, the multiplicity functional acquires denominators $\prod a_i$ and the pure-diagram normalization no longer matches the extremal products $\prod M_i$.
- **Local rings have no Betti table.** For $(R,\mathfrak{m})$ local non-graded, $m_i$ and $M_i$ are undefined; the associated graded ring can lose Cohen–Macaulayness, so the graded theorem does not transfer.
- Purely combinatorial or Hilbert-function-based arguments cannot work: the conjecture is false for tables satisfying all Hilbert-function constraints but not realized by actual modules, so realizability (a hard existence question) is intrinsic.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: both inequalities for graded Cohen–Macaulay modules over a standard-graded polynomial ring, and the upper bound in general. The residual gap has three precise components.

1. **Non-CM lower bound.** Find a function $L(\beta, \operatorname{depth})$ with $L \le e(S/I)$ specializing to $\frac{1}{c!}\prod m_i$ when $\operatorname{depth} S/I = \dim S/I$. Migliore–Nagel–Römer (*Trans. AMS* 360 (2008)) propose candidates using only the first $c$ steps and the unmixed part; these are verified in low codimension but unproved in general.
2. **Ambient generality.** Prove a Boij–Söderberg decomposition over quotient rings $S/J$ with $J$ a complete intersection, which would give multiplicity bounds for modules over such rings. Only $J$ a single quadric in $\le 2$ variables is done.
3. **Equality/rigidity.** Characterize, in the non-CM setting, when $e = \frac{1}{c!}\prod M_i$; the CM answer (purity, Herzog–Zheng) has no known analogue.

The single technical step blocking (1) and (2) is the construction of pure-type extremal objects and their dual cohomological functionals outside the polynomial-ring setting.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Boij–Söderberg over non-regular rings.** Groups at Michigan, Minnesota, and KTH continue the program of Eisenbud–Erman–Schreyer (*Filtering free resolutions*, *Compos. Math.* 149 (2013)), studying cones of Betti tables of modules over hypersurface and complete-intersection rings via matrix factorizations. Partial cone descriptions in embedding dimension 3 are reported *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Multigraded Boij–Söderberg theory** for $\mathbb{Z}^m$-graded modules over Cox rings of toric varieties, with multiplicity replaced by mixed multiplicities; virtual-resolution techniques are the main tool *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Combinatorial specializations.** Multiplicity bounds for Stanley–Reisner rings of simplicial complexes and for edge/binomial edge ideals, where $m_i, M_i$ have combinatorial readings, remain an active source of sharp cases.
- **Local analogues.** Work relating $e(R)$ to Betti numbers over local rings (Lech-type and Stillman-type bounds) is pursued as the non-graded shadow of the conjecture.

## 8. Future Work

- Formulate and test a depth-corrected lower bound; the natural candidate replaces $c$ by $\operatorname{codim}$ of the unmixed part and $m_i$ by degrees in the first $\operatorname{depth}$-many steps.
- Construct equivariant pure modules over complete-intersection rings (an analogue of Eisenbud–Fløystad–Weyman) to seed a decomposition theorem there.
- Extend the duality pairing between Betti tables and cohomology tables to sheaves on singular projective schemes.
- Derive effective consequences: the linear-functional viewpoint gives, for free, bounds on regularity, projective dimension, and Hilbert coefficients; systematizing which classical inequalities follow from cone membership is an open bookkeeping problem with real content.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Take $S = k[x,y]$ and $I = (x^2, xy, y^3)$.** Then $S/I$ is artinian, $c = 2$, Cohen–Macaulay.

*Multiplicity.* The monomials outside $I$ are $1, x, y, y^2$, so $e(S/I) = \dim_k S/I = 4$.

*Resolution.* Two minimal syzygies: $y\cdot x^2 - x\cdot(xy) = 0$ in degree $3$, and $y^2\cdot(xy) - x\cdot y^3 = 0$ in degree $4$. Hence
$$0 \to S(-3)\oplus S(-4) \to S(-2)^2\oplus S(-3) \to S \to S/I \to 0,$$
with Betti table (rows indexed by $j-i$)

```
       0  1  2
0:     1  .  .
1:     .  2  1
2:     .  1  1
```

So $m_1 = 2$, $M_1 = 3$, $m_2 = 3$, $M_2 = 4$, and the conjecture predicts
$$\tfrac{1}{2}(2\cdot 3) = 3 \;\le\; e(S/I)=4 \;\le\; \tfrac{1}{2}(3\cdot 4) = 6. \quad\checkmark$$

*Check via the multiplicity functional.* $\frac{1}{2!}\sum_{i,j}(-1)^i\beta_{i,j}j^2 = \frac12\big(-(2\cdot 4 + 1\cdot 9) + (1\cdot 9 + 1\cdot 16)\big) = \frac12(25-17) = 4$. $\checkmark$

*Boij–Söderberg decomposition.* The degree sequences present are $(0,2,3) \le (0,2,4) \le (0,3,4)$, with normalized pure diagrams (scaled to integers) $\pi(0,2,3)\sim(1,3,2)$, $\pi(0,2,4)\sim(1,2,1)$, $\pi(0,3,4)\sim(1,4,3)$. The greedy algorithm gives
$$\beta(S/I) = \tfrac12\,\pi(0,2,3) + \tfrac14\,\pi(0,2,4) + \tfrac14\,\pi(0,3,4),$$
checked entrywise: $\beta_{1,2} = \frac12\cdot 3 + \frac14\cdot 2 = 2$, $\beta_{1,3} = \frac14\cdot 4 = 1$, $\beta_{2,3} = \frac12\cdot 2 = 1$, $\beta_{2,4} = \frac14\cdot 1 + \frac14\cdot 3 = 1$, $\beta_{0,0}=\frac12+\frac14+\frac14=1$. The pure multiplicities are $\frac{2\cdot3}{2}=3$, $\frac{2\cdot4}{2}=4$, $\frac{3\cdot4}{2}=6$, and linearity gives
$$e = \tfrac12\cdot 3 + \tfrac14\cdot 4 + \tfrac14\cdot 6 = 4,$$
a convex combination lying between $3 = \frac{m_1m_2}{2}$ and $6 = \frac{M_1M_2}{2}$. This is the Eisenbud–Schreyer proof in miniature.

**Failure of the lower bound without Cohen–Macaulayness.** Let $J = (x^2, xy) = (x)\cap(x^2,y)$ in $k[x,y]$. Here $c = 1$, $\dim S/J = 1$, $\operatorname{depth} S/J = 0$, and the resolution is $0 \to S(-3) \to S(-2)^2 \to S$, so $m_1 = M_1 = 2$. The unique minimal prime of $J$ is $(x)$, of multiplicity $1$, so $e(S/J) = 1$. The upper bound $1 \le \frac{1}{1!}\cdot 2$ holds, but the lower bound would demand $2 \le 1$. The embedded component at $(x,y)$ contributes syzygies without contributing degree — exactly the mechanism described in Section 5.
