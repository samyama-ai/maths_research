# Casselman-Wallach Theorem

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/casselman-wallach-theorem` · **Status:** solved-recently

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $G$ be a real reductive Lie group of Harish-Chandra class with maximal compact subgroup $K$, Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}_0$ and complexification $\mathfrak{g}$. Harish-Chandra's algebraic linearization replaces a representation by its $(\mathfrak{g},K)$-module of $K$-finite vectors, discarding all topology. The problem: **can the topology be canonically restored?**

**Casselman–Wallach Theorem.** The functor
$$\Pi \;\longmapsto\; \Pi_{K\text{-fin}} \qquad (\text{$K$-finite vectors})$$
is an exact equivalence of categories
$$\mathcal{SF}(G) \;\xrightarrow{\;\sim\;}\; \mathcal{HC}(\mathfrak{g},K),$$
where $\mathcal{SF}(G)$ is the category of admissible smooth Fréchet representations of moderate growth (SF-representations) with continuous $G$-maps, and $\mathcal{HC}(\mathfrak{g},K)$ is the category of finite-length Harish-Chandra modules with $(\mathfrak{g},K)$-maps.

Concretely the statement bundles four assertions:

1. **Existence (globalization).** Every finite-length Harish-Chandra module $V$ admits an SF-globalization $V^\infty$ with $(V^\infty)_{K\text{-fin}}\cong V$.
2. **Uniqueness.** Any two SF-globalizations of $V$ are isomorphic as topological $G$-modules — the isomorphism extending $\mathrm{id}_V$ is unique.
3. **Automatic continuity.** Every $(\mathfrak{g},K)$-morphism $V\to W$ extends to a continuous $G$-morphism $V^\infty\to W^\infty$.
4. **Exactness.** $V\mapsto V^\infty$ is exact; in particular submodules globalize to *closed* subspaces and quotients carry the quotient topology (Hausdorff).

A complete proof must supply all four; a disproof would exhibit a finite-length $V$ with two non-isomorphic SF-globalizations, or a discontinuous $(\mathfrak{g},K)$-map between $K$-finite parts of SF-representations.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Smooth representations of moderate growth.** Fix a norm $\|g\|$ on $G$ arising from a faithful finite-dimensional representation (or the Harish-Chandra $\Xi$-function scale). A continuous representation $(\Pi,E)$ on a Fréchet space $E$ is *smooth* if $g\mapsto \Pi(g)v$ is $C^\infty$ for all $v$, and of *moderate growth* if for every continuous seminorm $p$ there are $N\ge 0$ and a continuous seminorm $q$ with
$$p\big(\Pi(g)v\big)\;\le\;\|g\|^{N}\,q(v)\qquad \text{for all } g\in G,\ v\in E .$$
Such $E$ is called an **SF-representation**; if $E$ is nuclear one writes SNF. The topology is generated by the Sobolev-type seminorms $p_u(v)=p(d\Pi(u)v)$, $u\in U(\mathfrak{g})$.

**Harish-Chandra module.** A $(\mathfrak{g},K)$-module $V$: a $U(\mathfrak{g})$-module with a locally finite $K$-action, compatible ($d(k\cdot)=\mathrm{Ad}$-equivariance, $k$-differential $=\mathfrak{k}$-action). It is *admissible* if $\dim \mathrm{Hom}_K(\sigma,V)<\infty$ for all $\sigma\in\hat K$, and *Harish-Chandra* if in addition finitely generated over $U(\mathfrak{g})$; equivalently (Harish-Chandra) it has finite length and is annihilated by a finite-codimension ideal of the center $Z(\mathfrak{g})$.

**Admissibility.** $\Pi$ is admissible if $\dim E^{\sigma} <\infty$ for $\sigma \in \hat K$, where $E^\sigma$ is the $\sigma$-isotypic part. Then $\Pi_{K\text{-fin}}=\bigoplus_{\sigma}E^\sigma$ is dense in $E$ and is a Harish-Chandra module.

**Key inputs.**
- *Dixmier–Malliavin (1978):* $E=E^\infty$ satisfies $C_c^\infty(G)\ast E=E$, so smooth vectors are exactly the Gårding span.
- *Harish-Chandra (1953):* admissibility of irreducible unitary/quasi-simple Banach representations; $Z(\mathfrak{g})$ acts by a character on irreducibles.
- *Casselman's subrepresentation theorem:* every irreducible Harish-Chandra module embeds in a principal series $\mathrm{Ind}_P^G(\sigma\otimes e^{\lambda})$, giving existence of a globalization inside $C^\infty(G/P,\mathcal{L}_\lambda)$.
- *Casselman–Osborne / $\mathfrak{n}$-homology:* $H_0(\mathfrak{n},V)=V/\mathfrak{n}V$ is finite dimensional for $V$ Harish-Chandra, the engine of the Bruhat/asymptotic filtration.

The canonical globalization can be described as
$$V^\infty \;=\; \mathrm{Hom}_{(\mathfrak{g},K)}\big(V^\vee,\, C^\infty(G)_{\text{mod}}\big)$$
(matrix-coefficient realization against the $K$-finite dual $V^\vee$), or as the smooth vectors $E^\infty$ of any admissible Banach globalization $E$ of $V$ (Casselman–Wallach: the answer does not depend on $E$).

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1953–66.** Harish-Chandra introduces $K$-finite vectors and the $(\mathfrak{g},K)$-module formalism; the loss of topology becomes the standing technical nuisance of the field.
- **1970s–80s.** Casselman proves the subrepresentation theorem and, with Miličić, the asymptotic-expansion theory of matrix coefficients. Casselman announces the globalization theorem in lectures and in ICM-adjacent notes in the early 1980s.
- **1989.** Casselman, *Canonical extensions of Harish-Chandra modules to representations of $G$* (Canad. J. Math. 41), gives the construction and proves uniqueness/automatic continuity modulo a technical point on the Fréchet structure.
- **1991.** du Cloux, *Sur les représentations différentiables des groupes de Lie algébriques* (Ann. Sci. ENS 24), develops the theory of $\mathcal{S}$-representations of algebraic groups and proves the corresponding equivalence in that setting.
- **1992.** Wallach, *Real Reductive Groups II*, Ch. 11, gives the first complete published proof for real reductive $G$, using the "Casselman–Jacquet" module, $\mathfrak{n}$-homology and delicate estimates on asymptotic expansions. This is the reference version; the theorem has been standard equipment since.
- **2014.** Bernstein–Krötz, *Smooth Fréchet globalizations of Harish-Chandra modules* (Israel J. Math. 199), give a conceptually new proof: a Sobolev-type lemma plus the theory of $\mathcal{SF}$-modules over the algebra $\mathcal{S}(G)$ of Harish-Chandra Schwartz functions, yielding the "Casselman–Wallach in the smooth category" package with cleaner functorial statements and applications to Schwartz-space homology.

SOTA: the theorem is proved, in at least three independent ways (Casselman/Wallach asymptotics; du Cloux's algebraic-group method; Bernstein–Krötz's Schwartz-algebra method). Research has migrated to *extensions* of it — families, non-reductive and non-linear groups, and homological refinements.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

Established (unconditional) cases and refinements:

- **Real reductive $G$ of Harish-Chandra class, finite-length $V$:** full theorem (Casselman 1989; Wallach 1992; Bernstein–Krötz 2014).
- **Linear algebraic groups over $\mathbb{R}$, including non-reductive ones:** du Cloux (1991) for $\mathcal{S}$-representations; covers e.g. Jacobi and mirabolic groups $P_n\subset GL_n(\mathbb{R})$.
- **Nash groups / almost-linear Nash groups:** Schwartz-function analogues (Aizenbud–Gourevitch, IMRN 2008; Sun, *Almost linear Nash groups*, 2015) give Casselman–Wallach-type globalization for $\mathcal{S}(X)$ on Nash manifolds $X$.
- **Other globalization functors on the same category:** Schmid's *minimal* (analytic-vector) globalization $V^\omega$ and the *maximal* (hyperfunction/distribution) globalization $V^{-\omega}$ are likewise exact equivalences (Schmid 1985; Kashiwara–Schmid 1994); $V^{\omega}\subset V^\infty\subset V^{-\infty}\subset V^{-\omega}$.
- **Rank one and principal series:** for $G=SL(2,\mathbb{R})$, $SU(1,1)$, $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ and all $\mathrm{Ind}_P^G$ of finite length, uniqueness is checkable directly from Fourier expansion on $K$ and the two-term asymptotics at $\infty$.
- **Failure boundary:** the finite-length hypothesis is necessary. For infinite-length admissible $V$ (e.g. $C^\infty(G/P)$-type modules of infinite length, or $V=\bigoplus_{n} V_n$ with unboundedly many constituents) globalizations exist but are **not** unique, and the extension functor is not exact.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

The difficulty (before resolution, and still in the extension problems) is that **algebra does not see topology**:

- *No a priori continuity.* A $(\mathfrak{g},K)$-map is defined only on a dense countable-dimensional subspace. Nothing in Banach-space theory forces its extension to be bounded; closed-graph arguments fail because the graph closure need not be a graph. One must instead *construct* the continuity from growth estimates on matrix coefficients — i.e. from analysis on $G$ at infinity, not from functional analysis.
- *Asymptotics are the only handle.* Casselman–Miličić expansions give $\langle \Pi(a_t)v,\xi\rangle \sim \sum_{\mu} e^{\langle \mu - \rho, t\rangle} p_\mu(t)$ with exponents determined by $\mathfrak{n}$-homology. Controlling seminorms requires uniformity of these expansions in $v$, which needs finite-dimensionality of $H_0(\mathfrak{n},V)$ — exactly where finite length enters.
- *Fréchet pathologies.* Quotients of Fréchet spaces by non-closed subspaces are non-Hausdorff; images of continuous maps need not be closed. Proving that $\mathfrak{n}V^\infty$ (or $H_0(\mathfrak{n},\mathcal{S}(G))$) is closed is a genuine theorem, not a formality — this is the Hausdorffness problem studied by Aizenbud–Gourevitch–Krötz–Liu.
- *Perturbation/deformation methods do not apply.* There is no small parameter; the statement is rigid and categorical.
- *Non-reductive and infinite-dimensional groups* lack a Cartan involution, a Harish-Chandra Schwartz algebra with the right convolution-idempotency, and $Z(\mathfrak{g})$-finiteness — every existing proof uses at least two of the three.

## 6. The Gap

For real reductive $G$ there is no gap: Section 4 covers Section 1. The live boundary is where the hypotheses are relaxed:

- **Finite length $\to$ admissible.** Precisely which infinite-length admissible modules admit a canonical globalization? No candidate criterion is proven.
- **Pointwise $\to$ families.** Given an algebraic family $\{V_\lambda\}$ of Harish-Chandra modules over a base $S$ (e.g. principal series in the continuous parameter, or a contraction family degenerating $G$ to its Cartan motion group), is $\lambda\mapsto V_\lambda^\infty$ a family of SF-representations, flat and exact over $\mathcal{O}(S)$? Known in special cases; the general statement is open.
- **Reductive $\to$ general Lie.** For a general (non-algebraic) Lie group, even the correct category of "moderate growth" representations is not agreed upon; du Cloux's proof uses the algebraic structure essentially.
- **Archimedean $\to$ non-Archimedean.** For $p$-adic $G$, smooth admissible representations are already algebraic, so the theorem is vacuous; the meaningful analogue concerns Bernstein-center-finite families and remains an analogy, not a theorem.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Homological refinements.** Bernstein–Krötz's $\mathcal{SF}$-module formalism is used to prove Hausdorffness of $\mathfrak{n}$-homology of Schwartz spaces and comparison of algebraic vs. smooth Whittaker/Bessel functionals (Aizenbud–Gourevitch–Krötz–Liu, *Math. Z.* 2016). Continued by the Paderborn (Krötz, Schlichtkrull) and Weizmann (Aizenbud, Gourevitch) groups.
- **Real spherical spaces.** Extending globalization and the subrepresentation theorem to $Z=G/H$ real spherical: Krötz–Schlichtkrull and collaborators; multiplicity bounds and temperedness criteria. *(frontier — verify)* uniform Casselman–Wallach statements for $\mathcal{S}(Z)$-modules.
- **Algebraic families.** Bernstein–Higson–Subag (IMRN 2018, 2020) develop algebraic families of Harish-Chandra pairs and contractions to Cartan motion groups; a Casselman–Wallach theorem in families is an explicitly stated goal. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Analytic globalizations and $\mathcal{D}$-modules.** Kashiwara–Schmid style comparison of $V^\omega, V^{-\omega}$ with twisted $\mathcal{D}$-modules on the flag variety continues, with applications to unitarity algorithms (Adams–van Leeuwen–Trapa–Vogan-adjacent work).
- **Automatic continuity for period integrals.** Growth estimates transferring $(\mathfrak{g},K)$-functionals to continuous functionals on $V^\infty$ — the workhorse for Archimedean Rankin–Selberg and Gan–Gross–Prasad results (Sun, Zhu, and collaborators).

## 8. Future Work

- Formulate and prove a **relative Casselman–Wallach theorem** over a base scheme/complex-analytic base, giving flatness of $\lambda\mapsto V_\lambda^\infty$ for standard families.
- Identify a workable class of **infinite-length admissible modules** (e.g. those with finitely generated $\mathfrak{n}$-homology in each degree) for which uniqueness survives.
- Extend to **non-linear coverings** (metaplectic groups) with uniform constants, and to **almost-linear Nash groups** in full generality.
- Make the proof **effective**: explicit Sobolev exponents $N$ in the moderate-growth estimate, in terms of infinitesimal character and $K$-type, for quantitative bounds on periods.
- Develop the **Whittaker/Bruhat-filtration** analogue of the theorem uniformly in the inducing parameter (Casselman–Hecht–Miličić program).

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take $G=SL(2,\mathbb{R})$, $K=SO(2)$, $P=$ upper triangular Borel, $G/P\cong S^1$ (circle of directions, $\theta\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}$). Consider the spherical (even) principal series with parameter $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$ realized on $C^\infty(S^1)_{\text{even}}$. With $H,E,F$ the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple and $v_n(\theta)=e^{in\theta}$, $n\in2\mathbb{Z}$, the $(\mathfrak{g},K)$-action on the $K$-finite part $V_\lambda=\bigoplus_{n\in2\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}v_n$ is
$$H\cdot v_n=n\,v_n,\qquad E\cdot v_n=\tfrac{\lambda+1+n}{2}\,v_{n+2},\qquad F\cdot v_n=\tfrac{\lambda+1-n}{2}\,v_{n-2}.$$

**Reducibility at $\lambda=1$.** Then $E\cdot v_{-2}=0$ and $F\cdot v_{2}=0$, so
$$W:=\bigoplus_{|n|\ge 2}\mathbb{C}v_n = D^+\oplus D^-$$
is a submodule (the two limits of discrete series), with $V_1/W\cong\mathbb{C}$ the trivial module. Length $=3$, so the theorem applies.

**Globalization side.** $E=C^\infty(S^1)_{\text{even}}$ is a nuclear Fréchet space; the action has moderate growth because $|f(g^{-1}\theta)\,j(g,\theta)^{(1+\lambda)/2}|\le \|g\|^{|{\rm Re}\lambda|+1}\sup|f|$ with $j$ the Jacobian cocycle, and the same holds after applying any $u\in U(\mathfrak{g})$. So $E\in\mathcal{SF}(G)$ and $E_{K\text{-fin}}=V_1$.

**What the theorem asserts, checked.** The submodule $W$ globalizes to
$$W^\infty=\overline{\mathrm{span}}\{v_n:|n|\ge2\}=\Big\{f\in C^\infty(S^1)_{\text{even}}:\ \int_0^{2\pi}f(\theta)\,d\theta=0\Big\},$$
a *closed* subspace of codimension $1$ (its defining functional $f\mapsto\int f$ is continuous — the $G$-map $E\to\mathbb{C}$). The sequence
$$0\longrightarrow W^\infty\longrightarrow C^\infty(S^1)_{\text{even}}\longrightarrow \mathbb{C}\longrightarrow 0$$
is exact with Hausdorff quotient, matching $0\to W\to V_1\to\mathbb{C}\to0$ — this is exactness of $(\cdot)^\infty$.

**Uniqueness in action.** Realize $D^+\oplus D^-$ instead as the smooth vectors of the Hilbert-space direct sum of the two limits of discrete series, $H^\infty$. Uniqueness says there is a *unique* topological $G$-isomorphism $H^\infty\to W^\infty$ restricting to the identity on $K$-finite vectors. Algebraically the map is forced: $v_n\mapsto v_n$. The content is that this Fourier-coefficient-wise bijection is bicontinuous — equivalently that the two families of seminorms are equivalent, which follows from the Sobolev estimate
$$\sup_n |a_n|(1+|n|)^{k}\ \asymp\ p_{\Omega^{m}}\big(\textstyle\sum_n a_n v_n\big),\qquad \Omega=\text{Casimir},\ m\gg k,$$
valid because the Casimir eigenvalue on the $K$-type $n$ is $\lambda^2-1$ *plus* the $K$-Laplacian eigenvalue $n^2$, so $U(\mathfrak{g})$-seminorms control polynomial decay of Fourier coefficients. Without finite length (say, an infinite direct sum of principal series with ${\rm Re}\,\lambda_j\to\infty$) the growth exponent $N$ is unbounded, the seminorm comparison collapses, and uniqueness fails — precisely the boundary described in Sections 4 and 6.
