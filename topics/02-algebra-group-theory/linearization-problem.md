# The Linearization Problem for Algebraic Group Actions on Affine Space

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/linearization-problem` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $0$ and let $G$ be a **reductive** algebraic group over $k$. Suppose $G$ acts algebraically (morphically) on affine space $\mathbb{A}^n_k$.

**Linearization Problem (Kambayashi, 1979).** Is every such action *linearizable*, i.e. is there an automorphism $\varphi \in \operatorname{Aut}_k(\mathbb{A}^n)$ and a representation $\rho: G \to GL_n(k)$ with
$$\varphi \circ (g \cdot x) = \rho(g)\,\varphi(x) \qquad \text{for all } g \in G,\ x \in \mathbb{A}^n\,?$$

Equivalently, in coordinates: is every action of $G$ by $k$-algebra automorphisms on the polynomial ring $k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ conjugate, inside $\operatorname{Aut}_k(k[x_1,\dots,x_n])$, to one preserving the linear span $\bigoplus_i k x_i$?

The answer is **no in general**: Schwarz (1989) produced non-linearizable actions of $O_2(\mathbb{C})$, and Knop (1991) showed every non-abelian reductive $G$ acts non-linearizably on some $\mathbb{A}^n$. The problem is therefore *open* in the following surviving, sharp forms:

1. **(Abelian case)** Is every action of a *diagonalizable* group — a torus $(k^\times)^r$ or a finite abelian group — on $\mathbb{A}^n$ linearizable?
2. **(Finite case)** Is every action of a finite group on $\mathbb{A}^n$ linearizable? Open already for $G = \mathbb{Z}/2$ on $\mathbb{A}^3$.
3. **(Low dimension)** Is every reductive action on $\mathbb{A}^4$ linearizable? Open already for $k^\times$ on $\mathbb{A}^4$.

A complete solution means either a conjugation procedure valid for the stated class, or an explicit action together with a proof that no automorphism of $\mathbb{A}^n$ conjugates it into $GL_n$.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Actions and invariants.** An action $\sigma: G \times \mathbb{A}^n \to \mathbb{A}^n$ dualizes to a coaction $\sigma^*: k[x] \to k[G] \otimes k[x]$ making $A = k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ a rational $G$-module. Reductivity gives complete reducibility of rational $G$-modules and, by Nagata/Hilbert, finite generation of the invariant ring $A^G$. The categorical quotient is
$$\pi: \mathbb{A}^n \longrightarrow \mathbb{A}^n /\!\!/ G = \operatorname{Spec} A^G,$$
whose fibres each contain a unique closed orbit (Luna). A linearizable action has $\mathbb{A}^n/\!\!/G \cong V/\!\!/G$ for a representation $V$, and a **fixed point** (the origin), so any action with $(\mathbb{A}^n)^G = \emptyset$ is already non-linearizable in a trivial way — for reductive $G$ acting on $\mathbb{A}^n$ existence of a fixed point is itself a nontrivial open question in general.

**Luna's slice theorem.** If $x \in \mathbb{A}^n$ is a fixed point with isotropy $G$, there is a $G$-stable étale neighbourhood modelled on the tangent representation $T_x\mathbb{A}^n$. So an action with a fixed point is *formally* and *étale-locally* linear; linearization asks whether this can be upgraded to a global polynomial change of coordinates.

**The tangent/Bass–Haboush mechanism.** Bass and Haboush (1987) proved that a $G$-variety $X$ with a fixed point $x$, whose quotient is "small", is linearizable when all $G$-equivariant vector bundles over the relevant representation are trivial. Concretely, if $E \to V$ is a $G$-vector bundle over a $G$-module $V$, then $E$ is *stably* trivial: $E \oplus (V \times W) \cong V \times W'$. If moreover the total space of $E$ is isomorphic to $\mathbb{A}^n$ as a variety but $E$ is **not** $G$-isomorphic to a trivial bundle $V \times F$, the induced $G$-action on $\mathbb{A}^n \cong E$ is a candidate non-linearizable action. This is exactly Schwarz's construction.

**Automorphism group in dimension 2.** By Jung (1942) and van der Kulk (1953),
$$\operatorname{Aut}_k(\mathbb{A}^2) = \operatorname{Aff}_2 \ast_{B} \operatorname{Jonq},$$
an amalgamated free product of the affine group and the de Jonquières (triangular) group over their intersection. Serre's theory of groups acting on trees then forces every bounded (in particular every reductive) subgroup into a conjugate of a factor — the engine behind all $n=2$ results. **No analogue of this structure theorem is known for $n \ge 3$**; $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{A}^3)$ is not generated by tame automorphisms (Shestakov–Umirbaev, 2004).

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1962.** Gutwirth: every $k^\times$-action on $\mathbb{A}^2$ is linearizable.
- **1966–67.** Białynicki-Birula: any effective action of a torus of dimension $\ge n-1$ on $\mathbb{A}^n$ is linearizable; a $(k^\times)^n$-action on $\mathbb{A}^n$ is diagonalizable. Fixed-point theorem: a torus acting on $\mathbb{A}^n$ has a fixed point.
- **1979.** Kambayashi formulates the general problem and settles $n=2$: every reductive (indeed every finite) group action on $\mathbb{A}^2$ in characteristic $0$ is linearizable.
- **1985.** Kraft–Popov: every **semisimple** group action on $\mathbb{A}^3$ is linear (in suitable coordinates).
- **1987.** Bass–Haboush: linearization criteria via equivariant $K$-theory; stable triviality of $G$-bundles over representations.
- **1989.** Schwarz: **counterexamples** — non-linearizable actions of $O_2(\mathbb{C})$ on $\mathbb{A}^4$, built from non-trivial $G$-vector bundles over representations.
- **1991.** Knop: every non-abelian reductive $G$ admits a non-linearizable action on some $\mathbb{A}^n$.
- **1992.** Kraft–Schwarz: actions with quotient $\dim \mathbb{A}^n/\!\!/G \le 1$ are linearizable (with explicit exceptions), a broad positive class.
- **1995–96.** Masuda–Petrie; Masuda–Moser-Jauslin–Petrie: the *equivariant Serre problem* is solved affirmatively for abelian reductive $G$ — all $G$-vector bundles over representations of an abelian reductive group are trivial. Consequence: **Schwarz's method cannot produce abelian counterexamples.**
- **1997.** Koras–Russell + Kaliman–Koras–Makar-Limanov–Russell: **every $k^\times$-action on $\mathbb{A}^3$ is linearizable**, completed by proving the Russell cubic $x + x^2y + z^2 + t^3 = 0$ is not isomorphic to $\mathbb{A}^3$.
- **1998–99.** Derksen–Kutzschebauch: non-linearizable *holomorphic* actions of any positive-dimensional reductive group on $\mathbb{C}^n$ for $n$ large. Asanuma: non-linearizable $k^\times$-actions on affine spaces in **positive characteristic** — so characteristic $0$ is essential.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Class | Status |
|---|---|
| $n = 1$; any $G$ | Linearizable (elementary; $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{A}^1)=\operatorname{Aff}_1$). |
| $n = 2$, char $0$, any reductive or finite $G$ | **Solved** — Kambayashi (1979) via Jung–van der Kulk. |
| $n = 3$, $G$ semisimple | **Solved** — Kraft–Popov (1985). |
| $n = 3$, $G = k^\times$ | **Solved** — Koras–Russell / KKML-R (1997). |
| Tori of rank $\ge n-1$ on $\mathbb{A}^n$ | **Solved** — Białynicki-Birula (1966/67). |
| $\dim \mathbb{A}^n/\!\!/G \le 1$ | **Solved** — Kraft–Schwarz (1992). |
| $G$ reductive with $\mathbb{A}^n \cong$ trivial $G$-bundle data | Linearizable — Bass–Haboush (1987). |
| $G$ non-abelian reductive, $n \gg 0$ | **False** — Schwarz (1989), Knop (1991). |
| char $p > 0$, $G = k^\times$ | **False** — Asanuma (1999). |
| Holomorphic category, $G$ positive-dimensional | **False** — Derksen–Kutzschebauch (1998). |

Open in particular: $\mathbb{Z}/m$ on $\mathbb{A}^3$ for every $m \ge 2$; $k^\times$ on $\mathbb{A}^4$; $(k^\times)^2$ on $\mathbb{A}^4$; all finite abelian groups on $\mathbb{A}^n$, $n \ge 3$.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No structure theory for $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{A}^n)$, $n \ge 3$.** The amalgamated-product decomposition that makes $n=2$ tractable has no known analogue; Shestakov–Umirbaev (2004) showed the Nagata automorphism of $\mathbb{A}^3$ is wild, so $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{A}^3)$ is strictly bigger than the tame subgroup and admits no known amalgam presentation. Fixed-point and conjugacy arguments in $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{A}^n)$ have nothing to bite on.
- **The equivariant Serre problem is *solved* for abelian groups — removing the only known counterexample machine.** Schwarz-type constructions require non-trivial $G$-vector bundles over representations, and these vanish for abelian reductive $G$ (Masuda–Moser-Jauslin–Petrie, 1996). Any abelian counterexample must be built by a genuinely new mechanism.
- **Recognizing $\mathbb{A}^n$ is hard.** Both directions need to decide whether a given smooth contractible affine variety (a quotient, a slice, a bundle total space) *is* affine space. For $n=3$ this required the Makar-Limanov invariant and a decade of work (the Russell cubic). No usable characterization of $\mathbb{A}^n$ exists for $n \ge 4$; the Zariski Cancellation Problem is open there.
- **Topological invariants are blind.** $\mathbb{A}^n$ is contractible and its quotients are often contractible too, so equivariant cohomology, $K$-theory and Smith theory give no obstruction — exactly why the counterexamples had to come from bundle theory rather than topology.
- **Non-reductive failure is total.** For $G_a$, Bass (1984) gave a non-triangulable action on $\mathbb{A}^3$; unipotent actions have infinitely generated-looking invariant theory (Nagata), so no technique transfers from that side.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: $n \le 2$ fully; $n=3$ for $k^\times$ and for semisimple $G$; quotients of dimension $\le 1$; large tori. False: non-abelian reductive $G$ in high dimension, char $p$, and the holomorphic category.

The gap is the **abelian reductive case in dimension $\ge 4$** (and the finite case in dimension $\ge 3$). Concretely, two steps are missing:

1. A replacement for the slice-theorem-to-global-coordinates passage that does not assume $\dim \mathbb{A}^n/\!\!/G \le 1$. Present proofs build the linearizing automorphism fibre by fibre over a curve; over a base of dimension $\ge 2$ the gluing obstruction lives in a non-abelian cohomology set with no computed vanishing theorem.
2. A criterion deciding when $\mathbb{A}^n/\!\!/G$ and the closed-orbit strata of a hypothetical exotic action force $\mathbb{A}^n \cong V$. In dimension $3$ this was supplied by the Makar-Limanov invariant; in dimension $4$ no such invariant is known to distinguish $\mathbb{A}^4$ from candidate exotic quotients.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Basel / Grenoble / Bourgogne school** (Kraft, van Santen, Regeta, Moser-Jauslin, Poloni): reconstructing $\mathbb{A}^n$ and representations from automorphism-group data. Kraft–Regeta–van Santen, *Is the affine space determined by its automorphism group?* (IMRN, 2021), shows abstract isomorphisms $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{A}^n)\cong \operatorname{Aut}(X)$ force $X \cong \mathbb{A}^n$ for many $X$ — a route to rigidity statements implying linearization for reductive subgroups. *(frontier — verify: extensions to $\mathbb{A}^4$ with torus actions.)*
- **Affine algebraic geometry / Makar-Limanov–Kaliman circle:** additive-group invariants (ML, Derksen invariant) applied to $k^\times$-quotients in dimension $4$; classification of Koras–Russell-type fourfolds.
- **Equivariant birational geometry (Kontsevich–Pestun–Tschinkel, Reichstein, Kresch–Tschinkel):** equivariant Burnside groups give new obstructions to conjugating finite group actions on rational varieties into linear ones. These give sharp results for actions on projective space and are being adapted to the affine setting. *(frontier — verify: no affine linearization consequence is yet established.)*
- **Positive characteristic and cancellation:** Gupta's counterexamples to Zariski cancellation in char $p$ sharpen the picture that char $0$ is the essential hypothesis.
- **Analytic side:** Kutzschebauch, Lárusson, Schwarz on Oka principles and holomorphic linearization of reductive actions on Stein manifolds — the counterexample source in the analytic category and a guide to what algebraic obstructions would have to look like.

## 8. Future Work

- Settle $k^\times$ acting on $\mathbb{A}^4$; leading proposals reduce this to classifying smooth contractible affine threefolds arising as $\mathbb{A}^4/\!\!/k^\times$ with a $\mathbb{Z}$-grading of $k[x_1,\dots,x_4]$.
- Decide $\mathbb{Z}/2$ on $\mathbb{A}^3$: equivalently, is every involution of $k[x,y,z]$ conjugate to a diagonal one? Suggested by Kraft as the most accessible open case.
- Prove or disprove the **equivariant cancellation** statement: if $V \oplus W \cong_G \mathbb{A}^n \times W$ then $\mathbb{A}^n \cong_G V$ for abelian $G$.
- Develop a Makar-Limanov-type invariant sensitive in dimension $4$.
- Import equivariant Burnside-group obstructions to the affine category to search for a *finite abelian* counterexample.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**A non-linear $k^\times$-action on $\mathbb{A}^2$, and its explicit linearization.**

Take the linear action $\lambda_t(x,y) = (tx,\; t^3 y)$ and the triangular automorphism
$$\varphi(x,y) = (x,\; y + x^2), \qquad \varphi^{-1}(u,v) = (u,\; v - u^2).$$
Conjugating, $\alpha_t := \varphi^{-1}\circ \lambda_t \circ \varphi$ gives
$$\lambda_t\varphi(x,y) = \big(tx,\ t^3y + t^3x^2\big), \qquad
\alpha_t(x,y) = \big(tx,\ t^3 y + t^3x^2 - t^2x^2\big) = \big(tx,\ t^3y + (t^3-t^2)x^2\big).$$
Check it is an action: $\alpha_s\alpha_t(x,y)$ has second coordinate $s^3\!\left(t^3y+(t^3-t^2)x^2\right)+(s^3-s^2)t^2x^2 = (st)^3y + \big((st)^3-(st)^2\big)x^2$. ✓ And $\alpha_1 = \mathrm{id}$.

$\alpha$ is visibly not linear (the term $(t^3-t^2)x^2$ is quadratic), yet it *is* linearizable by construction: $\varphi$ conjugates it back to $\lambda$.

**How one would find $\varphi$ without knowing it.** Grade $k[x,y]$ by the $\alpha$-weights. The action fixes only the origin, and $x$ is a weight-$1$ eigenvector. Look for a weight-$3$ eigenfunction of the form $y + c x^2$:
$$\alpha_t^*(y + cx^2) = t^3y + (t^3-t^2)x^2 + c\,t^2x^2 = t^3 y + t^3x^2 + (c-1)t^2 x^2 .$$
This is $t^3(y+cx^2)$ iff $c = 1$ and $c-1 = 0$ — consistent, giving $c=1$. So $(x,\, y+x^2)$ is a system of eigencoordinates, they generate $k[x,y]$, and the action is diagonalized. This is the whole method in dimension $2$: reductivity splits $k[x,y]$ into weight spaces, and one shows a *generating* set of eigenfunctions can be chosen to be a coordinate system.

**Where it breaks.** In dimension $3$ with a *hyperbolic* $k^\times$-action (weights of both signs), the eigenfunctions of low weight need not generate $k[x,y,z]$, and the quotient $\mathbb{A}^3/\!\!/k^\times$ is a possibly singular surface. Koras–Russell had to classify the resulting threefolds, and the argument only closed once the Russell cubic $x+x^2y+z^2+t^3=0$ — a smooth contractible threefold with a hyperbolic $k^\times$-action, and the exact shape of a would-be counterexample — was shown **not** isomorphic to $\mathbb{A}^3$ via the Makar-Limanov invariant. In dimension $4$ the analogous family is unclassified: that is precisely the open gap.
