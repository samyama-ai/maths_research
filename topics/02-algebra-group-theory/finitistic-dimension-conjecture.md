# Finitistic Dimension Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/finitistic-dimension-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $A$ be an Artin algebra (equivalently, for most purposes, a finite-dimensional algebra over a field $k$). Write $\operatorname{mod} A$ for finitely generated left $A$-modules and $\operatorname{Mod} A$ for all left $A$-modules.

**Little finitistic dimension:**
$$\operatorname{findim} A \;=\; \sup\{\operatorname{pd} M \;:\; M \in \operatorname{mod} A,\ \operatorname{pd} M < \infty\}.$$

**Big finitistic dimension:**
$$\operatorname{Findim} A \;=\; \sup\{\operatorname{pd} M \;:\; M \in \operatorname{Mod} A,\ \operatorname{pd} M < \infty\}.$$

**Second Finitistic Dimension Conjecture (open).** For every Artin algebra $A$, $\operatorname{findim} A < \infty$.

**First Finitistic Dimension Conjecture (false).** $\operatorname{findim} A = \operatorname{Findim} A$ for every Artin algebra. Disproved by Huisgen-Zimmermann (1992).

A resolution requires either a proof of finiteness valid for all Artin algebras, or an explicit algebra $A$ together with a sequence $M_1, M_2, \dots \in \operatorname{mod} A$ with $\operatorname{pd} M_n$ finite and unbounded. The finiteness of $\operatorname{Findim} A$ for finite-dimensional algebras is also open and is a formally stronger statement.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Fix $A$ Artin, $J = \operatorname{rad} A$, $A/J \cong \prod_{i=1}^n \operatorname{End}(S_i)$ with $S_1,\dots,S_n$ the simples. Every $M \in \operatorname{mod} A$ has a minimal projective resolution
$$\cdots \to P_2 \to P_1 \to P_0 \to M \to 0, \qquad \Omega^i M := \ker(P_{i-1}\to P_{i-2}),$$
and $\operatorname{pd} M = \sup\{i : \Omega^i M \neq 0\}$. The global dimension is $\operatorname{gldim} A = \sup_i \operatorname{pd} S_i$; the key point is that $\operatorname{findim} A$ **cannot** be computed from simples alone, since $\operatorname{pd} S_i = \infty$ is compatible with large finite projective dimensions elsewhere.

Basic facts:
- $\operatorname{findim} A \le \operatorname{Findim} A \le \operatorname{gldim} A$, with equality throughout when $\operatorname{gldim} A < \infty$.
- $\operatorname{findim} A = 0$ for self-injective $A$ (finite pd $\Rightarrow$ projective).
- $\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(A) := \{M \in \operatorname{mod}A : \operatorname{pd} M < \infty\}$ is closed under extensions, kernels of epimorphisms, and direct summands — it is a resolving subcategory.

**Igusa–Todorov functions.** Let $K$ be the free abelian group on iso-classes of indecomposable non-projective modules, and $L: K \to K$ the endomorphism induced by $M \mapsto \Omega M$. For $M \in \operatorname{mod} A$ let $\langle M\rangle \subseteq K$ be the subgroup generated by the indecomposable summands of $M$. Since $\operatorname{rank} L^m\langle M\rangle$ is non-increasing, there is a least $\phi(M) \ge 0$ with
$$L\big|_{L^m\langle M\rangle} \text{ injective for all } m \ge \phi(M),$$
and one sets $\psi(M) = \phi(M) + \sup\{\operatorname{pd} N : N \mid \Omega^{\phi(M)}M,\ \operatorname{pd} N<\infty\}$. The central lemma: for a short exact sequence $0 \to X \to Y \to Z \to 0$ with $\operatorname{pd} Z < \infty$,
$$\operatorname{pd} Z \;\le\; \phi(X \oplus Y) + 1 .$$
This converts "bounded $\phi$ on a generating family" into "bounded finitistic dimension".

**Related conjectures.** $\operatorname{findim} A < \infty$ implies the Nakayama Conjecture and the Generalized Nakayama Conjecture (Auslander–Reiten, 1975), and is implied by the Wakamatsu-tilting/Gorenstein symmetry circle of conjectures.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1960.** Bass introduces $\operatorname{findim}$ and $\operatorname{Findim}$ in *Finitistic dimension and a homological generalization of semi-primary rings* (Trans. AMS 95). The two conjectures are attributed there to Rosenberg and Zelinsky.
- **1971.** Raynaud–Gruson prove $\operatorname{Findim} R = \dim R$ (Krull dimension) for commutative Noetherian $R$ — the commutative case is fully settled.
- **1991.** Auslander–Reiten: if $\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(A)$ is contravariantly finite in $\operatorname{mod} A$, then $\operatorname{findim} A < \infty$. This is the first general structural criterion.
- **1992.** Huisgen-Zimmermann constructs a finite-dimensional monomial algebra with $\operatorname{findim} = 1 < 2 = \operatorname{Findim}$, killing the First Conjecture. Smalø (2000) makes the gap arbitrarily large: for every $n$ there is an algebra with $\operatorname{findim} = 1$, $\operatorname{Findim} = n$.
- **2005.** Igusa–Todorov: $\operatorname{repdim} A \le 3 \Rightarrow \operatorname{findim} A < \infty$. Combined with Rouquier's later examples of arbitrarily large representation dimension, this shows the criterion is not universal.
- **2019.** Rickard: if the injective $A$-modules generate the unbounded derived category $D(\operatorname{Mod}A)$ as a localizing subcategory, then $\operatorname{Findim} A < \infty$. This reframes the problem in triangulated/derived terms and recovers most previously known classes uniformly.

The conjecture is currently regarded as one of the two or three central open problems of the homological theory of Artin algebras.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

Finiteness of $\operatorname{findim} A$ (often $\operatorname{Findim} A$) is proven for:

- **Commutative Noetherian rings:** $\operatorname{Findim} R = \operatorname{Krull\,dim} R$ (Raynaud–Gruson 1971); local case $\operatorname{findim} R = \operatorname{depth} R$ (Auslander–Buchsbaum).
- **$\operatorname{gldim} A < \infty$:** trivially $\operatorname{findim} A = \operatorname{gldim} A \le$ known bounds.
- **Self-injective algebras:** $\operatorname{findim} A = \operatorname{Findim} A = 0$.
- **Representation-finite algebras** (finitely many indecomposables): finite, via Auslander's argument; more generally $\operatorname{repdim} A \le 3$ (Igusa–Todorov 2005).
- **Monomial algebras** $kQ/I$ with $I$ generated by paths: $\operatorname{findim}$ finite (Green–Kirkman–Kuzmanovich 1991); $\operatorname{Findim}$ finite by Rickard's criterion.
- **Radical cube zero:** $J^3 = 0 \Rightarrow \operatorname{findim} A < \infty$ (Green–Huisgen-Zimmermann, Math. Z. 206, 1991). The case $J^2 = 0$ is elementary: $\operatorname{findim} A \le 1$ and is computed from the separated quiver.
- **Algebras with $\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(A)$ contravariantly finite** (Auslander–Reiten 1991); includes algebras stably equivalent to hereditary ones.
- **Left serial algebras**, and Nakayama algebras, where $\operatorname{findim} A \le 2n-2$ for $n$ simples.
- **Igusa–Todorov algebras** (Wei 2009): if $\operatorname{mod}A$ admits a module $V$ with $\phi(V)$ controlling all syzygies, then $\operatorname{findim} A < \infty$; this class is closed under several standard constructions.
- **Endomorphism/idempotent reductions:** Xi's series (2004–2008) gives finiteness for $A$ when $\operatorname{findim} eAe < \infty$ and $J e A$ has small projective dimension, covering many triangular matrix and one-point extension algebras.
- **Group algebras** $kG$ of finite groups: self-injective, so $\operatorname{findim} = 0$; the interesting group-theoretic analogue is $\operatorname{findim}$ of $kG$ for infinite $G$, tied to $\underline{\operatorname{cd}}\, G$.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No invariant is known to bound $\operatorname{findim}$ in general.** Every successful proof factors through an auxiliary finiteness (representation dimension $\le 3$, contravariant finiteness, an Igusa–Todorov generator, derived generation by injectives). Rouquier's algebras of arbitrarily large representation dimension and Igusa–Todorov–Smalø's examples of non-contravariantly-finite $\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}$ show each such hypothesis genuinely fails on some algebras.
- **Syzygy dynamics are not eventually periodic.** The map $M \mapsto \Omega M$ on indecomposables can have infinite orbits of unbounded complexity; "domino effects" (Huisgen-Zimmermann) show local relations in the quiver propagate pd upward in ways that resist induction on the number of simples.
- **Failure of the big/little dictionary.** Since $\operatorname{findim} \neq \operatorname{Findim}$, techniques that produce direct limits (tilting, cotorsion pairs, Bass's original module-theoretic methods) prove statements about $\operatorname{Findim}$ that do not descend, and vice versa.
- **Induction on quiver size breaks.** Standard reductions (idempotent truncation $A \to eAe$, one-point extension) change $\operatorname{findim}$ unpredictably; $\operatorname{findim} eAe$ can be finite while the transfer requires an unavailable bound on $\operatorname{pd}(Ae)$ or $\operatorname{pd}(JeA)$.
- **Non-locality.** $\operatorname{findim}$ is not determined by $A/J^n$ for any fixed $n$ beyond $n=3$, and is not invariant under derived equivalence in a way that has yet been exploited (though $\operatorname{Findim}$ finiteness is derived-invariant by Rickard's criterion).

## 6. The Gap

Proven: finiteness whenever a *uniform generator* for syzygies exists — some object or subcategory (an Igusa–Todorov module, a contravariantly finite $\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}$, a compact generator built from injectives) whose complexity bounds all $\Omega^i M$ simultaneously.

Unproven: that such a generator exists for **every** Artin algebra. The precise missing step is a bound of the form
$$\operatorname{pd} M < \infty \;\Longrightarrow\; \operatorname{pd} M \le f(n, \ell)$$
for some function $f$ of the number of simples $n$ and the Loewy length $\ell$ — or, dually, a proof that the injective modules always generate $D(\operatorname{Mod}A)$ as a localizing subcategory. Equivalently: no known mechanism forbids an algebra whose $\Omega$-orbits realise every finite projective dimension.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Derived/triangulated approach.** Rickard's injective-generation criterion (Adv. Math. 2019) is the most active thread; groups in Bristol, Bielefeld, Stuttgart and Trondheim test injective generation for classical families (Hecke algebras, blocks of group algebras, Brauer graph and gentle algebras). *(frontier — verify)* extensions to infinite-dimensional and dg settings are being circulated.
- **Igusa–Todorov theory.** Ongoing work (Lanzilotta, Mata, Barrios, and coauthors in Montevideo) on $\phi$-dimension, relative Igusa–Todorov functions, and the question whether $\phi\dim A < \infty$ characterises finiteness of $\operatorname{findim}$.
- **Gorenstein homological algebra.** Marczinzik and collaborators compute $\operatorname{findim}$ for large algebra databases (QPA/GAP), testing FDC alongside the Nakayama and Gorenstein symmetry conjectures on all algebras of small dimension.
- **Tilting and cotorsion pairs.** Angeleri Hügel–Herbera–Trlifaj's programme relating $\operatorname{Findim}$ to (infinitely generated) tilting modules remains a live route for the big conjecture.
- **Reduction techniques.** Xi's idempotent/extension machinery continues to be refined (Beijing Normal), yielding new finiteness classes rather than a general proof.

## 8. Future Work

- Decide whether injective generation of $D(\operatorname{Mod} A)$ holds for all finite-dimensional algebras — a "yes" settles the big conjecture, a counterexample would sharply localise the difficulty.
- Prove or refute: $\operatorname{findim} A < \infty$ for all algebras with $J^4 = 0$. Radical cube zero is settled; $J^4 = 0$ is the first genuinely open Loewy-length case and is widely regarded as a decisive test.
- Determine whether $\phi\dim A < \infty$ for all Artin algebras (Lanzilotta–Mata); this implies FDC.
- Search for counterexamples systematically among monomial-like and special biserial algebras where syzygy combinatorics is computable but unbounded.
- Clarify derived invariance: is finiteness of the *little* finitistic dimension preserved under derived equivalence?

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Let $k$ be a field and $Q$ the quiver with vertices $\{1,2\}$, arrow $\alpha: 1 \to 2$, and loop $\varepsilon: 2 \to 2$. Put
$$A = kQ/(\varepsilon^2).$$

**Projectives.** Using left modules with $P_i = A e_i$ spanned by paths starting at $i$:
- Paths from $1$: $e_1,\ \alpha,\ \varepsilon\alpha$. So $P_1$ is uniserial of dimension $3$ with Loewy series $\begin{smallmatrix}1\\2\\2\end{smallmatrix}$.
- Paths from $2$: $e_2,\ \varepsilon$. So $P_2$ is uniserial of dimension $2$, $\begin{smallmatrix}2\\2\end{smallmatrix}$.

**Syzygies of the simples.**
- $\operatorname{rad} P_1 = \begin{smallmatrix}2\\2\end{smallmatrix} \cong P_2$, hence $\Omega S_1 = P_2$ and
$$\operatorname{pd} S_1 = 1.$$
- $\operatorname{rad} P_2 = S_2$, hence $\Omega S_2 = S_2$, so $\Omega^i S_2 = S_2$ for all $i$ and
$$\operatorname{pd} S_2 = \infty, \qquad \operatorname{gldim} A = \infty.$$

**Finitistic dimension.** Every indecomposable $M$ with no projective summand has $\Omega M$ a submodule of a projective. The only indecomposables are $S_1$ is not a submodule of anything projective; the indecomposable non-projectives are $S_2$, $\begin{smallmatrix}1\\2\end{smallmatrix}$, $S_1$. Computing: $\Omega\begin{smallmatrix}1\\2\end{smallmatrix} = S_2$, so $\operatorname{pd}\begin{smallmatrix}1\\2\end{smallmatrix} = \infty$. Hence
$$\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(A) = \operatorname{add}\{P_1, P_2, S_1\}, \qquad \operatorname{findim} A = \operatorname{pd} S_1 = 1 .$$
Since $\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}(A)$ has only finitely many indecomposables it is contravariantly finite, so Auslander–Reiten's criterion applies, and $\operatorname{Findim} A = 1$ as well.

**Igusa–Todorov check.** Take $V = S_2 \oplus \begin{smallmatrix}1\\2\end{smallmatrix}$. Then $L\langle V\rangle = \langle S_2 \rangle$ has rank $1$ and $L$ is injective on it ($\Omega S_2 = S_2$), so $\phi(V) = 1$; the lemma $\operatorname{pd} Z \le \phi(X\oplus Y)+1$ recovers the bound $\operatorname{findim} A \le 2$, consistent with the exact value $1$.

**What the example shows.** Infinite global dimension coexists with a small finitistic dimension: the obstruction $\Omega S_2 = S_2$ is a periodic orbit that simply never contributes to $\operatorname{findim}$. The conjecture asserts that no algebra manages to produce *non-periodic, non-terminating* syzygy behaviour that nevertheless yields modules of arbitrarily large finite projective dimension — and it is exactly this that no known technique rules out.
