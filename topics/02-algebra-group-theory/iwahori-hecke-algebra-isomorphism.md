# Iwahori-Hecke Algebra Isomorphism

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/iwahori-hecke-algebra-isomorphism` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $(W,S)$ and $(W',S')$ be Coxeter systems and let $\mathcal{H}(W,S;\mathbf{q})$ denote the associated Iwahori–Hecke algebra with parameter system $\mathbf{q}$ over a commutative ring $R$. The **isomorphism problem** asks:

> To what extent is the pair $\big((W,S),\mathbf{q}\big)$ recoverable from the $R$-algebra $\mathcal{H}(W,S;\mathbf{q})$?

Three linked questions:

1. **(Generic rigidity.)** Over $A=\mathbb{Z}[v^{\pm1}]$ with $v$ an indeterminate, does $\mathcal{H}_A(W,S)\cong\mathcal{H}_A(W',S')$ as $A$-algebras force $W\cong W'$? Is the pair $(W,\mathbf{q})$ determined up to the *known* symmetries: diagram automorphisms of $(W,S)$ and the inversion $v\mapsto v^{-1}$?
2. **(Specialization.)** For a field $K$ and $q\in K^\times$, classify the pairs $(W,q)$, $(W',q')$ with $\mathcal{H}_K(W;q)\cong\mathcal{H}_K(W';q')$. Equivalently: which invariants of the *specialized* algebra (semisimplicity, block structure, simple-module dimensions, decomposition matrix) separate parameters?
3. **(Unequal parameters and affine type.)** For multi-parameter algebras $\mathcal{H}(W;\{q_s\})$ and for affine Weyl groups, determine when two parameter systems give isomorphic — or Morita-equivalent — algebras.

A complete solution means: an explicit list of isomorphism classes, plus a proof that no unlisted isomorphism exists. A disproof of rigidity means an exhibited "exotic" isomorphism $\mathcal{H}(W)\cong\mathcal{H}(W')$ with $W\not\cong W'$ (or with parameters not related by the known symmetries).

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Coxeter system.** $(W,S)$ with presentation $W=\langle s\in S \mid s^2=1,\ (st)^{m(s,t)}=1\rangle$, $m(s,t)\in\{2,3,\dots,\infty\}$. Length $\ell(w)$ = minimal word length in $S$.

**Hecke algebra.** Let $A=\mathbb{Z}[v^{\pm1}]$, $q=v^2$. $\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_A(W,S)$ is free as an $A$-module with basis $\{T_w\}_{w\in W}$ and multiplication
$$T_sT_w=\begin{cases}T_{sw}, & \ell(sw)>\ell(w),\\ q\,T_{sw}+(q-1)T_w, & \ell(sw)<\ell(w),\end{cases}$$
equivalently generated by $\{T_s\}_{s\in S}$ subject to the braid relations $\underbrace{T_sT_tT_s\cdots}_{m(s,t)}=\underbrace{T_tT_sT_t\cdots}_{m(s,t)}$ and the quadratic relation
$$(T_s-q)(T_s+1)=0 .$$
With unequal parameters $\{q_s\}$ ($q_s=q_t$ whenever $s,t$ conjugate in $W$) the quadratic relation becomes $(T_s-q_s)(T_s+1)=0$.

**Symmetrizing trace.** $\tau(T_w)=\delta_{w,1}$ makes $\mathcal{H}$ a symmetric algebra: $(h,h')\mapsto\tau(hh')$ is nondegenerate, with dual basis $\{q^{-\ell(w)}T_{w^{-1}}\}$.

**Poincaré polynomial.** For $W$ finite with degrees $d_1,\dots,d_n$,
$$P_W(q)=\sum_{w\in W}q^{\ell(w)}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{q^{d_i}-1}{q-1}.$$

**Tits deformation theorem.** If $W$ is finite and $K\supseteq\mathbb{Q}(v)$ is a splitting field, then
$$\mathcal{H}_K(W)\;\cong\;K[W]$$
as $K$-algebras (Geck–Pfeiffer, Thm. 8.1.7). So over the generic field the algebra is *not* a deformation-sensitive object; all information about $q$ sits in the integral form and in specializations.

**Semisimplicity (Gyoja–Uno).** For $W$ finite, $K$ a field, $q\in K^\times$: $\mathcal{H}_K(W;q)$ is semisimple iff $P_W(q)\neq 0$ in $K$. In type $A_{n-1}$ ($W=\mathfrak{S}_n$) this reads $\prod_{i=1}^{n}[i]_q\neq0$ with $[i]_q=1+q+\cdots+q^{i-1}$, i.e. $e>n$ where $e=\min\{i\ge2:[i]_q=0\}$ (the *quantum characteristic*, $e=\infty$ if $q$ is not a root of unity and $\mathrm{char}\,K=0$).

**Known symmetries.** (i) $T_s\mapsto -q^{-1}T_s$ gives $\mathcal{H}(W;q)\cong\mathcal{H}(W;q^{-1})$ (the two eigenvalues $q,-1$ are exchanged after rescaling). (ii) Any automorphism of the Coxeter graph induces an algebra automorphism. (iii) Permuting parameters along a graph automorphism, e.g. $\mathcal{H}(B_2;(a,b))\cong\mathcal{H}(B_2;(b,a))$.

**Cellularity (Graham–Lehrer; Geck).** $\mathcal{H}$ carries a cellular structure with cell modules $\{S^\lambda\}$ from Kazhdan–Lusztig bases; simple modules $D^\lambda=S^\lambda/\mathrm{rad}$ arise for $\lambda$ with nonzero cell form, and the decomposition matrix $[S^\lambda:D^\mu]$ is unitriangular.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1964.** Iwahori introduces the algebra as $\mathrm{End}_{G}(\mathrm{Ind}_B^G 1)$ for $G=G(\mathbb{F}_q)$ a finite Chevalley group with Borel $B$; the parameter $q$ is literally the size of the residue field.
- **1965.** Iwahori–Matsumoto extend to $p$-adic groups, producing the affine Hecke algebra.
- **1968.** Bourbaki, *Groupes et algèbres de Lie* IV–VI: Coxeter combinatorics, exponents, Poincaré polynomials.
- **1979.** Kazhdan–Lusztig bases $\{C_w\}$ and cells; the first structural invariants sensitive to $W$ rather than $|W|$.
- **1986–87.** Dipper–James: representation theory of $\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{S}_n)$ at roots of unity; $e$ emerges as the governing invariant.
- **1989.** Gyoja–Uno settle semisimplicity for all finite $W$.
- **1996–2007.** Graham–Lehrer cellularity; Geck proves *all* finite-type Hecke algebras are cellular; Ariki's theorem links decomposition numbers to canonical bases of $U_q(\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_e)$.
- **2003.** Lusztig, *Hecke algebras with unequal parameters*: the asymptotic ring $J$ and the conjectural properties **P1–P15**, which for equal parameters give an isomorphism $\mathcal{H}\otimes_A \mathbb{Q}(v)\;\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\;J\otimes\mathbb{Q}(v)$, a parameter-independent model.
- **2012–present.** Solleveld's classification of irreducible modules for affine Hecke algebras with unequal parameters gives Morita-type comparisons across parameter families.

**Status.** Generic case: settled (Tits) but degenerate — it collapses all $q$. Semisimple specializations: settled. Non-semisimple specializations and integral forms: open in general; solved in type $A$ modulo decomposition numbers.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **Generic parameters, $W$ finite:** $\mathcal{H}_K(W)\cong K[W]$ for any splitting field of characteristic $0$ with $q$ not a root of unity. Complete (Tits deformation theorem).
- **Semisimple specializations, all finite types:** $\mathcal{H}_K(W;q)$ semisimple $\iff P_W(q)\neq0$; when semisimple it is isomorphic to $K[W]$, hence to $\mathcal{H}_K(W;q')$ for every other semisimple $q'$. Gyoja–Uno 1989.
- **Type $A_{n-1}$, all $q$:** $\mathcal{H}_K(\mathfrak{S}_n;q)$ over $K$ of characteristic $0$ is determined up to isomorphism by $(n,e)$, $e=$ quantum characteristic; simple modules are indexed by $e$-regular partitions of $n$ (Dipper–James), so $e\le n$ is recoverable from the algebra. Consequently $\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{S}_n;q)\cong\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{S}_n;q')$ over $\mathbb{C}$ iff $e(q)=e(q')$ or both $>n$.
- **Rank $\le 2$ and dihedral $I_2(m)$:** full explicit decomposition of $\mathcal{H}$ into matrix blocks; the multiset of simple-module dimensions separates parameter orbits.
- **Ariki–Koike / $G(r,1,n)$ (a wider family containing types $A$, $B$):** semisimplicity criterion in terms of $\prod_{i<j}\prod_{|d|<n}(q^d Q_i-Q_j)\prod_{i=1}^n[i]_q \neq 0$ (Ariki 1994) — parameters are recoverable up to explicit symmetries in the semisimple range.
- **Coxeter side.** Rigidity of $W$ itself (does the abstract group determine $(W,S)$?) is known for right-angled, even, and $2$-spherical cases and fails in general (Brady–McCammond–Mühlherr–Neumann 2002) — any full solution must respect these Coxeter-level coincidences, e.g. $W(I_2(6))\cong W(A_1)\times W(A_2)\rtimes$-type accidents in low rank.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Deformation collapse.** Tits' theorem makes the generic algebra parameter-blind: over $\mathbb{Q}(v)$ every finite-type $\mathcal{H}$ is a group algebra, so the problem has content only for integral forms or non-generic specializations. Standard Morita/character techniques see only the split semisimple picture and give no traction.
- **Non-semisimple invariants are unknown.** Below the semisimplicity threshold the isomorphism type is governed by decomposition matrices, which for types $E_7,E_8$ and for characteristic $\ell\mid |W|$ are not fully computed. Recovering $e$ or $\{q_s\}$ from block theory presupposes data that does not exist.
- **Unequal parameters break positivity.** Lusztig's conjectures P1–P15 are proved for equal parameters via geometry (intersection cohomology of Schubert varieties gives positivity of KL structure constants). No geometric model exists for arbitrary $\{q_s\}$, so the asymptotic ring $J$ — the natural parameter-free invariant — is not available in general.
- **Infinite $W$.** For infinite Coxeter groups $\mathcal{H}$ has no trace-form finiteness, no Wedderburn theory, and no analogue of $P_W(q)$; even the ring-theoretic invariants (centre, $K_0$) are hard to compute.
- **Coxeter-level non-rigidity.** Since $W$ can fail to determine $(W,S)$, the Hecke problem inherits genuine counterexample candidates and cannot be reduced to a group-theoretic statement.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: the semisimple regime is a single isomorphism class per $W$ (Section 4), and in type $A$ the invariant $(n,e)$ is complete. Conjectured: over $A=\mathbb{Z}[v^{\pm1}]$, $\mathcal{H}_A(W,S)\cong\mathcal{H}_A(W',S')$ implies $(W,S)\cong(W',S')$ up to diagram automorphism, with parameters matched up to $v\mapsto v^{-1}$.

The precise missing step: **a parameter-detecting invariant valid outside the semisimple range and outside type $A$.** Concretely, one needs a functorial invariant $I(\mathcal{H})$ — candidates are the centre $Z(\mathcal{H})$ as a graded ring, the Hochschild cohomology $HH^\bullet(\mathcal{H})$, or the two-sided cell decomposition with its $\mathbf{a}$-function — that (i) is computable for all finite types, and (ii) reconstructs $|S|$, the Coxeter matrix $m(s,t)$, and the orbit of $\mathbf{q}$. At present the KL cell structure is *defined* via $W$ and no proof exists that it is intrinsic to the algebra.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Asymptotic algebras beyond equal parameters.** Extending P1–P15 to unequal parameters, notably for type $B_n$ with asymptotic parameter regimes; work in the Lusztig–Bonnafé–Geck lineage (Aberdeen, Kaiserslautern, Besançon). *(frontier — verify)*
- **Categorification.** KLR/quiver Hecke algebras (Khovanov–Lauda, Rouquier) are graded and isomorphic to blocks of cyclotomic Hecke algebras via Brundan–Kleshchev; grading gives finer invariants than the ungraded ring, and there is active work on whether graded isomorphism forces parameter equality. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Affine and $p$-adic side.** Solleveld and collaborators (Nijmegen) compare affine Hecke algebras with different parameters via Morita equivalence and topological $K$-theory, motivated by the local Langlands correspondence for Bernstein blocks.
- **Homological invariants.** Computation of $HH^\bullet$ and of the Calabi–Yau / symmetric-algebra structure for small-rank Hecke algebras as candidate parameter detectors.

## 8. Future Work

- Prove that the two-sided cell partition and the $\mathbf{a}$-function are Morita/derived invariants of $\mathcal{H}$; this would recover $W$'s cell datum from the algebra alone.
- Complete decomposition matrices for $E_7$, $E_8$ at all $e$ and all $\ell$, closing the type-by-type route.
- Settle Lusztig's P1–P15 for unequal parameters, giving a uniform $J$-model in which the parameters appear only through an explicit change of basis.
- Treat the integral form: classify $\mathbb{Z}[v^{\pm1}]$-algebra isomorphisms directly, where Tits' collapse does not apply.
- Develop invariants for infinite (affine, hyperbolic) $W$, where $K$-theory of the completed algebra is the most promising handle.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take $W=\mathfrak{S}_3$ of type $A_2$, $S=\{s_1,s_2\}$, $m(s_1,s_2)=3$. Then $\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{S}_3;q)$ has $\dim=6$, basis $\{1,T_1,T_2,T_1T_2,T_2T_1,T_1T_2T_1\}$, relations
$$T_1T_2T_1=T_2T_1T_2,\qquad (T_i-q)(T_i+1)=0 .$$

**Poincaré polynomial.** Degrees are $2,3$, so
$$P_W(q)=\frac{q^2-1}{q-1}\cdot\frac{q^3-1}{q-1}=(1+q)(1+q+q^2)=1+2q+2q^2+q^3 .$$

**Semisimple range.** By Gyoja–Uno, $\mathcal{H}$ is semisimple iff $q\neq-1$ and $q$ is not a primitive cube root of unity. Then
$$\mathcal{H}\;\cong\;\mathbb{C}\oplus M_2(\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathbb{C}\;\cong\;\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_3],$$
with $1+4+1=6$. The one-dimensional modules are the *index* $T_i\mapsto q$ and the *sign* $T_i\mapsto -1$; the $2$-dimensional module is the deformed standard representation. **Every** semisimple $q$ gives the same algebra: the parameter is invisible.

**Degenerate specializations.**

- $q=-1$ ($e=2$): $2$-regular partitions of $3$ are $(3)$ and $(2,1)$. Here $S^{(2,1)}$ (dimension $2$) has composition factors $D^{(2,1)}$ and $D^{(3)}$, both of dimension $1$; $S^{(1^3)}\cong D^{(3)}$. So the simple modules have dimensions $\{1,1\}$ and $\dim\mathrm{rad}\,\mathcal{H}=6-2=4$.
- $q=\omega$, $\omega^3=1$, $\omega\neq1$ ($e=3$): $3$-regular partitions are $(3),(2,1)$; here $S^{(2,1)}=D^{(2,1)}$ stays irreducible of dimension $2$, and $S^{(1^3)}\cong D^{(3)}$. Simple-module dimensions $\{1,2\}$, and $\mathcal{H}/\mathrm{rad}\cong \mathbb{C}\oplus M_2(\mathbb{C})$, $\dim\mathrm{rad}=1$.

**Conclusion of the example.** The multisets $\{1,1\}$ and $\{1,2\}$ differ, so
$$\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{S}_3;-1)\;\not\cong\;\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{S}_3;\omega),$$
while all semisimple parameters coincide. Meanwhile the map $T_i\mapsto -q^{-1}T_i$ is an isomorphism $\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{S}_3;q)\cong\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{S}_3;q^{-1})$: it preserves the braid relation (scaling both generators equally) and sends the eigenvalue pair $\{q,-1\}$ to $\{-1,q^{-1}\}$. This is exactly the pattern the general conjecture asserts — parameters matter only through the quantum characteristic $e$, modulo $q\leftrightarrow q^{-1}$ — and exactly what nobody can prove uniformly beyond type $A$.
