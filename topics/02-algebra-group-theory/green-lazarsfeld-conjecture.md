# Green-Lazarsfeld Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/green-lazarsfeld-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

"Green–Lazarsfeld conjecture" names a family of statements about the **syzygies of an algebraic curve embedded by a line bundle**. The one still open — and the one meant here — is the **secant conjecture**, stated by Green and Lazarsfeld in the appendix to Green's 1984 Koszul cohomology paper and sharpened in their 1986 *Inventiones* paper.

**Conjecture (Green–Lazarsfeld secant).** Let $C$ be a smooth irreducible complex projective curve of genus $g$ and $L$ a globally generated line bundle on $C$ with
$$\deg L \;\ge\; 2g + p + 1 - 2h^1(C,L) - \operatorname{Cliff}(C).$$
Then $L$ **fails property $N_p$ if and only if $L$ is not $(p+2)$-very ample**, i.e. if and only if there exists an effective divisor $D$ on $C$ of degree $p+2$ with
$$h^0(C, L(-D)) \;\ge\; h^0(C,L) - p - 1 .$$

Geometrically: the embedded curve $\varphi_L(C)\subset \mathbb{P}^r$, $r=h^0(L)-1$, has a **$(p+2)$-secant $p$-plane** — $p+2$ points spanning only a $p$-plane instead of a $(p+1)$-plane.

The "only if" direction (secant $\Rightarrow$ failure of $N_p$) is a **theorem** of Green–Lazarsfeld. The open content is the converse: *the only obstruction to $p$ steps of linear syzygies, in the stated degree range, is a secant plane.* A proof must establish the Koszul vanishing $K_{p,2}(C;L)=0$ for every $(p+2)$-very ample $L$ in the range; a disproof requires one curve and one such $L$ with $K_{p,2}(C;L)\neq 0$.

Two siblings should not be confused with it: the **Green conjecture** on canonical curves (the case $L=K_C$; open in general, known for general curves by Voisin) and the **Green–Lazarsfeld gonality conjecture** (a theorem since Ein–Lazarsfeld 2015).

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Let $L$ be a line bundle on $C$ with $V=H^0(C,L)$, $r=\dim V-1$, and let $S=\operatorname{Sym}^\bullet V$ be the coordinate ring of $\mathbb{P}^r=\mathbb{P}(V)$. Set
$$R(C,L) \;=\; \bigoplus_{q\in\mathbb{Z}} H^0(C, L^{\otimes q}),$$
a graded $S$-module. Its minimal graded free resolution
$$0 \leftarrow R \leftarrow F_0 \leftarrow F_1 \leftarrow \cdots, \qquad F_i=\bigoplus_q S(-i-q)^{\,\beta_{i,q}},$$
has Betti numbers $\beta_{p,q}=\dim K_{p,q}(C;L)$, where the **Koszul cohomology** group $K_{p,q}$ is the middle homology of
$$\textstyle\bigwedge^{p+1} V \otimes H^0(L^{q-1}) \;\longrightarrow\; \bigwedge^{p} V \otimes H^0(L^{q}) \;\longrightarrow\; \bigwedge^{p-1} V \otimes H^0(L^{q+1}).$$

**Property $N_p$.** $L$ satisfies $N_p$ if $R(C,L)$ is generated in degree $\le 1$ and the resolution is linear for $p$ steps; equivalently
$$K_{i,q}(C;L)=0 \quad\text{for all } 0\le i\le p,\; q\ge 2 .$$
$N_0$ = projective normality, $N_1$ = ideal generated by quadrics, $N_2$ = quadrics with linear first syzygies. Since $\deg L$ is large in the stated range, $K_{i,q}=0$ automatically for $q\ge3$, so $N_p$ reduces to the single vanishing $K_{p,2}(C;L)=0$.

**$k$-very ampleness.** $L$ is $k$-very ample if $H^0(L)\to H^0(L|_D)$ is surjective for every effective $D$ with $\deg D=k$, i.e. $h^0(L(-D))=h^0(L)-k$ for all such $D$. Failure of $(p+2)$-very ampleness is exactly the secant condition of §1.

**Clifford index.** For $g\ge 4$,
$$\operatorname{Cliff}(C)=\min\{\deg A-2r(A)\;:\;A\in\operatorname{Pic}(C),\ h^0(A)\ge2,\ h^1(A)\ge2\},$$
with $r(A)=h^0(A)-1$. One has $\operatorname{gon}(C)-3\le\operatorname{Cliff}(C)\le\operatorname{gon}(C)-2$, and $\operatorname{Cliff}(C)=\lfloor (g-1)/2\rfloor$ for a general curve.

**Anchor theorems.**
- *(Green, 1984; Green–Lazarsfeld, 1986)* $\deg L \ge 2g+1+p \Rightarrow L$ satisfies $N_p$.
- *(Green–Lazarsfeld nonvanishing)* If $L=L_1\otimes L_2$ with $h^0(L_i)\ge 2$, then $K_{p,1}(C;L)\neq 0$ for $p=h^0(L_1)+h^0(L_2)-3$. Applying this to $L_1=\mathcal{O}(D)$ produces the secant $\Rightarrow$ failure implication.
- *(Ein–Lazarsfeld, 2015)* For $\deg L\gg 0$, $K_{p,1}(C;L)\neq 0 \iff 1\le p\le r-\operatorname{gon}(C)$ — the gonality conjecture.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1984.** Mark Green, *Koszul cohomology and the geometry of projective varieties* (J. Differential Geom. 19), builds the Koszul machinery and formulates the $K_{p,1}$ theorem. The appendix with Lazarsfeld states the Clifford-index picture and the nonvanishing that seeds both conjectures.
- **1986.** Green–Lazarsfeld, *On the projective normality of complete linear series on an algebraic curve* (Invent. Math. 83): $\deg L\ge 2g+1-2h^1(L)-\operatorname{Cliff}(C)\Rightarrow L$ normally generated unless a $2$-secant point pair (i.e. $L$ not $2$-very ample) intervenes. This is precisely the **$p=0$ case of the secant conjecture, proved**.
- **1986.** Schreyer, *Syzygies of canonical curves and special linear series* (Math. Ann. 275): first systematic computational evidence for syzygy conjectures on curves.
- **2002–2005.** Voisin proves Green's conjecture for general curves of even and odd genus via Hilbert schemes of points on $K3$ surfaces — the technique that dominates all later work.
- **2015.** Ein–Lazarsfeld settle the **gonality conjecture** for $\deg L\gg0$ (Publ. IHÉS 122); Rathmann later makes the bound effective.
- **2016.** Farkas–Kemeny prove the **generic secant conjecture** (Invent. Math. 203), the largest advance to date.
- **2017–2019.** Kemeny handles the extremal degree case for arbitrary gonality; Farkas–Kemeny extend linear-syzygy results to curves of prescribed gonality.

Status as of 2026: proved for $p=0$; proved for general $(C,L)$; proved in the extremal degree; **open for arbitrary special line bundles on arbitrary curves**.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **$p=0$ (projective normality), all curves, all $L$ in range** — Green–Lazarsfeld 1986.
- **High degree, $\deg L\ge 2g+1+p$** — Green's theorem gives $N_p$ unconditionally, and $(p+2)$-very ampleness is automatic; the conjecture is a theorem but vacuous of secants.
- **Extremal degree $\deg L = 2g+p+1-2h^1(L)-\operatorname{Cliff}(C)$, curves of arbitrary gonality** — Kemeny, *Compositio Math.* 153 (2017).
- **Generic case:** for a general curve $[C]\in\mathcal{M}_g$ and a general line bundle $L$ of degree $d$ in the conjecture's range, $L$ satisfies $N_p$ — Farkas–Kemeny, *Invent. Math.* 203 (2016). Proved by degeneration and by exhibiting the locus of counterexamples as a virtual divisor on a universal Brill–Noether space, then showing the divisor class is not effective.
- **$L=K_C$** reduces to Green's conjecture $K_{p,2}(C;K_C)=0$ for $p<\operatorname{Cliff}(C)$, known for: general curves of every genus (Voisin), curves on $K3$ surfaces, all curves with $\operatorname{Cliff}(C)\le 4$, and $d$-gonal curves with $g\ge 4d-7$ (Aprodu).
- **Small genus, computational.** Betti tables of curves of genus $g\le 15$ over finite fields and over $\mathbb{Q}$ have been computed in Macaulay2 (Schreyer school; Bopp–Schreyer, *Experimental Mathematics*, 2021) with no counterexample found. Positive characteristic is a separate story: Schreyer's char-2 and char-3 examples show related canonical-curve statements can fail.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Vanishing is much harder than nonvanishing.** Producing a nonzero Koszul class is a construction (Green–Lazarsfeld's $L_1\otimes L_2$ trick). Proving $K_{p,2}=0$ requires controlling a cohomology group with no functorial vanishing theorem behind it; Kodaira-type vanishing does not see the exterior-algebra grading.
- **Voisin's $K3$ method does not transfer.** The Hilbert-scheme-of-points computation on a $K3$ surface works because $K_C$ is the restriction of a line bundle whose sections are governed by the surface. A general $L$ of intermediate degree is not the restriction of anything on a surface with computable cohomology, so the whole apparatus is unavailable.
- **Degeneration loses semicontinuity in the wrong direction.** Betti numbers can only jump up under specialization, so a singular/ribbon degeneration proves vanishing only if the special fibre already has the vanishing — and the natural special fibres (nodal, stable, ribbons) tend to acquire extra secants.
- **Divisor-class arguments are confined to $\rho=0$.** Farkas–Kemeny's proof works because the locus of bad $(C,L)$ has expected codimension one on a space where the Picard group and effective cone are understood. Off the Brill–Noether-general locus the parameter space is singular and its cone of effective divisors is unknown, so "virtual divisor is not effective" cannot even be formulated.
- **The Clifford index enters as a min over infinitely many bundles.** The hypothesis $\deg L\ge 2g+p+1-2h^1-\operatorname{Cliff}(C)$ couples the bundle being tested to a global invariant of $C$; no local or deformation-theoretic argument sees this coupling.

## 6. The Gap

Proved: $p=0$; the extremal degree; general $(C,L)$; $L=K_C$ for special classes. Conjectured: all $p$, all curves, all globally generated $L$ in the range.

The exact missing step is a **uniform vanishing statement**: given $C$ arbitrary and $L$ globally generated with $\deg L\ge 2g+p+1-2h^1(L)-\operatorname{Cliff}(C)$ and $L$ $(p+2)$-very ample, show
$$K_{p,2}(C;L)=0 .$$
Every current proof supplies this only after either (a) fixing $p=0$, where a direct base-point-free-pencil-trick argument closes it, (b) putting $\deg L$ at the extreme of the range, where the secant locus is $0$-dimensional and countable, or (c) making $(C,L)$ generic, where a codimension count replaces the vanishing. No argument yet converts $(p+2)$-very ampleness — a statement about $\operatorname{Sym}^{p+2}C$ — into a statement about the $p$-th exterior power of $H^0(L)$ for arbitrary $C$.

- **Farkas (Humboldt) and Kemeny (Wisconsin–Madison)** continue the Koszul-divisor programme, aiming to relax the genericity in the 2016 theorem to "$C$ arbitrary, $L$ general in $\operatorname{Pic}^d(C)$". *(frontier — verify)*
- **Ein–Lazarsfeld and collaborators** develop asymptotic syzygy methods (secant varieties of the embedded curve, Hilbert schemes $C^{[k]}$, and the tautological bundles $E_{k,L}$ with $K_{p,q}$ realized as cohomology of $\wedge^p E_{k,L}$). This "vector bundle on $C^{[k]}$" formalism is the most promising uniform route.
- **Aprodu, Farkas, Papadima, Raicu, Weyman** — the representation-theoretic/Koszul-module school; their proof of Green's conjecture for generic curves in almost all characteristics (Publ. IHÉS, 2019) suggests characteristic-free tools that may apply to the secant conjecture. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Future Work

1. **Secant-variety reformulation.** Express $K_{p,2}(C;L)$ as cohomology on the $(p+1)$-st secant variety or on $C^{[p+2]}$, so that $(p+2)$-very ampleness becomes an embedding statement whose failure is literally the only obstruction (Ein–Lazarsfeld programme).
2. **Interpolate between $p=0$ and general $p$.** Settle $p=1$ (ideal generated by quadrics) for all curves in the full Clifford range; this is the smallest case where the base-point-free pencil trick fails.
3. **Positive characteristic.** Determine whether the secant conjecture, like Green's conjecture, admits characteristic-$p$ counterexamples for small $p$; a counterexample would sharpen the char-0 hypotheses.
4. **Relative/uniform degenerations.** Build degenerations of $(C,L)$ to chains of elliptic curves or to $\ell$-gonal ribbons in which both the Betti table and the secant loci are computable.
5. **Extend to higher dimensions.** Formulate and test a secant conjecture for $N_p$ of polarized surfaces, where already $p=0$ is delicate.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Claim: for $L=K_C$ the secant conjecture is exactly Green's conjecture.**

Let $C$ be non-hyperelliptic of genus $g\ge 4$ and $L=K_C$, so $\deg L=2g-2$, $h^0=g$, $h^1=1$, $r=g-1$.

*Range check.* The hypothesis $\deg L\ge 2g+p+1-2h^1-\operatorname{Cliff}(C)$ reads
$$2g-2 \;\ge\; 2g+p+1-2-\operatorname{Cliff}(C) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad p \le \operatorname{Cliff}(C)-1 .$$

*Translating the secant condition.* $K_C$ fails $(p+2)$-very ampleness iff there is an effective $D$ with $\deg D=p+2$ and $h^0(K_C-D)\ge h^0(K_C)-p-1=g-p-1$. By Riemann–Roch,
$$h^0(K_C-D)=h^0(D)-\deg D+g-1=h^0(D)+g-p-3 .$$
So the secant condition is $h^0(D)+g-p-3\ge g-p-1$, i.e. $h^0(D)\ge 2$: **$D$ moves in a pencil of degree $p+2$**. Such a pencil would give $\operatorname{Cliff}(C)\le (p+2)-2=p$, contradicting $p\le\operatorname{Cliff}(C)-1$. Hence in this range $K_C$ is always $(p+2)$-very ample, and the secant conjecture asserts
$$K_{p,2}(C;K_C)=0 \quad\text{for all } p<\operatorname{Cliff}(C),$$
which is precisely Green's conjecture.

**Numerical instance: $g=6$, $p=1$.** Let $C$ be a general curve of genus $6$: it is neither trigonal nor a smooth plane quintic, so $\operatorname{Cliff}(C)=\lfloor 5/2\rfloor=2$ and $p=1\le \operatorname{Cliff}-1$. The canonical curve sits in $\mathbb{P}^5$ with $\deg=10$. Property $N_1$ says the homogeneous ideal $I_C$ is generated by quadrics. Counting: $h^0(\mathbb{P}^5,\mathcal{O}(2))=\binom{7}{2}=21$ and $h^0(C,2K_C)=3g-3=15$, so $\dim (I_C)_2 = 21-15=6$ quadrics. The conjecture predicts these $6$ quadrics cut out $C$ — true, and classically visible: a general genus-6 canonical curve lies on a quintic del Pezzo surface in $\mathbb{P}^5$ cut by $5$ quadrics, with one further quadric carving out $C$.

**Where it would break.** If instead $C$ is trigonal ($\operatorname{Cliff}(C)=1$), take $D$ the $g^1_3$ divisor, $p=1$: $h^0(D)=2$, so $K_C$ has a $3$-secant line, and indeed $K_{1,2}(C;K_C)\neq 0$ — the canonical trigonal curve lies on a rational normal scroll and is *not* cut out by quadrics. Both directions of the conjecture are visible in this single pair of examples.
