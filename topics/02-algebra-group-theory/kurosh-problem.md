# Kurosh Problem

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/kurosh-problem` · **Status:** solved-recently

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Kurosh (1941) asked the ring-theoretic analogue of the Burnside problem:

> **Kurosh Problem.** Let $k$ be a field and let $A$ be a finitely generated (affine) associative $k$-algebra that is **algebraic** over $k$ — every $a \in A$ satisfies a nonzero polynomial $p(a)=0$ with $p \in k[x]$. Must $\dim_k A < \infty$?

Equivalently: is every algebraic algebra **locally finite** (every finitely generated subalgebra finite-dimensional)? The special case where every element is nilpotent (a **nil** algebra) is the *nil Kurosh problem*.

The general answer is **no**: Golod and Shafarevich (1964) constructed, over any field, a $3$-generated infinite-dimensional nil algebra. A complete resolution of the surviving programme requires deciding, for each natural subclass, whether algebraicity forces local finiteness. The principal case still open is the **division-ring Kurosh problem** (Kaplansky's question): must a finitely generated division algebra $D$, algebraic over its centre $Z(D)$, satisfy $\dim_{Z(D)} D < \infty$?

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Fix a field $k$ and $A$ an associative $k$-algebra, not necessarily unital.

- $A$ is **algebraic** if for all $a\in A$ there is $0\neq p\in k[x]$ with $p(a)=0$; **algebraic of bounded degree $n$** if $p$ may always be chosen with $\deg p \le n$.
- $A$ is **nil** if $\forall a\ \exists m$ with $a^m=0$; **nil of bounded index $n$** if $a^n=0$ for all $a$; **nilpotent** if $A^m=0$ for some $m$; **locally nilpotent** if every finitely generated subalgebra is nilpotent.
- $A$ satisfies a **polynomial identity (PI)** if some nonzero $f\in k\langle x_1,\dots,x_m\rangle$ vanishes under all substitutions from $A$.
- For affine $A$ with generating subspace $V \ni 1$, $V_n = (k+V)^n$, the **Gelfand–Kirillov dimension** is
$$\operatorname{GKdim}(A) \;=\; \limsup_{n\to\infty} \frac{\log \dim_k V_n}{\log n}.$$

**Graded Golod–Shafarevich inequality.** Let $B = k\langle x_1,\dots,x_d\rangle/I$ with $I$ generated by homogeneous elements, $r_i$ of degree $i$ ($r_0=r_1=0$), and $H_B(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}(\dim_k B_n)\,t^n$. Then, coefficientwise in $\mathbb{Z}[[t]]$,
$$H_B(t)\cdot\Bigl(1 - dt + \sum_{i\ge 2} r_i t^i\Bigr) \;\succeq\; 1 .$$
Consequently, if there exists $t_0\in(0,1)$ with $1 - d t_0 + \sum_{i\ge2} r_i t_0^{\,i} \le 0$, then $\dim_k B = \infty$.

**Theorems the problem rests on.**
1. *(Levitzki 1946; Kaplansky 1948)* A nil PI-algebra is locally nilpotent; an algebraic PI-algebra is locally finite.
2. *(Jacobson 1945)* An algebra algebraic of bounded degree $n$ over a field satisfies the identities of $n\times n$ matrices and is locally finite.
3. *(Shirshov height theorem, 1957)* If $A$ is generated by $\{a_1,\dots,a_d\}$ and satisfies a PI of degree $n$, then $A$ is spanned by products $u_1^{k_1}\cdots u_h^{k_h}$ with $u_j$ words of length $< n$ and $h \le H(d,n)$ — giving explicit dimension bounds in the bounded-degree case.
4. *(Amitsur 1956)* If $\dim_k A < |k|$ and $k$ is uncountable, the Jacobson radical $J(A)$ is nil — the bridge from Kurosh-type questions to the Köthe conjecture.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1941.** A. G. Kurosh poses the problem in *Izvestiya AN SSSR*, explicitly as the ring analogue of Burnside's periodic-group problem.
- **1945–48.** Jacobson settles bounded degree; Levitzki and Kaplansky settle the PI case. Kaplansky's structure theorem for primitive PI-rings ($A \cong M_n(D)$, $D$ a division ring finite over its centre) becomes the engine.
- **1957.** Shirshov's height theorem makes the PI answer effective.
- **1964.** Golod and Shafarevich publish the inequality; Golod deduces a finitely generated infinite-dimensional nil algebra over any field, and hence an infinite finitely generated periodic $p$-group — simultaneously refuting Kurosh and the general Burnside problem.
- **1979–91.** Zelmanov transports the positive theory to nonassociative settings: nil Jordan algebras of bounded index are locally nilpotent; Engel Lie algebras of bounded index are locally nilpotent, which underpins the solution of the restricted Burnside problem (Fields Medal, 1994).
- **2007.** Lenagan and Smoktunowicz (*JAMS*) construct an infinite-dimensional affine nil algebra of **finite** GK dimension, breaking the belief that nil counterexamples must grow exponentially.
- **2012.** Lenagan, Smoktunowicz and Young push the growth down to $\operatorname{GKdim}\le 3$.
- **Present.** Positive side complete for PI, bounded degree, GK dimension $\le 1$, Jordan and Engel-Lie settings; negative side complete for general associative algebras. Open: division rings, growth exactly $2$, and Köthe.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No identity to exploit.** Every positive result routes through a polynomial identity: PI gives Kaplansky's structure theory and Shirshov's combinatorics on words. Pointwise algebraicity is a $\forall a\,\exists p_a$ statement with no uniformity in $\deg p_a$, so no single multilinear identity can be extracted. Compactness/ultraproduct arguments fail because degrees are unbounded along the ultrafilter.
- **Radical theory is blocked by Köthe.** For $A$ nil, $A=J(A)$, and the natural strategy — build $A$ from nilpotent pieces — needs the Köthe conjecture (the sum of two nil left ideals is nil), itself open since 1930.
- **Growth is not an obstruction below dimension 3.** After Lenagan–Smoktunowicz, small GK dimension no longer implies local nilpotence, so counting arguments on $\dim V_n$ cannot by themselves force finiteness.
- **Division rings resist Golod–Shafarevich.** The construction produces algebras that are far from domains; localising a nil-free algebraic algebra at all nonzero elements requires an Ore condition that no known counterexample construction respects.
- **Characteristic sensitivity.** Nagata–Higman collapses in characteristic $p \le n$, and the Lie counterexamples of Petrogradsky and Shestakov–Zelmanov are purely characteristic-$p$ phenomena driven by self-similar (fractal) algebras; there is no characteristic-free machine.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: local finiteness under any *uniform* constraint — bounded degree, a PI, $\operatorname{GKdim}\le1$, bounded nil index in good characteristic. Refuted: the unrestricted statement, and the restriction to $\operatorname{GKdim}\le3$.

The residual gap has three sharp edges:

1. **Division rings.** Is there a finitely generated division $k$-algebra $D$, algebraic over $Z(D)$, with $\dim_{Z(D)} D = \infty$? A counterexample would need a nil-free Golod–Shafarevich-type object; a proof would need a substitute for Kaplansky's theorem valid without a PI.
2. **Growth exactly 2.** Does there exist an infinite-dimensional affine nil algebra with $\operatorname{GKdim} = 2$? The interval $(1,3]$ is now settled at the top; $(1,2]$ is not.
3. **Köthe.** Equivalent formulation: is $M_2(N)$ nil for every nil ring $N$? Its resolution would reorganise the whole positive theory.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Edinburgh / Smoktunowicz school.** Continued construction of nil and Jacobson-radical algebras with prescribed growth functions; a persistent target is realising every growth function above quadratic by a nil algebra *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Virginia (Ershov) and Golod–Shafarevich groups.** Ershov's programme on Golod–Shafarevich groups (property $(T)$, torsion quotients, Galois-theoretic applications) keeps the negative side of Kurosh in active use.
- **UCSD / Simon Fraser (Small, Bell).** Growth-theoretic attacks on Kaplansky's division-algebra question via GK dimension of Ore extensions and free subalgebras of quotient division rings.
- **Nonassociative frontier.** Self-similar and fractal Lie/Jordan algebras (Petrogradsky, Shestakov, Bartholdi) yielding restricted Lie algebras that are nil but not locally nilpotent, with ongoing work on Jordan superalgebra analogues *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Computational algebra.** Gröbner–Shirshov basis and Hilbert-series verification of Golod–Shafarevich-type presentations, used to certify infinite-dimensionality of explicit quotients.

## 8. Future Work

- Decide whether a nil affine algebra of $\operatorname{GKdim}=2$ exists; Bell's monomial-algebra techniques are the most promising route.
- Attack Kaplansky's division-algebra question through GK dimension: prove that a finitely generated algebraic division algebra must have finite GK dimension, then invoke Small–Stafford–Warfield-type dichotomies.
- Sharpen Shirshov height bounds (Belov–Kharitonov and successors) to obtain subexponential dimension estimates in the bounded-degree Kurosh theorem.
- Test Köthe in the graded setting, where Golod–Shafarevich methods are strongest.
- Transfer the self-similar constructions of characteristic $p$ Lie algebras back to associative algebras to control growth more finely.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Goal.** Exhibit the arithmetic behind Golod's counterexample with $d=3$ generators.

Let $F = k\langle x_1,x_2,x_3\rangle$ be the free algebra without constant term, graded by degree, $\dim F_n = 3^n$. Suppose we impose homogeneous relations with $r_i \le 1$ relation in each degree $i\ge2$, and set $B=F/I$.

The Golod–Shafarevich series is
$$g(t) \;=\; 1 - 3t + \sum_{i\ge 2} r_i t^i \;\le\; 1 - 3t + \frac{t^2}{1-t}\qquad (0<t<1).$$
Evaluate at $t_0 = \tfrac12$:
$$g(\tfrac12) \;\le\; 1 - \tfrac32 + \frac{(1/2)^2}{1/2} \;=\; 1 - 1.5 + 0.5 \;=\; 0 .$$
Since $g(t_0)\le 0$ while $H_B(t_0) > 0$ termwise, the inequality $H_B(t)g(t)\succeq 1$ cannot hold with $H_B$ a polynomial: a finite-dimensional $B$ would give $H_B(t_0)g(t_0)\le 0 < 1$. Hence $\dim_k B = \infty$.

**Making $B$ nil.** Assume $k$ countable and enumerate the elements of $F$ as $f_1,f_2,\dots$. Process them in order. Having used up all degrees $< n_j$, write $f_j = \sum_{m} f_j^{(m)}$ in homogeneous components and choose an exponent $N_j$ so large that every homogeneous component of $f_j^{N_j}$ has degree $\ge n_j$. Adjoin all those components as relations. Their degrees lie in a window; choosing the $N_j$ to grow fast enough, one arranges $r_i \le 1$ for every $i$, so the count above still applies. In the quotient $B$, each $f_j$ satisfies $f_j^{N_j} = 0$, so $B$ is nil, and $B$ is generated by three elements.

**Reading off the conclusion.** $B$ is a $3$-generated nil (hence algebraic) $k$-algebra with $\dim_k B = \infty$: the Kurosh problem is false as stated. Adjoining $1$ and taking $1+B$ inside $k+B$ over $k=\mathbb{F}_p$ gives an infinite finitely generated $p$-group, refuting the general Burnside problem by the same computation.

Contrast with the bounded case: if instead every $a\in B$ satisfied $a^2=0$, Nagata–Higman in characteristic $0$ would force $B^{3}=0$, so $B$ would be spanned by words of length $\le 2$ in three generators, giving $\dim_k B \le 3 + 9 = 12$. The whole difficulty of Kurosh lives in the passage from a *uniform* bound to a *pointwise* one.
