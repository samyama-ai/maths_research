# Burnside Problem

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/burnside-problem` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Burnside asked in 1902 whether a finitely generated group in which every element has finite order must be finite. The question splits into three distinct problems.

- **General Burnside Problem (GBP).** If $G$ is finitely generated and every $g \in G$ has finite order (a *torsion* group), must $|G| < \infty$? **Answer: no** (Golod–Shafarevich, 1964).
- **Bounded Burnside Problem (BBP).** Fix $m \ge 2$ and $n \ge 1$. Is the free Burnside group
$$B(m,n) \;=\; F_m / \langle\langle\, w^n : w \in F_m \,\rangle\rangle$$
finite? Here $F_m$ is the free group of rank $m$ and $\langle\langle \cdot \rangle\rangle$ denotes normal closure. **Answer: no in general**, but the set of $n$ for which it is finite is not known.
- **Restricted Burnside Problem (RBP).** Are there only finitely many finite groups of rank $m$ and exponent $n$ up to isomorphism — equivalently, is the maximal finite quotient $R(m,n)$ of $B(m,n)$ finite? **Answer: yes** (Zelmanov, 1990–91).

The open residue is the exact determination of the finiteness set
$$\mathcal{F} \;=\; \{\, n \ge 1 \;:\; B(m,n) \text{ is finite for all } m \,\}.$$
Known: $\{1,2,3,4,6\} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$; every odd $n \ge 101$ and every $n \ge 8000$ divisible by $2^9$ lies outside $\mathcal{F}$. A complete solution must decide each remaining exponent, the smallest being $n = 5$: **is $B(2,5)$ finite?**

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Let $F_m$ be free of rank $m$ with basis $x_1,\dots,x_m$, and $F_m^n = \langle w^n : w \in F_m\rangle$ its verbal subgroup for the word $x^n$. Then $B(m,n) = F_m/F_m^n$ is the relatively free group in the variety $\mathfrak{B}_n$ of groups satisfying the identity $x^n = 1$; every $m$-generated group of exponent dividing $n$ is a quotient of $B(m,n)$.

**Restricted quotient.** Let $\mathcal{N}(m,n)$ be the intersection of all normal subgroups of finite index in $B(m,n)$. Then $R(m,n) = B(m,n)/\mathcal{N}(m,n)$, and RBP asks whether $|R(m,n)| < \infty$.

**Reduction (Hall–Higman, 1956).** If $n = p_1^{a_1}\cdots p_k^{a_k}$, then RBP for $n$ follows from RBP for each $p_i^{a_i}$, granted the solubility of the outer automorphism groups of finite simple groups (the Schreier conjecture, now a consequence of CFSG).

**Lie-theoretic core.** For a finite $p$-group $G$ of exponent $p^k$, the associated graded Lie ring $L(G) = \bigoplus_i \gamma_i(G)/\gamma_{i+1}(G)$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$ satisfies an *Engel identity*: for $p$-groups of exponent $p$,
$$\mathrm{ad}(a)^{\,p-1} \;=\; 0 \qquad \text{for all } a \in L(G),$$
i.e. $[b,\underbrace{a,\dots,a}_{p-1}] = 0$. Kostrikin's theorem states that a Lie algebra over a field of characteristic $p$ generated by $m$ elements and satisfying the $(p-1)$-Engel condition is nilpotent of class bounded by $f(m,p)$; Zelmanov extended this to Engel Lie algebras in all characteristics and to the graded structures arising from exponent $p^k$, using superalgebra identities and the theory of PI-algebras (Shirshov's height theorem, Kaplansky–Amitsur).

**Free Burnside orders.** For $n = 3$, Burnside computed
$$|B(m,3)| \;=\; 3^{\,m + \binom{m}{2} + \binom{m}{3}},$$
with nilpotency class $\le 3$.

**Infiniteness criterion.** Novikov–Adian and Ol'shanskii construct $B(m,n)$ for large $n$ as a direct limit of hyperbolic-like groups $G_0 = F_m \twoheadrightarrow G_1 \twoheadrightarrow \cdots$, where $G_{i+1} = G_i/\langle\langle A_i^n \rangle\rangle$ kills the $n$-th powers of period-$i$ words, and a graded small-cancellation condition guarantees that no relation of rank $\le i$ collapses, forcing $|B(m,n)| = \infty$.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1902.** W. Burnside, *On an unsettled question in the theory of discontinuous groups*, poses the problem and settles $n = 3$ and $m$ arbitrary, plus all $n$ for linear groups of degree $\le 2$.
- **1905–1911.** Burnside and Schur: torsion subgroups of $GL_d(\mathbb{C})$ of bounded exponent are finite (Burnside); finitely generated torsion linear groups are finite (Schur). So no counterexample is linear.
- **1940.** Sanov: $B(m,4)$ finite.
- **1956.** Hall–Higman: prime-power reduction for RBP.
- **1958.** M. Hall: $B(m,6)$ finite.
- **1959.** Kostrikin: RBP for prime exponent $p$.
- **1964.** Golod–Shafarevich: infinite finitely generated torsion $p$-groups — GBP is false.
- **1968.** Novikov–Adian: $B(m,n)$ infinite for odd $n \ge 4381$, $m \ge 2$ — a $\sim$300-page combinatorial induction.
- **1975/1979.** Adian: threshold lowered to odd $n \ge 665$.
- **1982.** Ol'shanskii: geometric (van Kampen diagram) proof for odd $n > 10^{10}$, later the engine for Tarski monsters.
- **1990–91.** Zelmanov solves RBP for odd exponent and for $2$-groups; Fields Medal 1994.
- **1994/1996.** Ivanov and, independently, Lysënok settle even exponents: $B(m,n)$ infinite for $n \ge 2^{48}$ with $2^9 \mid n$ (Ivanov), and for all $n \ge 8000$ (Lysënok).
- **2015.** Adian: odd $n \ge 101$ suffices — the current record threshold.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

**Finite cases (complete lists of known $n \in \mathcal{F}$).**

| $n$ | Status | Concrete data |
|---|---|---|
| $1,2$ | trivial / elementary abelian | $|B(m,2)| = 2^m$ |
| $3$ | Burnside 1902 | $|B(2,3)| = 27$, $|B(3,3)| = 3^7 = 2187$ |
| $4$ | Sanov 1940 | $|B(2,4)| = 2^{12}$, $|B(3,4)| = 2^{69}$, $|B(4,4)| = 2^{422}$, $|B(5,4)| = 2^{2728}$ (Newman–O'Brien, coset enumeration / $p$-quotient algorithm) |
| $6$ | M. Hall 1958 | $|B(2,6)| = 2^{28}\cdot 3^{25}$; $B(m,6)$ is an extension of a $3$-group by a $2$-group |

**Infinite cases.** $B(m,n)$ is infinite (indeed of exponential growth, with a free subsemigroup on 2 generators) for $m \ge 2$ and: odd $n \ge 101$ (Adian 2015); $n \ge 8000$ even (Lysënok 1996); $n$ divisible by $2^9$ with $n \ge 2^{48}$ (Ivanov 1994).

**Restricted problem — fully solved.** $R(m,n)$ is finite for all $m,n$ (Zelmanov). Example: $|R(2,5)| = 5^{34}$, nilpotency class $12$ (Havas–Wall–Wamsley, 1974); $|R(2,7)| = 7^{20416}$, class $28$ (Vaughan-Lee, O'Brien).

**Structural results.** Every countable group of exponent $n$ (large odd) embeds in a $2$-generated one; $B(m,n)$ for large odd $n$ has a decidable word problem (Novikov–Adian) and its centre is trivial; free Burnside groups of large exponent are not amenable and satisfy no nontrivial identity beyond consequences of $x^n$.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No linear or geometric model.** By Burnside–Schur, infinite Burnside groups are not linear; by Tits' alternative they cannot contain free subgroups; they act on no nonpositively curved space with the usual properness. Every standard representation-theoretic tool is unavailable.
- **Small-cancellation machinery has an intrinsic exponent floor.** All infiniteness proofs run an induction on periods where a period-$i$ relator $A^n$ must be long enough for the cancellation estimates to close. The bookkeeping constants force $n \gtrsim 100$; the failure is quantitative, not conceptual, but no argument is known that survives $n$ small. Ivanov–Lysënok's even-exponent case needs additional layers ($n$ even permits involutions creating "dihedral" cancellation) pushing thresholds to $10^3$–$10^{14}$.
- **Zelmanov's method is inherently residual.** It bounds nilpotency class of *finite* quotients via Engel Lie algebras. It gives no control on $\mathcal{N}(m,n)$, so it cannot decide whether $B(m,n) \to R(m,n)$ is injective. Equivalently: RBP finiteness plus residual finiteness of $B(m,n)$ would give BBP finiteness, but residual finiteness of $B(2,5)$ is itself open.
- **Computation explodes.** $|R(2,5)| = 5^{34}$ is tractable; the class-$\ge 13$ quotients of $B(2,5)$ that would distinguish it from $R(2,5)$ are beyond current nilpotent-quotient and coset-enumeration ranges. Kostrikin's work shows $B(2,5)$ is finite iff a certain Engel-type Lie condition plus a collapse phenomenon holds — unverified.

## 6. The Gap

The proven region is $\mathcal{F} \supseteq \{1,2,3,4,6\}$ and $\mathcal{F}^c \supseteq \{\text{odd } n \ge 101\} \cup \{n \ge 8000\}$. The gap is the finite but unresolved window
$$n \in \{5,7,8,9,10,11,12,\dots\} \quad \text{up to } 8000 \ \text{(and up to }101\text{ for odd }n),$$
notably every $n \notin \{1,2,3,4,6\}$ below $101$. Two distinct barriers must be crossed:

1. **Downward (infiniteness).** Make the graded small-cancellation induction work when the relator $A^n$ is short relative to $|A|$ — i.e. remove the $n \ge 101$ constant. Nothing in the method is known to fail at $n = 5$; the estimates simply cease to be provable.
2. **Upward (finiteness).** For a specific small $n$ such as $5$, show $B(2,n) = R(2,n)$, i.e. that $B(2,n)$ is residually finite. No technique produces residual finiteness from an exponent identity.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Geometric periodic quotients.** R. Coulon's programme constructs periodic quotients of torsion-free hyperbolic groups and of groups acting on hyperbolic spaces, giving uniform "critical exponent" statements; joint work with Gruber and with Dahmani extends this to relatively hyperbolic and acylindrically hyperbolic settings. Focus: lowering the exponent bound by replacing combinatorial diagrams with curvature estimates. *(frontier — verify current thresholds)*
- **Rips-school cancellation theory.** Rips, Tent and Atkarskaya have developed graded ring/group cancellation frameworks aimed at a conceptually cleaner Novikov–Adian theorem; several preprints since 2021 target both readability and smaller exponents. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Lie-theoretic descent to $n=5$.** Continuation of Kostrikin's Around Burnside programme: characterising $B(2,5)$ via sandwich algebras and thin Lie algebras (Caranti, Mattarei, Vaughan-Lee school). No proof of finiteness or infiniteness has emerged.
- **Computational group theory.** Newman–O'Brien-style $p$-quotient and nilpotent-quotient algorithms in GAP/Magma continue to push exponent-$5$ and exponent-$7$ class bounds; these bound $R(m,n)$, not $B(m,n)$.
- **Burnside phenomena elsewhere.** Analogues in the Grigorchuk/Gupta–Sidki family of finitely generated infinite torsion groups of intermediate growth, and Burnside-type questions for automaton groups and for groups of homeomorphisms.

## 8. Future Work

- Determine whether $B(2,5)$ is residually finite; a negative answer combined with $|R(2,5)| = 5^{34}$ would prove $B(2,5)$ infinite.
- Reduce the odd threshold below $101$ toward $n = 7$; Adian repeatedly stated that the combinatorial apparatus is not intrinsically limited and that the constants are an artefact of the induction.
- Produce an infiniteness proof that is *uniform in $n$* by a curvature or asymptotic-cone argument rather than a period induction (Ol'shanskii's and Coulon's stated aim).
- Even exponents: close the gap between Lysënok's $n \ge 8000$ and the odd record; separate the role of involutions.
- Effective versions of Zelmanov's theorem: explicit bounds on the class of $R(m,p)$ would give algorithmic tests for candidate collapses at $n = 5, 7$.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Claim.** $B(2,3)$ is finite of order $27$, isomorphic to the group $UT(3,\mathbb{F}_3)$ of $3\times 3$ upper unitriangular matrices over $\mathbb{F}_3$.

*Step 1 — exponent 3 forces class 2.* Let $G = \langle x, y\rangle$ with $g^3 = 1$ for all $g$. From $(xy)^3 = 1$ we get $xyxyxy = 1$, hence
$$xyx \;=\; (yxy)^{-1} \;=\; y^{-1}x^{-1}y^{-1}.$$
Applying the same identity to the pairs $(x, y^{-1})$ and $(x^{-1}, y)$ and combining yields $[[x,y],x] = [[x,y],y] = 1$, so $c := [x,y] = x^{-1}y^{-1}xy$ is central. Thus $G' = \langle c \rangle$ is central of order dividing $3$, and $G/G' = \langle \bar x\rangle \times \langle \bar y \rangle \cong C_3 \times C_3$.

*Step 2 — upper bound.* $|G| \le |G'|\cdot|G/G'| \le 3 \cdot 9 = 27$.

*Step 3 — realisation.* Take
$$X = \begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad Y = \begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&1\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix} \in UT(3,\mathbb{F}_3).$$
Any $A \in UT(3,\mathbb{F}_3)$ is $I + N$ with $N^3 = 0$, so
$$A^3 = I + 3N + 3N^2 + N^3 = I \quad \text{over } \mathbb{F}_3 .$$
Hence $UT(3,\mathbb{F}_3)$ has exponent $3$, order $3^3 = 27$, and is generated by $X, Y$ with $[X,Y]$ the central elementary matrix $E_{13}(1)$. The bound in Step 2 is attained, so $B(2,3) \cong UT(3,\mathbb{F}_3)$ and $|B(2,3)| = 27$, matching $3^{\,2+\binom{2}{2\phantom{.}}\!}$-style formula $3^{m+\binom m2+\binom m3} = 3^{2+1+0} = 27$.

*Contrast with the open case.* Repeating Step 1 for $n = 5$ fails: exponent-$5$ groups are not nilpotent of bounded class as abstract groups. The best available statement is about the restricted quotient — the largest *finite* $2$-generator group of exponent $5$ has order $5^{34}$ and class $12$. Whether $B(2,5)$ equals this group of order $5^{34}$, or is infinite, is exactly the open Burnside problem at its smallest unresolved exponent.
