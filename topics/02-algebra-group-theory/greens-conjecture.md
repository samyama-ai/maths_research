# Green's Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/greens-conjecture` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $C$ be a smooth projective non-hyperelliptic curve of genus $g \ge 3$ over an algebraically closed field, canonically embedded by $|\omega_C|$ into $\mathbf{P}^{g-1}$. Green's conjecture asserts that the shape of the minimal free resolution of the homogeneous coordinate ring of the canonical curve is governed by a single Brill–Noether invariant, the Clifford index $\operatorname{Cliff}(C)$:

$$K_{p,2}(C,\omega_C) = 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad p < \operatorname{Cliff}(C).$$

The implication $\Leftarrow$ (vanishing) is the open content. The implication $\Rightarrow$ — equivalently, non-vanishing $K_{p,2} \ne 0$ for all $p \ge \operatorname{Cliff}(C)$ — is a theorem of Green and Lazarsfeld (1984).

A complete proof must establish, for **every** smooth curve $C$ (not merely a general one in moduli) and every $p < \operatorname{Cliff}(C)$, that the Koszul cohomology group $K_{p,2}(C,\omega_C)$ vanishes; a disproof requires one curve with $K_{p,2}(C,\omega_C) \ne 0$ for some $p < \operatorname{Cliff}(C)$. No counterexample is known in characteristic $0$.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Koszul cohomology.** For a projective variety $X$, a line bundle $L$ with $V = H^0(X,L)$, set $S = \operatorname{Sym}(V)$ and $\Gamma(L) = \bigoplus_{q\ge 0} H^0(X, L^{\otimes q})$. The group $K_{p,q}(X,L)$ is the cohomology at the middle of

$$\textstyle\bigwedge^{p+1} V \otimes H^0(L^{q-1}) \;\longrightarrow\; \bigwedge^{p} V \otimes H^0(L^{q}) \;\longrightarrow\; \bigwedge^{p-1} V \otimes H^0(L^{q+1}),$$

the maps being Koszul differentials. Equivalently, $\dim_k K_{p,q}(X,L) = b_{p,p+q}(X,L)$, the graded Betti number of $\Gamma(L)$ as an $S$-module: the number of minimal generators of the $p$-th syzygy module in degree $p+q$.

**Clifford index.** For a line bundle $A$ on $C$ with $h^0(A)\ge 2$ and $h^1(A)\ge 2$ (a *contributing* bundle), put $\operatorname{Cliff}(A) = \deg A - 2\bigl(h^0(A)-1\bigr)$, and

$$\operatorname{Cliff}(C) = \min\{\operatorname{Cliff}(A)\}, \qquad 0 \le \operatorname{Cliff}(C) \le \left\lfloor \tfrac{g-1}{2}\right\rfloor .$$

$\operatorname{Cliff}(C) = 0$ iff $C$ is hyperelliptic; $\operatorname{Cliff}(C)=1$ iff $C$ is trigonal or a smooth plane quintic. With $\operatorname{gon}(C)$ the gonality, one always has $\operatorname{Cliff}(C) \le \operatorname{gon}(C)-2$, and by Coppens–Martens $\operatorname{Cliff}(C) \in \{\operatorname{gon}(C)-2,\ \operatorname{gon}(C)-3\}$. A general curve of genus $g$ has $\operatorname{gon}(C) = \lfloor (g+3)/2\rfloor$ and $\operatorname{Cliff}(C) = \lfloor (g-1)/2 \rfloor$.

**Duality.** Canonical curves are arithmetically Gorenstein, and

$$K_{p,q}(C,\omega_C) \;\cong\; K_{g-2-p,\,3-q}(C,\omega_C)^{\vee},$$

so the Betti table has four rows ($q=0,1,2,3$) and is symmetric under $180^\circ$ rotation. Consequently Green's conjecture is equivalent to the *linear syzygy* statement $K_{p,1}(C,\omega_C) \neq 0$ for $p \le g-3-\operatorname{Cliff}(C)$ and $=0$ beyond.

**Classical anchors.** $p=0$: $K_{0,2}=0$ iff $C$ is non-hyperelliptic (Max Noether's theorem, projective normality of the canonical map). $p=1$: $K_{1,2}=0$ iff $C$ is neither trigonal nor a plane quintic (Enriques–Babbage–Petri: the canonical ideal is generated by quadrics). Green's conjecture is exactly the extrapolation of this pattern to all $p$.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

Mark Green formulated the conjecture in *Koszul cohomology and the geometry of projective varieties*, J. Differential Geom. **19** (1984), where the appendix by Green and Lazarsfeld proved the non-vanishing half by exhibiting explicit syzygies from a contributing pencil. Schreyer (Math. Ann. **275**, 1986) verified it for genus $\le 8$ and developed the standard-basis/scrollar techniques that settled the tetragonal case; Voisin (Crelle **387**, 1988) treated further low-Clifford-index cases.

The decisive advance was Voisin's proof of the **generic** case by Hodge theory on Hilbert schemes of points of K3 surfaces: even genus $g=2k+2$ (JEMS **4**, 2002) and odd genus $g=2k+1$ (Compositio **141**, 2005). Combined with Hirschowitz–Ramanan's divisor-class comparison on $\mathcal{M}_{2k+1}$ (Ann. Sci. ÉNS **31**, 1998), the odd-genus result upgrades from "general curve" to **every** curve of genus $2k+1$ with maximal Clifford index $k$.

A second, purely algebraic proof arrived with Aprodu–Farkas–Papadima–Raicu–Weyman, *Koszul modules and Green's conjecture*, Invent. Math. **218** (2019): the generic case is reduced to the vanishing of a Weyman–Koszul module attached to the Weyl algebra of the "K3 carpet", giving the result in characteristic $0$ and in characteristic $p > (g-2)/2$. Raicu–Sam (Compositio **157**, 2021) refined this via bi-graded Koszul modules and removed almost all characteristic restrictions.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **Non-vanishing direction:** proved in full generality (Green–Lazarsfeld, 1984).
- **Small $p$:** $p=0$ (Noether) and $p=1$ (Enriques–Babbage–Petri) hold for all curves; $p \le 2$ follows from the tetragonal analysis of Schreyer (1986, 1991) and Voisin (1988), covering all curves with $\operatorname{Cliff}(C)\le 4$.
- **Small genus:** all curves of genus $g \le 8$ (Schreyer 1986); extensive Macaulay2/Singular Betti-table computations extend explicit verification well beyond.
- **General curves:** true for a general $[C]\in\mathcal{M}_g$ for every $g$ (Voisin 2002, 2005), i.e. $K_{p,2}(C,\omega_C)=0$ for $p < \lfloor (g-1)/2\rfloor$.
- **Odd genus, maximal Clifford index:** true for *every* curve of genus $g=2k+1$ with $\operatorname{Cliff}(C)=k$ (Hirschowitz–Ramanan + Voisin).
- **Gonality strata:** Teixidor i Bigas (Duke **111**, 2002) for the general $r$-gonal curve with $g \ge 3r-7$; Aprodu (Math. Res. Lett. **12**, 2005) for general $d$-gonal curves in the full range $d \le \lfloor (g+2)/2 \rfloor$, via a "linear growth" condition on Brill–Noether loci $W^1_{d+n}(C)$.
- **Curves on K3 surfaces:** every smooth curve on an arbitrary K3 surface (Aprodu–Farkas, Compositio **147**, 2011), including cases where $\operatorname{Cliff}(C)$ is not computed by a pencil.
- **Positive characteristic:** generic case for $\operatorname{char} k = 0$ or $\operatorname{char} k > (g-2)/2$ (AFPRW 2019); extended to essentially all characteristics, with a residual small-characteristic exception in the even-genus case, by Raicu–Sam (2021) *(frontier — verify the exact excluded primes)*.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Koszul cohomology is not functorial in useful directions.** $K_{p,q}$ does not behave well under degeneration: it is upper semicontinuous, so vanishing on a general curve gives nothing about a special one. Every "generic" theorem is therefore blocked from spreading across moduli.
- **Voisin's method is intrinsically generic.** It computes $K_{p,2}$ of a curve $C\subset S$ on a K3 surface using the Hilbert scheme $S^{[k]}$ and a cohomological interpretation of the Koszul complex; it requires the K3 to be general (Picard rank 1) so that $\operatorname{Cliff}(C)$ is maximal. Curves with non-maximal Clifford index — precisely the hard part of the conjecture — do not embed in such surfaces.
- **The Hirschowitz–Ramanan trick does not iterate.** It works because in odd genus the syzygy locus and the gonality divisor are both divisors on $\mathcal{M}_{2k+1}$ with proportional classes; in even genus the corresponding loci have codimension $\ge 2$ and no class comparison forces equality. Extending the argument requires controlling multiplicities of syzygy loci in higher codimension, which is unknown.
- **No effective vanishing criterion.** For a given curve there is no known intrinsic reason forcing a Koszul class to vanish other than exhibiting a resolution; Green–Lazarsfeld duality converts the problem to producing linear syzygies, but the geometry producing them (scrolls, secant varieties, rational normal curves through the canonical model) is only understood for small Clifford index.
- **Characteristic and integrality.** The Koszul-module approach reduces to the vanishing of an explicit $\mathrm{GL}$-equivariant module; its resolution has torsion in small characteristic, so the argument degenerates exactly where computations suggest genuine failures.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: the conjecture on a Zariski-dense open subset of $\mathcal{M}_g$, on the whole of $\mathcal{M}_{2k+1}$ restricted to $\operatorname{Cliff}=k$, on all gonality strata for the *general* member, and on all $\operatorname{Cliff}(C)\le 4$.

Missing: the *every curve* statement for $\operatorname{Cliff}(C)\ge 5$ in the loci where $C$ is special within its gonality stratum — most sharply, curves of even genus $g=2k+2$ with $\operatorname{Cliff}(C)=k$ that are not general, and curves whose Clifford index is computed by a $g^r_d$ with $r\ge 2$ rather than a pencil (e.g. plane curves and their degenerations, where the linear-growth hypothesis of Aprodu fails). The exact step needed: a criterion for $K_{p,2}(C,\omega_C)=0$ that is stated in terms of $C$ alone — not of a general deformation, not of an ambient K3 — and that is stable under specialization inside a fixed Brill–Noether stratum.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Koszul modules and representation theory** (Aprodu, Farkas, Papadima †, Raicu, Weyman; Humboldt Berlin, Notre Dame, Wisconsin). The bi-graded Koszul module machinery of Raicu–Sam has become the default engine; work continues on non-generic Koszul modules attached to specific gonality strata and on the resolution of Weyman modules over $\mathbf{Z}$.
- **Syzygies of degenerate and reducible curves.** Eisenbud–Schreyer's analysis of K3 carpets and unions of scrolls (Acta Math. Vietnam. **44**, 2019) supplies degenerate models with computable Betti tables; extending them to carpets over higher-genus base curves is an active line.
- **Positive characteristic.** Bopp–Schreyer (Exp. Math. **30**, 2021) give a characteristic-$p$ formulation and computational evidence, including anomalies in small characteristic. Pinning down the exact set of bad primes is open *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Neighbouring conjectures.** Farkas–Kemeny's proof of the generic Green–Lazarsfeld secant conjecture (Invent. Math. **203**, 2016) and the Ein–Lazarsfeld gonality conjecture for line bundles of large degree (Publ. IHÉS **122**, 2015) supply techniques — vector-bundle and Hilbert-scheme methods — being pushed back toward the canonical case.

## 8. Future Work

- Find a degeneration-stable invariant refining $K_{p,2}$ whose vanishing is closed rather than open, converting generic results into universal ones.
- Prove an even-genus analogue of Hirschowitz–Ramanan by computing the class of the syzygy locus in $\mathcal{M}_{2k+2}$ in codimension $\ge 2$ (Farkas' programme on effective cones of $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_g$).
- Handle Clifford index computed by non-pencils: develop a scrollar/secant geometry for $g^r_d$ with $r\ge 2$ paralleling Schreyer's scrolls.
- Settle the characteristic-$p$ picture: determine whether the generic conjecture fails for any $(g,p)$, which would isolate the arithmetic content of the Koszul-module vanishing.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take $g=5$, so the canonical model is a curve $C\subset\mathbf{P}^4$ of degree $2g-2=8$, with $S=k[x_0,\dots,x_4]$.

**Quadrics.** $\dim S_2 = 15$ and $h^0(\omega_C^{2}) = 3g-3 = 12$, so $\dim I_2 = 3$ and $K_{1,1}(C,\omega_C)=3$ for every non-hyperelliptic $C$.

**Cubics.** $\dim S_3 = 35$, $h^0(\omega_C^{3}) = 5g-5 = 20$, so $\dim I_3 = 15$.

*Case A: $\operatorname{Cliff}(C)=2$ (general curve, $\operatorname{gon}=4$).* The three quadrics form a regular sequence, so $\dim(S_1\cdot I_2) = 3\cdot 5 = 15 = \dim I_3$: no cubic generators, $K_{1,2}=0$. The resolution is the Koszul complex

$$0 \to S(-6) \to S(-4)^{3} \to S(-2)^{3} \to S \to S/I_C \to 0,$$

giving $K_{0,2}=K_{1,2}=0$ and $K_{2,2}=3\neq 0$. Green's prediction — vanishing exactly for $p<2$ — holds on the nose.

*Case B: $\operatorname{Cliff}(C)=1$ ($C$ trigonal).* The $g^1_3$ sweeps out a cubic scroll $S_{\mathrm{scr}}\subset \mathbf{P}^4$ containing $C$. The scroll's ideal is resolved by Eagon–Northcott:

$$0 \to S(-3)^{2} \to S(-2)^{3} \to I_{S_{\mathrm{scr}}} \to 0,$$

so the same three quadrics now carry **two** linear syzygies. Hence $\dim(S_1\cdot I_2) = 15-2 = 13 < 15 = \dim I_C$ in degree 3, forcing two cubic generators:

$$\dim K_{1,2}(C,\omega_C) = 2 \neq 0,$$

and by duality $\dim K_{2,1} = 2$. The Betti table becomes

$$0 \to S(-6) \to S(-4)^{3}\oplus S(-3)^{2} \to S(-2)^{3}\oplus S(-3)^{2} \to S .$$

Both cases match $K_{p,2}=0 \iff p<\operatorname{Cliff}(C)$: the extra cubic generators appear precisely when the Clifford index drops, and they are produced by the geometry (the scroll) attached to the contributing pencil — the exact mechanism Green–Lazarsfeld proved always produces non-vanishing, and whose absence Green conjectured always forces vanishing.
