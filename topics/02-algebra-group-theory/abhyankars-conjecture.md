# Abhyankar's Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/abhyankars-conjecture` · **Status:** solved-recently

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p > 0$, let $X$ be a smooth connected projective curve over $k$ of genus $g$, let $S \subset X$ be a finite set of $n \ge 1$ closed points, and set $U = X \setminus S$ (an affine curve). For a finite group $G$, write $p(G)$ for the **quasi-$p$ part**: the (normal) subgroup generated by all Sylow $p$-subgroups of $G$.

**Conjecture (Abhyankar, 1957).** $G$ occurs as the Galois group of a connected finite étale Galois cover $V \to U$ — equivalently $G$ is a quotient of $\pi_1(U)$ — if and only if the prime-to-$p$ quotient $G/p(G)$ can be generated by $2g + n - 1$ elements.

The necessity of the condition is elementary (Section 2); the content is sufficiency. A complete proof must, for every such $G$, construct a connected $G$-Galois étale cover of $U$; a disproof would exhibit $G$, $X$, $S$ with $G/p(G)$ generated by $2g+n-1$ elements but no such cover.

**Status.** The conjecture is a **theorem**: Raynaud (1994) for $U = \mathbb{A}^1_k$ ($g = 0$, $n = 1$), Harbater (1994) for arbitrary affine curves. What remains open is the refined form — Abhyankar's **Inertia Conjecture**, which prescribes the local ramification behaviour of the covers — and the determination of $\pi_1(U)$ as a profinite group.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Fundamental group.** $\pi_1(U)$ denotes Grothendieck's algebraic fundamental group (SGA 1): the profinite group classifying finite étale covers of $U$, with finite quotients corresponding to connected Galois covers.

**Quasi-$p$ groups.** $G$ is *quasi-$p$* if $G = p(G)$, i.e. $G$ is generated by its $p$-elements. Examples: $\mathbb{Z}/p$, elementary abelian $p$-groups, $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$ and $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb{F}_p)$ for $p \ge 5$, $A_p$ for $p \ge 5$, $A_n$ for $n \ge p$ under mild conditions. Note $p(G/N) = p(G)N/N$, so $G/p(G)$ is the maximal prime-to-$p$-generated quotient in the relevant sense.

**Necessity.** By Grothendieck's specialization/lifting theorem, the prime-to-$p$ (tame) quotient of $\pi_1(U)$ is the profinite completion of the topological surface group:
$$\pi_1^{(p')}(U) \;\cong\; \Big\langle a_1,b_1,\dots,a_g,b_g,c_1,\dots,c_n \ \Big|\ \prod_{i=1}^{g}[a_i,b_i]\prod_{j=1}^{n}c_j = 1 \Big\rangle^{\widehat{\ \ }(p')},$$
a free profinite prime-to-$p$ group of rank $2g + n - 1$. Any quotient $G$ of $\pi_1(U)$ therefore has $G/p(G)$ generated by $2g+n-1$ elements.

**Riemann–Hurwitz with wild ramification.** For $f: Y \to X$ separable of degree $m$,
$$2g_Y - 2 \;=\; m(2g_X - 2) \;+\; \sum_{y \in Y} d_y, \qquad d_y \ge e_y - 1,$$
with equality iff $p \nmid e_y$ (tame). For a $G$-cover with $G$ cyclic of order $p$ and a single totally ramified point with Artin conductor jump $\delta$, $d_y = (p-1)(\delta+1)$.

**Artin–Schreier theory.** Degree-$p$ cyclic covers of a curve with function field $K \supset \mathbb{F}_p$ are given by $y^p - y = f$, $f \in K$, with $\mathrm{Gal} \cong \mathbb{Z}/p$ acting by $y \mapsto y + 1$; the cover is unramified exactly where $f$ has no pole (modulo $\wp(K) = \{h^p - h\}$).

**Key non-finiteness.** Unlike characteristic $0$, $\pi_1(\mathbb{A}^1_k)$ is infinitely generated and not topologically finitely generated: $\pi_1(\mathbb{A}^1)^{\mathrm{tame}} = 1$, yet infinitely many quasi-$p$ groups occur.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1957.** Shreeram S. Abhyankar, *Coverings of algebraic curves* (Amer. J. Math. 79), formulates the conjecture after computing Galois groups of explicit equations and observing the quasi-$p$ obstruction.
- **1960s–70s.** Grothendieck (SGA 1) determines the tame quotient by lifting to characteristic $0$ and specializing; the wild part resists this method entirely.
- **1980s.** Harbater develops **formal/rigid patching** (*Galois coverings of the arithmetic line*, LNM 1240, 1987), later Serre and others obtain sporadic realizations.
- **1990.** Serre proves the conjecture for $\mathbb{A}^1$ when $G$ is a **solvable** quasi-$p$ group (C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 311), by induction on the derived series using Artin–Schreier–Witt theory plus a counting argument.
- **1994.** **Raynaud** proves the affine-line case in full (*Revêtements de la droite affine en caractéristique $p>0$ et conjecture d'Abhyankar*, Invent. Math. 116), using semistable reduction of covers, rigid geometry, and a degeneration argument on the theta divisor.
- **1994.** **Harbater** deduces the general affine-curve case (Invent. Math. 117) by formal patching: he glues Raynaud's covers of $\mathbb{A}^1$ to a tame cover realizing $G/p(G)$ along a degenerating family.
- **1995.** **Pop** (Invent. Math. 120) proves the "Half Riemann Existence Theorem", showing every finite embedding problem for $\pi_1(U)$ with quasi-$p$ kernel is solvable — a strong structural strengthening.
- **2003–present.** Harbater (Crelle 559, 2003) and others push toward the group-theoretic structure of $\pi_1(U)$; attention shifts to the **Inertia Conjecture** and to $p$-rank/genus constraints on the covers (Bouw, Compositio 126, 2001).

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

The main conjecture is fully proved; the following track the graded history and the *refined* statements still partial.

| Case | Parameters | Status / source |
|---|---|---|
| Tame quotient | any $g,n$; $p \nmid |G|$ | Grothendieck, SGA 1 — free profinite of rank $2g+n-1$ |
| Solvable quasi-$p$ on $\mathbb{A}^1$ | $g=0$, $n=1$ | Serre 1990 |
| Explicit realizations on $\mathbb{A}^1$ | $A_p$, $\mathrm{PSL}_2(p)$, $\mathrm{SL}_2(p)$, $\mathrm{PSL}_2(p^m)$, $p\ge 5$ | Abhyankar 1992 (trinomial equations) |
| $\mathbb{A}^1$, all quasi-$p$ $G$ | $g=0,n=1$ | Raynaud 1994 |
| All affine curves | all $g \ge 0$, $n \ge 1$ | Harbater 1994 |
| Embedding problems, quasi-$p$ kernel | all $g,n$ | Pop 1995 |
| **Inertia Conjecture** — necessity | all quasi-$p$ $G$ | Raynaud 1994 |
| Inertia Conjecture — sufficiency | $G$ a $p$-group; $G = A_p$; $G=\mathrm{PSL}_2(p)$ and further alternating/linear families | partial; Bouw, Obus, Pries, Das, Kumar |

**Inertia Conjecture (Abhyankar).** Let $G$ be quasi-$p$ and $I \le G$ a subgroup of the form $P \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/m$ with $P$ a $p$-group and $p \nmid m$. Then $I$ occurs as the inertia group at the unique branch point $\infty$ of some connected $G$-Galois cover of $\mathbb{P}^1$ étale over $\mathbb{A}^1$ **iff** the conjugates $\{gPg^{-1} : g\in G\}$ generate $G$. Only "only if" is a theorem in general.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No lifting to characteristic $0$.** The tame case is settled by deformation from $\mathbb{C}$; wild covers do not lift (Riemann–Hurwitz obstruction: the different jumps by more than $e-1$), so the Riemann existence theorem is unavailable. Every proof must be intrinsically characteristic $p$.
- **$\pi_1(\mathbb{A}^1)$ is infinitely generated**, projective but not free, and has no known presentation. Standard profinite group theory (Iwasawa's theorem, Melnikov's criteria) needs a generation rank that does not exist here.
- **Degeneration control.** Raynaud's argument requires controlling the stable model of a family of covers as the branch locus collapses; the combinatorics of the special fibre (a tree of components with quasi-$p$ subquotients) is delicate and gives existence without explicit equations.
- **Non-constructivity.** Neither Raynaud's nor Harbater's proof produces equations; for a specific $G$ one gets no defining polynomial, no genus, no $p$-rank, no inertia data. This is precisely why the refined conjectures stay open.
- **Inertia is not patching-stable.** Formal patching lets one prescribe the group but scrambles the local behaviour at branch points, so it cannot by itself resolve the Inertia Conjecture.

## 6. The Gap

For the original statement, no gap remains: Sections 1 and 4 coincide since 1994. The live boundary is a level down:

1. **Local vs. global.** Proved: every admissible $G$ occurs. Open: for which subgroups $I \le G$ does a $G$-cover of $\mathbb{A}^1$ with inertia exactly $I$ at $\infty$ exist? The missing step is a construction that simultaneously prescribes the global monodromy $G$ and the higher ramification filtration of $I$; current patching sacrifices the second.
2. **Group vs. quotients.** Proved: the set of finite quotients of $\pi_1(U)$ is determined by $(g,n,p)$. Open: $\pi_1(U)$ itself. Two affine curves over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ with the same $(g,n)$ have the same finite quotients, yet Tamagawa-type results show $\pi_1$ can distinguish them — the group is *not* determined by the numerical invariants, and no presentation is known.
3. **Moduli.** Proved: existence. Open: the genus, $p$-rank, and dimension of the space of such covers.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Inertia Conjecture programme.** Andrew Obus, Rachel Pries, Manish Kumar, and Soumyadip Das have established sufficiency for growing families: $p$-group inertia, alternating groups $A_p$ and neighbours, and certain $\mathrm{PSL}_2$ and $\mathrm{SL}_2$ cases, typically via deformation of Artin–Schreier–Witt covers plus formal patching. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Purely Wild Inertia Conjecture** ($I$ a $p$-group, $m=1$): reduced to statements about the $p$-rank and the existence of degenerating families; active in the Das–Kumar line of work. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Structure of $\pi_1$.** Following Pop's embedding-problem theorem, work continues on whether $\pi_1(U)$ for $k$ of cardinality $\kappa$ is determined by $\kappa$ and the quasi-$p$ embedding data; the commutator subgroup and pro-$p$ quotients are studied by Kumar and by Harbater–Stevenson.
- **Anabelian side.** Tamagawa-style reconstruction of affine curves over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ from $\pi_1$ — a sharp contrast with the coarseness of the quotient set that Abhyankar's conjecture describes.
- **Institutions.** Penn (Harbater), CUNY/Baruch (Obus), Colorado State (Pries), IISER Mohali (Kumar), Heidelberg (Pop's school), IHES/Orsay tradition from Raynaud.

## 8. Future Work

- Prove the Inertia Conjecture for all quasi-$p$ $G$, or find a counterexample among sporadic simple groups where the Sylow-conjugate generation condition is tight.
- Make Raynaud–Harbater effective: produce explicit equations for a $G$-cover of $\mathbb{A}^1$ for a given quasi-$p$ $G$, extending Abhyankar's trinomial method beyond $A_p$ and $\mathrm{PSL}_2(p)$.
- Determine the minimal genus and the $p$-rank spectrum of connected $G$-covers of $\mathbb{A}^1$ (Bouw's $p$-rank bounds are the current tool).
- Settle whether $\pi_1(U)$ for $U$ over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ admits any presentation, or prove a "no free quotient" obstruction.
- Extend to higher dimension and to arithmetic base: Abhyankar-type statements for $\pi_1$ of affine varieties over $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$, and for curves over incomplete fields.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take $U = \mathbb{A}^1_k$, $k = \overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$. Here $g = 0$, $n = 1$, so $2g + n - 1 = 0$: the condition "$G/p(G)$ is generated by $0$ elements" means $G/p(G) = 1$, i.e. **$G$ is quasi-$p$**.

**(a) A prime-to-$p$ group cannot occur.** Suppose $\ell \ne p$ is prime and $Y \to \mathbb{P}^1$ is a connected $\mathbb{Z}/\ell$-cover, étale over $\mathbb{A}^1$. Branching is confined to $\infty$, is tame, and $\mathbb{Z}/\ell$ acts transitively on the fibre, so there is one point above $\infty$ with $e = \ell$ and $d = \ell - 1$. Riemann–Hurwitz:
$$2g_Y - 2 = \ell(2\cdot 0 - 2) + (\ell - 1) = -\ell - 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad g_Y = \frac{1-\ell}{2}.$$
For $\ell = 2$ this is $-\tfrac12 \notin \mathbb{Z}$; for $\ell \ge 3$ it is negative. Both impossible, so no such cover exists — matching the prediction, since $p(\mathbb{Z}/\ell) = 1 \ne \mathbb{Z}/\ell$.

**(b) $\mathbb{Z}/p$ does occur.** Let $Y : y^p - y = x$. The map $y \mapsto x$ is finite of degree $p$; since $\frac{\partial}{\partial y}(y^p - y - x) = -1$ is a unit, the cover $\mathbb{A}^1_y \to \mathbb{A}^1_x$ is étale, connected (the polynomial is irreducible over $k(x)$ because $x \notin \wp(k(x))$), and Galois with group $\mathbb{Z}/p$ acting by $y \mapsto y + c$, $c \in \mathbb{F}_p$. Consistency check at $\infty$: the pole order of $x$ is $1$, so the ramification jump is $\delta = 1$ and $d_\infty = (p-1)(\delta+1) = 2(p-1)$. Then
$$2g_Y - 2 = p(-2) + 2(p-1) = -2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad g_Y = 0,$$
so $Y \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ — exactly right, as $Y$ is the $y$-line. And $\mathbb{Z}/p$ is quasi-$p$.

**(c) Beyond abelian.** $\mathbb{Z}/p \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/m$ with $m \mid p-1$ acting faithfully is quasi-$p$ (its Sylow $p$-subgroup is normal with prime-to-$p$ quotient generated by nothing extra: $p(G) = \mathbb{Z}/p$ — so in fact $G/p(G) \cong \mathbb{Z}/m \ne 1$ and $G$ is **not** quasi-$p$, hence does not occur over $\mathbb{A}^1$). By contrast $A_p$ for $p \ge 5$ *is* quasi-$p$ (the $p$-cycles generate $A_p$), and Abhyankar (1992) realized it explicitly by a trinomial equation; Raynaud's theorem says every such group, including all quasi-$p$ sporadic simple groups, occurs — though without equations. The Inertia Conjecture then asks which $I \le A_p$ can be the inertia at $\infty$: the criterion predicts exactly those $I = P \rtimes \mathbb{Z}/m$ whose $p$-part $P$ has conjugates generating $A_p$, which for $P = \langle (1\,2\,\cdots\,p)\rangle$ holds, so $\mathbb{Z}/p$ itself should be realizable as inertia.
