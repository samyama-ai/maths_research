# Bouc's Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/boucs-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $p$ be a prime, $k$ an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$, and $P$ a finite $p$-group. Let $D(P)$ denote the **Dade group** of $P$ over $k$: the group of equivalence classes of capped endo-permutation $kP$-modules under tensor product. Inside $D(P)$ sits the subgroup

$$D^{\Omega}(P) \;=\; \big\langle\, \Omega_{P/Q} \;:\; Q < P \,\big\rangle$$

generated by the classes of **relative syzygies** $\Omega_{P/Q} = [\Delta(k[P/Q])]$, where $\Delta(k[X]) = \ker\big(k[X] \xrightarrow{\ \varepsilon\ } k\big)$ is the augmentation kernel.

**Bouc's Conjecture.** For every finite $p$-group $P$,
$$D(P) \;=\; D^{\Omega}(P),$$
i.e. every capped endo-permutation $kP$-module is equivalent to a $\mathbb{Z}$-linear combination of relative syzygies.

Equivalently, in the language of biset functors: the inclusion of $p$-biset functors $D^{\Omega} \hookrightarrow D$ is an isomorphism, so that $D$ is a quotient of the Burnside biset functor $B$.

**Status.** Bouc proved the conjecture for all odd $p$ (Invent. Math. 164 (2006)). The case $p = 2$ is the open one: for $2$-groups the torsion part of $D(P)$ carries classes whose expressibility as relative syzygies is not settled by the odd-$p$ machinery, and no general proof or counterexample is known. A complete resolution means either a proof that $D(P)/D^{\Omega}(P) = 0$ for all $2$-groups, or an explicit $2$-group $P$ and a capped endo-permutation module $M$ with $[M] \notin D^{\Omega}(P)$.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Endo-permutation modules.** A finitely generated $kP$-module $M$ is *endo-permutation* if
$$\operatorname{End}_k(M) \;\cong\; M \otimes_k M^{*}$$
is a permutation $kP$-module (has a $P$-stable $k$-basis). It is **capped** if $\operatorname{End}_k(M)$ has the trivial module $k$ as a direct summand; equivalently $M$ has an indecomposable direct summand of vertex $P$, its *cap*, unique up to isomorphism.

**Dade group.** Two capped endo-permutation modules are equivalent, $M \sim N$, iff their caps are isomorphic; equivalently iff $\operatorname{End}_k(M) \oplus \operatorname{End}_k(N)$ differ by a module induced from proper subgroups. The set of classes with
$$[M] + [N] = [M \otimes_k N], \qquad -[M] = [M^{*}], \qquad 0 = [k]$$
is the **Dade group** $D(P)$, a finitely generated abelian group (Dade 1978; Bouc–Thévenaz 2000).

**Relative syzygies.** For a finite $P$-set $X$ with $X^{P} = \emptyset$, the module $\Delta(k[X])$ is capped endo-permutation, and one sets $\Omega_X = [\Delta(k[X])] \in D(P)$. The defining relations are
$$\Omega_{X \times Y} \;=\; \Omega_X + \Omega_Y, \qquad \Omega_{X} = \Omega_{Y} \ \text{ if } X, Y \text{ have the same fixed-point data in the relevant sense},$$
the first being the essential one, since $\Delta(k[X\times Y]) \sim \Delta(k[X]) \otimes_k \Delta(k[Y])$. Writing $X = P/Q$ recovers $\Omega_{P/Q}$, and $\Omega_{P/1} = [\Omega k]$ is the ordinary Heller translate of $k$.

**Endotrivial modules.** $M$ is *endotrivial* if $\operatorname{End}_k(M) \cong k \oplus (\text{projective})$. Their classes form a subgroup $T(P) \le D(P)$, with a torsion-free part $TF(P)$ of rank determined by the poset of elementary abelian subgroups of rank $\ge 2$, and a torsion part $T(P)_t$ classified by Carlson–Thévenaz (Ann. of Math. 2005).

**Biset functor formalism.** $P \mapsto D(P)$ is a $p$-biset functor: it carries restriction, inflation, and (via tensor induction, Bouc 2000) induction and deflation maps compatible with composition of bisets. $D^{\Omega}$ is a subfunctor, and $X \mapsto \Omega_X$ induces a surjection of biset functors $B \twoheadrightarrow D^{\Omega}$ from the Burnside functor. Bouc's conjecture is thus a statement about the simple functor composition factors of $D/D^{\Omega}$.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1978.** Dade introduces endo-permutation modules and computes $D(P)$ for $P$ abelian, showing there that every class is a combination of relative syzygies. Endo-permutation modules arise as the source algebras of nilpotent blocks (Puig), which makes $D(P)$ a block-theoretic invariant, not a curiosity.
- **1988–2001.** Puig proves $D(P)$ is finitely generated. Alperin (2001) makes the relative-syzygy construction systematic; Bouc (*Tensor induction of relative syzygies*, Crelle 523 (2000)) shows $\Omega$-classes are stable under tensor induction, which is what makes $D^{\Omega}$ a biset subfunctor.
- **2000.** Bouc–Thévenaz (Invent. Math. 139) determine the structure of $D(P)$ for several families and formulate the generation question explicitly, with computations of the rank of $D(P)$ and of the torsion.
- **2004–2005.** Carlson–Thévenaz classify endotrivial modules: $T(P)$ torsion-free part (Invent. Math. 158, 2004) and torsion part (Ann. of Math. 162, 2005). This settles the "endotrivial layer" of $D(P)$.
- **2006.** **Bouc's theorem:** $D(P) = D^{\Omega}(P)$ for every $p$-group with $p$ odd (Invent. Math. 164, 189–231). The proof identifies $D^{\Omega}$ with an explicit quotient of $B$ and compares it against the rational representation functor $\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{Q}}$, using that for $p$ odd the Roquette $p$-groups (cyclic, and only those among the relevant sections) constrain the possible simple composition factors.
- **2007–present.** Bouc–Yalçın relate $D^{\Omega}$ for $p = 2$ to Borel–Smith functions and the units of the Burnside ring via the exact sequence of $2$-biset functors $0 \to B^{\times} \to C_b \to D^{\Omega} \to 0$ (J. Algebra 311, 2007), giving a computational handle on $D^{\Omega}(P)$ but not on the quotient $D(P)/D^{\Omega}(P)$.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **All $p$-groups, $p$ odd.** Theorem (Bouc 2006). This is the bulk of the conjecture.
- **Abelian $p$-groups, all $p$.** Dade (1978).
- **Cyclic, generalized quaternion, semidihedral $2$-groups.** $D(P)$ is completely computed; the modules of vertex $P$ are endotrivial or induced-from-proper, and every class is a syzygy combination.
- **Dihedral $2$-groups and $(almost)$ extraspecial $p$-groups.** Bouc–Mazza (J. Pure Appl. Algebra 192, 2004) compute $D(P)$ for extraspecial and almost extraspecial $p$-groups at both parities; the answers are generated by relative syzygies.
- **Endotrivial layer, all $p$.** By Carlson–Thévenaz, $T(P) \subseteq D^{\Omega}(P)$ for all $p$-groups: the torsion-free rank of $T(P)$ is realized by syzygies $\Omega_{P/Q}$ with $Q$ ranging over suitable subgroups, and the torsion classes ($\mathbb{Z}/2$, $\mathbb{Z}/4$ types occurring for cyclic/quaternion/semidihedral) are syzygy classes.
- **$2$-groups of order $\le 2^6$** and further small cases have been checked by explicit computation of $D^{\Omega}(P)$ via the Bouc–Yalçın sequence together with rank counts for $D(P)$ *(frontier — verify: coverage beyond order $64$ is scattered across individual papers rather than a single tabulation)*.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Parity failure of the biset-functor argument.** Bouc's odd-$p$ proof compares $D^{\Omega}$ with $\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{Q}}$ and exploits that for $p$ odd, the Roquette $p$-groups (those with a unique minimal noncentral structure) are cyclic. At $p = 2$ the Roquette groups also include $C_{2^n}$, $Q_{2^n}$, $SD_{2^n}$, $D_{2^n}$ — four extra families — so the classification of simple $2$-biset functors that could occur in $D/D^{\Omega}$ is genuinely larger and the vanishing argument does not close.
- **No lower bound machinery for $D(P)$.** All known constructions of capped endo-permutation modules (relative syzygies, tensor induction, Dade's abelian construction, gluing of local data) produce elements *inside* $D^{\Omega}(P)$. There is no independent invariant that detects a hypothetical class outside it, so one cannot even search for a counterexample effectively.
- **Torsion is not detected locally at $p = 2$.** Restriction to proper subgroups kills too much: $D(P) \to \prod_{Q<P} D(Q)$ is not injective on torsion at $p=2$, so induction on order fails exactly where it is needed.
- **The gluing problem.** Determining $D(P)$ from the local data $\{D(N_P(Q)/Q)\}$ requires solving a descent/gluing problem whose obstruction lives in $H^1$ of the orbit category of $P$ on nontrivial subgroups; this $H^1$ can be nonzero at $p = 2$.
- **Computation cost.** $D(P)$ is not computable by brute force: deciding whether a given indecomposable module is endo-permutation with vertex $P$ requires the full permutation-module decomposition of $\operatorname{End}_k(M)$, which blows up beyond $|P| \sim 2^7$.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: $D/D^{\Omega} = 0$ as $p$-biset functors for $p$ odd, and $D(P)/D^{\Omega}(P) = 0$ for every $2$-group whose Dade group has been computed individually.

Open: whether the $2$-biset functor $Q := D/D^{\Omega}$ vanishes. Concretely, the missing step is a classification of the simple $2$-biset functors $S_{H,V}$ that can appear as composition factors of $Q$, together with a proof that each such factor forces a contradiction. Bouc's odd-$p$ argument shows any such $S_{H,V}$ must have minimal group $H$ a Roquette $2$-group; the gap is the four non-cyclic Roquette families ($D_{2^n}$, $Q_{2^n}$, $SD_{2^n}$ and their interaction with $B^{\times}$), for which the required vanishing statement about $\operatorname{Ext}$-groups between $B^{\times}$, $C_b$ and $D$ is not available.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Biset-functor school (Amiens, Lausanne, Bilkent, Ankara).** Bouc, Thévenaz, Yalçın, Coşkun and collaborators continue to develop the functorial calculus: simple biset functors, the slice and section Burnside rings, and the functor $B^{\times}$ of Burnside ring units, which is the precise obstruction group in the $p=2$ sequence.
- **Endotrivial modules for arbitrary finite groups.** Carlson, Mazza, Nakano, Thévenaz and successors have moved from $p$-groups to $T(G)$ for finite groups of Lie type and sporadic groups; the Dade group of a general finite group $D(G)$ and its "$\Omega$-part" is the natural next target, and Bouc's conjecture is the $p$-group base case.
- **Fusion-system and block-theoretic reformulations.** Because endo-permutation modules are the source algebras of nilpotent blocks, Bouc's conjecture at $p = 2$ has been reformulated in terms of the Dade group of a fusion system $\mathcal{F}$ on $P$, with the aim of using saturation axioms in place of module constructions *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Machine-assisted computation.** GAP/Magma computations of $B^{\times}(P)$ and $C_b(P)$ for $2$-groups extend the verified range of $D^{\Omega}(P)$ ranks; the bottleneck remains an independent computation of $D(P)$ itself.

## 8. Future Work

- Compute $\operatorname{Ext}^1$ between the relevant simple $2$-biset functors and $D^{\Omega}$, to rule out extensions one composition factor at a time — the strategy Bouc used for odd $p$, made effective at $p = 2$.
- Extend the Bouc–Yalçın exact sequence $0 \to B^{\times} \to C_b \to D^{\Omega} \to 0$ to a four-term sequence involving $D$, which would express $D/D^{\Omega}$ as a cokernel of an explicit map of computable functors.
- Settle the conjecture for the first genuinely untested families at $p=2$: Sylow $2$-subgroups of $\mathrm{Sp}_4(q)$ and of the sporadic groups, and $2$-groups of maximal class crossed with elementary abelian groups.
- Develop a "detection on sections" theorem: prove that $D(P)/D^{\Omega}(P)$ injects into $\prod D(S)/D^{\Omega}(S)$ over Roquette sections $S$ of $P$, which would reduce the conjecture to four one-parameter families.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**(a) $P = C_p$, $p$ any prime.** The indecomposable $kC_p$-modules are $M_1, \dots, M_p$ with $\dim_k M_i = i$, where $M_p = kC_p$ is the unique projective. The augmentation sequence
$$0 \longrightarrow \Delta(kC_p) \longrightarrow kC_p \xrightarrow{\ \varepsilon\ } k \longrightarrow 0$$
identifies $\Delta(kC_p) \cong M_{p-1} = \Omega k$, so $\Omega_{P/1} = [\Omega k]$. Applying $\Omega$ again: the projective cover of $M_{p-1}$ is $kC_p$, with kernel of dimension $p - (p-1) = 1$, i.e. $\Omega^2 k \cong k$. Hence $\Omega_{P/1}$ has order $2$ and
$$D^{\Omega}(C_p) = \langle \Omega_{P/1}\rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 .$$
Since $C_p$ has only the subgroups $1$ and $P$, every permutation module is a sum of copies of $k$ and $kC_p$, so every capped endo-permutation module is endotrivial; thus $D(C_p) = T(C_p) = \{[k], [\Omega k]\} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$. Conclusion: $D(C_p) = D^{\Omega}(C_p)$ — the conjecture holds, at both parities.

**(b) $P = C_p \times C_p$: the syzygy relations.** $P$ has exactly $p+1$ maximal subgroups $M_0, \dots, M_p$, each of index $p$. For $i \neq j$, since $P$ is abelian and $M_iM_j = P$, the product $P$-set decomposes into a single orbit with stabilizer $M_i \cap M_j = 1$:
$$P/M_i \times P/M_j \;\cong\; P/1 .$$
Applying the syzygy relation $\Omega_{X\times Y} = \Omega_X + \Omega_Y$ gives
$$\Omega_{P/M_i} + \Omega_{P/M_j} \;=\; \Omega_{P/1} \qquad (i \neq j).$$
Taking three distinct indices $i, j, l$ (possible since $p+1 \ge 3$) yields $\Omega_{P/M_i} + \Omega_{P/M_j} = \Omega_{P/M_i} + \Omega_{P/M_l}$, hence $\Omega_{P/M_j} = \Omega_{P/M_l}$ for all $j \neq l$, and therefore all $p+1$ maximal syzygies coincide. Writing $\omega$ for this common class,
$$D^{\Omega}(C_p \times C_p) = \langle \omega \rangle, \qquad \Omega_{P/1} = 2\omega .$$
So $D^{\Omega}$ is cyclic, generated by a single relative syzygy of infinite order ($\omega$ restricts nontrivially to a rank-one subgroup complementary to $M_i$, and $TF(P) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ by Carlson–Thévenaz). Dade's computation of the Dade group of an abelian $p$-group shows $D(P)$ is exactly this group, so again $D(P) = D^{\Omega}(P)$.

The example makes the shape of the conjecture visible: the relations among $\Omega_X$ come entirely from the Burnside ring of $P$ (products of $P$-sets), so $D^{\Omega}(P)$ is always a quotient of $B(P)$ that is computable by counting orbits. Bouc's conjecture asserts that this purely combinatorial construction already exhausts the representation-theoretic group $D(P)$ — proved for all odd $p$, still open for $p = 2$.
