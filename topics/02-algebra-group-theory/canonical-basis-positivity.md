# Canonical Basis Positivity

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/canonical-basis-positivity` · **Status:** solved-recently (symmetric types settled; non-symmetric residue open)

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $\mathbf{U} = \mathbf{U}_v(\mathfrak{g})$ be the quantized enveloping algebra of a symmetrizable Kac–Moody algebra $\mathfrak{g}$ over $\mathbb{Q}(v)$, with negative half $\mathbf{f} \cong \mathbf{U}^-$. Lusztig (1990) and Kashiwara (1991) constructed a distinguished $\mathcal{A} = \mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]$-basis $\mathbf{B} \subset {}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{f}$, the **canonical (global crystal) basis**.

**Positivity Conjecture (Lusztig).** For all $b, b' \in \mathbf{B}$:

- **(P1) Multiplicative positivity.** $b\,b' = \sum_{b'' \in \mathbf{B}} m^{b''}_{b,b'}\, b''$ with $m^{b''}_{b,b'} \in \mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}]$.
- **(P2) Comultiplicative positivity.** $r(b) = \sum \hat{c}^{\,b_1,b_2}_{b}\, b_1 \otimes b_2$ with $\hat{c} \in \mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}]$, where $r$ is Lusztig's twisted comultiplication on $\mathbf{f}$.
- **(P3) Modified form.** The canonical basis $\dot{\mathbf{B}}$ of the modified algebra $\dot{\mathbf{U}} = \bigoplus_{\lambda,\mu \in X} {}_{\lambda}\mathbf{U}_{\mu}$ has structure constants in $\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}]$.
- **(P4) Based modules.** For any based module $(M, B(M))$ in Lusztig's sense, the action of $\dot{\mathbf{B}}$ on $B(M)$ has coefficients in $\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}]$, and the canonical basis of a tensor product $V(\lambda) \otimes V(\mu)$ expands positively.

A complete resolution requires either a proof of (P1)–(P4) for **all** symmetrizable Cartan data, or an explicit counterexample (a single structure constant with a negative coefficient). The symmetric (simply-laced) case is a theorem; the non-symmetric case remains conjectural.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Fix a Cartan datum $(I, \cdot)$ with $i\cdot i = 2d_i \in 2\mathbb{Z}_{>0}$, $a_{ij} = 2\frac{i\cdot j}{i\cdot i} \in \mathbb{Z}_{\le 0}$ for $i\neq j$. Set $v_i = v^{d_i}$, $[n]_i = \frac{v_i^n - v_i^{-n}}{v_i - v_i^{-1}}$, $[n]_i! = \prod_{k=1}^n [k]_i$. The datum is **symmetric** if $d_i = 1$ for all $i$ (simply-laced).

$\mathbf{f}$ is the $\mathbb{Q}(v)$-algebra on generators $\theta_i$, $i \in I$, modulo the quantum Serre relations
$$\sum_{p+p' = 1-a_{ij}} (-1)^{p}\, \theta_i^{(p)}\theta_j\theta_i^{(p')} = 0, \qquad \theta_i^{(n)} := \theta_i^n/[n]_i! .$$
It is $\mathbb{N}I$-graded, $\mathbf{f} = \bigoplus_{\nu} \mathbf{f}_\nu$, and carries a nondegenerate symmetric form $(\cdot,\cdot)$ with $(\theta_i,\theta_i) = (1-v_i^{-2})^{-1}$ and $(xy, z) = (x \otimes y, r(z))$. Let ${}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{f}$ be the $\mathcal{A}$-subalgebra generated by all $\theta_i^{(n)}$.

The **bar involution** is the $\mathbb{Q}$-algebra map with $\overline{v} = v^{-1}$, $\overline{\theta_i} = \theta_i$. Let $\mathcal{L}$ be the $\mathbb{Z}[v^{-1}]$-lattice spanned by PBW monomials (fixed reduced word, finite type) or by Kashiwara's operators $\tilde{F}_{i_1}\cdots\tilde{F}_{i_k}\cdot 1$. The canonical basis is characterized by
$$\overline{b} = b, \qquad b \equiv (\text{PBW/crystal monomial}) \ \operatorname{mod} v^{-1}\mathcal{L},$$
and is unique with these properties.

**Geometric model (symmetric type).** Let $Q$ be a quiver with vertex set $I$, $V = \bigoplus V_i$ an $I$-graded space of dimension $\nu$, $E_V = \bigoplus_{h \in \Omega}\operatorname{Hom}(V_{h'},V_{h''})$, acted on by $G_V = \prod_i GL(V_i)$. Lusztig (1991) defines a category $\mathcal{Q}_V$ of $G_V$-equivariant semisimple perverse sheaves on $E_V$, generated by direct images $\pi_!(\underline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}[\dim])$ from flag varieties of $Q$-representations, and proves
$$\bigoplus_{\nu} K_0(\mathcal{Q}_{V_\nu}) \;\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\; {}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{f}, \qquad [\mathrm{IC}] \mapsto b,$$
with $v$ acting by cohomological shift. Products correspond to induction (convolution) and $r$ to restriction; positivity of $m^{b''}_{b,b'}$ then reads
$$m^{b''}_{b,b'} = \sum_{n} \dim \mathcal{H}^{n}\big(\mathrm{Ind}(\mathrm{IC}_b \boxtimes \mathrm{IC}_{b'})\big)_{\mathrm{IC}_{b''}}\, v^{n} \in \mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}],$$
a consequence of the **decomposition theorem** of Beilinson–Bernstein–Deligne–Gabber: the induction of semisimple complexes of geometric origin is a direct sum of shifted simple perverse sheaves, so multiplicities are dimensions, hence nonnegative.

**Algebraic model (all symmetrizable types).** The quiver Hecke (KLR) algebras $R(\nu)$, defined by diagrammatic generators $e(\mathbf{i}), y_k, \psi_k$ with polynomial parameters $Q_{ij}(u,w)$, satisfy $\bigoplus_\nu K_0(R(\nu)\text{-}\mathrm{proj}) \cong {}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{f}$. Classes of indecomposable projectives $[P_b]$ form a basis with structure constants in $\mathbb{N}[q,q^{-1}]$ automatically (induction of projectives decomposes with nonnegative multiplicities). Positivity for $\mathbf{B}$ is therefore equivalent to the identification $\mathbf{B} = \{[P_b]\}$.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1990.** Lusztig, *Canonical bases arising from quantized enveloping algebras* (JAMS): construction in finite ADE type via PBW bases and the bar involution; positivity conjectured on the basis of the geometric interpretation.
- **1991.** Kashiwara constructs global crystal bases for all symmetrizable Kac–Moody types by the grand-loop argument, purely algebraically; the two constructions coincide (Lusztig 1990/1991; Grojnowski–Lusztig 1993).
- **1991.** Lusztig, *Quivers, perverse sheaves, and quantized enveloping algebras* (JAMS): the perverse-sheaf model, which proves (P1)–(P2) in symmetric type and (P3)–(P4) in symmetric type via the geometry of Nakajima quiver varieties and $\dot{\mathbf{U}}$.
- **1993.** *Introduction to Quantum Groups* collects the positivity statements (§14.4.13, §22–25) and states them for general symmetrizable data, where the definition of $\mathbf{B}$ is algebraic and no geometry is available.
- **2008–2011.** Khovanov–Lauda and Rouquier categorify $\mathbf{U}^-$ for arbitrary symmetrizable data; Varagnolo–Vasserot (Crelle 659, 2011) and Rouquier prove $\mathbf{B} = \{[P_b]\}$ in **symmetric** type, giving a second, sheaf-theoretic-free proof of (P1) there.
- **2012.** Kang–Kashiwara categorify integrable highest-weight modules $V(\Lambda)$ by cyclotomic KLR algebras $R^\Lambda(\nu)$ for all symmetrizable types.
- **Present.** Positivity is a theorem in symmetric type and a conjecture in non-symmetric type, where the KLR indecomposable basis is not known to agree with $\mathbf{B}$.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **All symmetric (simply-laced) Cartan data**, finite, affine and general Kac–Moody: (P1)–(P4) proved (Lusztig 1991, 1993 Part IV; Varagnolo–Vasserot 2011). This covers types $A_n$, $D_n$, $E_{6,7,8}$, $A^{(1)}_n$, $D^{(1)}_n$, $E^{(1)}_{6,7,8}$ and all symmetric hyperbolic data.
- **Rank 1** ($\mathfrak{sl}_2$): $\dot{\mathbf{B}}$ and all structure constants are explicit and manifestly positive (Lusztig 1993, §25.3).
- **Non-symmetric rank 2:** explicit canonical bases and positive structure constants computed in type $B_2$ (Xi Nanhua, *Canonical basis for type $B_2$*, J. Algebra 214, 1999) and, degree by degree, in type $G_2$ by direct computation; verification is complete for all graded pieces $\mathbf{f}_\nu$ with $|\nu|$ small (typically $|\nu| \le 8$–$10$ in published tables).
- **Tensor products in finite type $A$:** positivity of the canonical basis of $V(\lambda)\otimes V(\mu)$ follows from the $q$-Schur / affine type $A$ geometric picture (Lusztig 1992, *Canonical bases in tensor products*, PNAS 89).
- **Positivity of $\dot{\mathbf{B}}$-action on $V(\Lambda)$** in symmetric type via Nakajima quiver varieties; the analogous statement for cyclotomic KLR modules holds for the projective basis in all symmetrizable types.
- **Related settings:** positivity of cluster monomials in quantum cluster algebras arising from monoidal categorifications (Kang–Kashiwara–Kim–Oh, JAMS 31, 2018; Qin, Duke Math. J. 166, 2017) is proved in the same categorical framework.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No geometric model in non-symmetric type.** The quiver-variety construction requires $d_i = 1$; there is no variety whose $G_V$-equivariant perverse sheaves reproduce $\mathbf{f}$ for $B_n$, $C_n$, $F_4$, $G_2$. Folding a simply-laced quiver by a diagram automorphism produces $\sigma$-fixed data whose canonical basis is Lusztig's basis for the folded type only after passing to a Grothendieck group with signs; the decomposition theorem then controls the *unfolded* multiplicities, not the folded ones.
- **Failure of the decomposition theorem as a positivity engine.** Any equivariant-sheaf argument needs the objects to be of geometric origin. For non-symmetric data the natural candidates (KLR modules with unequal degrees $d_i$) are not known to be realized by IC sheaves; the graded multiplicity spaces have no known cohomological interpretation.
- **The KLR basis vs. the canonical basis.** $\{[P_b]\}$ is positive by construction, but proving $[P_b] = b$ requires bar-invariance and the $v^{-1}\mathcal{L}$-congruence for indecomposable projectives. In symmetric type this comes from purity/parity of IC stalks; in non-symmetric type no substitute for purity is available, and $\{[P_b]\}$ could in principle differ from $\mathbf{B}$ by an upper-triangular change of basis with negative entries.
- **Torsion phenomena.** Williamson's torsion-explosion examples show that integral/modular analogues of Kazhdan–Lusztig positivity (the $p$-canonical basis) behave wildly; this removes the heuristic that "any canonically defined basis is positive."
- **Known negativity for variants.** Structure constants of canonical-type bases for quantum supergroups and some $\imath$quantum groups (quantum symmetric pairs) genuinely fail to be positive (Li–Wang, *Positivity vs negativity of canonical bases*, arXiv:1501.00688). Positivity is therefore not a formal consequence of bar-invariance plus integrality.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: (P1)–(P4) whenever the Cartan datum is symmetric, plus rank $\le 2$ non-symmetric cases and finitely many computed degrees.

Open: the implication
$$\text{$(I,\cdot)$ symmetrizable, not symmetric} \;\Longrightarrow\; m^{b''}_{b,b'} \in \mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}].$$

The exact step to cross is the identification $\mathbf{B} = \{[P_b] : b\}$ inside $K_0\!\left(\bigoplus_\nu R(\nu)\text{-}\mathrm{proj}\right)$ for non-symmetric $R(\nu)$. Equivalently: prove that the graded endomorphism rings of indecomposable projective $R(\nu)$-modules are "pure" enough that $[P_b]$ is bar-invariant and congruent to a crystal monomial mod $v^{-1}\mathcal{L}$. A single non-symmetric $\nu$ with $m^{b''}_{b,b'}$ having a negative coefficient would disprove Lusztig's conjecture outright.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **KLR representation theory in non-symmetric type.** Groups around Kleshchev (Oregon), Kang–Kashiwara–Kim–Oh (KIAS/Seoul), Brundan and Webster (Virginia/UC Davis) study cuspidal and standard module theory for $R(\nu)$ in types $B$, $C$, $F$, $G$, aiming at a PBW-type filtration strong enough to force bar-invariance of $[P_b]$.
- **Geometrization via folding and Springer theory.** Attempts to realize non-symmetric $\mathbf{f}$ inside $\sigma$-equivariant derived categories of simply-laced quiver varieties, extending Lusztig's $\sigma$-analogue of the canonical basis; the obstruction is that the folded objects carry a sign twist. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Quantum symmetric pairs.** Bao–Wang's $\imath$canonical bases (Astérisque 402, 2018) supply a family where positivity holds in the geometrically realizable cases (type AIII via Bao–Kujawa–Li–Wang) and fails otherwise, sharpening what "symmetric" is doing in the proof.
- **Monoidal categorification and cluster positivity.** Kang–Kashiwara–Kim–Oh and Qin connect canonical-basis positivity to positivity of quantum cluster monomials via categories of quantum affine algebra modules; extensions to twisted (non-simply-laced) quantum affine algebras are active. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Machine-assisted search.** Systematic computation of $\mathbf{B}$ in types $B_3$, $C_3$, $F_4$, $G_2$ up to moderate degree, looking for a negative coefficient; no counterexample reported.

## 8. Future Work

- Construct a characteristic-free "purity" criterion for graded KLR projectives that does not pass through the decomposition theorem.
- Develop a Springer-theoretic or Hall-algebraic model of $\mathbf{f}$ for non-symmetric data — for example via valued quivers over finite fields with $|\mathbb{F}_{q^{d_i}}|$-graded structure, where counting points would supply nonnegative multiplicities.
- Isolate the exact property of the Cartan datum used in the Li–Wang negativity examples and prove it fails for symmetrizable Kac–Moody data.
- Push the computational frontier: an automated proof of positivity for all $\nu$ with $|\nu| \le N$ in type $G_2$ or $F_4$ would either raise confidence sharply or produce the counterexample.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take $\mathfrak{g} = \mathfrak{sl}_3$, type $A_2$, $I = \{1,2\}$, $a_{12}=a_{21}=-1$, $d_1=d_2=1$ (symmetric). The Serre relation in $\mathbf{f}$ is
$$\theta_1^2\theta_2 - [2]\,\theta_1\theta_2\theta_1 + \theta_2\theta_1^2 = 0, \qquad [2] = v+v^{-1}.$$

**Degree $\nu = \alpha_1+\alpha_2$.** $\dim \mathbf{f}_\nu = 2$; both $\theta_1\theta_2$ and $\theta_2\theta_1$ are bar-invariant and lie in ${}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbf{f}$, so
$$\mathbf{B}_{\alpha_1+\alpha_2} = \{\theta_1\theta_2,\ \theta_2\theta_1\}.$$

**Degree $\nu = 2\alpha_1+\alpha_2$.** Kostant partitions give $\dim \mathbf{f}_\nu = 2$, with
$$\mathbf{B}_{2\alpha_1+\alpha_2} = \{\theta_1^{(2)}\theta_2,\ \theta_2\theta_1^{(2)}\}, \qquad \theta_1^{(2)} = \theta_1^2/[2].$$
Dividing the Serre relation by $[2]$ gives the identity
$$\theta_1\theta_2\theta_1 = \theta_1^{(2)}\theta_2 + \theta_2\theta_1^{(2)}.$$

**Structure constants.** With $b = \theta_1 \in \mathbf{B}_{\alpha_1}$:
$$\theta_1 \cdot (\theta_1\theta_2) = \theta_1^2\theta_2 = (v+v^{-1})\,\theta_1^{(2)}\theta_2,$$
$$\theta_1 \cdot (\theta_2\theta_1) = \theta_1\theta_2\theta_1 = 1\cdot\theta_1^{(2)}\theta_2 + 1\cdot\theta_2\theta_1^{(2)}.$$
Every coefficient lies in $\mathbb{N}[v,v^{-1}]$, as (P1) predicts.

**Geometric reading.** For the $A_2$ quiver $1 \to 2$ and $V$ of dimension $(2,1)$, $E_V = \operatorname{Hom}(V_1,V_2) \cong \mathbb{C}^2$ has two $G_V$-orbits: the open orbit $\{\text{rank }1\}$ and $\{0\}$. The two IC sheaves $\mathrm{IC}(\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathrm{rank}\,1})$ and $\mathrm{IC}(\{0\})$ correspond exactly to $\theta_1^{(2)}\theta_2$ and $\theta_2\theta_1^{(2)}$. The convolution computing $\theta_1 \cdot (\theta_2\theta_1)$ decomposes as a direct sum of these two ICs with multiplicity one each — the coefficients $1,1$ above are dimensions of multiplicity spaces, so they cannot be negative. The whole content of the open case is that for $B_2$, $G_2$, $F_4$ and friends there is no such variety to count in.
