# Milnor's Conjecture on Quadratic Forms

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/milnors-conjecture-on-quadratic-forms` · **Status:** solved-recently

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $F$ be a field with $\operatorname{char} F \neq 2$. Let $W(F)$ be the Witt ring of nondegenerate symmetric bilinear forms over $F$, $I = I(F) \subset W(F)$ the fundamental ideal of even-rank forms, and $K^M_*(F)$ the Milnor $K$-ring. Milnor (1970) asked whether two natural graded maps are isomorphisms:

1. **Quadratic-form form (the "Milnor conjecture on quadratic forms").** The map
$$s_n : K^M_n(F)/2 \longrightarrow I^n/I^{n+1}, \qquad \{a_1,\dots,a_n\} \mapsto \langle\langle a_1,\dots,a_n\rangle\rangle \bmod I^{n+1}$$
is an isomorphism of graded rings for every $n \ge 0$.

2. **Norm-residue form (the mod-2 Bloch–Kato conjecture).** The Galois symbol
$$h_n : K^M_n(F)/2 \longrightarrow H^n\!\left(F, \mathbb{Z}/2\right), \qquad \{a_1,\dots,a_n\} \mapsto (a_1)\cup\cdots\cup(a_n)$$
is an isomorphism, where $H^n(F,\mathbb{Z}/2)$ is Galois cohomology of $\operatorname{Gal}(F_{\mathrm{sep}}/F)$.

Together these give the *cohomological classification of quadratic forms*: $I^n/I^{n+1} \cong H^n(F,\mathbb{Z}/2)$ via invariants $e_n$, so the filtration of the Witt ring is computed by Galois cohomology. A complete resolution requires proving both maps are bijective for **all** fields of characteristic $\neq 2$ and all $n$; a disproof would be a single field $F$ and degree $n$ with $\ker s_n \neq 0$ or $h_n$ non-surjective.

**Status.** Both statements are theorems. $h_n$ is Voevodsky (2003); $s_n$ is Orlov–Vishik–Voevodsky (2007), with an independent proof by Morel (2004). The page is retained because several natural generalizations (schemes, char 2 refinements, elementary proofs, effectivity) remain open.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Witt ring.** Isometry classes of nondegenerate symmetric bilinear forms over $F$ form a semiring under $\perp$ and $\otimes$; the Grothendieck–Witt ring $GW(F)$ is its group completion, and $W(F) = GW(F)/\mathbb{Z}\cdot \mathbb{H}$, where $\mathbb{H} = \langle 1,-1\rangle$ is the hyperbolic plane. Every class has a unique anisotropic representative (Witt decomposition).

**Fundamental ideal.** $\dim : W(F) \to \mathbb{Z}/2$ has kernel $I$, generated by binary forms $\langle 1, -a \rangle$. Powers $I^n$ are additively generated by **$n$-fold Pfister forms**
$$\langle\langle a_1,\dots,a_n\rangle\rangle \;=\; \langle 1,-a_1\rangle \otimes \cdots \otimes \langle 1,-a_n\rangle, \qquad a_i \in F^\times ,$$
of rank $2^n$. Pfister forms are round: $\langle\langle \mathbf{a}\rangle\rangle \cong c\,\langle\langle \mathbf{a}\rangle\rangle$ for any represented $c$, and a Pfister form is isotropic iff hyperbolic (Pfister, 1966).

**Milnor $K$-theory.**
$$K^M_*(F) \;=\; T_{\mathbb{Z}}\!\left(F^\times\right)\big/ \left\langle a \otimes (1-a) : a \neq 0,1 \right\rangle ,$$
so $K^M_0 = \mathbb{Z}$, $K^M_1 = F^\times$, and $K^M_n$ is generated by symbols $\{a_1,\dots,a_n\}$ subject to Steinberg relations. Write $k^M_n(F) = K^M_n(F)/2$.

**Well-definedness of $s_n$.** Steinberg is respected: $\langle\langle a, 1-a\rangle\rangle$ is isotropic (it represents $a + (1-a) = 1$ in the relevant subform), hence hyperbolic, hence zero in $W(F)$. Surjectivity of $s_n$ is immediate since Pfister forms generate $I^n$; the whole content is **injectivity**, i.e. that every relation among Pfister classes modulo $I^{n+1}$ comes from a Milnor $K$-theory relation.

**Low-degree invariants.** $e_0 = \dim \bmod 2$; $e_1 = $ signed discriminant $d_\pm \in F^\times/(F^\times)^2 = H^1$; $e_2 = $ Clifford (Hasse–Witt) invariant in $\operatorname{Br}(F)[2] = H^2(F,\mathbb{Z}/2)$; $e_3$ the Arason invariant.

**Key structural theorem (Arason–Pfister Hauptsatz, 1971).** A nonzero anisotropic form in $I^n$ has $\dim \ge 2^n$, and $\bigcap_{n\ge 0} I^n = 0$. This makes the filtration separated, so $\{I^n/I^{n+1}\}$ genuinely determines $W(F)$ up to the associated graded.

**Motivic input.** Voevodsky's proof runs through motivic cohomology $H^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{Z}/2)$, the identification $H^{n,n}(\operatorname{Spec} F,\mathbb{Z}/2) \cong k^M_n(F)$ (Nesterenko–Suslin–Totaro), the motivic Steenrod algebra, and Rost motives attached to Pfister quadrics.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1937.** Witt introduces $W(F)$ (*Theorie der quadratischen Formen in beliebigen Körpern*).
- **1966.** Pfister's multiplicative form theory; $I^n$ generated by $n$-fold Pfister forms.
- **1970.** Milnor, *Algebraic K-theory and quadratic forms* (Invent. Math. 9), defines $K^M_*$ and poses both questions, verifying $n \le 2$ modulo then-known results and computing global/local fields.
- **1971.** Arason–Pfister Hauptsatz.
- **1975.** Arason constructs $e_3$ and proves $\ker(e_3) = I^4$ in relevant cases.
- **1981.** Merkurjev proves $h_2$ is an isomorphism (Merkurjev's theorem), settling $n=2$ and the $2$-torsion Brauer group statement.
- **1982.** Kato proves the characteristic-2 analogue completely, using differential forms and $\Omega^n_F$ in place of Galois cohomology.
- **1986–1990.** Rost, then Merkurjev–Suslin, settle $n = 3$; Jacob–Rost and Szyjewski (1989) settle $n = 4$.
- **1996–2003.** Voevodsky's motivic program: $\mathbb{A}^1$-homotopy theory, motivic Eilenberg–MacLane spaces, motivic Steenrod operations, Rost's chain lemma and degree formula. Result: $h_n$ is an isomorphism for all $n$ (Publ. IHÉS 98, 2003).
- **2004.** Morel gives an independent proof of $s_n$ via $\mathbb{A}^1$-homotopy and the unramified Milnor–Witt sheaf.
- **2007.** Orlov–Vishik–Voevodsky (Annals 165) derive $s_n$ from an exact sequence for $k^M_*$ involving the function field of a Pfister quadric — the cleanest known deduction.
- **2011.** Voevodsky completes Bloch–Kato at all primes (Annals 174); Haesemeyer–Weibel (2019) give a book-length exposition.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **$n = 0,1$:** elementary — rank mod 2 and signed discriminant.
- **$n = 2$:** Merkurjev (1981), all fields, char $\neq 2$. Equivalent: $\operatorname{Br}(F)[2]$ is generated by quaternion algebras.
- **$n = 3$:** Rost (1986), Merkurjev–Suslin (1990).
- **$n = 4$:** Jacob–Rost (1989), Szyjewski (1989).
- **All $n$, $\operatorname{char} F = 2$:** Kato (1982), with $H^n$ replaced by $\Omega^n_F/(1+\wp)$-type groups.
- **All $n$, char $\neq 2$:** Voevodsky (2003) for $h_n$; OVV (2007) and Morel (2004) for $s_n$.
- **Special fields with elementary proofs:** finite fields ($k^M_n = 0$, $I^2 = 0$ for $n \ge 2$), $\mathbb{R}$ and real closed fields ($W \cong \mathbb{Z}$, all graded pieces $\mathbb{Z}/2$), local fields ($I^3 = 0$), global fields (Hasse–Minkowski plus class field theory; $I^3$ is torsion-free detected by signatures), fields of cohomological dimension $\le 2$, and $C_r$-fields.
- **Schemes:** Jacobson (2017) proves the Milnor conjecture for the Witt ring of a scheme $X$ with $2$ invertible, of finite Krull dimension, under hypotheses including $X$ regular with a suitable real spectrum; general schemes remain open.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

The obstacles here are historical — why the problem resisted 30 years — and residual, for the surviving generalizations.

- **No induction on $n$ within quadratic-form theory.** Classical tools (transfer, Springer's theorem, Cassels–Pfister subform theorem) control forms of small dimension. Beyond $n = 4$ the number of generators of $I^n$ grows and no finite list of "elementary" relations was known to generate all relations; the Hauptsatz gives only a dimension bound, not a presentation.
- **Function-field descent fails naively.** The standard method — pass to $F(q)$, the function field of a quadric, and compare kernels — requires knowing $\ker\big(k^M_n(F) \to k^M_n(F(q))\big)$. Computing this kernel is exactly as hard as the conjecture; before OVV it was known only in low degree.
- **Galois cohomology is not functorially computable.** $H^*(F,\mathbb{Z}/2)$ has no presentation by generators and relations for a general field; surjectivity of $h_n$ (that all classes are sums of symbols) is a genuinely nontrivial "decomposability" statement failing for many other cohomology theories.
- **Why the motivic machine was needed.** The proof requires: a triangulated category $DM(F)$ with Tate twists, motivic Steenrod operations $Q_i$ (whose construction needs the topological realization and a Milnor-basis argument), and the existence of *Rost motives* — direct summands of the motive of a Pfister quadric with prescribed cohomology. Each ingredient is external to quadratic-form theory. Rost's chain lemma and degree formula, needed to build these motives, took over a decade to write down completely.
- **Residual barriers.** For schemes, $W(X)$ has no Witt-decomposition and Pfister forms need not generate $I^n(X)$; the Gersten–Witt complex may fail exactness. In char 2, the split between symmetric bilinear and quadratic forms means "the" conjecture bifurcates, and Kato's differential-forms target has no obvious motivic model.

## 6. The Gap

For fields, the gap is closed: $s_n$ and $h_n$ are isomorphisms, and $e_n : I^n/I^{n+1} \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } H^n(F,\mathbb{Z}/2)$ is well-defined for all $n$. What remains is a set of precisely stated successors:

1. **Elementary proof.** No proof of injectivity of $s_n$ for $n \ge 5$ is known that avoids motivic homotopy theory. The specific missing object is a combinatorial presentation of $I^n/I^{n+1}$ by Pfister generators and quadratic-form relations.
2. **Schemes / rings.** For which $X$ (or commutative rings $R$ with $2 \in R^\times$) is $I^n(X)/I^{n+1}(X) \cong H^n_{\text{ét}}(X,\mathbb{Z}/2)$, and what corrects the failure — real-cohomology terms, Gersten complex obstruction, or a genuine counterexample?
3. **Effectivity.** Given $q \in I^n$ with $e_n(q) = 0$, produce an explicit witness expressing $q$ in $I^{n+1}$. Current proofs are non-constructive.
4. **Char 2 unification.** A single statement covering Kato's theorem and Voevodsky's, e.g. in terms of $\mathbb{Z}/2$-motivic cohomology of $\operatorname{Spec} F$ over $\mathbb{Z}$ rather than over a base field.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Quadratic form theory after OVV.** Karpenko, Merkurjev, Vishik and collaborators use the resolved conjecture as input to study *isotropy over function fields*, $J$-invariants, and possible dimensions of anisotropic forms in $I^n$ (Vishik's "excellent connections", Karpenko's incompressibility results). This is the most active downstream direction.
- **Motivic and $\mathbb{A}^1$-homotopy.** Bachmann, Hopkins, Hoyois, Levine and others study the Milnor–Witt $K$-theory sheaf $K^{MW}_*$, the $\eta$-inverted and very effective slice filtrations, and their relation to $I^n$. Bachmann's work on the $\rho$-inverted sphere gives a homotopy-theoretic reproof of the graded Witt-ring computation. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Witt groups of schemes.** Jacobson, Balmer, Walter, Zibrowius: comparison of $I^n(X)$ with étale and real cohomology, and Gersten-type conjectures for triangular Witt groups. Extension of the Milnor conjecture beyond regular affine schemes is an open program. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Formalization.** Efforts in Lean/mathlib have formalized Witt rings, Pfister forms and the Arason–Pfister Hauptsatz; a formal proof of Merkurjev's theorem, let alone OVV, is far off. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Institutions.** UCLA (Merkurjev), LMU Munich / Bielefeld (Karpenko, Vishik-adjacent groups), IAS and Institut Fourier (motivic homotopy), Universität Duisburg-Essen.

## 8. Future Work

- **Find a proof of injectivity of $s_n$ inside quadratic-form theory.** Merkurjev and Vishik have repeatedly flagged this: OVV reduces the conjecture to a $k^M$ exact sequence whose proof still uses motivic input. A quadratic-form proof would likely produce a presentation of $I^n/I^{n+1}$ and be effective.
- **Push to $I^n$-dimension questions.** Determine the possible values of $\dim q$ for anisotropic $q \in I^n$ — known for $n \le 4$ (Pfister, Hoffmann, Vishik), open for $n \ge 5$; the first unknown value in $I^5$ is a concrete target.
- **Extend to schemes and to $\mathbb{Z}[1/2]$-algebras**, using Gersten–Witt complexes and the real spectrum.
- **Bloch–Kato analogues** for Milnor–Witt $K$-theory and for higher Witt groups in the Balmer sense.
- **Effective/algorithmic content:** algorithms deciding, for a number field or function field, whether a given form lies in $I^n$.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Case $F = \mathbb{R}$: the conjecture verified in every degree by hand.**

*Witt ring.* Over $\mathbb{R}$ every form diagonalizes as $\langle 1,\dots,1,-1,\dots,-1\rangle$ with $p$ ones and $q$ minus-ones, and the anisotropic part is determined by the signature $\sigma = p - q$. Thus
$$\operatorname{sgn} : W(\mathbb{R}) \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ } \mathbb{Z}, \qquad I(\mathbb{R}) = \{\sigma \text{ even}\} = 2\mathbb{Z}.$$
Since $I$ is generated by $\langle 1,-a\rangle$ with $a \in \{\pm 1\}$, and $\langle 1,-1\rangle = 0$, $\langle 1,1\rangle$ has signature $2$, we get $I^n(\mathbb{R}) = 2^n\mathbb{Z}$ and
$$I^n/I^{n+1} \cong 2^n\mathbb{Z}/2^{n+1}\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/2 .$$

*Milnor K-theory mod 2.* $\mathbb{R}^\times/(\mathbb{R}^\times)^2 = \{\pm 1\}$, so $k^M_n(\mathbb{R})$ is generated by $\{-1,-1,\dots,-1\}$. Steinberg relations impose nothing that kills it (the class survives because $h_n$ below sends it to a nonzero element), and $k^M_*(\mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{F}_2[\epsilon]$ with $\epsilon = \{-1\}$ in degree 1. So $k^M_n(\mathbb{R}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$.

*Galois cohomology.* $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}) = \mathbb{Z}/2$ and $H^*(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathbb{Z}/2) = \mathbb{F}_2[x]$, $\deg x = 1$. So $H^n(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{Z}/2) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$, generated by $x^n = (-1)\cup\cdots\cup(-1)$.

*The maps.* $s_n(\{-1\}^n) = \langle\langle -1,\dots,-1\rangle\rangle = \langle 1,1\rangle^{\otimes n} = \langle 1,1,\dots,1\rangle$, a positive definite form of rank $2^n$ and signature $2^n$ — exactly a generator of $I^n/I^{n+1} = 2^n\mathbb{Z}/2^{n+1}\mathbb{Z}$. And $h_n(\{-1\}^n) = x^n \neq 0$. Both maps are surjections $\mathbb{Z}/2 \to \mathbb{Z}/2$, hence isomorphisms. Composing, $e_n$ is "signature divided by $2^n$, mod 2".

**Contrast $F = \mathbb{F}_q$, $q$ odd.** Here $|\mathbb{F}_q^\times/(\mathbb{F}_q^\times)^2| = 2$, but every $3$-dimensional form over a finite field is isotropic, so every $2$-fold Pfister form $\langle\langle a,b\rangle\rangle$ (rank 4) is isotropic, hence hyperbolic: $I^2(\mathbb{F}_q) = 0$. Correspondingly $k^M_n(\mathbb{F}_q) = 0$ for $n \ge 2$ (Steinberg forces all symbols to vanish since every element of $\mathbb{F}_q^\times$ is a norm from $\mathbb{F}_{q^2}$), and $\operatorname{cd}_2 \mathbb{F}_q = 1$ gives $H^n(\mathbb{F}_q,\mathbb{Z}/2) = 0$ for $n \ge 2$. All three groups vanish simultaneously — the conjecture holds trivially, and $W(\mathbb{F}_q)$ has order 4 or 8 depending on $q \bmod 4$.

**Degree 2, a nontrivial instance.** Over $F = \mathbb{Q}$, $e_2\big(\langle\langle -1,-1\rangle\rangle\big)$ is the class of the Hamilton quaternion algebra $\left(\frac{-1,-1}{\mathbb{Q}}\right)$ in $\operatorname{Br}(\mathbb{Q})[2]$, which is nonzero (ramified at $2$ and $\infty$). So $\langle\langle -1,-1\rangle\rangle \notin I^3(\mathbb{Q})$ — consistent with the Hauptsatz, since it is anisotropic of dimension $4 < 8$. Merkurjev's theorem is the statement that *every* class in $\operatorname{Br}(\mathbb{Q})[2]$ arises this way from a sum of such Pfister classes, and injectivity of $s_2$ says the only relations are the Milnor ones.
