# Koethe Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/koethe-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $R$ be an associative ring, not assumed unital, not assumed commutative.

**Köthe's conjecture (1930).** If $R$ has no nonzero nil two-sided ideal, then $R$ has no nonzero nil one-sided ideal.

Equivalently, in the form used in most of the literature:

**(K)** The sum of any two nil left ideals of $R$ is nil.

A proof must handle arbitrary associative rings — no chain conditions, no identities, no field of coefficients, no bound on nilpotency indices. A disproof requires exhibiting a ring $R$ with nil left ideals $I, J$ and elements $a \in I$, $b \in J$ such that $a + b$ is not nilpotent. Both are open after 96 years.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Nil and nilpotent.** $x \in R$ is *nilpotent* if $x^n = 0$ for some $n \geq 1$. A subset $S \subseteq R$ is *nil* if every element of $S$ is nilpotent; $S$ is *nilpotent* if $S^n = 0$ for some $n$, i.e. all products $s_1 s_2 \cdots s_n$ vanish. Nilpotent $\Rightarrow$ nil; the converse fails (e.g. $\bigoplus_{n\ge 1} x k[x]/(x^n)$).

**Radicals.** The *lower nil radical* (Baer, prime radical) $\mathrm{Nil}_*(R)$ is the intersection of all prime ideals, equivalently the smallest ideal with nil semiprime quotient. The *upper nil radical* $\mathrm{Nil}^*(R)$ (the Köthe radical) is the sum of all nil two-sided ideals; it is itself nil. The *Jacobson radical* $J(R)$ is the intersection of maximal modular left ideals. Always
$$\mathrm{Nil}_*(R) \subseteq \mathrm{Nil}^*(R) \subseteq J(R).$$
$R$ is *nil* if $R = \mathrm{Nil}^*(R)$, i.e. every element is nilpotent.

**Equivalent formulations.** The following are equivalent to (K):

1. $\mathrm{Nil}^*(R)$ contains every nil left ideal of $R$, for all $R$.
2. *(Krempa 1972)* For every nil ring $N$, the matrix ring $M_2(N)$ is nil. Equivalently $M_n(N)$ is nil for all $n \ge 2$; equivalently $M_n(I)$ is nil for every nil ideal $I \trianglelefteq R$.
3. *(Krempa 1972, via Amitsur 1956)* For every nil ring $N$, the polynomial ring $N[x]$ is Jacobson radical: $J(N[x]) = N[x]$.
4. *(Puczyłowski–Smoktunowicz 1998)* For every nil ring $N$, $N[x]$ is Brown–McCoy radical, i.e. $N[x]$ has no homomorphic image that is a unital simple ring.

Formulation (3) rests on **Amitsur's theorem**: for any ring $R$, $J(R[x]) = N[x]$ where $N = J(R[x]) \cap R$ is a nil ideal of $R$. So $J(N[x]) = N[x]$ would follow at once if $N[x]$ were nil — but that implication is blocked (§3).

**Growth data.** For an affine $k$-algebra $A$ generated by a finite-dimensional subspace $V$, the Gelfand–Kirillov dimension is
$$\mathrm{GK}(A) = \limsup_{n \to \infty} \frac{\log \dim_k (k + V + \cdots + V^n)}{\log n}.$$
GK dimension is the main quantitative handle on nil algebras.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1930.** Gottfried Köthe, studying rings whose quotient by the radical is completely reducible (*Math. Z.* 32), asks whether a ring with no nonzero nil ideal can have a nonzero nil one-sided ideal.
- **1948–1957.** Kaplansky and Amitsur: nil algebras satisfying a polynomial identity are locally nilpotent — Köthe holds for PI rings.
- **1956.** Amitsur: $J(R[x]) = N[x]$ with $N \trianglelefteq R$ nil. Amitsur further conjectures that $R$ nil $\Rightarrow R[x]$ nil.
- **1963–1969.** Levitzki, Herstein–Small, Lanski: nil one-sided ideals (indeed nil subrings) of rings with the ascending chain condition on annihilators, Goldie rings, or rings with Krull dimension are nilpotent.
- **1972.** Krempa's reductions (2)–(3) above turn Köthe into a statement about $2\times 2$ matrices over nil rings — still the central reformulation.
- **2000.** Smoktunowicz constructs a nil algebra $N$ over a countable field with $N[x]$ not nil (*J. Algebra* 233). This refutes Amitsur's conjecture and kills the most natural route to (3), while leaving Köthe itself open ($N[x]$ could still be Jacobson radical).
- **2002.** Smoktunowicz: a simple nil ring exists — evidence that nil rings are far wilder than the classical chain-condition intuition.
- **2007.** Lenagan–Smoktunowicz (*JAMS* 20): an infinite-dimensional affine nil algebra of finite GK dimension (bounded by about $20$; later refinements push it toward $3$). This settled a Small problem and showed growth restrictions alone will not force local nilpotence.
- **Status 2026.** No counterexample, no proof. The problem is listed among the principal open problems of noncommutative ring theory (Lam, *A First Course in Noncommutative Rings*, §10; Smoktunowicz, ICM 2006).

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

Köthe's conjecture is a **theorem** in each of the following classes.

| Class | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Rings with ACC on left (or right) annihilators | nil one-sided ideals are nilpotent | Levitzki (1963) |
| Right Noetherian rings; semiprime Goldie rings | nil subrings are nilpotent | Herstein–Small (1964), Lanski (1969) |
| Rings with right Krull dimension | nil subrings nilpotent | Lanski (1969), Gordon–Robson |
| Algebras satisfying a polynomial identity | nil PI algebras are locally nilpotent | Kaplansky (1948), Amitsur (1957) |
| Nil rings of bounded index $n$ (i.e. $x^n = 0$ for all $x$) | $M_k(N)$ is nil of bounded index; (K) holds | Klein (1984); cf. Nagata–Higman in char $0$ or char $> n$ |
| Algebras over a field, locally nilpotent case | Levitzki radical absorbs one-sided ideals | classical |
| Monomial algebras / algebras with a monomial basis closed under subwords | nil $\Rightarrow$ locally nilpotent | Belov, Borisenko, Latyshev (survey, 1997) |
| Rings that are $\mathbb{Z}$-graded, with nil ideals homogeneous | Köthe holds for the graded version | Smoktunowicz (2001) |

Also proven unconditionally: the sum of a nil left ideal and a **nilpotent** left ideal is nil; $\mathrm{Nil}^*(R)$ itself is nil; and $M_n(\mathrm{Nil}^*(R)) \subseteq \mathrm{Nil}^*(M_n(R))$ with equality equivalent to (K).

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No finiteness to induct on.** Every positive case above imports a chain condition, an identity, or a bound on indices. Köthe's statement allows a nil ring whose elements have unbounded nilpotency index and which contains no nonzero nilpotent ideal. Noetherian induction, Goldie's theorem and Ore localization all become unavailable simultaneously.
- **Nil is not closed under the natural constructions.** Smoktunowicz (2000) shows nilness is not preserved by $N \mapsto N[x]$. Combined with Krempa's reduction, this means the "obvious" route — prove $M_2(N)$ nil by proving $N[x]$ nil — is provably dead. One must show $J(N[x]) = N[x]$ without nilness, i.e. quasi-regularity of $1 + f$ for $f$ with non-nilpotent coefficients-in-context.
- **Combinatorics of words blows up.** For $a,b$ in nil left ideals, deciding whether $a+b$ is nilpotent means controlling all $2^n$ words of length $n$ in $a,b$ simultaneously. There is no known normal form or filtration; Golod–Shafarevich-type constructions produce nil algebras precisely by making such word combinatorics uncontrollable.
- **Counterexample candidates resist verification.** Golod's algebras, Smoktunowicz's simple nil ring and the finite-GK nil algebras are all built by inverse-limit / graded approximations. Verifying that a specific sum $a+b$ is *not* nilpotent in such a construction requires a lower bound on the Hilbert series of a subalgebra, and the constructions are designed to give upper bounds.
- **Radical theory is too coarse.** $\mathrm{Nil}^*$ is not a hereditary-enough radical to be computed from left-ideal data; equivalently, no known radical-theoretic invariant separates $\mathrm{Nil}^*(R)$ from the sum of nil left ideals.

## 6. The Gap

Everything proven lives on one side of a single boundary: **local nilpotence**. In each verified class, "nil" is upgraded to "locally nilpotent" (or nilpotent) by a finiteness hypothesis, and for locally nilpotent rings Köthe is trivial — the Levitzki radical is a two-sided ideal containing every locally nilpotent one-sided ideal. Since Golod's 1964 examples, nil $\ne$ locally nilpotent is known even for finitely generated algebras.

The precise unresolved step is Krempa's:

> Given a nil ring $N$ and $\alpha \in M_2(N)$, show $\alpha$ is nilpotent.

Concretely: for $a, b, c, d \in N$ all nilpotent, must $\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d\end{pmatrix}$ be nilpotent? Already the case $b = d = 0$ reduces to whether $a + $ (a conjugate-like term) is nilpotent; no argument is known that does not implicitly bound indices or invoke an identity. Crossing the gap means producing a nilpotency bound for $\alpha$ from the *pointwise* nilpotency of $N$, with no uniformity — or constructing $N$ where it fails.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Warsaw school (Puczyłowski, and successors).** Radical-theoretic reformulations: Brown–McCoy and Behrens radicals of $N[x]$, $N[x,y]$; behaviour of nil rings under polynomial and free-product extensions. Puczyłowski's problem lists remain the standard roadmap.
- **Edinburgh / growth-theoretic program (Smoktunowicz, Lenagan, and collaborators).** Constructing nil algebras with prescribed GK dimension; the current frontier is nil affine algebras of GK dimension arbitrarily close to $2$ and the question of whether GK dimension $< 3$ forces local nilpotence *(frontier — verify)*. A positive answer would give a new verified class in §4.
- **Bell–Small circle.** Nil subalgebras of algebras of finite GK dimension, primitivity of nil-related constructions, and the interaction between the Kurosh problem and Köthe.
- **Graded and filtered reductions.** Several groups pursue: if Köthe holds for all $\mathbb{Z}$-graded nil rings, does it hold in general? Associated-graded arguments lose nilness, which is the sticking point *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Computer-assisted search.** Attempts to search finitely presented nil algebras (via Gröbner–Shirshov bases) for a non-nil $2\times2$ matrix; the undecidability of nilness for finitely presented algebras limits this to bounded degrees.

## 8. Future Work

- Settle the finite-GK-dimension case: prove or refute that a nil affine algebra of GK dimension $< 3$ is locally nilpotent. This is the most concrete open sub-problem with an identified attack (growth of Hilbert series of subalgebras generated by two elements).
- Decide whether $J(N[x]) = N[x]$ for the specific nil ring $N$ of Smoktunowicz (2000) with $N[x]$ not nil. Either answer is informative: failure disproves Köthe, success is strong evidence and a new technique.
- Develop a "bounded-index approximation" theory: quantify how far a nil ring is from bounded index, and prove Köthe for rings of finite "index growth".
- Extend Klein's bounded-index matrix argument to indices growing slowly along a filtration.
- Attack the Brown–McCoy formulation (4): construct a homomorphism from $N[x]$ onto a unital simple ring for some nil $N$ — this alone would refute Köthe.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**The rank-one matrix trick — why $2\times2$ matrices decide the conjecture.**

Let $I, J$ be nil left ideals of $R$, $a \in I$, $b \in J$. Put
$$
M \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} a & a \\ b & b \end{pmatrix} \in M_2(I+J).
$$
Write $u = \binom{a}{b}$ (column) and $v = (1\;\;1)$ (row), so $M = uv$ and $vu = a+b$. Since matrix multiplication is associative,
$$
M^{\,n} \;=\; u\,(vu)^{n-1}\,v \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix}(a+b)^{n-1}\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1\end{pmatrix}
= \begin{pmatrix} a(a+b)^{n-1} & a(a+b)^{n-1} \\ b(a+b)^{n-1} & b(a+b)^{n-1}\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Now suppose $M^{\,n} = 0$. Then
$$
(a+b)^{n+1} \;=\; (vu)^{n+1} \;=\; v\,(uv)^{n}\,u \;=\; v\,M^{\,n}\,u \;=\; 0 .
$$
So **$a+b$ is nilpotent as soon as $M$ is** — and $M$ is a $2\times2$ matrix all of whose entries lie in the nil left ideal $I+J$. Conversely if $(a+b)^{m}=0$ then $M^{m+1}=u(vu)^m v = 0$. Hence
$$
a+b \text{ nilpotent} \iff \begin{pmatrix} a & a \\ b & b\end{pmatrix} \text{ nilpotent},
$$
which is exactly Krempa's reduction: Köthe $\iff$ $M_2(N)$ nil for every nil ring $N$.

**A case where the answer is forced.** Take $N$ nil of bounded index $2$: $x^2 = 0$ for all $x \in N$. Linearising $(x+y)^2 = 0$ gives $xy + yx = 0$, hence $xyx = -x^2y = 0$ for all $x,y$. So $N$ satisfies the polynomial identity $xyx = 0$ and is therefore locally nilpotent (Kaplansky–Amitsur); indeed $x(yz)x = 0$ gives $xyzx=0$, and $N$ is nilpotent on any two generators. Then for $a,b \in N$: $(a+b)^2 = ab + ba = 0$, so $a+b$ is nilpotent and $M^3 = u(vu)^2 v = 0$. Köthe holds trivially here.

**Where it breaks.** Replace "index $2$" by "each element nilpotent, index unbounded". Take $a, b$ with $a^{k}=0$, $b^{\ell}=0$ but $k,\ell$ depending on the elements. Expanding $(a+b)^{n}$ gives $2^{n}$ words $w_1w_2\cdots w_n$ with $w_i \in \{a,b\}$; no individual relation forces a word of length $n$ to vanish once $n > k+\ell$, and Golod's construction shows a nil algebra can be built precisely so that no such word-length bound exists. That is the entire content of the open problem.
