# Malle's Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/malles-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Fix a number field $k$ and a transitive permutation group $G \le S_n$. Let
$$N_k(G;X) \;=\; \\#\{\, K/k \ \text{in a fixed } \bar k \;:\; [K:k]=n,\ \operatorname{Gal}(\tilde K/k)\cong G \ \text{as a permutation group},\ \left|\mathrm{Nm}_{k/\mathbb Q}\mathfrak d_{K/k}\right| \le X \,\},$$
where $\tilde K$ is the Galois closure of $K/k$ and $\mathfrak d_{K/k}$ the relative discriminant. Malle (2002, 2004) conjectured that there are constants $c(k,G)>0$, $a(G)\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$ and $b(k,G)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge1}$, all given by explicit group-theoretic recipes (Section 2), with
$$N_k(G;X)\ \sim\ c(k,G)\, X^{a(G)} (\log X)^{b(k,G)-1}\qquad (X\to\infty).$$

Two graded forms are studied. **Weak Malle:** for every $\varepsilon>0$,
$$X^{a(G)} \ \ll_{k,G}\ N_k(G;X)\ \ll_{k,G,\varepsilon}\ X^{a(G)+\varepsilon}.$$
**Strong Malle:** the asymptotic above, with the predicted power of $\log$.

The strong form as originally stated is **false** — Klüners (2005) gave a counterexample with $G=C_3\wr C_2$ over $\mathbb Q$. The open problem is therefore: (i) prove the weak form for general $G$ (open for essentially all groups of degree $\ge 6$, and for all of $A_n,S_n$ with $n\ge 6$); (ii) find the correct replacement for $b(k,G)$ and prove it. A complete resolution means proving the corrected asymptotic for all $(k,G)$, or exhibiting a $G$ whose true growth exponent differs from $a(G)$.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Index of a permutation.** For $g\in G\le S_n$ set
$$\operatorname{ind}(g)\;=\;n-\\#\{\text{orbits of }\langle g\rangle \text{ on }\{1,\dots,n\}\}.$$
Equivalently $\operatorname{ind}(g)=\sum_i (\ell_i-1)$ over the cycle lengths $\ell_i$ of $g$. The index is a conjugacy-class invariant, so $\operatorname{ind}(c)$ is defined for classes $c$.

**The $a$-invariant.**
$$a(G)\;=\;\frac{1}{\displaystyle\min_{g\in G,\ g\ne 1}\operatorname{ind}(g)} .$$
The motivation is the tame conormal count: a prime $\mathfrak p$ of $k$ tamely ramified in $K$ with inertia generated by $g$ contributes $\mathrm{Nm}(\mathfrak p)^{\operatorname{ind}(g)}$ to the discriminant, so the number of available primes below $X$ scales like $X^{1/\operatorname{ind}(g)}$, and cheapest ramification dominates.

**The $b$-invariant.** Let $\mathrm{ind}(G)=\min_{g\ne1}\operatorname{ind}(g)$ and
$$\mathcal C_{\min}=\{\,\text{conjugacy classes }c\subset G\setminus\{1\} : \operatorname{ind}(c)=\mathrm{ind}(G)\,\}.$$
The absolute Galois group $G_k$ acts on $\mathcal C_{\min}$ through the cyclotomic character: $\sigma\cdot c = c^{\chi(\sigma)}$, where $c^m=\{g^m : g\in c\}$ and $\chi: G_k\to (\mathbb Z/|G|)^\times$. Then
$$b(k,G)\;=\;\\#\left(\mathcal C_{\min}/G_k\right).$$
Over $k=\mathbb Q$ the action is by the full group $(\mathbb Z/|G|)^\times$.

**Anchoring theorems.** The conjecture sits between two classical inputs: the Shafarevich theorem (every solvable $G$ is realizable over $\mathbb Q$), which supplies nonemptiness in the solvable case, and the Hermite–Minkowski finiteness theorem (finitely many fields of bounded degree and discriminant), which makes $N_k(G;X)$ finite. Upper bounds of any kind are constrained by Minkowski's bound $|d_K| \gg c^n$, which gives no power saving in $X$.

**Function-field analogue.** Over $\mathbb F_q(t)$, $N(G;q^m)$ counts $\mathbb F_q$-points on Hurwitz spaces $\mathrm{Hur}_{G,m}$; $a(G)$ is the expected dimension growth and $b(k,G)$ the number of geometrically irreducible components of top dimension. This translation (Ellenberg–Venkatesh–Westerland circle of ideas) is where the failure of the $b$-invariant is most transparent.

## 3. History & State of the Art

- **1971.** Davenport–Heilbronn count cubic fields: $N^+_{\mathbb Q}(S_3;X)\sim X/(12\zeta(3))$ (totally real), $N^-\sim X/(4\zeta(3))$. This is the first nontrivial case, predating the conjecture by 30 years.
- **1985–1989.** Mäki, then Wright, settle all abelian $G$ in the regular representation over any number field, with the correct power of $\log$.
- **1995–2006.** Degree-only upper bounds: Schmidt's $N_n(X)\ll X^{(n+2)/4}$ (Astérisque 228), then Ellenberg–Venkatesh's $N_n(X)\ll X^{\exp(C\sqrt{\log n})}$ — subexponential in $n$, but still far above the conjectured $O(X^{1+\varepsilon})$ for $S_n$.
- **2002/2004.** Malle formulates the conjecture in *J. Number Theory* and gives extensive numerical support in *Experimental Mathematics*, including tables for degrees $\le 5$ and many imprimitive groups.
- **2004–2005.** Klüners–Malle prove weak Malle for all nilpotent $G$ in the regular representation. Klüners then disproves the strong form with $G=C_3\wr C_2$ (Section 10).
- **2005/2010.** Bhargava's quartic and quintic counts give $N_{\mathbb Q}(S_4;X)\sim c_4 X$ and $N_{\mathbb Q}(S_5;X)\sim c_5 X$ via higher composition laws.
- **2015.** Türkelli reformulates $b$ using components of Hurwitz schemes, repairing the known counterexamples.
- **2021–2022.** Alberts refines the conjecture through $H^1$-statistics; Jiuya Wang proves Malle for $S_n\times A$ ($n\le 5$, $A$ abelian) by a hard-to-soft "invariant" argument; Lemke Oliver–Thorne give the first improvement on Schmidt's exponent for all large $n$.

| Numerics | Tables in degrees $\le 5$ and selected degree 6–8 groups, $X$ up to $\sim10^{11}$ | Malle 2004; Klüners' databases |

Lower bounds of the shape $N_k(G;X)\gg X^{a(G)}$ are known for a much wider class than upper bounds — e.g. all $G$ with a suitable "cheap" generating class, by explicit construction of ramified extensions (Klüners–Malle).

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No parametrization beyond degree 5.** Bhargava's proofs rest on prehomogeneous vector spaces whose orbits classify rings of rank $n$; such spaces exist for $n\le 5$ only ($\mathrm{Sym}^3\mathbb Z^2$, $\mathbb Z^2\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^2\mathbb Z^3$, $\mathbb Z^4\otimes\wedge^2\mathbb Z^5$). For $n=6$ the moduli of sextic rings is not a single reductive orbit problem, and geometry-of-numbers counting has no starting point.
- **Cusps dominate.** Even where a parametrization exists, the count is a lattice-point count in an unbounded fundamental domain; the cuspidal region contains almost all points, and separating reducible from irreducible orbits requires case-specific averaging that has resisted generalization.
- **Class-group torsion barrier.** Ellenberg–Venkatesh reduce $N_n(X)$ to bounds on $\\#\mathrm{Cl}(K)[\ell]$. The trivial bound $\\#\mathrm{Cl}(K)[\ell]\ll |d_K|^{1/2+\varepsilon}$ is what makes the resulting exponents grow with $n$; the conjectured $|d_K|^{\varepsilon}$ is itself open for every $(\ell,n)$ pair except a handful.
- **Inductive/embedding methods lose the top term.** Counting $G$-fields by first counting a quotient $G/N$ and then twisted $N$-covers requires uniformity in the base field's discriminant. The needed uniformity — a bound for $N_k(N;X)$ polynomial in $d_k$ with the *right* exponent — is exactly as hard as the conjecture.
- **The invariant $b$ is not intrinsic to $G$.** Klüners' counterexample shows the log-power is sensitive to which roots of unity are forced into $\tilde K$ by ramification; no purely character-theoretic recipe on $\mathcal C_{\min}$ can see this. The corrected invariant (Türkelli, Alberts) involves component counts of Hurwitz spaces, which are themselves computable only in examples.

## 6. The Gap

Proved territory is: (a) $G$ abelian or nilpotent (regular representation), where class field theory replaces the counting problem by a lattice count over ray class groups; (b) $n\le5$ with $G=S_n$, where a composition law exists; (c) sporadic solvable families with abelian normal subgroups. Everything proved uses either class field theory or a rank-$\le5$ parametrization.

The precise barrier is the first case with **no abelian normal subgroup and no parametrization**: e.g. $G=S_6$ in its degree-6 action, or $A_5$ in degree 5 with $a=1/2$. Here even the weak upper bound $N(G;X)\ll X^{a(G)+\varepsilon}$ is unknown; the best available is the degree-only bound, which exceeds the target by a power of $X$ growing with $n$. Crossing the gap requires one of: a moduli interpretation of rank-$n$ rings for $n\ge6$ that supports counting; subconvex bounds for $\ell$-torsion in class groups; or an unconditional Hurwitz-space point count over number fields (currently available only over $\mathbb F_q(t)$, and only in the large-$q$ limit).

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Inductive/twisting methods.** Alberts, Lemke Oliver, J. Wang and M. M. Wood have developed a framework counting $G$-extensions by fibering over a quotient and summing twisted $H^1$-counts; it recovers all previously known solvable cases uniformly and yields new families with a normal abelian subgroup. *(frontier — verify: the fully general "inductive methods for counting number fields" preprint and its stated hypotheses.)*
- **Nilpotent case with correct log-power.** Koymans and Pagano have extended Klüners–Malle to the sharp asymptotic for nilpotent $G$. *(frontier — verify final published form.)*
- **Hurwitz spaces / function fields.** Following Ellenberg–Venkatesh–Westerland, homological stability for braid-group homology is used to count $G$-covers of $\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb F_q}$; the outstanding issue is stability ranges independent of $q$. Groups at Wisconsin, MIT and Bonn are active here.
- **Torsion in class groups.** Any power saving over $\\#\mathrm{Cl}(K)[\ell]\ll|d_K|^{1/2}$ valid for all $K$ of degree $n$ immediately improves the degree-$n$ upper bounds (Ellenberg–Venkatesh, Lemke Oliver–Thorne, Anderson–Gafni–Lemke Oliver–et al.).
- **Alternative orderings.** Counting by conductor, by Artin conductor of a fixed representation, or by "product of ramified primes" gives cleaner conjectures with fewer counterexamples; Wood's work on ordering-dependence is the reference point.

## 8. Future Work

1. **Settle $S_6$ or $A_5$ weakly.** Either would be the first non-solvable case outside the composition-law range and would validate a genuinely new method.
2. **Prove a uniform version.** Bounds for $N_k(G;X)$ with explicit polynomial dependence on $\mathrm{disc}(k)$ are the missing lemma in every inductive argument; formulating and proving the right "uniformity conjecture" is a stated priority of the Alberts–Lemke Oliver–Wang–Wood program.
3. **A canonical $b$-invariant.** Reconcile Türkelli's Hurwitz-component definition with Alberts' cohomological one, and test both against systematically generated counterexample families $G\wr C_m$.
4. **Local mass formulae.** Bhargava's mass heuristics predict the constant $c(k,G)$ as an Euler product; verifying the predicted $c$ even in a proved case beyond $n=5$ would be strong evidence.
5. **Secondary terms.** Extend Bhargava–Shankar–Tsimerman's $X^{5/6}$ cubic secondary term to quartics/quintics, sharpening what "$\sim$" can mean.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**(a) $G=S_3$ in degree 3, $k=\mathbb Q$.** Conjugacy classes: identity; transpositions $(12)$ with cycle type $2+1$, so $3$ orbits become $2$ and $\operatorname{ind}=3-2=1$; $3$-cycles with one orbit, $\operatorname{ind}=3-1=2$. Hence $\mathrm{ind}(G)=1$ and $a(G)=1$. The minimal-index set $\mathcal C_{\min}$ is the single class of transpositions, so the cyclotomic action has one orbit and $b(\mathbb Q,S_3)=1$. Prediction:
$$N_{\mathbb Q}(S_3;X)\sim c X (\log X)^0 = cX.$$
Davenport–Heilbronn confirm this with $c=\tfrac1{12\zeta(3)}+\tfrac1{4\zeta(3)}=\tfrac{1}{3\zeta(3)}\approx 0.2774$ when both signs of discriminant are counted.

**(b) Klüners' counterexample: $G=C_3\wr C_2=(C_3\times C_3)\rtimes C_2\le S_6$.** The action is on two blocks of size 3. Compute indices:

- $(a,1)$, $a\ne1$: cycle type $3+1+1+1$, orbits $=4$, $\operatorname{ind}=6-4=2$.
- $(a,b)$, $a,b\ne1$: type $3+3$, orbits $=2$, $\operatorname{ind}=4$.
- block-swapping involutions: type $2+2+2$, orbits $=3$, $\operatorname{ind}=3$.

So $\mathrm{ind}(G)=2$, $a(G)=1/2$. The minimal classes are the $(a,1)$ and $(1,b)$ types, fused by conjugation and by the cyclotomic action $c\mapsto c^{-1}$ into a single orbit, giving $b(\mathbb Q,G)=1$ and the prediction $N_{\mathbb Q}(G;X)\sim cX^{1/2}$.

Klüners instead produces a sub-family already exceeding this. Every $K$ with Galois group $G$ has a quadratic resolvent field $F$ (the fixed field of the index-2 subgroup $C_3\times C_3$). Restrict to $F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})=\mathbb Q(\zeta_3)$. Then $\tilde K/F$ is an unramified-away-from-ramification $C_3\times C_3$-extension, and by Kummer theory over a field containing $\zeta_3$ the cyclic cubic extensions of $F$ are parametrized by classes in $F^\times/(F^\times)^3$; counting those with norm-discriminant $\le X$ gives
$$\\#\{K : F=\mathbb Q(\zeta_3),\ |d_K|\le X\}\ \gg\ X^{1/2}\log X .$$
The extra $\log X$ comes from the free $\mathbb Z$-rank of the relevant Kummer group once $\zeta_3\in F$ — an arithmetic accident invisible to $\mathcal C_{\min}$. Hence $b(\mathbb Q,G)\ge 2$ in truth while the recipe returns $1$. The $a$-invariant survives; only the log-power is wrong, which is why the weak form remains the live conjecture.
