# Cancellation Problem for Affine Spaces

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/cancellation-problem-for-affine-spaces` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $k$ be a field and let $X$ be an affine $k$-variety. The **Zariski Cancellation Problem** (ZCP) asks:

> If $X \times \mathbb{A}^1_k \cong \mathbb{A}^{n+1}_k$ as $k$-varieties, does it follow that $X \cong \mathbb{A}^n_k$?

Equivalently, in ring-theoretic form: if $A$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra and
$$A[y] \;\cong_k\; k[x_1,\dots,x_{n+1}],$$
is $A \cong_k k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$?

A complete resolution means either a proof valid for all $n$ and all $k$, or an explicit $A$ with $A^{[1]} \cong k^{[n+1]}$ and $A \not\cong k^{[n]}$. The latter has been achieved when $\operatorname{char} k = p > 0$ and $n \ge 3$ (Gupta, 2014). The problem is **open for $n \ge 3$ in characteristic $0$**, and this is the case usually meant by "the cancellation problem for affine spaces" today.

Notation: $R^{[n]}$ denotes a polynomial ring in $n$ variables over $R$. Isomorphisms are $k$-algebra isomorphisms, *not* required to respect the projection $A[y] \to A$; requiring that makes the problem trivial.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Setting.** $A$ is an affine domain over $k$, $X = \operatorname{Spec} A$.

**Locally nilpotent derivations (LNDs).** A $k$-derivation $D : A \to A$ is locally nilpotent if for each $a \in A$ there is $n$ with $D^n(a) = 0$. In characteristic $0$, $\exp(tD)$ defines a $\mathbb{G}_a$-action on $X$, and $\operatorname{LND}(A)$ corresponds bijectively to such actions. Two invariants:
$$\mathrm{ML}(A) \;=\; \bigcap_{D \in \operatorname{LND}(A)} \ker D, \qquad \mathrm{Dk}(A) \;=\; k\text{-algebra generated by } \bigcup_{D \neq 0} \ker D .$$
For $A = k^{[n]}$ one has $\mathrm{ML}(A) = k$ and $\mathrm{Dk}(A) = A$ (for $n \ge 2$). In characteristic $p$ the correct substitute uses **exponential maps**: $k$-algebra homomorphisms $\varphi : A \to A^{[1]} = A[u]$ with $\varphi|_{u=0} = \mathrm{id}$ and $\varphi$ satisfying the co-action identity; $A^\varphi = \{a : \varphi(a) = a\}$ is the ring of invariants, and $\mathrm{ML}, \mathrm{Dk}$ are defined by the same formulas over nontrivial $\varphi$.

**The key failure of naive invariants.** $\mathrm{ML}$ is not stable: $\mathrm{ML}(A) = k$ does not follow from, nor imply, $\mathrm{ML}(A^{[1]}) = k$ in a usable direction; there exist $A \not\cong k^{[3]}$ with $\mathrm{ML}(A^{[1]}) = k$.

**Asanuma threefolds.** Let $\operatorname{char} k = p > 0$, and take integers $m \ge 2$, $e \ge 1$, $s \ge 1$ with $p^e \nmid sp$. Set
$$A \;=\; \frac{k[x,y,z,t]}{\bigl(x^m y - z^{p^e} - t - t^{sp}\bigr)} .$$
Asanuma's theory of $\mathbb{A}^n$-fibrations shows $A^{[1]} \cong k^{[4]}$. Such $A$ are $k$-forms of $\mathbb{A}^3$ trivialized by the purely inseparable extension $k^{1/p^e}$.

**Necessary conditions on a counterexample.** If $X \times \mathbb{A}^1 \cong \mathbb{A}^{n+1}$ then $X$ is smooth (char $0$), affine, rational, factorial ($\operatorname{Pic}(X) = 0$), with $\mathcal{O}(X)^\times = k^\times$, all reduced cohomology vanishing, and $X$ is $\mathbb{A}^1$-contractible in the Morel–Voevodsky homotopy category. So every classical topological or cohomological invariant is blind to the question.

**Logarithmic Kodaira dimension.** For a smooth affine surface $S$ with SNC completion $(\bar S, D)$, $\bar\kappa(S) = \kappa(\bar S, K_{\bar S} + D)$. It satisfies $\bar\kappa(S \times \mathbb{A}^1) = -\infty$ and is the engine of the $n = 2$ solution.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **Origin.** The problem descends from Zariski's question on cancellation for function fields (if $K(x) \cong k(x_1,\dots,x_{n+1})$, is $K$ rational?) and was formulated in the polynomial-ring form by Abhyankar, Eakin and Heinzer (1972). The birational version is false: Beauville, Colliot-Thélène, Sansuc and Swinnerton-Dyer (1985) produced stably rational non-rational threefolds.
- **1972.** Abhyankar–Eakin–Heinzer: cancellation holds for $n = 1$ over any field, in fact for curves: $A^{[m]} \cong B^{[m]}$ with $\dim A = 1$ implies $A \cong B$. Hochster (1972) gave the first counterexample to cancellation for general (non-polynomial) coefficient rings.
- **1979–1981.** Fujita, and independently Miyanishi–Sugie, proved $n = 2$ in characteristic $0$; Russell extended it to all characteristics. Proof: $\bar\kappa$ plus classification of affine-ruled rational surfaces.
- **1989.** Danielewski: cancellation fails for general affine surfaces ($x z = y^2 - 1$ vs $x^2 z = y^2 - 1$), showing no soft argument can work.
- **1996.** Makar-Limanov: the Koras–Russell cubic $X = \{x + x^2 y + z^2 + t^3 = 0\} \subset \mathbb{A}^4$ is a smooth contractible threefold with $\mathrm{ML}(X) \ne k$, hence $X \not\cong \mathbb{A}^3$ — the first candidate counterexample.
- **2007.** Dubouloz: $\mathrm{ML}(X \times \mathbb{A}^1) = k$ for Koras–Russell threefolds, killing the ML invariant as a cancellation obstruction.
- **2014.** Gupta: ZCP **fails** for $\mathbb{A}^3$ in characteristic $p > 0$, and then for $\mathbb{A}^n$, all $n \ge 3$, characteristic $p$. Method: a residue-field/Derksen-invariant computation on Asanuma threefolds.
- **2016–2018.** Hoyois–Krishna–Østvær and Dubouloz–Fasel: Koras–Russell threefolds are $\mathbb{A}^1$-contractible — motivic homotopy gives no obstruction either.
- **Current SOTA.** Solved: $n \le 2$ (all $k$); $n \ge 3$, $\operatorname{char} k = p > 0$ (fails). Open: $n \ge 3$, $\operatorname{char} k = 0$.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **$n = 1$, any field, any characteristic:** cancellation holds (Abhyankar–Eakin–Heinzer 1972). More strongly, $A^{[m]} \cong k^{[1+m]} \Rightarrow A \cong k^{[1]}$.
- **$n = 2$, $\operatorname{char} k = 0$:** holds (Fujita 1979; Miyanishi–Sugie 1980). **$n=2$, $\operatorname{char} k = p$:** holds (Russell 1981). Also stable: $A^{[m]} \cong k^{[2+m]} \Rightarrow A \cong k^{[2]}$.
- **$n \ge 3$, $\operatorname{char} k = p > 0$:** **fails.** Explicit: $A = k[x,y,z,t]/(x^m y - z^{p^e} - t - t^{sp})$ with $m \ge 2$, $e \ge 1$, $p^e \nmid sp$ satisfies $A^{[1]} \cong k^{[4]}$, $A \not\cong k^{[3]}$ (Gupta, *Invent. Math.* 195, 2014); $A^{[n-3]} \not\cong k^{[n]}$ gives all $n \ge 3$ (*Adv. Math.* 264, 2014).
- **Fibration-restricted cases:** if $A^{[1]} = k^{[n+1]}$ and the isomorphism can be chosen compatible with an $\mathbb{A}^1$-fibration over a base of dimension $\le 1$, cancellation holds (Sathaye 1983 for $\mathbb{A}^2$-fibrations over a DVR; Asanuma–Bhatwadekar 1997 for $\mathbb{A}^2$-fibrations over 1-dimensional Noetherian domains).
- **Trivial-invariant classes:** if $X$ carries a fixed-point-free $\mathbb{G}_a$-action with the quotient an affine space of the right dimension, cancellation follows.
- **Negative benchmarks (non-affine-space $X$):** cancellation fails for surfaces (Danielewski 1989: $S_1 \not\cong S_2$ but $S_1 \times \mathbb{A}^1 \cong S_2 \times \mathbb{A}^1$) and for coefficient rings (Hochster 1972).

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **All classical invariants vanish.** A hypothetical counterexample $X$ is smooth, contractible, factorial, rational, with trivial units, trivial Picard group, and $\mathbb{A}^1$-contractible. Singular cohomology, K-theory, Chow groups, and motivic invariants therefore cannot separate $X$ from $\mathbb{A}^n$.
- **$\mathbb{G}_a$-invariants are unstable.** $\mathrm{ML}$ and $\mathrm{Dk}$ do distinguish $X$ from $\mathbb{A}^n$ (Makar-Limanov 1996), but they do not survive multiplication by $\mathbb{A}^1$: Dubouloz (2007) showed $\mathrm{ML}(X \times \mathbb{A}^1) = k$ for Koras–Russell threefolds. Any invariant that is to prove cancellation must be *stable*, and no stable nontrivial invariant of $\mathbb{A}^3$-like threefolds is known.
- **The char-$p$ proof does not transfer.** Gupta's counterexamples are purely inseparable $k$-forms of $\mathbb{A}^3$: they become $\mathbb{A}^3$ over $k^{1/p^e}$. Frobenius, hence this entire mechanism, is absent in characteristic $0$. Conversely, the char-$p$ failure removes any hope of a characteristic-free argument.
- **Surface techniques do not lift.** The $n = 2$ proof rests on $\bar\kappa$ and the classification of open algebraic surfaces. There is no classification of smooth affine threefolds with $\bar\kappa = -\infty$; the logarithmic minimal model program in dimension $3$ for open varieties is incomplete, and $\bar\kappa$ is not known to be a strong enough invariant there.
- **Automorphism groups are wild.** $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{A}^n)$ for $n \ge 3$ is not generated by tame automorphisms (Shestakov–Umirbaev, 2004, for $n=3$), so structural arguments that worked for $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{A}^2)$ (amalgamated free product) have no analogue.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: $n \le 2$ everywhere; disproven: $n \ge 3$ in char $p$. The gap is exactly $\{n \ge 3, \operatorname{char} k = 0\}$.

Sharply: one must either (a) construct a stable, computable invariant $I$ with $I(A) = I(A^{[1]})$ and $I(k^{[n]})$ characterizing polynomial rings — a "cancellation-stable" refinement of the Makar-Limanov invariant; or (b) exhibit a characteristic-$0$ $\mathbb{A}^3$-like threefold $X$ with $X \times \mathbb{A}^1 \cong \mathbb{A}^4$. For (b) the only serious candidates are the Koras–Russell threefolds, and the missing step is deciding whether $X \times \mathbb{A}^1 \cong \mathbb{A}^4$ for $X = \{x + x^2y + z^2 + t^3 = 0\}$. Everything known about $X$ is consistent with both answers.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Gupta's school (ISI Kolkata).** Continued analysis of the family $x^m y = F(x,z,t)$, criteria for such threefolds to be $\mathbb{A}^3$, and the "epimorphism"/"embedding" problems that share the same machinery. Gupta's ICM 2022 sectional lecture is the current reference point.
- **Dubouloz, Fasel, Poloni (Dijon/Grenoble/Basel).** $\mathbb{A}^1$-homotopy of affine threefolds, deformation families of Koras–Russell varieties, and the search for stable obstructions. Consensus in this school: $X \times \mathbb{A}^1 \cong \mathbb{A}^4$ for Koras–Russell $X$ is plausible. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Freudenburg, Daigle, Kaliman (Western Michigan / Ottawa / Miami).** LND theory: rigidity, triangulability, and structure of $\mathbb{G}_a$-actions on $\mathbb{A}^4$, aiming to classify quotients.
- **Motivic obstruction theory.** Attempts to use Chow–Witt groups / $\mathbb{A}^1$-homotopy sheaves as stable invariants have so far confirmed indistinguishability rather than produced obstructions.
- **Positive-characteristic follow-up.** Extension of Gupta-type counterexamples to non-perfect base fields and to $\mathbb{A}^2$-fibrations over higher-dimensional bases. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Future Work

1. Decide $X \times \mathbb{A}^1 \cong \mathbb{A}^4$ for the Koras–Russell cubic — the single most-cited concrete target.
2. Construct a *stable* invariant: a functor $I$ with $I(A^{[1]}) = I(A)$ detecting non-polynomiality. Candidates: derived/equivariant refinements of $\mathrm{Dk}$, or the "$\mathbb{G}_a$-fibration graph" at infinity.
3. Develop a log-MMP for smooth affine threefolds with $\bar\kappa = -\infty$, mirroring the $n = 2$ proof.
4. Settle the linearization problem for $\mathbb{G}_m$-actions on $\mathbb{A}^4$; Koras–Russell threefolds arose exactly as quotients in this program, so the two questions are coupled.
5. Study cancellation over non-closed and non-perfect fields in characteristic $0$, where forms of $\mathbb{A}^n$ give a Galois-cohomological handle absent over $\bar{k}$.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Danielewski surfaces: cancellation fails one dimension below the target.**

Over $k = \mathbb{C}$, for $n \ge 1$ set
$$S_n \;=\; \{\, x^n z = y^2 - 1 \,\} \subset \mathbb{A}^3 .$$
Each $S_n$ is a smooth affine surface, and the projection $\pi_n : S_n \to \mathbb{A}^1$, $(x,y,z) \mapsto x$, has fibres $\mathbb{A}^1$ over $x \ne 0$ and two disjoint lines $\{y = \pm 1\}$ over $x = 0$.

**Step 1 — they are not isomorphic.** Compute Makar-Limanov invariants. On $S_1$ the two derivations
$$D_1 = x\,\partial_y + 2y\,\partial_z, \qquad D_2 = x\,\partial_y' \text{-type on the symmetric chart}$$
have kernels $k[x]$ and $k[z]$ respectively, so $\mathrm{ML}(S_1) \subseteq k[x] \cap k[z] = k$. For $n \ge 2$ every nonzero LND of $\mathcal{O}(S_n)$ has kernel $k[x]$, so $\mathrm{ML}(S_n) = k[x] \ne k$. Since $\mathrm{ML}$ is an isomorphism invariant, $S_1 \not\cong S_2$.

**Step 2 — they become isomorphic after one $\mathbb{A}^1$.** Both $\pi_n$ factor through the line with doubled origin $W$ (glue two copies of $\mathbb{A}^1$ along $\mathbb{A}^1 \setminus \{0\}$): $S_n \to W$ is a $\mathbb{G}_a$-torsor, with class in $H^1(W, \mathcal{O}_W) \cong k$ given (up to scale) by $x^{-n}$. Form the fibre product
$$P \;=\; S_1 \times_W S_2 .$$
The first projection $P \to S_1$ is a $\mathbb{G}_a$-torsor over the *affine* scheme $S_1$, and $H^1(S_1, \mathcal{O}) = 0$, so it is trivial: $P \cong S_1 \times \mathbb{A}^1$. Symmetrically $P \cong S_2 \times \mathbb{A}^1$. Hence
$$S_1 \times \mathbb{A}^1 \;\cong\; S_2 \times \mathbb{A}^1, \qquad S_1 \not\cong S_2 .$$

**Why this matters for ZCP.** The same torsor-triviality trick is exactly what one would need for $\mathbb{A}^3$: find a threefold $X \ne \mathbb{A}^3$ and a common $\mathbb{G}_a$-torsor $P$ with $P \cong X \times \mathbb{A}^1 \cong \mathbb{A}^3 \times \mathbb{A}^1$. Step 1 also shows what breaks: $\mathrm{ML}(S_1 \times \mathbb{A}^1) = \mathrm{ML}(S_2 \times \mathbb{A}^1) = k$, so the invariant that separated $S_1$ from $S_2$ is destroyed by the very product one wants to analyse. That is the precise mechanism by which the Makar-Limanov obstruction fails for the Koras–Russell cubic in Section 5.
