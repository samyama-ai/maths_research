# Kashiwara's Crystal Base Theory

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/kashiwaras-crystal-base-theory` · **Status:** solved-recently

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $\mathfrak{g}$ be a Kac–Moody algebra with generalized Cartan matrix $A=(a_{ij})_{i,j\in I}$ and let $U_q(\mathfrak{g})$ be its quantized enveloping algebra over $\mathbb{Q}(q)$. The **crystal base problem** asks:

> Does every integrable highest-weight $U_q(\mathfrak{g})$-module $V(\lambda)$, and the negative half $U_q^-(\mathfrak{g})$, admit a *crystal base* — a pair $(L,B)$ consisting of a free $A_0$-lattice $L$ ($A_0\subset\mathbb{Q}(q)$ the ring of rational functions regular at $q=0$) stable under Kashiwara's modified operators $\tilde e_i,\tilde f_i$, and a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis $B$ of $L/qL$ permuted (up to $0$) by the induced operators — and is it unique up to isomorphism? Does it lift to a **global (canonical) basis** of the module itself over $\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]$?

Kashiwara answered yes for **symmetrizable** $A$ (1990–1991). The statement is therefore **solved** in that generality; what remains open, and what this page tracks, is the residual program:

1. **Non-symmetrizable Kac–Moody algebras.** No crystal base theory is known when $A$ admits no symmetrizing diagonal matrix. Open.
2. **Positivity.** Are the structure constants of the global basis of $U_q^-(\mathfrak{g})$ in $\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}[q,q^{-1}]$? Proven for symmetric $A$; open in general for symmetrizable non-symmetric $A$.
3. **Level-zero and non-integrable settings.** Crystal bases for arbitrary (non-integrable, level-zero, or superalgebra) modules.

A complete resolution of (1) means: construct $(L(\infty),B(\infty))$ for non-symmetrizable $A$ with the tensor-product rule intact, or prove no such structure exists.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Quantum group.** For a symmetrizable GCM with $d_i a_{ij}=d_j a_{ji}$, $q_i=q^{d_i}$, $U_q(\mathfrak g)$ is generated by $e_i,f_i\ (i\in I)$ and $q^h\ (h\in P^\vee)$ with
$$[e_i,f_j]=\delta_{ij}\frac{t_i-t_i^{-1}}{q_i-q_i^{-1}},\qquad t_i=q^{d_i h_i},$$
plus the $q$-Serre relations $\sum_{k=0}^{1-a_{ij}}(-1)^k e_i^{(k)}e_j e_i^{(1-a_{ij}-k)}=0$, where $x^{(k)}=x^k/[k]_i!$ and $[k]_i=\frac{q_i^k-q_i^{-k}}{q_i-q_i^{-1}}$.

**Kashiwara operators.** Every $u$ in an integrable module has a unique $i$-string decomposition
$$u=\sum_{n\ge 0} f_i^{(n)}u_n,\qquad e_i u_n=0 .$$
Define
$$\tilde f_i u=\sum_{n\ge0} f_i^{(n+1)}u_n,\qquad \tilde e_i u=\sum_{n\ge1} f_i^{(n-1)}u_n .$$
These are **not** algebra elements; they are $\mathbb{Q}(q)$-linear operators that renormalize $e_i,f_i$ so as to survive the $q\to0$ limit.

**Crystal base.** A pair $(L,B)$ with $L$ a free $A_0$-submodule, $\mathbb{Q}(q)\otimes_{A_0}L=V$, $L=\bigoplus_\mu L_\mu$, $\tilde e_iL\subset L$, $\tilde f_iL\subset L$; $B$ a $\mathbb{Q}$-basis of $L/qL$ with $B=\bigsqcup_\mu B_\mu$, $\tilde e_iB\subset B\sqcup\{0\}$, $\tilde f_iB\subset B\sqcup\{0\}$, and
$$\tilde f_i b=b' \iff b=\tilde e_i b' \quad (b,b'\in B).$$

**Theorem (Kashiwara 1990, 1991).** For symmetrizable $A$, $V(\lambda)$ has a crystal base $(L(\lambda),B(\lambda))$, unique up to isomorphism; $U_q^-(\mathfrak g)$ has $(L(\infty),B(\infty))$; and
$$B(\lambda_1)\otimes B(\lambda_2)\cong B(L(\lambda_1)\otimes L(\lambda_2))$$
with the combinatorial **tensor rule** (setting $\varepsilon_i(b)=\max\{n:\tilde e_i^nb\ne0\}$, $\varphi_i(b)=\max\{n:\tilde f_i^nb\ne0\}$):
$$\tilde f_i(b_1\otimes b_2)=\begin{cases}\tilde f_ib_1\otimes b_2 & \varphi_i(b_1)>\varepsilon_i(b_2)\\ b_1\otimes \tilde f_ib_2 & \varphi_i(b_1)\le\varepsilon_i(b_2)\end{cases}$$

**Global basis (Kashiwara 1993).** With $\bar{\ }$ the bar involution ($q\mapsto q^{-1}$, $e_i\mapsto e_i$, $f_i\mapsto f_i$) and $U^-_{\mathbb{Z}}$ the $\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]$-form, the map
$$L(\infty)\cap\overline{L(\infty)}\cap U^-_{\mathbb{Z}}\ \longrightarrow\ L(\infty)/qL(\infty)$$
is an isomorphism; the preimage $\{G(b)\}_{b\in B(\infty)}$ is the global basis, and coincides with Lusztig's canonical basis for symmetric $A$ (Lusztig 1990).

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1990.** Kashiwara, *Crystalizing the $q$-analogue of universal enveloping algebras* (Comm. Math. Phys. 133): the $q\to0$ "crystallization" idea, motivated by the physics of the ferromagnetic limit of solvable lattice models.
- **1990.** Lusztig, *Canonical bases arising from quantized enveloping algebras* (JAMS 3): independent construction via PBW bases and, for symmetric types, perverse sheaves on quiver varieties. Positivity follows geometrically.
- **1991.** Kashiwara, Duke Math. J. 63: the full existence and uniqueness theorem for symmetrizable $A$, proven by the **grand loop argument** — a simultaneous induction on weight over roughly a dozen interlocking statements, none provable in isolation.
- **1993.** Kashiwara, *Global crystal bases of quantum groups* (Duke 69): lifting $B$ to $G(b)$.
- **1992–1995.** Combinatorial models: KMN$^2$ perfect crystals and path realizations for affine types; Littelmann's path model (Ann. of Math. 142, 1995) giving a Lie-theoretic, $q$-free construction of $B(\lambda)$ and a proof of the Littlewood–Richardson rule for all symmetrizable types.
- **1997.** Kashiwara–Saito (Duke 89): geometric realization of $B(\infty)$ by irreducible components of Lusztig's nilpotent variety.
- **2008–2012.** Categorification: Khovanov–Lauda and Rouquier's quiver Hecke (KLR) algebras $R(\beta)$; Varagnolo–Vasserot (2011) and Rouquier prove the self-dual indecomposable projectives categorify the canonical basis in symmetric type; Kang–Kashiwara (Invent. Math. 190, 2012) categorify $V(\lambda)$ for all symmetrizable types via cyclotomic quotients $R^\lambda(\beta)$.
- **2015–present.** Monoidal categorification of cluster algebras in quantum affine categories (Kang–Kashiwara–Kim–Oh, Invent. Math. 211, 2018), and crystal-theoretic input to the theory of $R$-matrices.

| Rank 2 non-symmetrizable | Vacuous: every rank-2 GCM is symmetrizable |

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No invariant bilinear form.** Symmetrizability is exactly what supplies the nondegenerate Kashiwara form $(\ ,\ )$ on $U_q^-$ with $(f_iu,v)=(u,e_i'v)$. Every step of the grand loop uses it to prove almost-orthogonality $(G(b),G(b'))\in\delta_{bb'}+qA_0$. Without a form, $L(\infty)$ cannot even be characterized.
- **No PBW basis and no geometry.** Lusztig's construction needs a braid group action producing root vectors, plus, for positivity, a quiver variety whose perverse sheaves have positive stalk multiplicities. Non-symmetrizable GCMs have no known quiver, no known root vectors, and the Weyl group has no useful longest-element combinatorics in the required sense.
- **Serre relations do not obviously define the right algebra.** For non-symmetrizable $A$ it is not known whether $U_q^-$ defined by $q$-Serre relations has the "correct" size (the analogue of Gabber–Kac fails or is unproven), so even the target of a crystal base is ill-defined.
- **Positivity in non-symmetric type.** KLR categorification gives graded projectives $\leftrightarrow$ canonical basis, but in non-symmetric type $R(\beta)$ is defined over a base with unequal parameters $d_i$; the required indecomposability and self-duality statements need $R(\beta)$-modules over a field where Hom-spaces are free of the expected graded rank, which is only known under extra hypotheses (Kang–Kashiwara–Oh's supercategorification handles $\mathfrak{b}$-type shifts, not the general case).
- **Grand loop rigidity.** The 1991 induction is a package; it cannot be run on a subset of the statements, so weakening one hypothesis (symmetrizability) collapses all of it at once.

## 6. The Gap

Proven (Sec. 4): existence, uniqueness, tensor rule, global basis, and categorification for **symmetrizable** $A$; positivity for **symmetric** $A$.

The general statement (Sec. 1) additionally requires:

1. A substitute for the Kashiwara bilinear form when $D A$ is never symmetric — equivalently, a purely combinatorial or categorical characterization of $L(\infty)$ that does not invoke $(\ ,\ )$.
2. A proof that for symmetrizable non-symmetric $A$, $\dim_q \mathrm{Hom}(P_b,P_{b'})$ in the KLR category has coefficients in $\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}[q,q^{-1}]$ matching $G(b)G(b')=\sum c^{b''}_{bb'}G(b'')$ — this is the exact positivity step.

The crossing point for (1) is the construction of an integral form of $U_q^-(\mathfrak g)$ for non-symmetrizable $A$ with a bar-invariant, $q\to0$-stable lattice.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **KLR / quiver Hecke program** (Kashiwara and collaborators, RIMS Kyoto; Kang, Kim, Oh, Park in Seoul/KIAS): monoidal categorification of cluster algebras via quantum affine categories $\mathscr{C}_{\mathfrak g}^0$, where crystal combinatorics controls the cluster seeds.
- **Positivity beyond symmetric type** via KLR algebras with unequal parameters and via Hall algebras of $\imath$quivers / modulated graphs (Lu–Wang's $\imath$quantum groups and their canonical bases). *(frontier — verify)*
- **Non-symmetrizable Kac–Moody**: sporadic attempts using Borcherds-style generalizations and 2-representation theory; no positive result. Consensus is that a genuinely new invariant is needed.
- **Crystals for quantum supergroups and $\imath$quantum groups**: Bao–Wang's canonical bases for quantum symmetric pairs; crystal analogues only partially constructed.
- **Geometric Satake and crystals**: Mirković–Vilonen cycles give a second model of $B(\lambda)$; matching MV-cycle and Lusztig-data crystal structures explicitly remains combinatorially delicate.

## 8. Future Work

- Find a form-free axiomatization of $B(\infty)$ (candidate: characterization via the $\ast$-crystal structure and Kashiwara's embeddings $B(\infty)\hookrightarrow B(\infty)\otimes B_i$), then test it on non-symmetrizable rank 3.
- Establish whether the $q$-Serre algebra for non-symmetrizable $A$ has a triangular decomposition of the expected size — a prerequisite, and arguably the real problem.
- Complete positivity in non-symmetric symmetrizable type by proving self-duality of indecomposable projective $R(\beta)$-modules over $\mathbb{Z}$.
- Extend crystal bases to level-zero and non-integrable modules over quantum affine algebras beyond the extremal-weight case.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take $\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_2$, $I=\{1\}$, $A=(2)$. The two-dimensional module $V(1)$ has crystal $B(1)=\{u_+,u_-\}$ with $\tilde f u_+=u_-$, $\tilde f u_-=0$, $\tilde e u_-=u_+$, and
$$\varepsilon(u_+)=0,\ \varphi(u_+)=1,\qquad \varepsilon(u_-)=1,\ \varphi(u_-)=0 .$$

**Decompose $B(1)\otimes B(1)$** using the tensor rule of Section 2.

- $\tilde f(u_+\otimes u_+)$: $\varphi(u_+)=1>\varepsilon(u_+)=0$, so act on the left: $=u_-\otimes u_+$.
- $\tilde f(u_-\otimes u_+)$: $\varphi(u_-)=0\le\varepsilon(u_+)=0$, so act on the right: $=u_-\otimes u_-$.
- $\tilde f(u_-\otimes u_-)$: $\varphi(u_-)=0\le\varepsilon(u_-)=1$, act right: $\tilde f u_-=0$, so $=0$.

This is a string of length 3 with weights $2,0,-2$: exactly $B(2)$.

- $\tilde f(u_+\otimes u_-)$: $\varphi(u_+)=1\le\varepsilon(u_-)=1$, act right: $\tilde fu_-=0$.
- $\tilde e(u_+\otimes u_-)$: $\varphi(u_+)=1\ge\varepsilon(u_-)=1$, act left: $\tilde eu_+=0$.

So $u_+\otimes u_-$ is an isolated vertex of weight $0$: a copy of $B(0)$.

Hence $B(1)\otimes B(1)\cong B(2)\sqcup B(0)$, recovering $V(1)^{\otimes2}\cong V(2)\oplus V(0)$ **by pure combinatorics at $q=0$**, with no Clebsch–Gordan computation. Note the asymmetry of the rule: $u_-\otimes u_+$ lies in the $B(2)$ string while $u_+\otimes u_-$ is the singlet — the crystal breaks the tie that the classical tensor product leaves symmetric.

Lifting to the global basis, the singlet is the bar-invariant vector
$$G(u_+\otimes u_-)=u_+\otimes u_- - q\,u_-\otimes u_+,$$
the quantum antisymmetrizer, which reduces mod $q$ to $u_+\otimes u_-$ as required. The whole difficulty of Section 1 is that constructing such a bar-invariant, $A_0$-integral lift in general is what the grand loop does — and what has no known substitute when $A$ is not symmetrizable.
