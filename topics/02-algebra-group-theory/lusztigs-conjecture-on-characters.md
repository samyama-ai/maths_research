# Lusztig's Conjecture on Characters

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/lusztigs-conjecture-on-characters` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $G$ be a connected reductive algebraic group, split and simply connected, over an algebraically closed field $k$ of characteristic $p > 0$, with maximal torus $T$, Borel $B$, weight lattice $X(T)$, dominant weights $X(T)^+$, Weyl group $W$, and Coxeter number $h$. For $\lambda \in X(T)^+$ let $L(\lambda)$ be the irreducible $G$-module of highest weight $\lambda$ and $\chi(\lambda)$ the Weyl character.

**Lusztig's conjecture (1980).** Assume $p \ge h$. For every $\lambda \in X(T)^+$ in the *Jantzen region*
$$\langle \lambda + \rho, \alpha^\vee\rangle \le p(p-h+2) \quad \text{for all simple coroots } \alpha^\vee,$$
one has
$$\operatorname{ch} L(\lambda) \;=\; \sum_{w \in W_p,\ w\cdot\lambda \,\uparrow\, x\cdot\lambda} (-1)^{\ell(x)-\ell(w)}\, P_{w_0 w,\, w_0 x}(1)\, \chi(w\cdot\lambda),$$
where $x \in W_p$ is chosen with $x\cdot\lambda = \lambda$ in the lowest dominant alcove's orbit (see §2), $W_p$ is the affine Weyl group acting by the dot action, $P_{y,w}$ are Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials of $W_p$, and $w_0$ is the longest element of $W$.

A complete resolution means: determine the exact set of pairs $(\Phi, p)$ — root system and characteristic — for which the formula holds, or replace it by a formula valid for all $p \ge h$. The statement as literally conjectured (all $p \ge h$, all $\Phi$) is **false**: Williamson (2017) produced counterexamples. What survives, and what is now largely proved, is a corrected formula in terms of *$p$-Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials*.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Affine Weyl group and alcoves.** $W_p = W \ltimes p\mathbb{Z}\Phi^\vee$ acts on $X(T)\otimes\mathbb{R}$ by the dot action
$$w\cdot\lambda = w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho, \qquad \rho = \tfrac12\sum_{\alpha>0}\alpha .$$
It is generated by affine reflections $s_{\alpha,np}:\lambda \mapsto \lambda - (\langle \lambda+\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle - np)\alpha$. Its fundamental domain is the lowest alcove
$$A_0=\{\lambda : 0<\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle<p \ \ \forall \alpha\in\Phi^+\},$$
nonempty exactly when $p \ge h$, $h = \langle\rho,\alpha_0^\vee\rangle+1$ for the highest root $\alpha_0$.

**Weyl characters.** $\chi(\lambda) = \sum_i (-1)^i \operatorname{ch} H^i(G/B, \mathcal{L}(\lambda))$; for $\lambda$ dominant $\chi(\lambda)$ is the ordinary Weyl character, given by Weyl's formula, and equals $\operatorname{ch}\Delta(\lambda)$ for the Weyl module. The decomposition problem is to compute the integers $[\Delta(\lambda):L(\mu)]$ in
$$\chi(\lambda)=\sum_{\mu \le \lambda} [\Delta(\lambda):L(\mu)] \operatorname{ch} L(\mu).$$

**Kazhdan–Lusztig basis.** In the Hecke algebra $\mathcal{H}$ of $W_p$ over $\mathbb{Z}[v^{\pm1}]$, the KL basis satisfies $\underline{H}_w = \sum_y h_{y,w}(v) H_y$ with $h_{y,w}=v^{\ell(w)-\ell(y)}P_{y,w}(v^{-2})$. Geometrically $P_{y,w}(q)=\sum_i \dim \mathcal{H}^{2i}_{y}(IC_w)\,q^i$ for intersection cohomology of Schubert varieties in the affine flag variety, **with $\mathbb{Q}$-coefficients**.

**The $p$-canonical basis.** Replacing $\mathbb{Q}$ by $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$ (equivalently, using indecomposable parity/Soergel bimodules mod $p$) gives $^p\underline{H}_w=\sum_y {}^p h_{y,w}(v)H_y$ and $p$-KL polynomials $^pP_{y,w}$. Always $^pP_{y,w}=P_{y,w}$ for $p \gg 0$; equality for all $y,w$ in the relevant range is precisely what Lusztig's conjecture asserts. The corrected statement (Riche–Williamson) computes $\operatorname{ch} L(\lambda)$, and characters of indecomposable tilting modules $T(\lambda)$, from $^p h_{y,w}$ in the antispherical module $\mathcal{N}=\operatorname{sgn}\otimes_{\mathcal{H}_W}\mathcal{H}$.

**Quantum analogue.** For $U_\zeta(\mathfrak{g})$, $\zeta$ a primitive $\ell$-th root of unity in $\mathbb{C}$, $\ell>h$, the same formula with $W_\ell$ is *Lusztig's quantum conjecture* — a theorem.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1980.** Lusztig states the conjecture in *Some problems in the representation theory of finite Chevalley groups* (Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. 37), after the Kazhdan–Lusztig conjecture for category $\mathcal{O}$ (1979). Original hypothesis $p\ge h$ was later usually strengthened to $p \ge 2h-3$ in the literature.
- **1993–95.** Kazhdan–Lusztig (*Tensor structures arising from affine Lie algebras I–IV*, JAMS 1993–94) and Kashiwara–Tanisaki (Duke, 1995) prove the negative-level affine KL conjecture, giving the **quantum** case for all $\ell>h$.
- **1994.** Andersen–Jantzen–Soergel (Astérisque 220) prove *independence of $p$*: characters in the Jantzen region are given by the same combinatorics for all $p$ larger than an unspecified bound $N(\Phi)$. Combined with the quantum theorem, Lusztig's formula holds for $p \gg 0$.
- **2008–12.** Fiebig's moment-graph program gives a second proof and the first **explicit** bound (J. reine angew. Math. 673, 2012) — astronomically larger than $h$ (super-exponential in the rank).
- **2013/2017.** Williamson, *Schubert calculus and torsion explosion* (JAMS 30, 2017; appendix with Kontorovich and McNamara), disproves the conjecture: torsion in $IC$ stalks of $SL_n$ Schubert varieties grows so fast that exceptional primes for $SL_n$ grow **at least exponentially in $n$**, so no linear or polynomial bound in $h$ can hold. The same machinery disproves James's conjecture for symmetric groups.
- **2018–2020.** Riche–Williamson (Astérisque 397, 2018) formulate and, for $GL_n$, prove the $p$-canonical replacement; Achar–Makisumi–Riche–Williamson (JAMS 31, 2018) and Bezrukavnikov–Riche extend it to general reductive $G$ with $p>h$. This is the current SOTA: a correct character formula whose combinatorics is, however, not computable in closed form.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **Quantum case:** true for all $\ell > h$, every type (Kazhdan–Lusztig; Kashiwara–Tanisaki; Andersen–Jantzen–Soergel).
- **Large $p$:** true for each fixed $\Phi$ and all $p \ge N(\Phi)$ (AJS 1994; Fiebig 2012 with explicit $N$).
- **Rank $\le 3$:** verified for all $p\ge h$ — $A_1$ (trivially, all $p$), $A_2$, $B_2$, $G_2$, $A_3$, $B_3$, $C_3$ — via Jantzen's alcove computations and later verifications (see Jantzen, *Representations of Algebraic Groups*, 2nd ed., II.8, and its notes). For $G_2$ the formula holds for $p\ge 5$; $p=2,3$ are exceptional.
- **$SL_n$ small rank:** decomposition numbers matching the formula are known for $n \le 5$ and moderate $p$ by computer calculation of $^pP_{y,w}$.
- **Counterexamples:** type $A_{n-1}$ for suitable large $n$ and primes $p$ far above $h=n$; the exceptional primes produced grow like $\varphi^{n}$ (Fibonacci growth of entries in products of elementary matrices), all with $p \ge h$.
- **Structural partial truths:** the formula is correct "in the first $p^2$-region up to $p$-power twists" only in the sense of Steinberg's tensor product theorem; multiplicity-one and Jantzen-filtration consequences hold more broadly (Fiebig, Soergel).

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Torsion in intersection cohomology.** The bridge from geometry to representation theory (Soergel 2000; Fiebig; Riche–Williamson) forces $IC$ sheaves with $\overline{\mathbb{F}}_p$-coefficients. Their stalks carry torsion whose size is governed by entries of products of matrices in $SL_2(\mathbb{Z})$ — these grow exponentially, so "bad" primes cannot be bounded polynomially in $h$. Characteristic-$0$ Hodge-theoretic input (hard Lefschetz, purity, decomposition theorem), which drives the classical KL proof, simply fails mod $p$.
- **No positivity or recursion mod $p$.** $^pP_{y,w}$ has no known recursion, no interpretation as a Poincaré polynomial of a canonical filtration, and no positivity-based combinatorial model. Elias–Williamson's Hodge theory of Soergel bimodules (Annals 180, 2014) proves positivity for $P_{y,w}$ but has no mod-$p$ analogue.
- **Non-effectivity of localization.** AJS-style deformation arguments show independence of $p$ but produce bounds by comparing finitely many combinatorial datasets, giving no control over which primes are exceptional.
- **Infinite-dimensional flag variety.** The affine flag variety is not of finite type; standard perverse-sheaf finiteness and Weil-conjecture arguments must be replaced by parity-sheaf technology, which is coefficient-sensitive by design.

## 6. The Gap

Proved: the formula for $p \ge N(\Phi)$, $N$ explicit but enormous; and a *replacement* formula, valid for all $p > h$, expressed in $^p h_{y,w}$. Open: (i) the true threshold $N_{\min}(\Phi)$ — the smallest $p_0$ such that the classical formula holds for all $p \ge p_0$ — is unknown for every type of rank $\ge 4$, with the gap between Williamson's exponential lower bound and Fiebig's super-exponential upper bound spanning many orders of magnitude; (ii) no algorithm computes $^pP_{y,w}$ faster than brute-force diagrammatic calculation, so the "solved" formula does not yield numbers. The barrier to cross is a mod-$p$ substitute for hard Lefschetz that would bound torsion in $IC$ stalks in terms of $\ell(w)$ and $h$.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Diagrammatic Hecke categories.** Elias–Williamson calculus, Smith–Treumann localization (Riche–Williamson; Ciappara) and Frobenius-kernel-equivariant methods are the main computational engine; groups at Sydney (Williamson), Clermont-Ferrand (Riche), MIT (Bezrukavnikov), Bonn/MPIM.
- **Explicit $^pP$ tables.** Ongoing machine computation of $p$-canonical bases for affine $A_2$–$A_4$ and $\widetilde{B}_2$; patterns suggest sparse but unbounded deviations from $P_{y,w}$. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Bounds on exceptional primes.** Attempts to reduce Fiebig's bound to something singly exponential in the rank using torsion-explosion duality. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Tilting-first approach.** Donkin's tilting conjecture and $\operatorname{ch} T(\lambda)$ as the primary unknown, with $\operatorname{ch}L(\lambda)$ derived — now the standard reformulation.
- **Categorical Langlands.** Bezrukavnikov–Riche's modular affine Hecke category and regular unipotent centralizer program, aiming to make the antispherical module computable via local geometric Langlands. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Future Work

- Find a mod-$p$ hard Lefschetz or a torsion-bounding theorem for affine Schubert varieties; this is the consensus key step (Williamson, Takagi lectures).
- Determine $N_{\min}$ for type $A_4$ / $B_4$ exactly by exhaustive $p$-KL computation — the smallest tractable test case beyond verified rank $\le 3$.
- Develop a combinatorial model (crystal- or LS-path-like) for $^p h_{y,w}$ in the antispherical module.
- Transfer results to symmetric groups: decomposition numbers for $S_n$ in characteristic $p$ via Schur–Weyl, replacing the disproved James conjecture.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take $G=SL_2$, so $X(T)^+=\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$, $h=2$, $\rho=1$, and $\chi(n)$ has dimension $n+1$. The affine Weyl group $W_p$ is infinite dihedral, generated by $s_{k}:\ n \mapsto 2(kp-1)-n$ for $k\in\mathbb{Z}$. All Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials of an infinite dihedral group are $P_{y,w}=1$, and the $p$-canonical basis coincides with the KL basis in rank 1, so the conjecture predicts a pure alternating sum with coefficients $\pm 1$.

Let $p=3$ and $\lambda=4$. Then $\lambda+\rho=5$, and $3 < 5 < 6$, so $\lambda$ lies in the second alcove; the wall it reflects across is $n+\rho=3$, i.e. $s_1\cdot 4 = 2(3-1)-4 = 0$. The chain has length two ($x=s_1$, $w=e$), so Lusztig's formula reads
$$\operatorname{ch} L(4)=\chi(4)-P_{\,\cdot,\cdot}(1)\,\chi(0)=\chi(4)-\chi(0),$$
giving $\dim L(4) = 5 - 1 = 4$.

Check independently: $4 = 1 + 3\cdot 1$, so Steinberg's tensor product theorem gives $L(4)\cong L(1)\otimes L(1)^{[3]}$, of dimension $2\cdot 2=4$, with character $(x+x^{-1})(x^{3}+x^{-3}) = x^{4}+x^{2}+x^{-2}+x^{-4}$. Subtracting: $\chi(4)-\chi(0) = (x^4+x^2+1+x^{-2}+x^{-4}) - 1$, the same. Equivalently $[\Delta(4):L(4)]=[\Delta(4):L(0)]=1$.

Rank 1 is thus a case where the conjecture holds for every $p$, because there is no room for torsion in the (one-dimensional) $IC$ stalks. Williamson's counterexamples require products of many reflections, so that $IC$ stalk torsion — governed by entries of long products of elementary $2\times2$ matrices — can exceed $p$; that phenomenon is invisible at rank 1 and rank $\le 3$, which is exactly why small-rank verification gave false confidence for thirty years.
