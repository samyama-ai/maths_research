# Kac-Moody Algebra Classification

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/kac-moody-algebra-classification` · **Status:** solved-recently

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Classify the Kac–Moody algebras $\mathfrak{g}(A)$ attached to generalized Cartan matrices (GCMs) $A$, together with their root systems, root multiplicities and characters.

The problem splits into layers of decreasing resolution:

1. **Diagram level.** Classify GCMs up to permutation of indices. *Solved in outline*: Vinberg's trichotomy (finite / affine / indefinite) plus the Cartan–Killing list (finite) and Kac's list of affine diagrams. *Solved recently in a hard subcase*: all **hyperbolic** Dynkin diagrams are now enumerated (Carbone et al. 2010).
2. **Root-system level.** For a given $A$, determine the set $\Delta \subset Q$ of roots and the multiplicity function $\mathrm{mult}: \Delta \to \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$ in closed form. *Solved for finite and affine type; open for every indefinite $A$*, including rank 2.
3. **Isomorphism/structure level.** Decide when $\mathfrak{g}(A) \cong \mathfrak{g}(A')$, which $\mathfrak{g}(A)$ carry an automorphic denominator function, and classify the associated Kac–Moody groups and their lattices.

A complete resolution of layer 2 would mean: an effective formula (or generating function) for $\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)$ for all $\alpha \in \Delta(A)$ and all $A$, or a proof that no such uniform description exists. A disproof of a specific sub-conjecture (e.g. Frenkel's bound for $E_{10}$) requires exhibiting one root violating it.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

A **generalized Cartan matrix** is $A = (a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^n$ with
$$a_{ii}=2,\qquad a_{ij}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\le 0}\ (i\neq j),\qquad a_{ij}=0 \iff a_{ji}=0 .$$
A *realization* is a triple $(\mathfrak{h},\Pi,\Pi^\vee)$ with $\dim\mathfrak{h}=2n-\mathrm{rank}(A)$, $\Pi=\{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n\}\subset\mathfrak{h}^*$, $\Pi^\vee=\{\alpha_1^\vee,\dots,\alpha_n^\vee\}\subset\mathfrak{h}$, $\langle\alpha_j,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=a_{ij}$. Then $\mathfrak{g}(A)$ is generated by $\mathfrak{h}$ and $e_i,f_i$ with Chevalley–Serre relations
$$[e_i,f_j]=\delta_{ij}\alpha_i^\vee,\quad [h,e_i]=\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle e_i,\quad [h,f_i]=-\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle f_i,$$
$$(\mathrm{ad}\,e_i)^{1-a_{ij}}e_j=0,\qquad (\mathrm{ad}\,f_i)^{1-a_{ij}}f_j=0 \quad (i\neq j).$$
By the **Gabber–Kac theorem** (1981) these relations define the derived algebra of the maximal quotient when $A$ is symmetrizable.

Root space decomposition: $\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta}\mathfrak{g}_\alpha$, $\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)=\dim\mathfrak{g}_\alpha$. The Weyl group $W\subset GL(\mathfrak{h}^*)$ is generated by $r_i(\lambda)=\lambda-\langle\lambda,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle\alpha_i$; it is a Coxeter group. Roots split into **real** ($\alpha\in W\Pi$, $\mathrm{mult}=1$) and **imaginary** ($\mathrm{mult}$ can grow).

**Vinberg trichotomy.** For $A$ indecomposable exactly one holds, where $u>0$ means all coordinates positive:
- *finite*: $\exists u>0$ with $Au>0$; equivalently $\det A>0$ and all proper principal minors positive;
- *affine*: $\exists u>0$ with $Au=0$; corank 1;
- *indefinite*: $\exists u>0$ with $Au<0$.

$A$ is **hyperbolic** if it is indefinite and every proper connected subdiagram is of finite or affine type. If $A$ is symmetrizable, $DA$ symmetric with $D$ positive diagonal, then $\mathfrak{g}$ carries an invariant form $(\cdot|\cdot)$ with $(\alpha_i|\alpha_j)=d_i a_{ij}$.

**Weyl–Kac character formula** for an integrable highest-weight module $L(\Lambda)$:
$$\mathrm{ch}\,L(\Lambda)=\frac{\sum_{w\in W}(-1)^{\ell(w)}e^{w(\Lambda+\rho)-\rho}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_+}(1-e^{-\alpha})^{\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)}},$$
with denominator identity $\prod_{\alpha\in\Delta_+}(1-e^{-\alpha})^{\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)}=\sum_{w\in W}(-1)^{\ell(w)}e^{w\rho-\rho}$.

**Peterson's recursion** converts the denominator identity into an algorithm. With $c_\beta:=\sum_{k\ge 1}\tfrac1k\,\mathrm{mult}(\beta/k)$ (terms with $\beta/k\notin Q_+$ omitted),
$$(\beta\,|\,\beta-2\rho)\,c_\beta=\sum_{\substack{\beta'+\beta''=\beta\\ \beta',\beta''\in Q_+\setminus\{0\}}}(\beta'\,|\,\beta'')\,c_{\beta'}c_{\beta''}.$$
This determines all multiplicities but only height-by-height, with cost exponential in height.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1967–68.** Kac (Izv. Akad. Nauk, 1968) and Moody (J. Algebra, 1968) independently define the algebras while classifying graded Lie algebras of finite growth. Vinberg's trichotomy for GCMs (1971) fixes the coarse classification.
- **1974.** Kac's character formula and, with Peterson, the theory of affine root multiplicities: for affine $A$ of rank $n$, $\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)$ for imaginary $\alpha$ equals the number of $i$ with $\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}\delta$ weighted by the type, i.e. it is bounded (values $\le n$) and periodic.
- **1983.** Feingold–Frenkel study $\mathcal{F} = \mathfrak{g}(A)$ for the rank-3 hyperbolic $A=\begin{pmatrix}2&-2&0\\-2&2&-1\\0&-1&2\end{pmatrix}$ and relate multiplicities to Fourier coefficients of genus-2 Siegel modular forms.
- **1988.** Borcherds introduces generalized Kac–Moody (Borcherds–Kac–Moody) algebras, allowing imaginary simple roots; his 1992 proof of the moonshine conjecture uses the Monster Lie algebra.
- **1988.** Kac–Moody–Wakimoto compute $E_{10}$ root multiplicities at low level, testing **Frenkel's conjecture** $\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)\le p_8\!\left(1-\tfrac{(\alpha|\alpha)}{2}\right)$ (partitions into 8 colours).
- **2010.** Carbone–Chung–Cobbs–McRae–Nandi–Naqvi–Narayan complete the classification of hyperbolic Dynkin diagrams, with Weyl-orbit and root-length data.
- **2007–2017.** Finiteness results for reflective hyperbolic lattices (Nikulin; Agol–Belolipetsky–Storm–Whyte) and Ma's finiteness of $2$-reflective lattices of signature $(2,n)$ bound the supply of Lorentzian Kac–Moody algebras with automorphic denominator.
- **2009–2018.** Caprace–Rémy prove simplicity of Kac–Moody groups over sufficiently large finite fields; Marquis's textbook (2018) consolidates the group-theoretic side.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Class | Status |
|---|---|
| Finite type ($\det A>0$) | Fully classified: $A_n,B_n,C_n,D_n,E_{6,7,8},F_4,G_2$. All multiplicities $=1$. |
| Affine type | Fully classified: $X_N^{(r)}$, $r\in\{1,2,3\}$; imaginary root multiplicities are $\le \mathrm{rank}$, explicit and $\delta$-periodic. |
| Hyperbolic type | Diagrams fully enumerated: none in rank $>10$; infinitely many in rank 3; finitely many (238 in total, of which 142 symmetrizable) in ranks $4\le n\le 10$. Rank 10 contains exactly $E_{10},BE_{10},CE_{10},DE_{10}$. |
| Rank-2 indefinite $\begin{pmatrix}2&-a\\-b&2\end{pmatrix}$, $ab\ge5$ | Multiplicities computable to any fixed height; closed form known only for $a=b=2$ (affine) and partially for $ab=4$ boundary cases. |
| $HA_1^{(1)}$ (Feingold–Frenkel rank 3) | Level-1 and level-2 multiplicities in closed form (Feingold–Frenkel 1983; Kang 1994); general level open. |
| $E_{10}$ | Multiplicities known by computer to height in the thousands and by level up to $\ell\le 4$–$6$ via $\mathfrak{sl}_{10}$ decomposition (Kac–Moody–Wakimoto 1988; Fischbacher–Nicolai 2003). Frenkel's bound holds in all computed cases. |
| Lorentzian BKM algebras with reflective automorphic denominator | Finite in number; classified in rank 3 and constrained in all ranks by Gritsenko–Nikulin, Ma, and Wang. |

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Weyl group is not finite and not amenable to a Weyl integration formula.** For indefinite $A$, $W$ is an infinite Coxeter group acting on a hyperbolic or higher-signature lattice; the alternating sum in the denominator identity has no finite truncation adapted to a given root.
- **No modular structure outside signature $(1,n)$/$(2,n)$.** The affine miracle — that $\mathrm{ch}\,L(\Lambda)$ is a modular function — comes from $W \cong W_{\mathrm{fin}}\ltimes T$ and the theta decomposition. Indefinite $W$ has no such semidirect structure, so the analytic machinery (Macdonald identities, theta functions) has nothing to act on.
- **Peterson recursion is exponential.** Computing $\mathrm{mult}(\beta)$ at height $h$ requires all lower heights; the number of $\beta\in Q_+$ of height $h$ in rank 10 grows like $h^9$, and multiplicities themselves grow exponentially in $\sqrt{-(\alpha|\alpha)}$.
- **Non-symmetrizable $A$ has no invariant form.** All of Peterson's recursion, the Casimir, and the character formula's proof use $(\cdot|\cdot)$. For the 96 non-symmetrizable hyperbolic diagrams, and for general indefinite $A$, the standard toolkit is simply unavailable.
- **No known vertex-algebra realization.** Affine algebras and the Monster Lie algebra have free-field/lattice-VOA constructions that make multiplicities into dimensions of graded pieces. For $E_{10}$ no such realization is known; the "no-ghost" theorem gives only the coset embedding $\mathfrak{g} \subset \mathfrak{g}_{II_{1,9}}$ (a BKM), producing upper bounds rather than equalities.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: the diagram classification, and multiplicities in finite/affine type plus finitely many low-level strata of specific hyperbolics. Wanted: a multiplicity function for a general indefinite $A$.

The precise barrier is the **decomposition of the coset $\mathfrak{g}_{II_{1,9}} / E_{10}$**. Borcherds' fake monster algebra $\mathfrak{g}_{II_{1,9}}$ has multiplicities given by $p_{24}$ coefficients and contains $E_{10}$; the difference $\mathrm{mult}_{\mathfrak{g}_{II_{1,9}}}(\alpha)-\mathrm{mult}_{E_{10}}(\alpha)$ is the character of an unidentified $E_{10}$-module. No conjectural description of that module exists. Equivalently: no indefinite Kac–Moody algebra is known to possess a denominator function that is automorphic, and Ma/Wang finiteness suggests almost none do — so a proof cannot proceed by finding one.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **$E_{10}$ / $E_{11}$ and M-theory.** AEI Potsdam (Nicolai, Kleinschmidt) and IHES (Damour, Henneaux) continue the cosmological-billiards programme: $E_{10}$ level decomposition against $\mathfrak{sl}_{10}$ and $D_9$, matched to supergravity fields. Level-$\ge 7$ decompositions remain computationally hard.
- **Automorphic denominators.** Haowu Wang, Brandon Williams and Nils Scheithauer classify reflective modular forms; the emerging picture is that Borcherds products exist only in signature $(2,n)$ with $n$ bounded, closing off the automorphic route in high rank *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Reflective hyperbolic lattices.** Bogachev, Belolipetsky and collaborators push explicit classification of reflective lattices in ranks $\le 6$ via Vinberg's algorithm, directly constraining Lorentzian Kac–Moody algebras.
- **Kac–Moody groups.** Rousseau, Marquis, Capdeboscq and Carbone study completions, twin buildings and finite-field quotients; classification of maximal subgroups of $G(\mathbb{F}_q)$ for indefinite type is active.
- **Computational.** Open-source Peterson-recursion implementations (Carbone's group) now tabulate multiplicities for all rank-$\le 6$ hyperbolics to height $\sim 100$ *(frontier — verify)*.

## 8. Future Work

- Find a vertex-algebra or geometric model whose graded dimensions reproduce $\mathrm{mult}_{E_{10}}$, e.g. via a conjectural $E_{10}$ "moonshine" module.
- Prove or refute Frenkel's conjecture $\mathrm{mult}(\alpha)\le p_{d-2}(1-(\alpha|\alpha)/2)$ for $E_{10}$; counterexamples are known for some rank-3 hyperbolics, so the mechanism protecting $E_{10}$ is unclear.
- Extend Kang's homological method (Kostant-type Euler characteristics for $\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}_-\oplus\mathfrak{g}_0\oplus\mathfrak{n}_+$) beyond level 2 for hyperbolic algebras with an affine subdiagram.
- Develop invariant-form-free tools for the 96 non-symmetrizable hyperbolic diagrams.
- Determine whether $\mathrm{mult}$ is computable in polynomial time in $\mathrm{ht}(\alpha)$ — no lower bound is known either way.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take the rank-2 indefinite GCM
$$A=\begin{pmatrix}2&-3\\-3&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det A = 4-9=-5<0 .$$
It is symmetric, so $d_1=d_2=1$ and $(\alpha_1|\alpha_1)=(\alpha_2|\alpha_2)=2$, $(\alpha_1|\alpha_2)=-3$. Choose $\rho$ with $(\rho|\alpha_i)=1$. The Weyl group is infinite dihedral, generated by $r_1,r_2$ with $(r_1r_2)^\infty$.

**Multiplicity of $\beta=\alpha_1+\alpha_2$.** $\beta$ is not a proper multiple, so $c_\beta=\mathrm{mult}(\beta)$.
$$(\beta|\beta)=2+2+2(-3)=-2,\qquad (\beta|2\rho)=2(1+1)=4,\qquad (\beta|\beta-2\rho)=-6 .$$
The only ordered splittings are $(\alpha_1,\alpha_2)$ and $(\alpha_2,\alpha_1)$, with $c_{\alpha_i}=1$:
$$\text{RHS}=2(\alpha_1|\alpha_2)=-6 \;\Longrightarrow\; \mathrm{mult}(\alpha_1+\alpha_2)=1 .$$
Since $(\beta|\beta)=-2\neq 2$, this is an **imaginary** root of multiplicity 1.

**Multiplicity of $\gamma=2\alpha_1+\alpha_2$.**
$$(\gamma|\gamma)=4(2)+4(-3)+2=-2,\qquad (\gamma|2\rho)=2(2+1)=6,\qquad (\gamma|\gamma-2\rho)=-8 .$$
Splittings: $\{\alpha_1,\ \alpha_1+\alpha_2\}$ and $\{\alpha_2,\ 2\alpha_1\}$, each counted twice. Note $c_{2\alpha_1}=\tfrac12\mathrm{mult}(\alpha_1)+\mathrm{mult}(2\alpha_1)=\tfrac12$ because $2\alpha_1$ is not a root.
$$\text{RHS}=2(\alpha_1|\alpha_1+\alpha_2)\cdot 1\cdot 1+2(\alpha_2|2\alpha_1)\cdot 1\cdot\tfrac12=2(-1)+2(-6)\tfrac12=-2-6=-8 .$$
Hence $c_\gamma=1$ and $\mathrm{mult}(2\alpha_1+\alpha_2)=1$.

Continuing, multiplicities begin to grow: e.g. $\mathrm{mult}(3\alpha_1+2\alpha_2)>1$, and by height $\sim 20$ they are in the thousands. Every value here is *computed*, not *predicted*: for this $2\times 2$ matrix there is still no known closed formula or generating function for $\mathrm{mult}(m\alpha_1+n\alpha_2)$. That failure at $n=2$ is the whole classification problem in miniature.
