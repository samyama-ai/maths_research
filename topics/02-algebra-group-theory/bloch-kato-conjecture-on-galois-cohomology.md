# Bloch-Kato Conjecture on Galois Cohomology (Norm Residue Isomorphism Theorem)

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/bloch-kato-conjecture-on-galois-cohomology` · **Status:** solved-recently

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $k$ be a field, $\ell$ a prime with $\operatorname{char} k \neq \ell$, and $\mu_\ell$ the group of $\ell$-th roots of unity in a separable closure $k^{\mathrm{sep}}$. The **norm residue homomorphism**

$$
\chi_{n,\ell} \colon K^M_n(k)/\ell \;\longrightarrow\; H^n_{\text{ét}}\big(k, \mu_\ell^{\otimes n}\big) = H^n\big(\mathrm{Gal}(k^{\mathrm{sep}}/k), \mu_\ell^{\otimes n}\big)
$$

is defined on symbols by $\{a_1,\dots,a_n\} \mapsto (a_1)\cup\cdots\cup(a_n)$, where $(a) \in H^1(k,\mu_\ell)$ is the Kummer class of $a \in k^\times$.

**Conjecture (Bloch–Kato, 1986).** $\chi_{n,\ell}$ is an isomorphism for all $n \geq 0$, all primes $\ell \neq \operatorname{char} k$, and all fields $k$.

A complete proof must handle all $n$ simultaneously (the statement is not a finite check for any fixed $k$ of infinite cohomological dimension) and all $\ell$; a disproof requires exhibiting a field, prime and degree where $\chi_{n,\ell}$ has nonzero kernel or cokernel.

**Status.** The conjecture is a **theorem**, now called the **norm residue isomorphism theorem**: Voevodsky proved $\ell = 2$ (the Milnor conjecture) in 2003, and the general case in *Annals of Mathematics* 174 (2011), building on Rost's theory of norm varieties and a patching argument completed by Weibel (2009). The full verified proof is assembled in the Haesemeyer–Weibel monograph (2019). This page is retained because (i) the proof is among the deepest in modern algebra and its architecture drives ongoing work, and (ii) several close relatives and generalizations remain genuinely open (Section 6).

**Warning on naming.** This is *not* the Bloch–Kato conjecture on Tamagawa numbers of motives (special values of $L$-functions), which is still open in general; see Bloch–Kato (1990).

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Milnor K-theory.** For a field $k$,

$$
K^M_*(k) = T^*(k^\times)\big/ \big\langle a \otimes (1-a) : a \neq 0,1 \big\rangle,
$$

the tensor algebra on the abelian group $k^\times$ modulo the Steinberg relation. Its degree-$n$ part $K^M_n(k)$ is generated by symbols $\{a_1,\dots,a_n\}$. One has $K^M_0(k)=\mathbb{Z}$, $K^M_1(k)=k^\times$, and $K^M_2(k)=K_2(k)$ (Matsumoto).

**Galois cohomology.** With $G_k = \mathrm{Gal}(k^{\mathrm{sep}}/k)$, Kummer theory gives the exact sequence $1 \to \mu_\ell \to (k^{\mathrm{sep}})^\times \xrightarrow{\ \ell\ } (k^{\mathrm{sep}})^\times \to 1$ and, by Hilbert's Theorem 90, the isomorphism

$$
k^\times/(k^\times)^\ell \;\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\; H^1(k,\mu_\ell).
$$

The Steinberg relation maps to $0$ under cup product, so $\chi_{n,\ell}$ is well defined.

**Motivic reformulation.** Let $\mathbb{Z}(n)$ denote Voevodsky's motivic complexes on the Nisnevich site of smooth $k$-schemes, with motivic cohomology $H^{p,q}(X,\mathbb{Z}) = H^p_{\mathcal{M}}(X,\mathbb{Z}(q))$. Two identifications are basic:

$$
H^{n}_{\mathcal{M}}\big(\operatorname{Spec} k, \mathbb{Z}(n)\big) \cong K^M_n(k), \qquad
H^{n}_{\mathcal{M}}\big(\operatorname{Spec} k, \mathbb{Z}/\ell(n)\big) \cong K^M_n(k)/\ell .
$$

Bloch–Kato in degree $n$ is then equivalent (Suslin–Voevodsky, Geisser–Levine) to the **Beilinson–Lichtenbaum conjecture** $\mathrm{BL}(n)$: the change-of-topology map

$$
H^{p}_{\mathcal{M}}\big(X,\mathbb{Z}/\ell(q)\big) \;\longrightarrow\; H^{p}_{\text{ét}}\big(X,\mu_\ell^{\otimes q}\big)
$$

is an isomorphism for $p \le q$ and injective for $p = q+1$, for all smooth $X$ over $k$. Equivalently $\tau^{\le q} R\pi_* \mu_\ell^{\otimes q} \simeq \mathbb{Z}/\ell(q)$ for $\pi$ the map from the étale to the Nisnevich site.

**Rost's norm varieties.** For a nonzero symbol $\underline{a} = \{a_1,\dots,a_n\} \in K^M_n(k)/\ell$, a **$\nu_{n-1}$-variety** (norm variety) is a smooth proper $k$-variety $X$ of dimension $\ell^{n-1}-1$ splitting $\underline{a}$ (i.e. $\underline{a}_{k(X)} = 0$) with $s_{\ell^{n-1}-1}(X) \not\equiv 0 \bmod \ell^2$, where $s_d$ is the degree of the $d$-th Milnor characteristic number. Their existence (Rost; Suslin–Joukhovitski for the construction, via Chow groups of Pfister-like quadrics and Rost motives) is the geometric engine of the proof.

**Consequences used as inputs/outputs.** The Milnor conjecture on quadratic forms — $I^n(k)/I^{n+1}(k) \cong H^n(k,\mathbb{Z}/2)$ for the powers of the fundamental ideal of the Witt ring — follows from the $\ell=2$ case; the Quillen–Lichtenbaum conjecture comparing Quillen $K$-theory with étale $K$-theory in high degrees follows from the general case.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1970.** Milnor introduces $K^M_*(k)$ in *Algebraic K-theory and quadratic forms* and asks whether $K^M_n(k)/2 \to H^n(k,\mathbb{Z}/2)$ and $K^M_n(k)/2 \to I^n/I^{n+1}$ are isomorphisms — the Milnor conjecture.
- **1976.** Tate proves the case $n=2$ for global fields, and gives $H^2$ interpretations via the Brauer group.
- **1981–82.** Merkurjev proves $n=2$, $\ell=2$; Merkurjev–Suslin prove $n=2$ for all $\ell$ — the Merkurjev–Suslin theorem, identifying $K_2(k)/\ell \cong H^2(k,\mu_\ell^{\otimes 2}) = {}_\ell\mathrm{Br}(k)(1)$.
- **1986.** Bloch and Kato, in *p-adic étale cohomology* (Publ. IHÉS 63), formulate the general conjecture for all $n$ and $\ell$.
- **1986–1990.** Rost proves Hilbert 90 for $K_3$ in the quadratic case; Merkurjev–Suslin establish $n=3$, $\ell=2$ and then general $\ell$ for $n=3$ (with $\mu_\ell \subset k$).
- **1996–2003.** Voevodsky's motivic homotopy theory, the Steenrod algebra in motivic cohomology, and the motivic Adams-type argument yield *Motivic cohomology with $\mathbb{Z}/2$-coefficients* (Publ. IHÉS 98, 2003): the Milnor conjecture.
- **2003–2011.** For odd $\ell$ the proof requires Rost's norm varieties, motivic Eilenberg–MacLane spaces (Voevodsky, Publ. IHÉS 112, 2010) and a Bockstein/degree argument. Weibel (*J. Topology* 2, 2009) supplies the missing patching step, and Voevodsky's *On motivic cohomology with $\mathbb{Z}/\ell$-coefficients* (Ann. of Math. 174, 2011) completes the theorem.
- **2019.** Haesemeyer and Weibel publish a fully checked, self-contained proof: *The Norm Residue Theorem in Motivic Cohomology*, Annals of Mathematics Studies 200.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

The historical difficulty was structural, and the reasons standard techniques failed remain instructive:

- **No induction on symbols alone.** $K^M_n(k)/\ell$ is generated by symbols but $H^n(k,\mu_\ell^{\otimes n})$ has no comparable presentation; surjectivity in degree $n$ cannot be deduced from degree $n-1$ by elementary means. Merkurjev–Suslin's degree-2 argument used $K$-cohomology of Severi–Brauer varieties, and that method does not extend past $n=3$.
- **Failure of classical topology.** The naive analogue over $\mathbb{C}$ is a statement about singular cohomology of $\operatorname{Spec}$ of a field, which is a point: there is no ambient space to run a spectral sequence over. The proof therefore had to *build* the correct homotopy category ($\mathbb{A}^1$-homotopy theory, $\mathbf{DM}(k)$) first.
- **Splitting varieties are non-generic.** For each symbol one needs a specific smooth proper variety of dimension exactly $\ell^{n-1}-1$ with a prescribed characteristic number mod $\ell^2$. For $\ell = 2$ these are Pfister quadrics — explicit. For odd $\ell$ no explicit family exists; Rost's construction is inductive and highly non-obvious, and its write-up (Suslin–Joukhovitski) is itself a substantial paper.
- **Motivic Steenrod operations.** Voevodsky's argument needs $\mathrm{Sq}^{2i}$-analogues acting on motivic cohomology with the correct bidegrees; constructing them required identifying motivic Eilenberg–MacLane spaces and proving that motivic cohomology operations are generated by the expected ones — false naively over fields of positive characteristic without extra care, and requiring resolution of singularities inputs at intermediate stages.
- **Degree formulas.** Rost's degree formula, controlling $\deg$ of zero-cycles on norm varieties modulo $\ell$, was the arithmetic bridge; earlier methods (Galois descent, transfer arguments) give only $\ell$-divisibility statements too weak to conclude.

## 6. The Gap

For the statement in Section 1 there is no gap: it is proven. The live boundaries are the generalizations:

- **Integral coefficients.** Beilinson–Soulé vanishing, $H^{p}_{\mathcal{M}}(k,\mathbb{Z}(q)) = 0$ for $p \le 0 < q$, remains **open** and is not implied by the norm residue theorem.
- **Schemes and rings, not fields.** The natural extension to smooth schemes over a base, to local rings (the Gersten conjecture in mixed characteristic), and to $p$-adic settings via prismatic/syntomic cohomology, is only partially established.
- **Tamagawa-number Bloch–Kato.** The conjecture on special values $L(M,0)$ and Tamagawa measures (Bloch–Kato 1990; the equivariant version of Burns–Flach) is open outside abelian cases over $\mathbb{Q}$ and specific motives.
- **Effectivity.** The proof gives no bound on the number of symbols needed to represent a class in $H^n(k,\mu_\ell^{\otimes n})$, nor an algorithm to produce one. This is a real gap: the theorem is an isomorphism statement with no constructive inverse.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Simplification programs.** Efforts to replace the norm-variety machinery with lighter arguments continue in the motivic homotopy communities at Oslo, Regensburg, Bonn (MPIM), and UCLA/USC. No symbol-free proof of the odd-$\ell$ case exists. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Prismatic and syntomic analogues.** Bhatt–Morrow–Scholze-style integral $p$-adic Hodge theory has produced $p$-adic comparisons in the spirit of the original Bloch–Kato 1986 paper; extending norm-residue statements to $\ell = p$ over general bases is active. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Effective/constructive symbol length.** Bounds on symbol length for degree-$n$ classes over fields of bounded cohomological dimension (work in the Merkurjev–Karpenko school on essential dimension and canonical dimension) remain quantitative and incomplete.
- **Equivariant Tamagawa number conjecture.** Iwasawa-theoretic approaches (Burns, Flach, Kakde, Venjakob) continue to convert main-conjecture inputs into ETNC cases.

## 8. Future Work

- Produce a proof of the odd-$\ell$ case that avoids Rost varieties, or at least a modular write-up isolating exactly which geometric input is unavoidable.
- Attack Beilinson–Soulé vanishing, the remaining integral obstruction to a full description of $\mathbb{Z}(q)$ as a complex concentrated in the right range.
- Develop constructive symbol decompositions: given an explicit cocycle in $H^n(k,\mu_\ell^{\otimes n})$, output symbols. This is essentially untouched for $n \ge 3$.
- Extend the theorem from fields to regular schemes and to $\ell = \operatorname{char}$ with prismatic coefficients.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Case $k = \mathbb{Q}_p$, $n = 2$, $\ell$ prime with $\ell \neq p$, assume $\mu_\ell \subset \mathbb{Q}_p$ (i.e. $\ell \mid p-1$).**

*Right-hand side.* Local class field theory gives $\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{Q}_p) \cong \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ via the invariant map, so
$$
H^2(\mathbb{Q}_p, \mu_\ell) \cong {}_\ell\mathrm{Br}(\mathbb{Q}_p) \cong \tfrac{1}{\ell}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/\ell,
$$
and twisting by $\mu_\ell \cong \mathbb{Z}/\ell$ (trivial Galois action, by assumption) gives $H^2(\mathbb{Q}_p,\mu_\ell^{\otimes 2}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\ell$.

*Left-hand side.* By Moore's theorem the tame symbol induces $K_2(\mathbb{Q}_p)/\ell \cong \mu_\ell(\mathbb{Q}_p) \cong \mathbb{Z}/\ell$, the isomorphism being the $\ell$-th Hilbert symbol $(\,\cdot\,,\cdot\,)_\ell$.

*The map.* $\chi_{2,\ell}(\{a,b\}) = (a)\cup(b)$ corresponds under $\mathrm{inv}$ to the class of the cyclic algebra $(a,b)_\ell$, whose invariant is $\frac{v}{\ell}$ where $\zeta^{v} = (a,b)_\ell$. So $\chi_{2,\ell}$ is exactly the Hilbert symbol, a known isomorphism — Bloch–Kato holds here by explicit computation.

*Numerical instance.* Take $p = 5$, $\ell = 2$, $\zeta_2 = -1 \in \mathbb{Q}_5$. Then $\{5, u\}$ with $u$ a nonsquare unit (e.g. $u = 2$, since $2$ is not a square mod $5$) has tame symbol $\overline{u}^{(5-1)/2} = 2^2 = 4 \equiv -1 \bmod 5$, so $(5,2)_2 = -1$: the quaternion algebra $\left(\frac{5,2}{\mathbb{Q}_5}\right)$ is the unique division algebra of index $2$ over $\mathbb{Q}_5$, with invariant $\tfrac{1}{2}$. Hence $\chi_{2,2}(\{5,2\}) \neq 0$ and generates $H^2(\mathbb{Q}_5,\mu_2^{\otimes 2}) = \mathbb{Z}/2$.

*Where the hard case starts.* Push to $n = 3$, $\ell = 3$ over a field like $k = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_3)(t_1,t_2,t_3)$. The class $\{t_1,t_2,t_3\}$ is nonzero, and to prove injectivity one must exhibit a norm variety of dimension $\ell^{n-1}-1 = 8$ splitting it with $s_8(X) \not\equiv 0 \bmod 9$. For $\ell=2$ the analogue is the $3$-fold Pfister quadric $\langle\!\langle t_1,t_2,t_3\rangle\!\rangle = 0$ of dimension $2^2-1 = 3$ — completely explicit. For $\ell = 3$ no such closed formula is known; Rost's inductive construction is required. That contrast is precisely the difficulty separating the 2003 and 2011 theorems.
