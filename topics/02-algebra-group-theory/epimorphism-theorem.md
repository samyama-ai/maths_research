# Epimorphism Theorem

> **Topic:** Algebra & Group Theory · **ID:** `02-algebra-group-theory/epimorphism-theorem` · **Status:** solved-recently

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $k$ be a field and $k^{[n]}$ denote a polynomial ring in $n$ variables over $k$.

**Epimorphism Theorem (Abhyankar–Moh–Suzuki, $n=2$).** Let $\operatorname{char} k = 0$ and let $f \in k[X,Y]$ satisfy
$$k[X,Y]/(f) \;\cong\; k^{[1]}.$$
Then $f$ is a *variable* (coordinate): there exists $g \in k[X,Y]$ with $k[f,g] = k[X,Y]$.

Equivalently, in geometric form: every closed embedding $\mathbb{A}^1_k \hookrightarrow \mathbb{A}^2_k$ is *rectifiable* — there is an automorphism $\Phi \in \operatorname{Aut}_k(\mathbb{A}^2)$ carrying the embedded line onto the coordinate axis $\{Y=0\}$.

This statement is a **theorem** in characteristic zero (Abhyankar–Moh 1975; Suzuki 1974). It is **false** in characteristic $p>0$. The catalog entry remains live because the natural generalisation is open:

**Epimorphism Problem / Abhyankar–Sathaye Conjecture ($n\ge 3$).** Let $\operatorname{char} k = 0$ and $f \in k[X_1,\dots,X_n]$ with
$$k[X_1,\dots,X_n]/(f) \;\cong\; k^{[n-1]}.$$
Is $f$ a variable of $k[X_1,\dots,X_n]$?

A complete solution requires either a proof for all $n$ and all fields of characteristic $0$, or an explicit $f$ (necessarily $n\ge 3$) whose quotient is a polynomial ring but which is not part of a coordinate system.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Variables.** $f \in R = k^{[n]}$ is a variable if $R = k[f, g_2,\dots,g_n]$ for some $g_i$; a *stable variable* if $f$ becomes a variable in $k^{[n+m]}$ for some $m \ge 1$. Being a variable implies $R/(f) \cong k^{[n-1]}$; the epimorphism problem asks for the converse.

**Automorphisms of the plane (Jung–van der Kulk).** For any field $k$,
$$\operatorname{Aut}_k(\mathbb{A}^2) \;=\; \mathrm{Af}_2 \ast_{\,\mathrm{Af}_2 \cap \mathrm{BA}_2} \mathrm{BA}_2,$$
the amalgamated free product of the affine group with the triangular (de Jonquières) group $\mathrm{BA}_2 = \{(X,Y)\mapsto(aX+q(Y),\,bY+c)\}$. Every plane automorphism is a finite composite of affine and triangular maps — the tool that converts a degree reduction into an actual rectification.

**Abhyankar–Moh degree criterion.** Let $\varphi:\mathbb{A}^1\to\mathbb{A}^2$, $t\mapsto (x(t),y(t))$, be a closed embedding with $\operatorname{char} k = 0$ and $\deg x = m$, $\deg y = n$, both $\ge 1$. Then
$$m \mid n \quad\text{or}\quad n \mid m .$$
Iterated use of this criterion (subtract $c\,y^{n/m}$ or $c\,x^{m/n}$) strictly lowers $\max(m,n)$, terminating at $(1,0)$ up to affine change — hence rectification.

**Semigroup / one-place-at-infinity machinery.** The image curve $C = \varphi(\mathbb{A}^1)$ has one place at infinity. Abhyankar's approximate roots produce a sequence $d_0 = m,\ d_i = \gcd(d_{i-1}, \text{characteristic exponent})$ and the value semigroup $\Gamma(C)\subseteq \mathbb{Z}$ at that place. The Abhyankar–Moh inequality is a numerical statement about $\Gamma(C)$; rationality of $C$ and $\mathbb{A}^1$-ness force $\Gamma(C)$ to be generated by a single element, i.e. $m\mid n$ or $n\mid m$.

**Higher-dimensional reformulations.** For $n=3$, if $f$ is a variable then the fibration $f:\mathbb{A}^3 \to \mathbb{A}^1$ is a trivial $\mathbb{A}^2$-bundle. Two structural theorems govern the search:

- *Miyanishi–Sugie / Fujita:* a smooth affine surface $S$ over $\mathbb{C}$ with $\bar\kappa(S) = -\infty$, $\operatorname{Pic}(S)=0$, and trivial units is $\cong \mathbb{A}^2$.
- *Sathaye's DVR criterion:* if $R$ is a DVR with fraction field $K$ and residue field $\kappa$, and $A$ is an $R$-algebra with $A\otimes_R K \cong K^{[2]}$, $A\otimes_R \kappa \cong \kappa^{[2]}$, plus faithful flatness, then $A \cong R^{[2]}$.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1942/1953.** Jung, and van der Kulk in arbitrary characteristic, determine $\operatorname{Aut}_k(\mathbb{A}^2)$.
- **1956–1972.** Segre, and later Nagata, give characteristic-$p$ embeddings of $\mathbb{A}^1$ in $\mathbb{A}^2$ that are not rectifiable, showing the theorem is characteristic-sensitive.
- **1974.** Suzuki proves the $\mathbb{C}$-case by topological/analytic means (Euler-characteristic and monodromy arguments for polynomial maps $\mathbb{C}^2\to\mathbb{C}$).
- **1975.** Abhyankar and Moh give the algebraic proof over any field of characteristic $0$, via the semigroup of a meromorphic curve. This is the Epimorphism Theorem proper.
- **1976–1983.** Sathaye proves the linear-plane case; Russell studies simple birational extensions; Sathaye's DVR criterion (1983) becomes the standard patching tool.
- **1979.** Ganong reworks the char-$p$ theory (one place at infinity, the "$p$-th power trick"), isolating exactly where the char-$0$ argument breaks.
- **1990s.** Kraft's Bourbaki exposé *Challenging problems on affine $n$-space* fixes the modern list, with the epimorphism problem alongside cancellation, linearisation and the Jacobian conjecture.
- **2001–2002.** Vénéreau's polynomials in $k^{[4]}$ become the leading candidate counterexamples; Kaliman proves that a polynomial $f\in\mathbb{C}[X,Y,Z]$ all of whose fibres are isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}^2$ is a variable.
- **2014 onward.** Gupta's resolution of Zariski cancellation in positive characteristic, and the Bhatwadekar–Dutta–Das–Gupta program on fibrations over two-dimensional bases, supply the current $n=3$ toolkit.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Case | Status |
|---|---|
| $n=2$, $\operatorname{char} k = 0$ | **Theorem** (Abhyankar–Moh 1975; Suzuki 1974) |
| $n=2$, $\operatorname{char} k = p$, one of $\deg x,\deg y$ dividing the other, or $\gcd=1$ | True (elementary degree descent) |
| $n=2$, $\operatorname{char} k = p$, general | **False**; Segre–Nagata counterexamples |
| $n=3$, $\deg_Z f = 1$ ("linear planes", $f = a(X,Y)Z - b(X,Y)$) | True (Sathaye 1976; Russell 1976) |
| $n=3$, $f = a(X)Z - b(X,Y)$, and more generally $f$ with a coefficient in $k[X]$ | True (Sathaye; Bhatwadekar–Dutta 1994 over a DVR) |
| $n=3$, all fibres of $f$ isomorphic to $\mathbb{C}^2$ | True (Kaliman 2002) |
| $n=3$, $f$ of the form $X^m Y - F(X,Z,T)$-type families | Settled in wide subfamilies (Gupta 2014) |
| $n=3$, $f$ with a $\mathbb{G}_a$- or $\mathbb{G}_m$-action compatible with the fibration | True (Daigle–Freudenburg, several classes) |
| Vénéreau polynomials $v_m = Y + X^m\!\left(XZ + Y(YU+Z^2)\right)\in k^{[4]}$ | Variables for $m\ge 3$ (Vénéreau); $v_1$ **open** |
| Any $n$, $f$ a *stable* variable | Does not settle the problem: stable variables need not be known variables |

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No amalgamated structure above dimension 2.** $\operatorname{Aut}_k(\mathbb{A}^3)$ is not generated by affine and triangular maps: Shestakov–Umirbaev proved the Nagata automorphism is wild. Degree-descent arguments therefore have no target normal form to descend to.
- **Loss of the degree criterion.** The Abhyankar–Moh inequality is a statement about a *single* place at infinity on a curve. For $n\ge 3$ the boundary of a compactified $\mathbb{A}^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{A}^n$ is a divisor with complicated dual graph; there is no numerical semigroup replacement.
- **Characteristic $p$ shows the theorem is not formal.** In char $p$, $t\mapsto t^p$ is a bijective non-isomorphism of curves; Frobenius twists let one build embeddings whose degrees violate the divisibility condition. Any proof for $n\ge3$ must use characteristic-$0$ input (derivations, $\mathbb{G}_a$-actions, resolution) and cannot be purely combinatorial.
- **Fibre-wise information is insufficient.** Even knowing every fibre of $f:\mathbb{A}^3\to\mathbb{A}^1$ is $\mathbb{A}^2$ requires Kaliman's heavy machinery (topology of $\mathbb{C}^3$, Euler characteristics of generic fibres); for a general $f$ one only knows one fibre.
- **Cancellation is entangled.** A counterexample to the epimorphism problem would likely produce an exotic $\mathbb{A}^2$-fibration and interact with the Zariski cancellation problem for $\mathbb{A}^3$ over $\mathbb{C}$, itself open.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: rectifiability for $n=2$ in char $0$, and for $n=3$ under a structural hypothesis — low degree in one variable, a group action, or global control of *all* fibres.

Wanted: the case $n=3$, char $0$, $f$ arbitrary, given only that the single fibre $f^{-1}(0)\cong\mathbb{A}^2$.

The precise barrier: given $A = \mathbb{C}[X,Y,Z]$ and $f$ with $A/(f)\cong\mathbb{C}^{[2]}$, show that the generic fibre $A\otimes_{\mathbb{C}[f]}\mathbb{C}(f)$ is a polynomial ring in two variables over $\mathbb{C}(f)$ — i.e. that no exotic $\mathbb{C}(f)$-form of the affine plane occurs — and then descend from $\mathbb{C}(f)$ to $\mathbb{C}[f]$ using a DVR/patching criterion. Both halves are unsolved in general: the first is a $\mathbb{A}^2$-form recognition problem over a non-algebraically-closed field, the second is exactly where Sathaye's criterion's flatness hypotheses fail.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **ISI Kolkata (Gupta, Dutta, Das, and students).** Commutative-algebra attack on $\mathbb{A}^2$-fibrations over two-dimensional noetherian bases; results on families $b(X,Y)Z^n - a(X,Y)$ and on residual variables. This is the most systematic ongoing $n=3$ program.
- **Western Michigan / Ottawa (Freudenburg, Daigle).** Locally nilpotent derivations and $\mathbb{G}_a$-actions: classify $f$ for which the kernel of some derivation contains $f$, and study Vénéreau-type polynomials as stable-but-perhaps-not variables.
- **Dijon / Grenoble (Dubouloz and collaborators).** Affine-geometric side: $\mathbb{A}^1$- and $\mathbb{A}^2$-fibrations, Zariski cancellation, and exotic affine spaces; the same techniques that produced non-cancellative threefolds.
- **Kaliman (Miami) and the topological school.** Extending fibre-wise criteria in $\mathbb{C}^3$ to cases where only generic fibres are controlled.
- *(frontier — verify)* Periodic preprints claim rectification for new families in $\mathbb{A}^3$ or a determination of the status of $v_1$; none has been confirmed to settle the general $n=3$ case.

## 8. Future Work

- Prove or refute that every $f\in\mathbb{C}[X,Y,Z]$ with $f^{-1}(0)\cong\mathbb{A}^2$ has generic fibre a polynomial ring over $\mathbb{C}(f)$ — reduce the whole problem to $\mathbb{A}^2$-forms.
- Decide whether $v_1$ is a variable of $k^{[4]}$; a negative answer refutes the conjecture for $n=4$ and is the cheapest available counterexample route.
- Extend Sathaye's DVR criterion to two-dimensional regular bases with a controlled non-flat locus.
- Classify $\mathbb{A}^2$-fibrations $\mathbb{A}^3\to\mathbb{A}^1$ with at most one degenerate fibre, using LND theory plus Miyanishi–Sugie.
- Determine the sharp characteristic-$p$ statement: for which degree pairs $(m,n)$ with $p\mid\gcd(m,n)$ does rectifiability still hold?

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**A rectifiable embedding, made explicit.** Over $k=\mathbb{Q}$, build an embedding by composing triangular automorphisms of $\mathbb{A}^2$:
$$\sigma_1:(X,Y)\mapsto (X,\,Y+X^2), \qquad \sigma_2:(X,Y)\mapsto (X+Y^2,\,Y).$$
Applying $\sigma_2\circ\sigma_1$ to the axis $t\mapsto(t,0)$ gives
$$\varphi(t) = \bigl(t+t^4,\; t^2\bigr).$$
Degrees: $\deg x = 4$, $\deg y = 2$, and $2\mid 4$ — the Abhyankar–Moh criterion holds, as it must.

*Rectification.* Set $u = X - Y^2$. On the curve, $u(t) = (t+t^4) - t^4 = t$. Then $Y - u^2 = t^2 - t^2 = 0$. So the image is the zero set of
$$f(X,Y) \;=\; Y - (X-Y^2)^2,$$
and $\{\,X-Y^2,\; Y-(X-Y^2)^2\,\}$ is the coordinate system produced by $\sigma_1^{-1}\circ\sigma_2^{-1}$. Hence $k[X,Y] = k[\,X-Y^2,\; f\,]$: $f$ is a variable, $\deg f = 4$, and $k[X,Y]/(f)\cong k[t]$.

**A non-example, and why the criterion catches it.** Take $\psi(t) = (t^3, t^2)$. Degrees are $(3,2)$: neither divides the other, so by Abhyankar–Moh this cannot be a closed embedding in char $0$. Indeed the image is the cuspidal cubic $X^2 = Y^3$, singular at the origin, and $k[X,Y]/(X^2-Y^3) \cong k[t^2,t^3] \subsetneq k[t]$ is not a polynomial ring.

**Characteristic $p$ failure.** Let $\operatorname{char} k = p$ and
$$\varphi_p(t) = \bigl(t^{p^2},\; t^{p^2+p}+t\bigr) = (x,y).$$
This *is* a closed embedding: in char $p$,
$$y^p - x^{p+1} = \bigl(t^{p^3+p^2}+t^p\bigr) - t^{p^3+p^2} = t^p,$$
so $t = y - x\,(y^p - x^{p+1})$ lies in $k[x,y]$, giving $k[x,y]=k[t]$ and hence $k[X,Y]/(f)\cong k^{[1]}$ for the defining $f$. But $\deg x = p^2$, $\deg y = p^2+p$, with $\gcd = p$ and neither dividing the other. Since plane automorphisms in any characteristic are tame (van der Kulk) and no degree-lowering move applies, $\varphi_p$ is not rectifiable. This is the Segre–Nagata phenomenon: the epimorphism theorem is genuinely a characteristic-$0$ statement, and any attack on $n\ge 3$ must import characteristic-$0$ input.
