# Sarnak's Mobius Disjointness Conjecture

> **Topic:** Number Theory · **ID:** `01-number-theory/sarnaks-mobius-disjointness-conjecture` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Sarnak's Möbius Disjointness Conjecture postulates that the Möbius function $\mu(n)$ from prime number theory does not correlate with any deterministic sequence generated by a topological dynamical system of zero entropy. 

Formally, let $(X, T)$ be a deterministic dynamical system, meaning $X$ is a compact metric space and $T: X \to X$ is a continuous map such that the topological entropy of $T$ is zero. Let $f \in C(X)$ be a continuous real or complex-valued function on $X$. Sarnak's conjecture states that for any initial point $x \in X$, the sequence $f(T^n x)$ is orthogonal to the Möbius function in the sense of Cesàro averages:

$$ \lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^{N} \mu(n) f(T^n x) = 0 $$

A complete proof of this conjecture requires demonstrating this cancellation for *every* dynamical system with zero topological entropy, which implies that the sequence of primes (and their parity encoded by $\mu$) behaves as a highly random sequence that cannot be simulated or resonated with by any low-complexity deterministic process.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

The conjecture bridges analytic number theory and topological dynamics, relying heavily on the following mathematical constructs:

**The Möbius Function:** 
The arithmetic function $\mu: \mathbb{N} \to \{-1, 0, 1\}$ is defined as:
- $\mu(1) = 1$
- $\mu(n) = (-1)^k$ if $n$ is a square-free integer with exactly $k$ distinct prime factors.
- $\mu(n) = 0$ if $n$ has a squared prime factor ($p^2 \mid n$).

**Topological Entropy:** 
Let $d$ be the metric on $X$. For $n \in \mathbb{N}$, define the Bowen-Dinaburg metric $d_n(x,y) = \max_{0 \le i < n} d(T^i x, T^i y)$. A set $S \subset X$ is $(n, \epsilon)$-separated if for any $x \neq y$ in $S$, $d_n(x,y) > \epsilon$. Let $s(n, \epsilon)$ be the maximal cardinality of an $(n, \epsilon)$-separated set. The topological entropy $h_{top}(T)$ is defined as:

$$ h_{top}(T) = \lim_{\epsilon \to 0} \limsup_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} \log s(n, \epsilon) $$

A system with $h_{top}(T) = 0$ is considered "deterministic." If $h_{top}(T) > 0$, the system exhibits chaotic divergence of trajectories.

**Chowla's Conjecture:** 
Sarnak's conjecture is intimately related to Chowla's conjecture, which asserts that the Möbius function has no autocorrelation. For any distinct non-negative integers $h_1, \dots, h_k$ (where $k \ge 1$):

$$ \lim_{N \to \infty} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^{N} \mu(n+h_1) \dots \mu(n+h_k) = 0 $$

Sarnak established that Chowla's conjecture implies the Möbius Disjointness Conjecture.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

Peter Sarnak formally proposed this conjecture in a series of lectures at the Institute for Advanced Study (IAS) in 2012. It emerged from the deep-seated heuristic in number theory that the distribution of primes contains no structural periods or deterministic patterns. 

Historically, instances of this disjointness were known long before the conjecture was formalized. Davenport's 1937 theorem, proving that $\mu(n)$ is disjoint from circle rotations (which form a zero-entropy system), was the earliest major milestone. 

The state of the art has progressed by verifying the conjecture for increasingly complex classes of zero-entropy systems. Notable breakthroughs include Green and Tao's work on nilsequences (2012) and Bourgain, Sarnak, and Ziegler's resolution for horocycle flows (2013). More recently, Tao's resolution of the logarithmically averaged version of the Chowla conjecture for two-point correlations (2015) sparked a wave of results proving the *logarithmic* version of Sarnak's conjecture for many systems, though the standard Cesàro version remains broadly open.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

The conjecture has been rigorously proven for a wide array of specific zero-entropy systems:

1. **Kronecker Systems (Circle Rotations):** Proven by Davenport (1937). Here $X = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ and $T(x) = x + \alpha$.
2. **Nilmanifolds:** Green and Tao (2012) proved that $\mu$ is strongly orthogonal to nilsequences, covering systems where $X = G/\Gamma$ ($G$ a nilpotent Lie group, $\Gamma$ a discrete cocompact subgroup).
3. **Horocycle Flows:** Bourgain, Sarnak, and Ziegler (2013) proved it for horocycle flows on finite-volume quotients of $SL(2, \mathbb{R})$.
4. **Automatic Sequences:** Mauduit and Rivat (2010) established disjointness for the Thue-Morse sequence and sum-of-digits functions (which correspond to finite-state automata dynamical systems).
5. **Interval Exchange Transformations (IETs):** Proven by Bourgain (2007) for 3-interval exchanges and generalized to typical IETs by Chaika and Eskin.
6. **Zero-Entropy Shifts with Low Word Complexity:** Proven for subshifts where the number of words of length $n$, $p(n)$, grows slower than exponentially (e.g., polynomial growth).

## 5. Principal Obstacles

The main bottleneck to a full resolution lies in the disparity between the *macroscopic* nature of topological entropy and the *microscopic* (pointwise) cancellation required by the disjointness sum. 

1. **Lack of a Structure Theorem:** There is no overarching structural classification for arbitrary zero-entropy systems. While positive entropy systems possess Bernoulli factors, and structured zero-entropy systems (like nilmanifolds) possess algebraic rigidity, general zero-entropy systems can exhibit highly "wild" geometry and intermediate complexity (e.g., word complexity growing like $e^{\sqrt{n}}$).
2. **The Parity Problem in Sieve Theory:** Bounding $\sum \mu(n) f(T^n x)$ inherently runs into the parity problem. Number theorists bypass this using Vaughan's or Heath-Brown's identities, which decompose the sum into Type I and Type II bilinear forms. Evaluating these bilinear forms requires establishing resonance cancellations within the dynamical system. Without algebraic structure or Fourier analysis (which fail on general metric spaces), bounding Type II sums for arbitrary $f(T^n x)$ is currently intractable.

## 6. The Gap

The precise mathematical boundary between what is proven (Section 4) and the general conjecture (Section 1) is the transition from **structured or low-complexity zero-entropy** to **unstructured intermediate-complexity zero-entropy**. 

To fully resolve the conjecture, one must cross the barrier of "intermediate word growth" (e.g., systems with complexity $p(n) \sim e^{n^{\alpha}}$ for $0 < \alpha < 1$). Crossing this gap requires either a completely novel factorization theorem in ergodic theory for all zero-entropy systems, or a radically new prime number theorem capable of yielding bilinear bounds without exploiting continuous symmetries or Fourier decompositions.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

Current research is highly active at the intersection of ergodic theory and multiplicative number theory, heavily utilizing the Matomäki-Radziwiłł theorems on multiplicative functions in short intervals. 

Leading schools (including researchers like Frantzikinakis, Host, Kra, and Lemanczyk) are exploring the "Logarithmic Sarnak Conjecture" as a stepping stone. In this variant, the standard Cesàro average is replaced by a logarithmic average: $\frac{1}{\log N} \sum_{n=1}^N \frac{\mu(n)}{n} f(T^n x) \to 0$.

Recent preprints have focused on smooth diffeomorphisms of compact manifolds that possess zero entropy but complex topological features. * (frontier — verify)* New claims suggest that local Gowers uniformity norms in sparse arithmetic regimes might provide a proxy for structural decomposition in arbitrary metric spaces, potentially bridging the intermediate complexity gap.

## 8. Future Work

Leading mathematicians have outlined several strategic pathways:
- **Distal Systems:** Proving the conjecture for general distal systems of infinite rank. Currently, only finite-step nilmanifolds (which are specific finite-rank distal systems) are fully solved.
- **Removing Logarithmic Weights:** Many recent breakthroughs on Chowla's and Sarnak's conjectures rely on logarithmic averaging (which heavily dampens high-frequency oscillations). A major open goal is to "upgrade" logarithmic disjointness to standard disjointness for intermediate complexity systems.
- **Unique Ergodicity:** Leveraging the disjointness of $\mu(n)$ against systems that are uniquely ergodic but possess no measurable eigenvalues.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

The simplest non-trivial illustration of Sarnak's conjecture is **Davenport's Theorem (1937)**, which proves disjointness for irrational circle rotations. 

Let the state space be the unit circle $X = \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$. Let the dynamical system be an irrational rotation $T(x) = x + \alpha$ where $\alpha \notin \mathbb{Q}$. Since rotations are isometries, points never spread apart, meaning $h_{top}(T) = 0$.

Let the continuous function be $f(x) = e^{2\pi i x}$. The conjecture requires us to prove:
$$ \lim_{N\to\infty} \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=1}^{N} \mu(n) e^{2\pi i n \alpha} = 0 $$

**Proof Sketch:**
1. To bound this sum, one applies **Vaughan's Identity**, which decomposes the Möbius function over the range $1 \le n \le N$ into a manageable number of bilinear sums known as Type I and Type II sums.
2. A typical Type II sum takes the form:
   $$ \sum_{m \sim M} \sum_{k \sim K} a_m b_k e^{2\pi i m k \alpha} $$
   where $a_m, b_k$ are unknown bounded coefficients, and $M, K$ are large parameters such that $M \cdot K \approx N$.
3. Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to the outer sum (over $m$) allows us to eliminate the arbitrary coefficients $a_m$. We are left with bounding geometric progressions involving the continuous phase $e^{2\pi i k \alpha}$.
4. Because $\alpha$ is irrational, the sequence $k \alpha \pmod 1$ is uniformly distributed (Weyl's Criterion). Therefore, the geometric series $\sum e^{2\pi i (k_1 - k_2) m \alpha}$ exhibits massive cancellation when $k_1 \neq k_2$.
5. This analytic cancellation propagates backward through the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, proving that the Type II sums are $o(N)$. 

This explicitly demonstrates how the purely deterministic, structured oscillation of $e^{2\pi i n \alpha}$ fails to correlate with the prime-factor parity encoded in $\mu(n)$.
