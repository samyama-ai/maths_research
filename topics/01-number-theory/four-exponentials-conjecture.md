# Four Exponentials Conjecture

> **Topic:** Number Theory · **ID:** `01-number-theory/four-exponentials-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

The Four Exponentials Conjecture is a foundational open problem in transcendental number theory concerning the algebraic nature of exponential functions.

**Conjecture Statement:** Let $x_1, x_2$ be two complex numbers that are linearly independent over the rational numbers $\mathbb{Q}$. Let $y_1, y_2$ also be two complex numbers that are linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$. Then, at least one of the following four numbers is transcendental:
$$ e^{x_1 y_1}, e^{x_1 y_2}, e^{x_2 y_1}, e^{x_2 y_2} $$

Equivalently, the conjecture can be stated in the negative: if $e^{x_i y_j}$ are all algebraic numbers for $i \in \{1,2\}$ and $j \in \{1,2\}$, then either $x_1, x_2$ are linearly dependent over $\mathbb{Q}$, or $y_1, y_2$ are linearly dependent over $\mathbb{Q}$. A complete proof requires showing that no such two-dimensional pairs of algebraic exponential evaluations can exist without rational linear dependence.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

The problem operates at the intersection of linear algebra, field extensions, and complex analysis. Let $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ denote the field of algebraic numbers embedded within the complex numbers $\mathbb{C}$. A number is transcendental if it belongs to $\mathbb{C} \setminus \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$.

Let $\mathcal{L}$ be the $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space of logarithms of non-zero algebraic numbers:
$$ \mathcal{L} = \{ z \in \mathbb{C} \mid e^z \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}^\times \} $$

The conjecture can be formally reframed in terms of matrices over $\mathcal{L}$. Let $M$ be a $2 \times 2$ matrix whose entries are in $\mathcal{L}$:
$$ M = \begin{pmatrix} \log \alpha_{11} & \log \alpha_{12} \\ \log \alpha_{21} & \log \alpha_{22} \end{pmatrix} $$
where $\alpha_{ij} \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}^\times$. The Four Exponentials Conjecture asserts that if the two rows of $M$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$ and the two columns of $M$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$, then the rank of $M$ over $\mathbb{C}$ is 2. 

Equivalently, the determinant of $M$ must be non-zero:
$$ (\log \alpha_{11})(\log \alpha_{22}) - (\log \alpha_{12})(\log \alpha_{21}) \neq 0 $$

This formulation directly grounds the conjecture as a statement about the algebraic independence and absence of non-trivial multiplicative relations between logarithms of algebraic numbers.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

The history of the conjecture is closely tied to the development of modern transcendental number theory in the mid-20th century. While similar questions were pondered by Atle Selberg, Carl Ludwig Siegel, and Theodor Schneider, the explicit formulation of the Four Exponentials Conjecture is credited independently to Serge Lang (1966) and Kanakanahalli Ramachandra (1968).

Both Lang and Ramachandra formalized the problem as the natural next step after their independent proofs of the **Six Exponentials Theorem**, which successfully establishes the analogous claim for a $2 \times 3$ matrix (i.e., at least one of six exponentials is transcendental).

The State of the Art (SOTA) for this specific family of problems remains halted at the Six Exponentials Theorem. A broader generalisation known as the **Strong Four Exponentials Conjecture** (formulated by Michel Waldschmidt) posits that if $x_1, x_2$ and $y_1, y_2$ are $\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent, then at least one of the four products $x_i y_j$ does not belong to $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\mathcal{L}$ (the $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$-vector space generated by logarithms of algebraic numbers).

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

While the $2 \times 2$ case remains entirely open, several adjacent cases and higher-dimensional variants have been rigorously solved:

- **The Six Exponentials Theorem (Verified):** The conjecture holds if one dimension is increased to 3. If $y_1, y_2, y_3$ are $\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent and $x_1, x_2$ are $\mathbb{Q}$-linearly independent, at least one of the six values $e^{x_i y_j}$ for $1 \le i \le 2$ and $1 \le j \le 3$ is definitively transcendental.
- **Baker's Theorem (1D Restriction):** If one of the spaces is restricted to algebraic numbers (e.g., $x_1 = 1, x_2 = \beta$ where $\beta \in \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ is irrational), the problem reduces to Baker's theorem on linear forms in logarithms, which completely resolves the transcendence of $e^{y_1 + \beta y_2}$ and proves linear independence over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$.
- **Elliptic Analogues:** Partial results have been achieved when ordinary exponential functions are replaced with the exponential map of an elliptic curve with complex multiplication, heavily utilizing the geometry of Abelian varieties (e.g., work by Masser and Wüstholz).

## 5. Principal Obstacles

The fundamental bottleneck preventing a proof lies in the limitations of auxiliary analytic functions, which form the bedrock of the Schneider-Lang criterion and Baker's method. 

Standard transcendence proofs operate by constructing a polynomial function $F(z)$ with algebraic coefficients that vanishes (has zeros) at a large number of specific lattice points. By Siegel's Lemma, such an auxiliary function can only be guaranteed to exist if the number of free parameters (degrees of freedom) strictly exceeds the number of linear conditions (zeros).

To extrapolate these zeros to a denser set using the Schwarz Lemma and eventually derive a contradiction, the growth rate of the function requires a specific density inequality. For a matrix of size $d \times l$, standard extrapolation techniques require:
$$ dl > d + l $$

- For the Six Exponentials Theorem ($d=2, l=3$): $2 \times 3 = 6 > 2 + 3 = 5$. The strict inequality holds, providing enough "slack" to force the function to vanish identically.
- For the Four Exponentials Conjecture ($d=2, l=2$): $2 \times 2 = 4 \ngtr 2 + 2 = 4$. 

Because $4$ is not strictly greater than $4$, the dimensional parity is perfectly balanced. This leaves zero analytical slack. Consequently, standard auxiliary functions cannot be proven to vanish identically on the required domain, rendering classical extrapolation techniques mathematically impotent.

## 6. The Gap

The exact mathematical barrier that must be crossed to resolve the conjecture is bypassing the $dl > d+l$ density inequality. The gap lies in formulating a radically new zero-estimate (in the sense of Masser and Wüstholz) for commutative algebraic groups that functions when the rank of the period lattice perfectly matches the dimension of the parameter space. Alternatively, an entirely new method that does not rely on constructing auxiliary polynomials via the Pigeonhole Principle must be invented to handle the exact $2 \times 2$ dimensional parity.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

Active research on the Four Exponentials Conjecture is deeply intertwined with broader pursuits in Diophantine geometry and algebraic groups.
- **Interpolation Determinants:** Michel Laurent's method of interpolation determinants successfully bypassed the need for explicit auxiliary functions to prove the Six Exponentials Theorem, relying instead on the non-vanishing of determinants of evaluation matrices. Researchers are currently attempting to refine this method for the $2 \times 2$ case.
- **Schanuel's Conjecture:** Many schools of thought view the Four Exponentials problem through the lens of Schanuel's Conjecture, which dictates the transcendence degree of $\mathbb{Q}(z_1, \dots, z_n, e^{z_1}, \dots, e^{z_n})$. Since Four Exponentials is a direct corollary of Schanuel's, progress on algebraic independence directly fuels this problem.
- **$p$-adic Variants:** There is heavy focus on the $p$-adic analogue of the Four Exponentials Conjecture, which has profound and direct implications for Leopoldt's conjecture regarding the non-vanishing of $p$-adic regulators of number fields.
- **Motivic Periods:** **(frontier — verify)** Recent preprints indicate attempts to leverage Hodge theory and the theory of motivic periods to bypass classical zero-estimate constraints, though these remain highly speculative.

## 8. Future Work

Leading mathematicians suggest the following open pathways:
1. **Algorithmic/Computational Bounds:** Finding computational ways to strictly bound the lower limits of interpolation determinants in the $2 \times 2$ case, potentially identifying a contradiction for specific small heights.
2. **Refining Zero-Estimates:** Developing non-trivial lower bounds for polynomials on commutative algebraic groups where the period lattice rank matches the dimension, possibly by introducing new structural hypotheses that fall short of full complex multiplication.
3. **Sharp Strong Conjectures:** Proving the "Sharp" Strong Four Exponentials Conjecture, which aims to reduce the hypotheses in Waldschmidt's formulations to the absolute minimal linear independence conditions.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

To ground the abstract statement, consider the natural logarithms of four prime numbers. 
Let $x_1 = \ln 2$ and $x_2 = \ln 3$. Since $\ln 2 / \ln 3$ is irrational, they are linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$.
Let $y_1 = \ln 5$ and $y_2 = \ln 7$. Similarly, they are linearly independent over $\mathbb{Q}$.

According to the conjecture, we evaluate the four exponentials $e^{x_i y_j}$:
1. $E_{11} = e^{(\ln 2)(\ln 5)} = 2^{\ln 5}$
2. $E_{12} = e^{(\ln 2)(\ln 7)} = 2^{\ln 7}$
3. $E_{21} = e^{(\ln 3)(\ln 5)} = 3^{\ln 5}$
4. $E_{22} = e^{(\ln 3)(\ln 7)} = 3^{\ln 7}$

**The current gap in human knowledge:** As of right now, modern mathematics cannot definitively prove that *any single one* of these four numbers (e.g., $2^{\ln 5}$) is transcendental, or even irrational. The famous Gelfond-Schneider theorem ($a^b$ is transcendental for algebraic $a$ and algebraic irrational $b$) fails here because the exponents (like $\ln 5$) are transcendental, not algebraic.

**What the conjecture provides:** The Four Exponentials Conjecture boldly bypasses this ignorance. It asserts that if all four numbers $2^{\ln 5}, 2^{\ln 7}, 3^{\ln 5}, 3^{\ln 7}$ were algebraic, it would force a rational linear dependence between either the rows ($\ln 2, \ln 3$) or the columns ($\ln 5, \ln 7$). Because we rigorously know both pairs are linearly independent, the conjecture mathematically guarantees that **at least one** of these four specific numbers must be transcendental, perfectly capturing the boundary between known dependencies and unknown individual properties.
