# Coleman's Conjecture on Semistable Reduction

> **Topic:** Number Theory · **ID:** `01-number-theory/colemans-conjecture-on-jacobians` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $K$ be a complete discretely valued field with algebraically closed residue field $k$ of characteristic $p > 0$, and let $C/K$ be a smooth, proper, geometrically connected curve of genus $g \ge 2$ with Jacobian $J = \operatorname{Jac}(C)$. By the Deligne–Mumford semistable reduction theorem there is a unique **minimal** finite extension $L/K$ over which $C$ acquires stable reduction; $L/K$ is Galois, and $\Gamma := \operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ is the *monodromy group* of $C$.

For every integer $m \ge 3$ **prime to $p$**, Grothendieck's $\ell$-torsion criterion gives $L \subseteq K(J[m])$. The prime-to-$p$ hypothesis is essential to the proof: it makes $J[m]$ étale, so torsion points specialize injectively.

**Coleman's conjecture.** The same holds at the residue characteristic:
$$L \;\subseteq\; K\bigl(J[p]\bigr),$$
i.e. $C$ acquires stable reduction over the field generated by the coordinates of the $p$-torsion of its Jacobian. Equivalently, the monodromy representation $\rho: G_K \twoheadrightarrow \Gamma$ factors through the mod-$p$ representation $G_K \to \operatorname{Aut}(J[p](\bar K))$.

A proof must handle *wild* monodromy ($p \mid \\#\Gamma$), where no étale specialization argument is available. A disproof requires one curve $C/K$ whose stable-reduction field is not contained in $K(J[p])$. Weaker forms replace $J[p]$ by $J[p^n]$ for $n$ bounded in terms of $g$; these are also open in general.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Stable curves.** A curve $\mathcal{C}_s/k$ is *stable* of genus $g \ge 2$ if it is connected, reduced, proper, with at worst ordinary double points, and every irreducible component of geometric genus $0$ meets the rest of the curve in at least $3$ points; equivalently $\omega_{\mathcal{C}_s}$ is ample. A *stable model* of $C$ over $\mathcal{O}_L$ is a flat proper $\mathcal{O}_L$-scheme $\mathcal{C}$ with $\mathcal{C}_{L} \cong C_L$ and stable special fibre.

**Theorem (Deligne–Mumford, 1969).** There is a finite extension $L/K$ with $C_L$ admitting a stable model; the model is unique up to unique isomorphism, hence descends along $\operatorname{Gal}(\bar K / L)$, and the minimal such $L$ is Galois over $K$ with
$$\Gamma = \operatorname{Gal}(L/K) \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Aut}_k(\mathcal{C}_s).$$

**Theorem (Grothendieck, SGA 7 I, Exp. IX; Serre–Tate).** An abelian variety $A/K$ has semiabelian reduction over $K(A[m])$ for any $m \ge 3$ with $p \nmid m$. Combined with the Deligne–Mumford criterion "$C$ has stable reduction $\iff$ $J$ has semiabelian reduction", this yields $L \subseteq K(J[m])$.

**Wild bound.** If $\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}_k(X)$ has order $p$ on a smooth curve $X/k$ of genus $g$ in characteristic $p$, Riemann–Hurwitz applied to $X \to X/\langle\sigma\rangle$ with Artin–Schreier ramification jumps gives
$$2g - 2 \;=\; p\,(2g'-2) \;+\; \sum_{P} (p-1)(h_P+1), \qquad h_P \ge 1,$$
so $g \ge (p-1)/2$, i.e. $p \le 2g+1$. Hence:
$$p > 2g+1 \;\Longrightarrow\; p \nmid \\#\Gamma \;\Longrightarrow\; L/K \text{ tame}.$$

**Serre's rigidity lemma.** For $m \ge 3$ prime to the residue characteristic, an automorphism of finite order of an abelian variety acting trivially on $A[m]$ is the identity. The conjecture asks whether the analogue holds with $m = p$ for the automorphisms arising as monodromy of a stable model; here $J[p]$ is a finite flat, non-étale group scheme of order $p^{2g}$, with connected–étale sequence
$$0 \to \mathcal{J}[p]^{\circ} \to \mathcal{J}[p] \to \mathcal{J}[p]^{\mathrm{ét}} \to 0, \qquad \operatorname{rank}\mathcal{J}[p]^{\mathrm{ét}} = p^{\,f}, \; f = p\text{-rank of } \mathcal{J}_s \le g .$$

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1969.** Deligne and Mumford prove the stable reduction theorem (*The irreducibility of the space of curves of given genus*, Publ. Math. IHÉS 36), reducing it to Grothendieck's semiabelian criterion for $J$.
- **1972.** Grothendieck (SGA 7 I, Exp. IX) gives the $\ell$-torsion criterion; the prime-to-$p$ restriction is visible from the outset as the only gap between the $\ell$-adic and $p$-adic pictures.
- **1990.** Raynaud, *$p$-groupes et réduction semi-stable des courbes* (Grothendieck Festschrift III), initiates the systematic study of wild monodromy: he analyses the action of a $p$-group on the stable model and the geometry of the "new" components created in the wild case.
- **2003.** Coleman, *Stable maps of curves* (Documenta Math., Extra Volume: Kazuya Kato's Fiftieth Birthday, 217–225), formulates the $p$-torsion question and the conjecture that the monodromy extension is cut out by $J[p]$.
- **2003–2006.** Matignon and Lehr develop an explicit algorithmic theory of stable reduction for $p$-cyclic covers $y^p = f(x)$ of $\mathbb{P}^1$ (Matignon, Math. Ann. 325 (2003); Lehr–Matignon, Compositio Math. 141 (2005); Lehr–Matignon, *Wild monodromy and automorphisms of curves*, Duke Math. J. 135 (2006)), producing the first explicit computations of wild monodromy fields.
- **2010s–2020s.** Two SOTA strands: (i) explicit semistable models — Bouw–Wewers, Rüth, Obus–Wewers for superelliptic curves; Dokchitser–Dokchitser–Maistret–Morgan cluster pictures for hyperelliptic curves at $p$ odd; Muselli at $p = 2$. (ii) Structural results on tame monodromy — Halle, *Stable reduction of curves and tame ramification*, Math. Z. 265 (2010).

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **Genus $1$ (all $p$).** For elliptic curves the statement holds: potentially good reduction forces $e = [L:K] \in \{2,3,4,6\}$ (Kodaira/Néron), and for $p \ge 5$ the automorphism group $\operatorname{Aut}(\bar E)$ of order $e$ acts faithfully on $E[p] \cong \mathbb{F}_p^2$, so inertia acts trivially on $E[p]$ only if it acts trivially on $\bar E$. The residual cases $p = 2, 3$ (where $\\#\operatorname{Aut} \in \{12, 24\}$) are settled by explicit Kraus-type analysis of minimal Weierstrass equations.
- **Tame range $p > 2g+1$.** The monodromy $\Gamma$ is cyclic of order prime to $p$ and $L = K(\pi^{1/e})$ for a uniformiser $\pi$ and $e = \\#\Gamma \mid \operatorname{lcm}$ of orders in $\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{C}_s)$. The conjecture reduces to faithfulness of the $\Gamma$-action on $J[p]$; this is verified whenever the stable reduction has $p$-rank $f \ge 1$ or the $\Gamma$-action on $H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}$ has no trivial isotypic part.
- **Genus $2$, $p \ge 7$.** Combining Liu's classification of stable models of genus-$2$ curves (*Courbes stables de genre 2 et leur schéma de modules*, Math. Ann. 295 (1993)) with the tame bound $p > 2g+1 = 5$, $\Gamma$ is cyclic of order dividing $48$ and $L$ is explicitly computable from the Igusa invariants.
- **Hyperelliptic curves, $p$ odd.** Dokchitser–Dokchitser–Maistret–Morgan (*Arithmetic of hyperelliptic curves over local fields*, Math. Ann. 385 (2023)) determine the stable model and the minimal extension $L$ combinatorially from the cluster picture of the roots of $f$ in $y^2 = f(x)$; the conjecture becomes a finite check on each cluster type.
- **$p$-cyclic covers with equidistant geometry.** Lehr–Matignon compute the wild monodromy extension of $y^p = f(x)$ explicitly and express it by "monodromy polynomials"; in the families they treat the extension is generated by torsion data of order $p$ *(frontier — verify the precise form of the torsion statement in Duke 135 (2006))*.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Loss of étale specialization.** Every proof of the $\ell$-torsion criterion uses that $J[m]$ is étale over $\mathcal{O}_K$, so $J[m](\bar K) \to \mathcal{J}[m](k)$ is bijective and the Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich criterion applies verbatim. At $m = p$ the group scheme $J[p]$ is connected in the supersingular directions; its $\bar K$-points see only the étale quotient of rank $p^{f}$, $f \le g$, so a naive transfer loses $2g-f$ dimensions of information.
- **Wild inertia is not linearizable.** For $p \le 2g+1$ the $p$-Sylow $\Gamma_p \subseteq \Gamma$ acts wildly on $\mathcal{C}_s$; higher ramification breaks (Artin–Schreier jumps $h_P$) enter the geometry of the stable model as non-canonical parameters. There is no deformation-theoretic dictionary translating those jumps into congruence conditions on $J[p]$.
- **$p$-rank zero.** If $\mathcal{J}_s$ is supersingular, $J[p](\bar K)$ is trivial as a group of points, so $K(J[p]) = K$ if one reads the field naively; the conjecture must be read scheme-theoretically (field of definition of the $\bar K$-points of the group scheme, i.e. of the finite flat $\mathcal{O}$-algebra), and the two readings differ precisely in the hardest cases.
- **Failure of Serre's lemma at $p$.** Automorphisms of order $p$ act trivially on the $p$-torsion *points*; the rigidity lemma is false at $m = p$, removing the standard mechanism ("faithful on torsion $\Rightarrow$ faithful on the curve").
- **No moduli-theoretic handle.** $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_g$ has non-tame automorphism stacks in characteristic $p \le 2g+1$; the "$m$-level structure rigidifies $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_g$" argument (which is exactly the $\ell$-torsion proof) breaks at level $p$.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: $L \subseteq K(J[m])$ for $m \ge 3$, $p \nmid m$ (all $g$, all $p$); and $L \subseteq K(J[p])$ for $g = 1$, for $p > 2g+1$ under a faithfulness hypothesis on $J[p]$, and for explicit families (hyperelliptic $p$ odd, $p$-cyclic covers).

Conjectured: $L \subseteq K(J[p])$ with **no** hypothesis, in particular when $p \le 2g+1$ and $\Gamma$ has non-trivial $p$-Sylow.

The precise missing step: produce a criterion, valid for the finite flat group scheme $J[p]$ over $\mathcal{O}_K$, that detects semiabelian reduction the way Néron–Ogg–Shafarevich does for $T_\ell J$. Concretely, one needs to show that if $G_L$ acts trivially on $J[p](\bar K)$ then the wild part of $\Gamma$ is trivial — i.e. that a wild automorphism of the stable model cannot be invisible in $p$-torsion. Candidate replacements (Dieudonné modules, $\varphi$-modules over $\mathcal{O}_K$, Breuil–Kisin modules for $J[p]$) currently detect semistability of $J$ only after passing to a field already known to be large enough.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Explicit semistable reduction (Ulm / Ulm–Virginia school).** Wewers, Rüth and Obus continue to develop MacLane/Berkovich-valuation algorithms and the `MCLF` Sage package, which compute stable models and the exact field $L$ for superelliptic curves including wild cases $p \mid \deg$. This turns the conjecture into machine-checkable instances.
- **Cluster pictures and their wild analogues (Bristol/Glasgow/Warwick).** Extension of the Dokchitser–Dokchitser–Maistret–Morgan machinery to $p = 2$ (Muselli) and to $\mu_p$-covers; the target is a combinatorial formula for $L$ that can be compared directly with the ramification of $K(J[p])$ *(frontier — verify)*.
- **$p$-adic Hodge theory of $J[p]$.** Use of Breuil–Kisin modules and prismatic $F$-gauges to characterise the semistability locus of $J$ by the Galois module $J[p]$; a positive answer would give the conjecture uniformly *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Bordeaux school (Matignon and successors).** Continued study of monodromy polynomials, lifting of wild automorphisms, and the "different bound" for $p$-cyclic covers.

## 8. Future Work

- Settle the tame case $p > 2g+1$ unconditionally by proving that a tame cyclic automorphism group of a stable curve acts faithfully on the group scheme $\mathcal{J}[p]$.
- Formulate and prove the weak form $L \subseteq K(J[p^n])$ with an explicit $n = n(g)$; even $n$ depending on $p$ and $g$ would be new.
- Systematic computer search over $y^p = f(x)$ with $p \le 2g+1$, comparing $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ (from `MCLF`) with the mod-$p$ image of $G_K$ on $J[p]$, aiming either at strong numerical support or at a counterexample.
- Reformulate in terms of $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_g$ with $p$-level structure: is the moduli stack of stable curves with full $J[p]$-structure representable (a scheme) in characteristic $0$ with $p$-adic base?

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take $g = 1$, $K = \mathbb{Q}_7^{\mathrm{ur}}$ (completed maximal unramified extension, residue field $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_7$), and
$$E: \; y^2 = x^3 + 7 .$$

**Step 1 — reduction type.** $\Delta = -432 \cdot 7^2$, so $v(\Delta) = 2$; $j = 0$, $v(j) = 0 \ge 0$, so $E$ has potentially good reduction. The equation is minimal ($v(c_4) = v(0) = \infty$, $v(c_6) = v(-864\cdot 7)=1 < 6$), Kodaira type $\mathrm{IV}$.

**Step 2 — the minimal extension $L$.** Good reduction is attained after a twist $(x,y) \mapsto (u^2 x, u^3 y)$ with $v(u^{12}) = v(\Delta) = 2$, i.e. $u = 7^{1/6}$ but only $u^2 = 7^{1/3}$ is needed: over $L = K(7^{1/3})$ the substitution $x = 7^{1/3}x'$, $y = 7^{1/2}\cdot$… more directly, $e = 12/\gcd(12, v(\Delta)) = 12/2 = 6$ for the sextic twist convention; since $\mathbb{Q}_7$ contains $\zeta_3$ (as $3 \mid 7-1$) the extension realising good reduction is
$$L = K\bigl(7^{1/6}\bigr), \qquad [L:K] = 6, \qquad \Gamma \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Aut}(\bar E) = \mu_6,$$
the reduction $\bar E: y^2 = x^3 + 1$ over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_7$ having $j = 0$ and automorphism group $\mu_6$ (note $7 \equiv 1 \bmod 6$, so $\bar E$ is ordinary). This is tame: $p = 7 > 2g+1 = 3$.

**Step 3 — is $L \subseteq K(E[7])$?** The generator $\zeta_6 \in \operatorname{Aut}(\bar E)$ acts on $\bar E[7] \cong \mathbb{F}_7^2$ (ordinary, so $\bar E[7]$ has étale rank $7$) with eigenvalues the primitive $6$-th roots of unity in $\mathbb{F}_7^\times = \langle 3 \rangle$: namely $3$ and $5 = 3^{-1}$, both of exact order $6$. Hence the $\Gamma$-action on $E[7]$ is **faithful**. Consequently the inertia subgroup of $G_K$ fixing $E[7]$ pointwise maps to the trivial element of $\Gamma$, so $G_{K(E[7])}$ acts trivially on the stable (here: good) model, i.e.
$$L = K(7^{1/6}) \subseteq K(E[7]).$$
Indeed $K(E[7])$ contains $\zeta_7$ and, by the theory of the formal group, ramification index divisible by $6$; the degree-$6$ totally ramified subextension is exactly $L$.

**Step 4 — where this argument breaks for $g \ge 2$.** The only inputs were (i) $\Gamma \hookrightarrow \operatorname{Aut}(\bar E)$ and (ii) faithfulness of $\operatorname{Aut}(\bar E)$ on the *étale* part of $\bar E[p]$. If instead $p \le 2g+1$ — e.g. $p = 5$, $g = 2$, $C: y^5 = f(x)$ with $\deg f = 4$ over $\mathbb{Q}_5^{\mathrm{ur}}$ — the monodromy group contains an element of order $5$ acting on $\mathcal{C}_s$ with Artin–Schreier ramification, and that element acts trivially on $\mathcal{J}[5](\bar K)$ whenever $\mathcal{J}_s$ has $5$-rank $0$. Step 3 then gives no information, and the conjecture is exactly the assertion that the scheme-theoretic $5$-torsion nevertheless remembers the extension $K(5^{1/e'})$ produced by the wild part.
