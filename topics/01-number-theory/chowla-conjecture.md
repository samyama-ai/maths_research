# Chowla Conjecture

> **Topic:** Number Theory · **ID:** `01-number-theory/chowla-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

The Chowla Conjecture asserts that the values of the Liouville function $\lambda(n)$ (or equivalently, the Möbius function $\mu(n)$) evaluated at consecutive integers are asymptotically independent. It posits that the parity of the number of prime factors of $n$ does not systematically influence the parity of the number of prime factors of $n+h$.

Formally, let $\lambda(n)$ be the Liouville function. For any integer $k \ge 1$ and any set of distinct non-negative integers $h_1, h_2, \dots, h_k$, the conjecture states that:

$$ \sum_{n \le x} \lambda(n+h_1) \lambda(n+h_2) \dots \lambda(n+h_k) = o(x) $$

as $x \to \infty$. 

When formulated in terms of the Möbius function $\mu(n)$, which vanishes on numbers containing a square factor, the conjecture states that for distinct non-negative integers $h_1, \dots, h_k$ and non-negative integers $a_1, \dots, a_k$ with at least one $a_i$ being odd:

$$ \sum_{n \le x} \mu(n+h_1)^{a_1} \dots \mu(n+h_k)^{a_k} = o(x) $$

A complete proof would require demonstrating that these sums exhibit sublinear growth for all possible tuples of shifts and any $k$, thereby confirming that the signs of these functions behave pseudo-randomly.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

The conjecture relies heavily on the study of arithmetic multiplicative functions. 

- **Liouville function $\lambda(n)$:** Defined as $\lambda(n) = (-1)^{\Omega(n)}$, where $\Omega(n)$ is the total number of prime factors of $n$, counted with multiplicity. It is a completely multiplicative function, meaning $\lambda(ab) = \lambda(a)\lambda(b)$ for all positive integers $a, b$.
- **Möbius function $\mu(n)$:** Defined as $\mu(n) = (-1)^{\omega(n)}$ if $n$ is square-free, and $0$ otherwise, where $\omega(n)$ is the number of distinct prime factors of $n$.

The Chowla conjecture can be seen as a special case of the broader **Sarnak's Möbius Disjointness Conjecture**, which posits that the Möbius function is orthogonal to any sequence generated by a deterministic dynamical system.

It is also closely related to the **Hardy-Littlewood Prime Tuples Conjecture**. If the Hardy-Littlewood conjecture is true, it dictates the distribution of prime configurations, which in turn would imply the Chowla Conjecture. However, Chowla's conjecture is considered strictly weaker and potentially more tractable since it only requires distinguishing the parity of $\Omega(n)$ rather than identifying primes.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

The conjecture was first explicitly stated by Sarvadaman Chowla in his 1965 book, *The Riemann Hypothesis and Hilbert's Tenth Problem*. For decades, the problem remained entirely intractable even for the simplest case of $k=2$ and $h_1=0, h_2=1$ (i.e., proving that $\sum \lambda(n)\lambda(n+1) = o(x)$).

A massive breakthrough was achieved in the 2010s regarding the **Logarithmic Chowla Conjecture**, which replaces the natural density with logarithmic density:

$$ \sum_{n \le x} \frac{\lambda(n+h_1) \dots \lambda(n+h_k)}{n} = o(\log x) $$

In 2015, Kaisa Matomäki, Maksym Radziwiłł, and Terence Tao proved the logarithmic version for $k=2$. Following this, Tao (2016) proved the logarithmic version for all odd $k$, introducing entropy decrement methods. Finally, Tao and Joni Teräväinen (2018) resolved the logarithmic Chowla conjecture for all $k$.

Despite these immense strides in the logarithmically weighted case, the original (unweighted) Chowla conjecture for natural density remains unproven for all $k \ge 2$.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **$k=1$ Case:** For a single point, the conjecture states that $\sum_{n \le x} \lambda(n) = o(x)$. This is mathematically equivalent to the Prime Number Theorem and has been proven for over a century.
- **Logarithmic Weights:** The logarithmically averaged formulation of the conjecture has been proven for all integers $k \ge 1$ and any shifts $h_i$ (Matomäki, Radziwiłł, Tao, Teräväinen).
- **Averaged Shifts:** It has been shown by Matomäki, Radziwiłł, and Tao that the unweighted Chowla conjecture holds if one averages over the shifts $h_i \le H$ as long as $H \to \infty$ arbitrarily slowly with $x$.
- **Function Fields:** Over $\mathbb{F}_q[t]$ (polynomials over a finite field), the analogue of the Chowla conjecture is a theorem in the large $q$ limit, proven by Carmon and Rudnick (2014).

## 5. Principal Obstacles

The primary barrier to solving the Chowla Conjecture is the **Parity Problem** of sieve theory. Standard sieve methods are famously incapable of distinguishing between integers with an odd number of prime factors and those with an even number. Because the Chowla conjecture is fundamentally a question about evaluating exact parity fluctuations of prime factors, classical sieve theory breaks down.

The recent successes heavily rely on the logarithmic measure $\frac{dn}{n}$, which is scale-invariant. This scale invariance allows researchers to use ergodic techniques, specifically the "entropy decrement argument" pioneered by Tao. However, the natural measure $dn$ lacks this scale invariance. Without it, the entropy argument fails to bound the correlations of the multiplicative functions across varying scales. High-frequency structural anomalies in small intervals cannot be ruled out under the natural density using current methods.

## 6. The Gap

The exact mathematical gap is the transition from **logarithmic density to natural density**. 

We know that:
$$ \frac{1}{\log x} \sum_{n \le x} \frac{\lambda(n)\lambda(n+1)}{n} \to 0 $$

But we do not know if:
$$ \frac{1}{x} \sum_{n \le x} \lambda(n)\lambda(n+1) \to 0 $$

To cross this gap, mathematicians must develop new techniques to bound the "exceptional sets" of intervals where $\lambda(n)$ correlates strongly with $\lambda(n+1)$. While logarithmic averaging suppresses these rare exceptional intervals, natural averaging gives them too much weight. A completely novel method to bound localized correlations of multiplicative functions without relying on scale invariance is required.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

Active research continues at the intersection of analytic number theory and ergodic theory. 
- **Higher-Order Fourier Analysis:** Researchers are attempting to combine Gowers norms and additive combinatorics with the multiplicative structure of integers to bypass the parity problem.
- **Localisation Techniques:** *(frontier — verify)* Recent preprints explore "localized entropy methods" aiming to patch the gap between $1/n$ weights and uniform weights by studying the behavior of $\lambda(n)$ in ultra-short intervals dependent on the prime factorization of shifted parameters.
- **Sarnak's Conjecture:** Significant efforts at institutions like the Institute for Advanced Study (IAS) and Oxford focus on the broader Sarnak Möbius disjointness conjecture for specific dynamic systems (e.g., nilmanifolds), using the logarithmic Chowla results as a springboard.

## 8. Future Work

Leading mathematicians suggest the following pathways:
1. **The 2-Point Unweighted Case:** Proving $\sum_{n \le x} \lambda(n)\lambda(n+1) = o(x)$ is the holy grail of current multiplicative number theory. 
2. **Quantitative Bounds:** Deriving power-saving error terms for the logarithmic versions. Current proofs of the logarithmic Chowla conjecture provide extremely poor (inverse Ackermann type) decay rates.
3. **Elliott-Halberstam Connections:** Finding direct implications between bounded gaps in primes (using the Maynard-Tao framework) and correlation bounds of the Liouville function.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

To understand the problem practically, let us look at the 2-point conjecture ($k=2, h_1=0, h_2=1$). The conjecture demands that $\sum_{n \le x} \lambda(n)\lambda(n+1)$ exhibits cancellation.

Let us manually calculate the terms for $n$ from $1$ to $9$:

First, evaluate $\lambda(n) = (-1)^{\Omega(n)}$:
- $\lambda(1) = 1$ (by definition)
- $\lambda(2) = -1$ (1 prime factor)
- $\lambda(3) = -1$ (1 prime factor)
- $\lambda(4) = 1$ ($2^2 \implies 2$ prime factors)
- $\lambda(5) = -1$ (1 prime factor)
- $\lambda(6) = 1$ ($2 \times 3 \implies 2$ prime factors)
- $\lambda(7) = -1$ (1 prime factor)
- $\lambda(8) = -1$ ($2^3 \implies 3$ prime factors)
- $\lambda(9) = 1$ ($3^2 \implies 2$ prime factors)
- $\lambda(10) = 1$ ($2 \times 5 \implies 2$ prime factors)

Now, evaluate the product sequence $a_n = \lambda(n)\lambda(n+1)$:
- $n=1: 1 \times (-1) = -1$
- $n=2: (-1) \times (-1) = 1$
- $n=3: (-1) \times 1 = -1$
- $n=4: 1 \times (-1) = -1$
- $n=5: (-1) \times 1 = -1$
- $n=6: 1 \times (-1) = -1$
- $n=7: (-1) \times (-1) = 1$
- $n=8: (-1) \times 1 = -1$
- $n=9: 1 \times 1 = 1$

The sum of $a_n$ up to $x=9$ is:
$(-1) + 1 + (-1) + (-1) + (-1) + (-1) + 1 + (-1) + 1 = -3$

The sequence of products $a_n$ appears to be a random sequence of $+1$ and $-1$. The Chowla conjecture essentially states that if you flip a coin to decide if a number has an even or odd number of prime factors, the parity of the current number tells you absolutely nothing about the parity of the next number. Therefore, summing these "random" $+1$ and $-1$ variables up to large $x$ will result in a sum that grows strictly slower than $x$ (it is $o(x)$), confirming the absence of local correlations.
