# Brumer-Stark Conjecture

> **Topic:** Number Theory · **ID:** `01-number-theory/brumer-stark-conjecture` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

The Brumer-Stark Conjecture is a profound statement in algebraic number theory that generalizes Stickelberger’s Theorem to arbitrary abelian extensions of number fields. It posits a deep relationship between the special values of Artin $L$-functions at $s=0$ and the structure of the ideal class group of a number field as a Galois module.

Specifically, let $k$ be a totally real number field and $K$ be a CM-field such that $K/k$ is a finite abelian extension with Galois group $G$. Let $S$ be a finite set of places of $k$ containing all archimedean places and all finite places that ramify in $K$. From the $S$-imprimitive partial zeta functions of the extension, one can construct a formal sum called the **Stickelberger element** $\Theta_{S} \in \mathbb{Q}[G]$. 

Let $W_K$ be the number of roots of unity in $K$. The Brumer-Stark conjecture asserts two claims:
1. **Integrality (Deligne-Ribet):** The element $W_K \Theta_{S}$ belongs to the integral group ring $\mathbb{Z}[G]$.
2. **Annihilation and Kummer Condition:** For every fractional ideal $\mathfrak{a}$ of $K$, the ideal $\mathfrak{a}^{W_K \Theta_{S}}$ is principal. Furthermore, it is generated by an element $\alpha \in K^\times$ (called a *Stark unit* or *anti-unit*) that satisfies $|\alpha|_v = 1$ for all archimedean places $v$ of $K$, and the field extension $K(\alpha^{1/W_K})/k$ is an abelian extension.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

The conjecture relies on the rigorous formulation of partial zeta functions and Galois group rings. 

Let $k$ be a totally real number field (every embedding $k \hookrightarrow \mathbb{C}$ lands in $\mathbb{R}$) and $K$ be a CM-field (a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real field). Let $G = \text{Gal}(K/k)$ be abelian.

Let $S$ be a finite set of places of $k$ containing $S_\infty$ (the infinite places) and $S_{\text{ram}}$ (the places that ramify in $K$). For any $\sigma \in G$, the **$S$-imprimitive partial zeta function** is defined for $\text{Re}(s) > 1$ by the Dirichlet series:

$$ \zeta_S(s, \sigma) = \sum_{\substack{\mathfrak{a} \subset \mathcal{O}_k \\ (\mathfrak{a}, S) = 1 \\ (\mathfrak{a}, K/k) = \sigma}} \mathbf{N}(\mathfrak{a})^{-s} $$

where the sum runs over all integral ideals $\mathfrak{a}$ of $k$ coprime to the places in $S$, $\mathbf{N}(\mathfrak{a})$ is the absolute norm, and $(\mathfrak{a}, K/k)$ is the Artin symbol mapping $\mathfrak{a}$ to an element of $G$.

By the foundational work of Klingen and Siegel, $\zeta_S(s, \sigma)$ admits a meromorphic continuation to the entire complex plane and is analytic at $s=0$. Moreover, $\zeta_S(0, \sigma) \in \mathbb{Q}$.

The **Stickelberger element** $\Theta_{K/k, S}$ is defined as:

$$ \Theta_S = \sum_{\sigma \in G} \zeta_S(0, \sigma^{-1}) \sigma \quad \in \mathbb{Q}[G] $$

Let $C_K$ be the ideal class group of $K$, which carries a natural $\mathbb{Z}[G]$-module structure. The conjecture asserts that if $W_K = |\mu_K|$ is the cardinality of the group of roots of unity in $K$, then $W_K \Theta_S \in \mathbb{Z}[G]$, and for any ideal class $[\mathfrak{a}] \in C_K$:

$$ [\mathfrak{a}]^{W_K \Theta_S} = 1 \text{ in } C_K $$

Meaning $\mathfrak{a}^{W_K \Theta_S} = (\alpha)$ for some $\alpha \in K$. The additional **Kummer condition** requires that $K(\sqrt[W_K]{\alpha})$ is an abelian extension of $k$, which places stringent restrictions on the arithmetic nature of the generator $\alpha$.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

The origin of the conjecture dates back to 1890 when Ludwig Stickelberger proved the theorem for the cyclotomic extensions $K = \mathbb{Q}(\mu_m)$ over $k = \mathbb{Q}$, showing that a specific element in the group ring constructed from fractional parts of rational numbers annihilates the class group of the cyclotomic field.

In the 1970s, Harold Stark formulated a series of sweeping conjectures regarding the leading terms of the Taylor expansions of Artin $L$-functions at $s=0$. Inspired by Stark's work, Armand Brumer (1979) posited the Brumer-Stark conjecture as a strict class-group annihilator statement for abelian extensions. 

The Integrality part ($W_K \Theta_S \in \mathbb{Z}[G]$) was proven independently by Deligne and Ribet (using $p$-adic modular forms) and Cassou-Noguès (using Shintani zeta functions) in 1979.

The annihilation and Kummer conditions were fiercely attacked via Iwasawa theory. Andrew Wiles (1990) resolved the $p$-part of the conjecture for totally real fields for all odd primes $p$, assuming the Iwasawa $\mu$-invariant vanishes. 

The monumental State of the Art (SOTA) breakthrough occurred when **Samit Dasgupta and Mahesh Kakde (2023)** completely and unconditionally proved the Brumer-Stark conjecture for all abelian extensions. Consequently, the classical, abelian rank-0 Brumer-Stark conjecture is considered solved. However, the problem program is marked as **partially-solved** globally because the profound generalizations—specifically the **Equivariant Brumer-Stark Conjecture** (for non-abelian extensions) and the **Rubin-Stark Conjecture** (for arbitrary ranks of $L$-function vanishing)—remain open and represent the active frontier.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

The classical abelian formulation is fully solved, but looking at the broader structural problem, the verified cases are:

- **Absolute abelian fields ($k = \mathbb{Q}$):** Proven by Stickelberger (1890).
- **Function Fields:** Proven unconditionally by David Hayes in the 1980s using Drinfeld modules.
- **Quadratic Extensions:** Solved for relative quadratic extensions $K/k$ where $k$ is totally real (Tate).
- **The $p$-odd part:** Proven by Wiles (1990) under the $\mu=0$ assumption, and advanced unconditionally by Greither, Popescu, and Kurihara using the Equivariant Main Conjecture of Iwasawa Theory.
- **The Classical Abelian Case:** Fully solved by Dasgupta and Kakde (Annals of Mathematics, 2023) by constructing a highly ingenious exact sequence and utilizing $p$-adic integrals and Ribet's method on generalized modular varieties.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

With the abelian case resolved, the principal obstacles apply to the **Non-Abelian (Equivariant) Brumer-Stark Conjecture** and higher-rank Stark Conjectures:

1. **Zero Divisors in Non-Abelian Group Rings:** For a non-abelian Galois group $G$, the group ring $\mathbb{Z}[G]$ has a highly complex ideal structure with zero divisors. The straightforward notion of an "annihilator ideal" is insufficient. Instead, one must work with the Grothendieck group of finitely generated projective $\mathbb{Z}[G]$-modules, or derived categories.
2. **Trivial Stickelberger Elements:** When the $L$-function $L_S(s, \chi)$ vanishes to order $r \ge 2$ at $s=0$, the Stickelberger element evaluating at $s=0$ trivially vanishes ($\Theta_S = 0$). To obtain non-trivial information, one must use the leading coefficient of the Taylor expansion (the Rubin-Stark element). Proving the integrality and annihilation properties of these higher-rank elements is exponentially more difficult, as there is no analogue of Deligne-Ribet's proof for $r \ge 2$.
3. **Explicit Construction (Hilbert's 12th Problem):** The Brumer-Stark conjecture proves the *existence* of the Stark unit $\alpha$, but does not provide an algorithm to systematically construct or compute it for an arbitrary totally real field.

## 6. The Gap

The exact boundary between the solved and unsolved lies at the transition from commutative algebra to non-commutative algebra, and from order of vanishing $r=0, 1$ to $r \ge 2$. 

To fully resolve the non-abelian Brumer-Stark conjecture, one must cross the barrier of the **Equivariant Tamagawa Number Conjecture (ETNC)**. The ETNC formulates the special values of $L$-functions as generators of specific determinant modules in derived categories of motives. The gap requires unconditionally proving the ETNC for the Tate motive $h^0(\text{Spec}(K))(1)$ over non-abelian group rings $\mathbb{Z}[G]$, particularly dealing with the notoriously difficult primes $p$ dividing the order of the non-abelian group $G$.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

Active research on the frontier is currently localized in non-commutative Iwasawa theory and higher-rank Stark conjectures:

- **Non-Commutative Iwasawa Theory:** Schools led by Coates, Fukaya, Kato, Sujatha, and Venjakob are working to lift the Dasgupta-Kakde exact sequences to non-commutative $p$-adic Lie extensions.
- **Derived Tamagawa Number Conjectures:** Researchers like Burns, Kurihara, and Sano are utilizing derived categorical tools to construct a generalized "Brumer-Stark element" that lives in the relative $K_0$-group of $\mathbb{Z}[G]$.
- *(frontier — verify)* Recent preprints attempt to leverage Dasgupta-Kakde’s Eisenstein congruences on Hilbert modular varieties and lift them to the non-abelian setting via automorphic representations on $GL_n(F)$, providing a pathway to the Equivariant Brumer-Stark conjecture.

## 8. Future Work

Leading mathematicians suggest the following open pathways:
- **Algorithmic Stark Units:** Developing a computationally effective framework for extracting the Stark unit $\alpha$ for totally real fields of degree $\ge 3$, which would serve as a major step toward resolving Hilbert's 12th Problem (explicit class field theory for arbitrary number fields).
- **The Gross-Stark Conjecture Variations:** Extending the $p$-adic analytic constructions used by Dasgupta to study the derivatives of $p$-adic $L$-functions and verifying higher-rank Gross-Stark variants.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Consider the classic setup that reduces to Stickelberger's Theorem. Let $k = \mathbb{Q}$ and $K = \mathbb{Q}(\mu_5)$ where $\mu_5$ is the primitive $5$-th root of unity. 
The Galois group $G = \text{Gal}(K/k) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z})^\times = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$.
Let $\sigma_a \in G$ denote the automorphism mapping $\zeta \mapsto \zeta^a$. 

Let $S = \{\infty, 5\}$. The imprimitive partial zeta function evaluated at $s=0$ for $\mathbb{Q}$ yields the fractional part formula:
$$ \zeta_S(0, \sigma_a) = \frac{1}{2} - \left\{ \frac{a}{5} \right\} = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{a}{5} $$

Calculating this for each element in $G$:
- For $a=1$: $\zeta_S(0, \sigma_1) = 1/2 - 1/5 = 3/10$
- For $a=2$: $\zeta_S(0, \sigma_2) = 1/2 - 2/5 = 1/10$
- For $a=3$: $\zeta_S(0, \sigma_3) = 1/2 - 3/5 = -1/10$
- For $a=4$: $\zeta_S(0, \sigma_4) = 1/2 - 4/5 = -3/10$

The Stickelberger element is constructed using $\sigma_a^{-1}$. Note that modulo 5, $1^{-1} = 1$, $2^{-1} = 3$, $3^{-1} = 2$, and $4^{-1} = 4$.
$$ \Theta_S = \frac{3}{10}\sigma_1 + \frac{1}{10}\sigma_3 - \frac{1}{10}\sigma_2 - \frac{3}{10}\sigma_4 $$

The number of roots of unity in $K = \mathbb{Q}(\mu_5)$ is $W_K = 10$ (since $\pm \mu_5$ generates 10 roots). 
Multiplying the Stickelberger element by $W_K$:
$$ W_K \Theta_S = 10 \left( \frac{3}{10}\sigma_1 + \frac{1}{10}\sigma_3 - \frac{1}{10}\sigma_2 - \frac{3}{10}\sigma_4 \right) = 3\sigma_1 + 1\sigma_3 - 1\sigma_2 - 3\sigma_4 $$

Observe that the coefficients $\{3, 1, -1, -3\}$ are all integers, perfectly demonstrating the Integrality claim: **$W_K \Theta_S \in \mathbb{Z}[G]$**. 

In this specific case, the ideal class group of $\mathbb{Q}(\mu_5)$ is trivial ($h=1$), so the annihilation property ($[\mathfrak{a}]^{W_K \Theta_S} = 1$) is trivially true. However, it guarantees that for any ideal $\mathfrak{a}$, $\mathfrak{a}^{W_K\Theta_S}$ is generated by an explicit unit $\alpha$ that satisfies the deep Kummer condition, linking these algebraic values to class field theory.
