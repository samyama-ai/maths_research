# Dynamical Mordell-Lang Conjecture

> **Topic:** Number Theory · **ID:** `01-number-theory/dynamical-mordell-lang-conjecture` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $X$ be a quasiprojective variety over a field $K$ of characteristic $0$, let $\Phi : X \to X$ be a morphism, let $\alpha \in X(K)$, and let $V \subseteq X$ be a closed subvariety. Consider the **return set**

$$S = \{\, n \in \mathbb{N} : \Phi^n(\alpha) \in V(K) \,\}.$$

**Conjecture (Dynamical Mordell–Lang, DML).** $S$ is a finite union of arithmetic progressions, where a single point (an AP of common difference $0$) is allowed.

Equivalently: the orbit $\mathcal{O}_\Phi(\alpha) = \{\Phi^n(\alpha)\}_{n \ge 0}$ meets $V$ in a set that is, up to a finite exceptional set, exactly the union of the intersections with $V$ of finitely many *periodic* sub-orbits. A complete proof must establish this for all $(X,\Phi,\alpha,V)$ as above; a disproof requires one quadruple whose return set is not eventually periodic. Reduction to $K = \overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ or to a finitely generated field is standard (spreading out), so the conjecture is arithmetic in nature even though its statement is geometric.

Two boundary conditions are essential. **Characteristic $0$**: in characteristic $p$ the statement is false as written (Lech's example, §4). **$\Phi$ a morphism, not a rational map**: for rational self-maps the orbit may fail to be well-defined, and the naive extension is not expected to hold without an additional "no indeterminacy along the orbit" hypothesis.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Linear case.** For $X = \mathbb{G}_m^N$ (or any semiabelian variety $G$) and $\Phi$ a group endomorphism, DML follows from the classical **Mordell–Lang theorem** of Faltings, Vojta and Laurent: if $V \subseteq G$ is a closed subvariety and $\Gamma \le G(K)$ a finitely generated subgroup, then

$$V(K) \cap \Gamma = \bigcup_{i=1}^{r} \left( \gamma_i + H_i(K) \cap \Gamma \right)$$

for finitely many $\gamma_i \in \Gamma$ and algebraic subgroups $H_i \subseteq G$ with $\gamma_i + H_i \subseteq V$. Taking $\Gamma$ the group generated by $\alpha$ and its images gives eventual periodicity of $S$.

**Skolem–Mahler–Lech (SML).** The one-dimensional linear-recurrence shadow of DML: if $(a_n)_{n\ge0}$ satisfies a linear recurrence over a field of characteristic $0$, then $\{n : a_n = 0\}$ is a finite union of arithmetic progressions. Writing $a_n = \mathbf{c}^{\mathsf T} A^n \mathbf{v}$, this is DML for $X = \mathbb{A}^N$, $\Phi = A$ linear, $V = \{\mathbf{c}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{x} = 0\}$.

**The $p$-adic arc method.** The dominant technique. One embeds the relevant finitely generated ring into $\mathbb{Z}_p$ for a suitable prime $p$ of good reduction and seeks an integer $N \ge 1$ and, for each residue $r \in \{0,\dots,N-1\}$, a **$p$-adic analytic arc**

$$\theta_r : \mathbb{Z}_p \longrightarrow X(\mathbb{Q}_p), \qquad \theta_r(m) = \Phi^{Nm+r}(\alpha),$$

i.e. an interpolation of the arithmetic subsequence by a convergent power series. Given such an arc, if $V$ is cut out by $f$, then $g_r(m) = f(\theta_r(m)) \in \mathbb{Q}_p\langle m\rangle$ is analytic, and **Strassmann's theorem** applies: a nonzero power series $\sum_{k} c_k m^k$ with $c_k \to 0$ has at most $N_0$ zeros in $\mathbb{Z}_p$, where $N_0$ is the largest index with $|c_{N_0}| = \max_k |c_k|$. So $g_r$ either vanishes identically (giving a full AP $r + N\mathbb{Z}$) or has finitely many zeros. Summing over $r$ yields exactly the DML conclusion.

**Existence of arcs.** If $\Phi$ is étale at the points of the orbit closure and $p$ is a prime of good reduction with $p > 2$, then $\Phi$ acts on a residue disc $\mathfrak{D} \cong \mathfrak{m}_p^{\dim X}$ and some iterate $\Phi^N$ restricted to $\mathfrak{D}$ is "close to the identity" in the $p$-adic sense, hence embeds in a one-parameter analytic flow $\Phi^{Nm} = \exp(m \log \Phi^N)$. This is the mechanism behind Bell's theorem and its descendants.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1934–1953.** Skolem, Mahler and Lech successively prove SML, over $\mathbb{Q}$, over algebraic number fields, and over arbitrary fields of characteristic $0$. Skolem's $p$-adic method is the ancestor of all later work.
- **1960s.** Lech observes that SML fails in characteristic $p$.
- **1980s–90s.** Faltings and Vojta prove Mordell–Lang for abelian varieties; Laurent and McQuillan extend to semiabelian varieties.
- **2006.** J. Bell proves a "generalised Skolem–Mahler–Lech theorem for affine varieties": DML for automorphisms of affine varieties in characteristic $0$ (*J. London Math. Soc.*, 2006).
- **2008.** Ghioca, Tucker and Zieve prove DML-type statements for orbits of one-variable polynomials, and formulate the conjecture in its modern generality (*Invent. Math.* 171, 2008).
- **2010.** Bell, Ghioca and Tucker prove DML for **all étale endomorphisms** of quasiprojective varieties in characteristic $0$ (*Amer. J. Math.*, 2010) — the flagship general result.
- **2014–2017.** Xie proves DML for birational polynomial morphisms of $\mathbb{A}^2$ (*Math. Ann.*, 2014) and then, in a long memoir, for **all polynomial endomorphisms of $\mathbb{A}^2_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}$** (*Astérisque* 394, 2017), using valuative trees and adelic intersection theory rather than $p$-adic arcs alone.
- **2016.** Bell–Ghioca–Tucker's monograph *The Dynamical Mordell–Lang Conjecture* (AMS Math. Surveys and Monographs 210) becomes the standard reference.
- **2020s.** Positive-characteristic analogues are formulated with Moosa–Scanlon **$F$-sets** replacing arithmetic progressions and proved for semiabelian varieties (Corvaja–Ghioca–Scanlon–Zannier).

No general-dimension unconditional proof exists; the conjecture is open already for endomorphisms of $\mathbb{A}^3$ and for $(\mathbb{P}^1)^3$ in full generality.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Class | Status | Reference |

| Group endomorphisms of semiabelian varieties, char $0$ | Proved (from Mordell–Lang) | Faltings/Vojta/Laurent |
| Linear self-maps of $\mathbb{A}^N$ (all $N$), i.e. SML | Proved 1935–1953 | Skolem, Mahler, Lech |
| Automorphisms of affine varieties, char $0$ | Proved | Bell 2006 |
| Étale endomorphisms of quasiprojective $X$, any $\dim X$, char $0$ | Proved | Bell–Ghioca–Tucker 2010 |
| Split maps $(f_1,\dots,f_N)$ on $(\mathbb{P}^1)^N$ with $V$ a curve | Proved | Ghioca–Tucker–Zieve 2008, 2012 |
| Polynomial endomorphisms of $\mathbb{A}^2_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}$, $\dim = 2$ | Proved | Xie 2017 |
| Birational self-maps of projective surfaces (many cases) | Proved | Xie 2015 |
| Endomorphisms of $\mathbb{A}^N$ with no periodic critical points at $\alpha$'s orbit, plus a density-of-good-primes input | Proved | Benedetto–Ghioca–Kurlberg–Tucker 2012 |
| Char $p$, arbitrary $\Phi$, "AP" conclusion | **False** | Lech's example |

Lech's counterexample in characteristic $p$: over $\mathbb{F}_p(t)$, the sequence $a_n = (1+t)^n - t^n - 1$ vanishes exactly for $n \in \{0\} \cup \{p^k : k \ge 0\}$, which is not a finite union of arithmetic progressions. The correct characteristic-$p$ statement replaces APs with $F$-sets — finite unions of sets built from sums of geometric progressions in $p$ — and is a theorem for semiabelian varieties.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Ramification kills the arc.** The $p$-adic interpolation $\Phi^{Nm} = \exp(m\log \Phi^N)$ requires $\Phi$ to be locally invertible on the relevant residue disc. At a critical point the derivative degenerates, iterates contract discs at a rate that outruns the convergence radius of $\log$, and no analytic arc through the orbit exists. Every unconditional higher-dimensional theorem to date is either étale, or two-dimensional, or reducible to a group.
- **Reduction data is uncontrolled.** Even for étale-off-a-divisor maps, one needs a prime $p$ of good reduction at which the orbit avoids the critical locus modulo $p$. Proving that such $p$ exist in positive density is itself an open arithmetic problem (it is where the Benedetto–Ghioca–Kurlberg–Tucker result inserts a hypothesis).
- **No Mordell–Lang to borrow.** For a general $\Phi$ the orbit is not contained in a finitely generated group, so Faltings–Vojta is unavailable. There is no known height inequality forcing $\Phi^n(\alpha) \in V$ to be rare: canonical heights along orbits grow like $\lambda^n$, but $V$ can contain points of every height.
- **Failure of induction on dimension.** $V$ need not be $\Phi$-invariant, and the standard trick of replacing $X$ by the Zariski closure of the orbit gives a variety on which $\Phi$ is only a dominant rational map — precisely the setting where the conjecture is not expected in naive form.
- **Xie's method does not lift.** The $\mathbb{A}^2$ proof uses valuative trees at infinity, local intersection theory of curve germs, and a classification of surface dynamics; these have no $n$-dimensional analogue.

## 6. The Gap

The proven region is: (i) étale maps, any dimension; (ii) arbitrary maps, dimension $\le 2$ over $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$; (iii) group-theoretic maps. The gap is exactly **non-étale endomorphisms in dimension $\ge 3$**, and, inside dimension $2$, non-polynomial endomorphisms over general characteristic-$0$ fields. The precise missing step: given $\Phi : \mathbb{A}^3 \to \mathbb{A}^3$ whose orbit repeatedly meets the ramification divisor $R = \{\det D\Phi = 0\}$, produce either a $p$-adic analytic parametrization of some subsequence $\Phi^{Nm+r}(\alpha)$, or an alternative finiteness mechanism (height, equidistribution, or arithmetic-geometric intersection) that bounds $|S \cap [0,T]|$ sublinearly unless a periodic sub-orbit lies in $V$. A single unconditional theorem — "for every $\Phi$ and every $\alpha$ there exist infinitely many primes $p$ and an integer $N$ such that $\Phi^N$ is analytically conjugate to a flow on the residue disc of $\alpha$" — would essentially close the conjecture.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Xie's programme (Beijing/CNRS).** Extending the surface techniques via adelic curves and the recently developed theory of algebraic dynamics on Berkovich spaces; also connected to the Zariski-dense-orbit (Zhang) conjecture, for which Xie proved the projective-surface case (*J. Amer. Math. Soc.*, 2022).
- **Bell–Ghioca–Satriano (Waterloo/UBC).** Uniform-boundedness refinements: bounding the number of $n \le T$ with $\Phi^n(\alpha) \in V$ in terms of degrees only, and "gap"-type results on orbit growth. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Positive characteristic / $F$-sets.** Moosa–Scanlon $F$-set formalism, extended beyond semiabelian varieties to Drinfeld modules and additive polynomials; model-theoretic input from difference-field theory (Chatzidakis–Hrushovski).
- **Model theory.** Attempts to derive DML from a suitable "$\sigma$-variety" trichotomy in ACFA; this recovers the semiabelian and isotrivial cases but not the general ramified case. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Quantitative $p$-adic analysis.** Sharper Strassmann-type bounds and effective versions giving explicit bounds on the exceptional finite part of $S$ for étale maps.

## 8. Future Work

1. **Weaken étale to "étale along the orbit for infinitely many primes."** Prove the density statement for good primes conjectured in Benedetto–Ghioca–Kurlberg–Tucker; this alone yields DML for large classes of $\mathbb{A}^N$-endomorphisms.
2. **Attack $(\mathbb{P}^1)^3$ with split maps and $V$ a surface** — the smallest case Ghioca–Tucker–Zieve techniques do not cover.
3. **Height-theoretic route:** develop an arithmetic-intersection inequality on adelic/Berkovich models producing $|S\cap[0,T]| = o(T)$ for non-preperiodic $\alpha$, then bootstrap to eventual periodicity.
4. **Uniform DML:** bound the number of arithmetic progressions and the size of the exceptional set in terms of $\deg\Phi$, $\deg V$, $\dim X$.
5. **Unify with unlikely intersections:** relate DML to Zilber–Pink-type statements for dynamical systems, where $V$ is replaced by a family of subvarieties.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Setup.** $X = \mathbb{A}^2_{\mathbb{Q}}$, $\Phi(x,y) = (2x,\,3y)$, $\alpha = (1,1)$, $V = \{x + y = 2\}$. Then $\Phi^n(\alpha) = (2^n, 3^n)$ and

$$S = \{ n \ge 0 : 2^n + 3^n = 2 \}.$$

**Elementary answer.** $2^n+3^n$ is strictly increasing, equals $2$ at $n=0$, so $S = \{0\}$ — a single arithmetic progression of difference $0$. DML holds trivially.

**The same conclusion by the $p$-adic arc method** (this is the technique that generalises). Take $p = 5$; $2$ and $3$ are $5$-adic units. Since $\mathbb{F}_5^\times$ has order $4$, set $N = 4$ and split $n = 4m + r$, $r \in \{0,1,2,3\}$. Then $2^{4}=16$ and $3^{4}=81$ satisfy $|16-1|_5 = |81-1|_5 = 1/5 < 1$, so $\log_5 16$ and $\log_5 81$ converge and

$$\theta_r(m) = \left(2^r \exp(m \log 16),\; 3^r \exp(m\log 81)\right)$$

is a $5$-adic analytic map $\mathbb{Z}_5 \to \mathbb{A}^2(\mathbb{Q}_5)$ interpolating $\Phi^{4m+r}(\alpha)$. Apply $f = x+y-2$:

$$g_r(m) = 2^r e^{m a} + 3^r e^{m b} - 2 = (2^r+3^r-2) + m(2^r a + 3^r b) + \sum_{k\ge2} \frac{2^r a^k + 3^r b^k}{k!} m^k,$$

with $a = \log_5 16$, $b = \log_5 81$, $|a|_5 = |b|_5 = 1/5$.

*Case $r=0$.* Constant term $= 0$. Linear coefficient $a+b = \log_5(16\cdot 81) = \log_5 1296$, and $1296 - 1 = 1295 = 5\cdot 259$ with $5 \nmid 259$, so $|a+b|_5 = 1/5$. For $k \ge 2$, $|(a^k+b^k)/k!|_5 \le 5^{-k}\cdot 5^{(k-1)/4} < 5^{-1}$. Hence the maximum coefficient norm $1/5$ is attained only at $k=1$, and **Strassmann's theorem** gives at most one zero of $g_0$ in $\mathbb{Z}_5$. Since $g_0(0)=0$, that zero is $m=0$: the residue class $r=0$ contributes exactly $n=0$.

*Cases $r=1,2,3$.* $g_r(0) = 2^r+3^r-2 \in \{3, 11, 35\}$, all nonzero, and reduction mod $5$ gives $g_r(m) \equiv 2^r+3^r-2 \pmod 5$ for $r=1,2$, so no integer zeros there; for $r=3$ the same Strassmann count (max norm at $k=1$) allows at most one zero, and $2^3+3^3 = 35 > 2$ excludes it by growth.

**Why the conclusion is "APs" and not "finite".** Modify to $\Phi(x,y) = (2x,\,-2y)$, $\alpha=(1,1)$, $V = \{x = y\}$. Then $\Phi^n(\alpha) = (2^n, (-2)^n)$ lies on $V$ iff $n$ is even, so $S = 2\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}$ is an infinite arithmetic progression. Here $g_0 \equiv 0$ on the residue class $r=0$ — the "identically vanishing" branch of Strassmann — which is exactly how infinite progressions arise, and why the conjecture cannot assert finiteness.
