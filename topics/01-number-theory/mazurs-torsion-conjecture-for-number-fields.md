# Mazur's Torsion Conjecture for Number Fields

> **Topic:** Number Theory · **ID:** `01-number-theory/mazurs-torsion-conjecture-for-number-fields` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Mazur's Torsion Conjecture for Number Fields (also closely related to the Strong Uniform Boundedness Conjecture) concerns the precise classification and absolute bounding of the torsion subgroups of elliptic curves over algebraic number fields.

Let $K$ be a number field of degree $d = [K:\mathbb{Q}]$. For an elliptic curve $E$ defined over $K$, the Mordell-Weil theorem states that the group of $K$-rational points $E(K)$ is finitely generated:
$$ E(K) \cong E(K)_{\text{tors}} \oplus \mathbb{Z}^r $$
where $E(K)_{\text{tors}}$ is a finite abelian group and $r \ge 0$ is the rank.

The general problem breaks down into two major open conjectures:
1. **The Explicit Classification Problem:** For a fixed degree $d \ge 1$, completely determine the finite set $\Phi(d)$ consisting of all isomorphism classes of groups that can appear as $E(K)_{\text{tors}}$ for some elliptic curve $E$ over some number field $K$ of degree $d$. (This is currently open for $d \ge 4$).
2. **The Strong Torsion Conjecture (Polynomial Boundedness):** There exist absolute constants $C > 0$ and $\gamma > 0$ such that for any number field $K$ of degree $d$ and any elliptic curve $E/K$, the order of the torsion subgroup is polynomially bounded by $d$:
$$ |E(K)_{\text{tors}}| \le C \cdot d^\gamma $$

While Loïc Merel proved the weak Uniform Boundedness Theorem (that $\Phi(d)$ is finite for any $d$ and thus bounded by *some* constant $B(d)$), Merel's bound and subsequent improvements are exponential in $d$. The polynomial bound and the explicit computation of $\Phi(d)$ for larger degrees remain profoundly open.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

The problem lies at the intersection of arithmetic geometry, algebraic number theory, and the theory of modular curves.

An elliptic curve $E/K$ is defined by a Weierstrass equation:
$$ E: y^2 + a_1xy + a_3y = x^3 + a_2x^2 + a_4x + a_6 $$
with $a_i \in K$ and non-zero discriminant $\Delta$.

The algebraic structure of the torsion subgroup is known to be generated by at most two elements over the algebraic closure $\overline{K}$:
$$ E(\overline{K})_{\text{tors}} \cong \bigoplus_{m=1}^{\infty} (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}) $$
Over a specific number field $K$, the rational torsion subgroup must take the form:
$$ E(K)_{\text{tors}} \cong \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/mn\mathbb{Z} $$
for some positive integers $m, n$.

The existence of a point of exact order $N$ on $E/K$ corresponds to a $K$-rational point on the modular curve $Y_1(N)$, which is the affine curve whose complex points parameterize isomorphism classes of pairs $(E, P)$ where $E$ is an elliptic curve and $P$ is a point of exact order $N$.
The compactified modular curve $X_1(N)$ is a projective algebraic curve defined over $\mathbb{Q}$.
To classify torsion over a field of degree $d$, one must find all $N$ such that $X_1(N)$ has a point of degree $d$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ that is not a cusp. This is intrinsically tied to the gonality (the minimal degree of a non-constant morphism to $\mathbb{P}^1$) of $X_1(N)$. If the $\mathbb{Q}$-gonality of $X_1(N)$ is strictly greater than $d$, then $X_1(N)$ cannot have infinitely many degree $d$ points.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

The history of this problem is punctuated by landmark results in arithmetic geometry:
- **1977 (Mazur):** Barry Mazur proved the conjecture for $d=1$ (over $\mathbb{Q}$), establishing that $\Phi(1)$ consists of exactly 15 groups. This involved a deep study of the Eisenstein ideal of the Hecke algebra acting on the Jacobian $J_0(N)$.
- **1992 (Kamienny, Kenku, Momose):** Extended Mazur's techniques to quadratic fields ($d=2$), proving that $\Phi(2)$ consists of exactly 26 groups.
- **1996 (Merel):** Loïc Merel proved the Uniform Boundedness Conjecture, showing that for all $d$, $\Phi(d)$ is finite. He achieved this by utilizing the winding quotient of $J_0(N)$ and formal immersions, proving a bound $p \le d^{3v_2(d)+...}$.
- **1999 (Parent):** Pierre Parent made the bound explicit for the maximal prime $p$ dividing the order of a torsion point: $p \le (1+3^{d/2})^2$.
- **2021 (Derickx, Etropolski, van Hoeij, Morrow, Zureick-Brown):** Classified $\Phi(3)$ (cubic fields), finding exactly 26 groups (remarkably, the same number as quadratic fields, though slightly different groups).

The State of the Art centers on bounds for the highest prime $p$ and highest prime power $p^k$ that can divide $|E(K)_{\text{tors}}|$. For $d=4, 5, 6, 7$, the explicit lists of possible torsion subgroups are partially computed but lack unconditional completeness due to theoretical limits in bounding points on specific high-genus curves.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

The conjecture for the explicit classification is solved completely for degrees $d=1, 2, 3$:

- **$d = 1$:** $\Phi(1)$ consists of 15 groups:
  - $\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}$ for $1 \le N \le 10$, and $N = 12$
  - $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/2N\mathbb{Z}$ for $1 \le N \le 4$
- **$d = 2$:** $\Phi(2)$ consists of the 15 groups in $\Phi(1)$, plus 11 others:
  - $\mathbb{Z}/11\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/14\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/15\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/16\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/18\mathbb{Z}$
  - $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$
  - $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$
  - $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$
- **$d = 3$:** $\Phi(3)$ consists of the 15 groups in $\Phi(1)$, plus 11 others:
  - $\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/14\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/16\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/18\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/21\mathbb{Z}$
  - $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/14\mathbb{Z}$
  - $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$, $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}$
  - $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$

For the **Strong Torsion Conjecture**, a polynomial bound has been proven strictly for elliptic curves with Complex Multiplication (CM) by Clark et al. (2014), where the torsion is bounded by $O(d \log \log d)$.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

The fundamental obstacle to classifying $\Phi(d)$ for larger $d$ is that one must find all rational points on an enormous number of curves of high genus. By Faltings' Theorem, a curve of genus $g \ge 2$ has finitely many rational points. However, Faltings' proof is famously ineffective; it does not provide an algorithm to compute these points or even bound their size.
While the Method of Chabauty-Coleman (and its non-abelian extensions) allows one to find rational points on curves when the rank of the Jacobian is less than the genus ($r < g$), this condition often fails for the necessary modular curves $X_1(N)$ as $N$ grows.

For the polynomial bound (Strong Torsion), Merel's technique fundamentally relies on bounding the prime $p$ such that $X_1(p)$ possesses a point of degree $d$. His use of the winding quotient and formal immersions inherently introduces a factorial or exponential dependence on $d$ when relating the Hecke operators to the degree of the field. A drastically new theoretical tool, potentially bypassing the winding quotient entirely or utilizing a deeper arithmetic intersection theory on the moduli stack of elliptic curves, is required to achieve a polynomial bound.

## 6. The Gap

The boundary between current knowledge and the full resolution of the conjecture lies in:
1. **Effective Arithmetic Geometry:** Extending the classification from $d=3$ to $d=4$ requires mapping out the low-degree points of specific high-genus modular curves like $X_1(17)$ or $X_1(19)$ over quartic fields. The gap here is computational and relies on developing effective quadratic Chabauty methods and more advanced sieve techniques.
2. **From Exponential to Polynomial:** The gap in the Strong Torsion Conjecture is theoretical. We know $|E(K)_{\text{tors}}| \le \exp(C \sqrt{d})$. Bridging the gap to $C \cdot d^\gamma$ necessitates an entirely new perspective on how the degrees of points on modular curves grow asymptotically with their genus.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

Active research in this area is being heavily driven by both theoretical bounds and massive computational paradigms:
- **The Arithmetic of Modular Curves:** Researchers are systematically computing points on $X_1(M, N)$ for higher dimensions to rule out candidate torsion groups.
- **Gonality Bounds:** Improving lower bounds on the gonality of modular curves $X_1(N)$ and $X_0(N)$ over $\mathbb{Q}$ and number fields is a highly active strategy. If the $\mathbb{Q}$-gonality is larger than $d$, sporadic points are still possible, but there are no parameterized families of torsion points.
- **Quadratic and Non-Abelian Chabauty:** The ongoing development of the non-abelian Chabauty method (spearheaded by Minhyong Kim and made explicit by Balakrishnan, Dogra, and others) is being heavily deployed to compute exact sets of points on modular curves where classical Chabauty fails.
- `*(frontier — verify)*` Recent preprints have claimed explicit classification of $\Phi(4)$ and partial results for $\Phi(5)$ utilizing massive parallel computation of Selmer groups and $p$-adic $L$-functions on modular Jacobians.

## 8. Future Work

Leading mathematicians suggest the following pathways:
- **Complete Classification for $d \le 7$:** Using higher Chabauty methods to completely resolve the sporadic points on modular curves for quartic, quintic, sextic, and septic fields.
- **Abelian Varieties:** Formulating and proving the analog of Merel's theorem for Abelian Varieties of dimension $g > 1$. The general uniform boundedness for abelian surfaces ($g=2$) over $\mathbb{Q}$ remains an intensely sought-after goal.
- **Conditional Proofs:** Exploring whether assuming the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH) or the ABC Conjecture can yield a conditional proof of the Strong Torsion (polynomial) bound.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

To understand how torsion grows when we extend the base field, consider the elliptic curve $E$ defined over $\mathbb{Q}$:
$$ E: y^2 = x^3 + 4 $$
Over $\mathbb{Q}$, we can search for rational points. By the Nagell-Lutz theorem, any torsion point $(x,y)$ must have integer coordinates and $y^2$ divides the discriminant (or $y=0$). The discriminant $\Delta = -27(4)^2 = -432$. The only rational point of finite order is the point at infinity $\mathcal{O}$, so $E(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}} = \{\mathcal{O}\}$.

Now, extend the field to a quadratic field ($d=2$), specifically $K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})$. Let $\omega = \frac{-1 + \sqrt{-3}}{2}$ be a primitive cube root of unity.
Consider the point $P = (0, 2) \in E(K)$.
Let's compute $2P$ using the standard elliptic curve addition formula.
The slope $\lambda$ of the tangent at $P = (x_1, y_1)$ is:
$$ \lambda = \frac{3x_1^2}{2y_1} = \frac{0}{4} = 0 $$
The x-coordinate of $2P$ is:
$$ x_2 = \lambda^2 - 2x_1 = 0 - 0 = 0 $$
The y-coordinate of $2P$ is:
$$ y_2 = \lambda(x_1 - x_2) - y_1 = 0 - 2 = -2 $$
So, $2P = (0, -2)$.
Notice that $P = (0, 2)$ and $2P = (0, -2) = -P$. Therefore, $3P = \mathcal{O}$.
The point $P$ is a torsion point of exact order 3. By moving from a degree 1 field to a degree 2 field, the torsion subgroup grew from trivial to containing a subgroup isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$.

This demonstrates the core idea: adjoining algebraic numbers to the base field naturally realizes the coordinates of the algebraic torsion points (which inherently lie in $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$). The explicit classification problem asks to quantify and classify exactly which finite abelian groups can be "captured" in this way for a fixed degree of extension.
