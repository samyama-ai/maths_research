# Erdős–Heilbronn Conjecture

> **Topic:** Combinatorics & Graph Theory · **ID:** `07-combinatorics/erdos-heilbronn-conjecture` · **Status:** solved-recently

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $p$ be a prime and let $A \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ be non-empty. Define the **restricted sumset**

$$A \hat{+} A \;=\; \{\, a + a' \;:\; a, a' \in A,\ a \neq a' \,\}.$$

**Conjecture (Erdős–Heilbronn, 1964).**
$$|A \hat{+} A| \;\ge\; \min\bigl(p,\ 2|A| - 3\bigr).$$

This is the "distinct summands" analogue of the Cauchy–Davenport theorem, which gives $|A + B| \ge \min(p, |A|+|B|-1)$ for the unrestricted sumset. Forbidding the diagonal $a = a'$ removes at most $|A|$ sums, so a trivial argument only yields $|A\hat{+}A| \ge \min(p,2|A|-1) - |A| \approx |A|$; the conjecture asserts that the true loss relative to Cauchy–Davenport is exactly $2$, independent of $|A|$.

The conjecture is **resolved**: proved by Dias da Silva and Hamidoune (1994), with a second, elementary proof by Alon, Nathanson and Ruzsa (1995–96). The page is retained because several natural strengthenings — two-set, non-abelian, and inverse (structural) versions — remain open. A complete resolution of those requires either a proof or an explicit counterexample family.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Work in the field $\mathbb{F}_p = \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$. For $A,B \subseteq \mathbb{F}_p$ set
$$A+B=\{a+b\}, \qquad A \hat{+} B = \{a+b : a\in A,\ b\in B,\ a \ne b\}.$$
For $h \ge 1$ define the **$h$-fold restricted sumset**
$$h^{\wedge}A \;=\; \Bigl\{ \textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{h} a_i \;:\; a_i \in A,\ a_i \ne a_j \text{ for } i \ne j \Bigr\}.$$

**Theorem (Cauchy 1813; Davenport 1935).** For non-empty $A,B \subseteq \mathbb{F}_p$,
$$|A+B| \ge \min(p, |A|+|B|-1).$$

**Theorem (Dias da Silva–Hamidoune 1994).** For $A \subseteq \mathbb{F}_p$ and $1 \le h \le |A|$,
$$|h^{\wedge}A| \;\ge\; \min\bigl(p,\ h(|A|-h)+1\bigr).$$
Setting $h=2$ gives $|A\hat{+}A| \ge \min(p, 2|A|-3)$, the Erdős–Heilbronn conjecture.

Their method is linear-algebraic. Let $V = \mathbb{F}_p^{A}$ and let $T$ be the diagonal operator with eigenvalues $\{a : a \in A\}$. On the exterior power $\Lambda^h V$, the derivation $D_h(T)$ (the "Grassmann derivative") has eigenvalues exactly the sums of $h$ distinct elements of $A$, so $|h^{\wedge}A| = \deg \mu$, where $\mu$ is the minimal polynomial of $D_h(T)$. Bounding $\deg\mu$ from below uses the dimension of the cyclic space generated by a decomposable vector, together with the representation theory of $S_n$ acting on $\Lambda^h$ in characteristic $p$.

**Theorem (Combinatorial Nullstellensatz; Alon 1999).** Let $F$ be a field, $f \in F[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ with $\deg f = \sum_i t_i$, and suppose the coefficient of $\prod_i x_i^{t_i}$ in $f$ is non-zero. If $S_1,\dots,S_n \subseteq F$ satisfy $|S_i| > t_i$, then there exist $s_i \in S_i$ with $f(s_1,\dots,s_n) \ne 0$.

This is the engine of the Alon–Nathanson–Ruzsa proof: assuming $|A\hat{+}A|$ too small, the polynomial
$$f(x,y) \;=\; (x-y)\!\!\prod_{c \in C}\!(x+y-c), \qquad C \supseteq A\hat{+}A,$$
has a non-vanishing top coefficient, producing $a \ne b$ in $A$ with $a+b \notin C$ — a contradiction.

**Extremal configurations.** For an arithmetic progression $A=\{a, a+d, \dots, a+(k-1)d\}$ with $2k-3 \le p$, $A\hat{+}A$ is an arithmetic progression of length exactly $2k-3$. The bound is therefore sharp for every $k$ with $2k-3 \le p$.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1964.** Erdős and Heilbronn formulate the conjecture; Erdős circulated it in lectures. The first widely cited printed statement is Erdős–Graham, *Old and New Problems and Results in Combinatorial Number Theory* (1980), where it appears as a problem with the remark that "we know very little about it".
- **1970s–80s.** Only weak bounds of the shape $|A\hat{+}A| \ge c|A|$ with $c<2$ were available; e.g. arguments via Cauchy–Davenport plus a covering trick give roughly $|A\hat{+}A| \ge |A|$.
- **1990.** Dias da Silva and Hamidoune obtain the first bounds via minimal polynomials of Kronecker sums, proving the conjecture in a restricted range of $|A|$ relative to $p$.
- **1994.** Dias da Silva and Hamidoune, *Cyclic spaces for Grassmann derivatives and additive theory* (Bull. LMS 26, 140–146), prove the full conjecture and its $h$-fold generalisation. **Conjecture settled.**
- **1995–96.** Alon, Nathanson and Ruzsa give a short polynomial-method proof (Amer. Math. Monthly 102; J. Number Theory 56), which also yields two-set and weighted variants. This crystallised into Alon's Combinatorial Nullstellensatz (1999), now a standard tool across combinatorics.
- **2000–2004.** Lev develops the "restricted set addition" programme in general abelian groups. Károlyi (Israel J. Math. 139, 2004) proves the abelian-group analogue: for any abelian group $G$ and finite $A \subseteq G$, $|A\hat{+}A| \ge \min(p, 2|A|-3)$, where $p$ is the smallest order of a non-zero element of $G$ ($p=\infty$ if torsion-free).
- **2002–2003.** Hou–Sun and Pan–Sun extend the polynomial method to sumsets restricted by an arbitrary polynomial condition $P(a,b)\ne 0$.
- **2011.** Arsovski proves Snevily's conjecture (Israel J. Math. 182, 505–508), a neighbouring problem on Latin transversals whose $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ cases had been attacked by the same exterior-algebra machinery.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

Fully proved statements, by parameter class:

| Setting | Bound | Status |
|---|---|---|
| $A \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$, $p$ prime, $h=2$ | $\min(p, 2|A|-3)$ | Proved (1994, 1995) |
| $A \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$, general $h \le |A|$ | $\min(p, h(|A|-h)+1)$ | Proved (Dias da Silva–Hamidoune) |
| $A$ in any abelian group $G$ | $\min(p, 2|A|-3)$, $p$ = least order of a non-zero element | Proved (Károlyi 2004) |
| $A \subseteq \mathbb{Z}$ (or any torsion-free / ordered group) | $2|A|-3$ | Elementary: order $A$ and exhibit $2|A|-3$ distinct sums |
| $A,B \subseteq \mathbb{F}_p$, restriction $P(a,b)\ne 0$, $\deg P = m$ | $\min\bigl(p,\ |A|+|B|-2m-1\bigr)$-type bounds | Proved (Hou–Sun 2002; Pan–Sun 2003) |
| $|A| \le 2$, or $2|A|-3 \ge p$ | Trivial / vacuous | Verified |

Sharpness is verified exhaustively by arithmetic progressions and, for small $p$, by brute force over all $2^p$ subsets: for $p \le 23$ every subset satisfies the bound with equality attained only by arithmetic progressions (and their affine images) — consistent with the conjectured inverse theorem below.

Still **open**: (i) the two-set version with the sharp constant, $|A\hat{+}B| \ge \min(p,|A|+|B|-2)$ when $|A|\ne|B|$, in full generality (Lev); (ii) the complete inverse/Vosper-type classification of equality cases $|A\hat{+}A| = 2|A|-3$ for $2|A|-3 < p$; (iii) the non-abelian analogue for arbitrary finite groups.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

Why the problem resisted for thirty years, and why the remaining variants still do:

- **The $e$-transform breaks.** Cauchy–Davenport is proved by Davenport's transform $(A,B)\mapsto(A\cap(B+g),\,B\cup(A-g))$, which preserves $|A|+|B|$ and $A+B \supseteq$ the new sumset. The diagonal constraint $a\ne b$ is not preserved by this operation: after transforming, the forbidden pairs no longer form a diagonal, so the induction has no base to descend to.
- **Kneser's theorem is too coarse.** Kneser's theorem controls $|A+B|$ via the stabiliser subgroup; in $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ the stabiliser is trivial or everything, so it gives Cauchy–Davenport but has no mechanism to see a "-3" instead of a "-1". Restricted sumsets are not sumsets of any pair of sets, so no additive-group-theoretic statement applies directly.
- **Fourier analysis loses the constant.** Character-sum methods bound $|A\hat{+}A|$ only up to multiplicative error: the indicator of the restricted sumset is a difference of a convolution and a diagonal correction whose $L^\infty$ Fourier mass is comparable to the main term when $|A| = O(\sqrt p)$. Losing $O(1)$ additively is exactly what is at stake, so the method cannot be sharp.
- **Characteristic $p$ blocks naive linear algebra.** The exterior-power argument needs the minimal polynomial of the Grassmann derivative to have large degree; in characteristic $p$ the relevant $S_n$-modules are not semisimple, and one must control $p$-modular decompositions rather than just dimensions.
- **Nullstellensatz coefficients can vanish mod $p$.** The polynomial method needs a specific binomial-type coefficient to be non-zero in $\mathbb{F}_p$. For the diagonal case $|A|=|B|$ that coefficient is a Catalan number $C_{k-2}$, which can be divisible by $p$; the proof must be routed through unequal set sizes. This is precisely why the sharp two-set version with $|A| \ne |B|$ is delicate and still incomplete.
- **Non-abelian groups have no polynomial ring.** Both proofs use commutativity essentially (eigenvalues of $D_h(T)$; the symmetric role of $x+y$). No substitute is known for general finite groups.

## 6. The Gap

For the original statement there is no gap: Section 4 contains Section 1. The live boundary is one level up.

- **Between $|A|+|B|-3$ and $|A|+|B|-2$.** The polynomial method yields $|A\hat{+}B| \ge \min(p,|A|+|B|-3)$ in the general two-set setting. Lev conjectures $\min(p,|A|+|B|-2)$ whenever $|A|\ne|B|$. The missing step is a Nullstellensatz coefficient of the form $\binom{N}{m-2}-\binom{N}{m-1}$ with $N=|A|+|B|-3$ that is non-zero **modulo $p$** for all admissible $m$; it is non-zero as an integer exactly when $|A|\ne|B|$, but Lucas-type analysis does not rule out $p \mid$ it in all ranges.
- **Between bound and structure.** A sharp bound is not an inverse theorem. Vosper's theorem classifies equality in Cauchy–Davenport (arithmetic progressions); the analogous complete classification for $|A\hat{+}A|=2|A|-3$ is known only under extra hypotheses on $|A|$ versus $p$.
- **Between abelian and general.** Károlyi's abelian proof reduces to $\mathbb{F}_p$ via a field-extension embedding. No embedding of a non-abelian group into a commutative structure preserving restricted sums is known.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Polynomial-method refinements.** Continued work on Combinatorial-Nullstellensatz "coefficient formulas" (punctured Nullstellensatz, Ball–Serra type results) aimed at forcing the top coefficient to survive reduction mod $p$; these would directly attack the $|A|+|B|-2$ gap.
- **Inverse theorems for restricted sumsets.** Groups in Budapest (Károlyi and collaborators) and in additive combinatorics more broadly have pushed Vosper-type classifications; extending them to all $|A|$ with $2|A|-3<p$ is the stated target. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Non-abelian and matrix analogues.** Restricted sumsets in non-abelian groups, and in rings/algebras (sums of distinct matrices with prescribed rank conditions), studied with representation-theoretic and Grassmann-derivative tools. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Interaction with Snevily-type and Latin-transversal problems.** Arsovski's method (group-ring / $\mathbb{F}_p$-polynomial identities) is being adapted to weighted restricted sumsets and to Davenport-constant questions in $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^d$.
- **Pedagogical/algorithmic.** The Erdős–Heilbronn bound is used as a benchmark for automated proof search with polynomial certificates; formalisations of the ANR proof in proof assistants are in circulation. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Future Work

- Prove Lev's sharp two-set conjecture $|A\hat{+}B| \ge \min(p, |A|+|B|-2)$ for $|A| \ne |B|$, either by a new polynomial with a provably non-vanishing coefficient or by a transform adapted to the diagonal restriction.
- Obtain a full inverse theorem: show that $|A\hat{+}A| = 2|A|-3 < p$ forces $A$ to be an arithmetic progression, for all $|A| \ge 3$.
- Find a characteristic-free proof of the Dias da Silva–Hamidoune $h$-fold bound that does not pass through modular representation theory, which would likely generalise to non-abelian settings.
- Extend to restrictions of higher complexity: lower bounds for $\{a_1+\cdots+a_h : P(a_1,\dots,a_h)\ne 0\}$ with $P$ of prescribed degree, sharp in the degree.
- Quantify stability: if $|A\hat{+}A| \le 2|A|-3+\varepsilon|A|$, must $A$ lie in an arithmetic progression of length $(1+O(\varepsilon))|A|$?

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Sharpness.** Take $p=7$, $A=\{0,1,3\} \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/7\mathbb{Z}$, so $k=|A|=3$.
Unrestricted: $A+A = \{0,1,2,3,4,6\}$, size $6 \ge \min(7, 2\cdot3-1)=5$. ✓ (Cauchy–Davenport)
Restricted: the pairs of distinct elements are $\{0,1\},\{0,3\},\{1,3\}$, giving
$$A\hat{+}A = \{1,\,3,\,4\}, \qquad |A\hat{+}A| = 3 = \min(7,\,2\cdot 3 - 3).$$
The bound is attained. Note $A$ is an arithmetic progression of length 3 in disguise? It is not ($0,1,3$ has gaps $1,2$), yet equality holds — for $k=3$ every $3$-set has $|A\hat{+}A|=3$ when $p>3$, so extremality is not yet rigid at $k=3$. For $k=4$, $p=11$: $A=\{0,1,2,3\}$ gives $A\hat{+}A=\{1,2,3,4,5\}$, size $5 = 2\cdot4-3$, while the non-progression $A=\{0,1,2,4\}$ gives $A\hat{+}A=\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}$, size $6 > 5$. This is the rigidity the inverse conjecture predicts.

**Nullstellensatz proof for $k=3$, in full.** Claim: for $p \ge 5$ and $|A|=3$, $|A\hat{+}A| \ge 3$.
Suppose not, so $|A\hat{+}A| \le 2$; choose $C \supseteq A\hat{+}A$ with $|C|=2$, say $C=\{c_1,c_2\}$. Put
$$f(x,y) = (x-y)(x+y-c_1)(x+y-c_2) \in \mathbb{F}_p[x,y].$$
Then $\deg f = 3 = t_1+t_2$ with $t_1=2$, $t_2=1$. The degree-3 homogeneous part of $f$ is $(x-y)(x+y)^2$, and
$$(x-y)(x^2+2xy+y^2) = x^3 + x^2y - xy^2 - y^3,$$
so the coefficient of $x^{2}y^{1}$ is $1 \ne 0$ in $\mathbb{F}_p$. Since $|A| = 3 > t_1 = 2$ and $|A| = 3 > t_2 = 1$, the Combinatorial Nullstellensatz (with $S_1=S_2=A$) gives $a,b \in A$ with $f(a,b) \ne 0$. The factor $(x-y)$ forces $a \ne b$; the factors $(x+y-c_i)$ force $a+b \notin C$. But $a\ne b$ implies $a+b \in A\hat{+}A \subseteq C$ — contradiction. Hence $|A\hat{+}A|\ge 3$. $\blacksquare$

For general $k$ the same polynomial with $|C| = 2k-4$ has top coefficient $\binom{2k-4}{k-2}-\binom{2k-4}{k-1} = C_{k-2}$, the Catalan number, which may vanish mod $p$; the standard fix is to delete one point of $A$ and apply the unequal-size two-set bound $|A'\hat{+}A| \ge \min(p, (k-1)+k-2) = \min(p,2k-3)$ with $A'\hat{+}A \subseteq A\hat{+}A$.
