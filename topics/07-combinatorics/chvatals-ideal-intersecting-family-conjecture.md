# Chvátal's Conjecture on Intersecting Families in Ideals

> **Topic:** Combinatorics & Graph Theory · **ID:** `07-combinatorics/chvatals-ideal-intersecting-family-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $X$ be a finite set and let $\mathcal{F} \subseteq 2^X$ be an **ideal** (also: downset, hereditary family, abstract simplicial complex with the empty set): if $A \in \mathcal{F}$ and $B \subseteq A$ then $B \in \mathcal{F}$. A subfamily $\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ is **intersecting** if $A \cap B \neq \emptyset$ for all $A, B \in \mathcal{G}$. For $x \in X$, the **star** of $x$ in $\mathcal{F}$ is
$$\mathcal{F}(x) \;=\; \{A \in \mathcal{F} : x \in A\},$$
which is always intersecting.

> **Conjecture (Chvátal, 1972/74).** Every ideal $\mathcal{F}$ has a maximum-size intersecting subfamily that is a star. Equivalently,
> $$\max\{|\mathcal{G}| : \mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F} \text{ intersecting}\} \;=\; \max_{x \in X} |\mathcal{F}(x)|.$$

The inequality $\ge$ is trivial; the content is $\le$. The conjecture does **not** claim uniqueness — non-star maxima may coexist (Section 10). A complete proof must handle every finite ideal; a disproof is a single ideal $\mathcal{F}$ with an intersecting $\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ satisfying $|\mathcal{G}| > \max_x |\mathcal{F}(x)|$. Since such a counterexample is a finite object, the problem is decidable for each fixed $|X|$ but the search space (number of ideals on $[n]$, i.e. Dedekind numbers) grows doubly exponentially.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Ground set and notation.** $X = [n] = \{1,\dots,n\}$, $2^{[n]}$ the power set, $\binom{[n]}{k}$ the $k$-uniform slice. $\mathcal{F}$ is generated by its maximal members $\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F})$: $\mathcal{F} = \{A : A \subseteq M \text{ for some } M \in \mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F})\}$. The **rank** of $\mathcal{F}$ is $\max_{A \in \mathcal{F}} |A|$.

**Degree.** $d_{\mathcal{F}}(x) = |\mathcal{F}(x)|$. Write $\nu(\mathcal{F})$ for the maximum size of an intersecting subfamily and $\Delta(\mathcal{F}) = \max_x d_\mathcal{F}(x)$. The conjecture is $\nu(\mathcal{F}) = \Delta(\mathcal{F})$.

**The half bound.** The map $A \mapsto A \setminus \{x\}$ is injective on $\mathcal{F}(x)$ with image in $\mathcal{F} \setminus \mathcal{F}(x)$, so $\Delta(\mathcal{F}) \le |\mathcal{F}|/2$. Dually, an ideal on $[n]$ with $\emptyset \in \mathcal{F}$ admits a partition into pairs of disjoint sets (Berge; Schönheim), giving
$$\nu(\mathcal{F}) \;\le\; \lfloor |\mathcal{F}|/2 \rfloor .$$
This is the only unconditional upper bound of the right order; Chvátal's conjecture asks to replace $|\mathcal{F}|/2$ by the typically much smaller $\Delta(\mathcal{F})$.

**Shifting (compression).** For $i < j$ define $S_{ij} : 2^{[n]} \to 2^{[n]}$ by
$$S_{ij}(A) = \begin{cases}(A \setminus \{j\}) \cup \{i\}, & j \in A,\; i \notin A,\\ A, & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}$$
and $S_{ij}(\mathcal{A}) = \{S_{ij}(A) : A \in \mathcal{A}\} \cup \{A \in \mathcal{A} : S_{ij}(A) \in \mathcal{A}\}$. A family is **left-compressed** (shifted) if $S_{ij}(\mathcal{A}) = \mathcal{A}$ for all $i<j$. Shifting preserves cardinality and the intersecting property, and maps ideals to ideals — but the *ideal* $\mathcal{F}$ and the *subfamily* $\mathcal{G}$ cannot in general be shifted simultaneously, which is the crux (Section 5).

**Measure form.** For $p \in (0,1)$ let $\mu_p(\mathcal{A}) = \sum_{A \in \mathcal{A}} p^{|A|}(1-p)^{n-|A|}$. The $p$-biased analogue asks whether $\max\{\mu_p(\mathcal{G}) : \mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{F} \text{ intersecting}\} = \max_x \mu_p(\mathcal{F}(x))$; $p = 1/2$ recovers the counting statement. Correlation inequalities of Harris–Kleitman type (an ideal and a filter are negatively correlated under $\mu_p$) are the natural tool in this formulation.

**Ambient benchmark.** For $\mathcal{F} = \binom{[n]}{k}$ (not an ideal, but the classical model) the Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem gives $\nu = \binom{n-1}{k-1}$ for $n \ge 2k$, attained by a star. Chvátal's conjecture is the downset analogue of "EKR extremal families are stars", stripped of uniformity.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1972/74 — origin.** Václav Chvátal posed the conjecture at the Ohio State Hypergraph Seminar and published it with the first positive case: it holds for **left-compressed ideals** (*Intersecting families of edges in hypergraphs having the hereditary property*, LNM 411, Springer, 1974).
- **1974 — Kleitman & Magnanti.** Quantitative work on how many subsets an intersecting collection "generates", yielding $|\mathcal{F}| \ge 2|\mathcal{G}|$ type bounds and small-rank verifications (JCTA 16).
- **1974–75 — Sterboul, Berge, Schönheim.** Sterboul treated low-rank ideals; Berge and Schönheim independently established the pairing/partition bound $\nu(\mathcal{F}) \le \lfloor|\mathcal{F}|/2\rfloor$ for every ideal, still the sharpest general upper bound.
- **1987 — Frankl's survey** of the shifting technique codifies why compression proofs stop where they do.
- **1992 — Snevily.** *A new result on Chvátal's conjecture* (JCTA 61) weakens Chvátal's full-compression hypothesis to a partial-shifting condition, the largest single enlargement of the verified class since 1974.
- **2009–2011 — Borg.** Extends the theme to $t$-intersecting subfamilies of hereditary families and to cross-intersecting variants, again under compression hypotheses.
- **2018 — Friedgut, Kahn, Kalai & Keller.** *Chvátal's conjecture and correlation inequalities* (JCTA 156, 22–43) reformulates the conjecture as a family of correlation inequalities, proves several special cases, and identifies which natural strengthenings fail. This reframing is the current methodological state of the art.

No counterexample is known; no general proof is known. The conjecture has been open for over 50 years.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Class | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Left-compressed (shifted) ideals | Conjecture true | Chvátal 1974 |
| Ideals stable under partial shifting (weaker than full compression) | Conjecture true | Snevily 1992 |
| Ideals of rank $\le 3$ (all members of size at most 3) | Conjecture true | Sterboul 1974; Kleitman–Magnanti 1974 |
| $\mathcal{F} = 2^{[n]}$ (full power set) | $\nu = 2^{n-1} = \Delta$ | elementary |
| $\mathcal{F}$ generated by one set | $\nu = \Delta = 2^{k-1}$ for $|M|=k$ | elementary |
| $\mathcal{F}$ covered by two stars | Conjecture true | Kleitman–Magnanti 1974 |
| $t$-intersecting analogue, compressed hereditary families | True for compressed case | Borg, *JLMS* 79 (2009) |
| Correlation-inequality special cases, incl. biased-measure ranges | True | Friedgut–Kahn–Kalai–Keller 2018 |
| All ideals on small ground sets $n \le 5$ (exhaustive check over the $\le 7581$ ideals) | True | folklore / routine computation |

General bound valid for all ideals: $\Delta(\mathcal{F}) \le \nu(\mathcal{F}) \le \lfloor|\mathcal{F}|/2\rfloor$. The ratio $\lfloor|\mathcal{F}|/2\rfloor / \Delta(\mathcal{F})$ can be arbitrarily large (take $\mathcal{F}$ generated by $m$ pairwise disjoint pairs: $|\mathcal{F}| = 3^m$… truncated example in Section 10), so the gap is not a constant factor.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

1. **Shifting destroys the subfamily or the ideal.** Compression is the workhorse of extremal set theory, but $S_{ij}$ applied to make $\mathcal{F}$ left-compressed does not act coherently on a fixed intersecting $\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$: shifting $\mathcal{G}$ inside $2^{[n]}$ can push sets out of $\mathcal{F}$. There is no known "simultaneous compression" that preserves $\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$, intersection, and cardinality. Every proof since 1974 buys progress by *assuming* the ideal is already (partly) compressed.
2. **No uniformity, no Katona-type averaging.** Kruskal–Katona, the cycle method and the Ahlswede–Khachatrian machinery all live on uniform slices $\binom{[n]}{k}$. An ideal mixes all ranks, and its degree sequence $(d_\mathcal{F}(x))_x$ can be wildly non-uniform, so slice-by-slice arguments give bounds that sum to $|\mathcal{F}|/2$, not $\Delta(\mathcal{F})$.
3. **Spectral/eigenvalue methods have no host graph.** The Hoffman–Delsarte ratio bound proves EKR on the Kneser graph because that graph is vertex-transitive with known spectrum. The "disjointness graph" of an arbitrary ideal has no symmetry and no controllable spectrum; the ratio bound then reproduces only $|\mathcal{F}|/2$.
4. **Fourier/hypercontractivity needs product structure.** Junta approximation (Friedgut, Keller–Lifshitz) applies to families of prescribed measure in the biased cube; an ideal is an arbitrary monotone-decreasing set of constraints, and conditioning on $\mathcal{F}$ breaks the product measure that hypercontractivity requires.
5. **The correlation reformulation is itself open.** FKKK show the conjecture is implied by a Harris/Kleitman-style correlation statement, but the required inequality is *not* an instance of the FKG lattice condition — the relevant events are not both monotone in the same direction after conditioning on $\mathcal{F}$.
6. **No stability handle.** Because non-star maxima exist (Section 10), one cannot argue "the extremal family is close to a star, then bootstrap"; the extremal set is genuinely non-unique.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: the conjecture for ideals possessing a shifting symmetry (full or partial), for rank $\le 3$, and for structurally thin ideals covered by few stars. Claimed: all ideals.

The exact barrier is a **transfer principle**: given an arbitrary ideal $\mathcal{F}$ and a maximum intersecting $\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$, produce either
(a) an operation $\Phi$ with $\Phi(\mathcal{F})$ left-compressed, $\Phi(\mathcal{G}) \subseteq \Phi(\mathcal{F})$ intersecting, $|\Phi(\mathcal{G})| \ge |\mathcal{G}|$ and $\Delta(\Phi(\mathcal{F})) \le \Delta(\mathcal{F})$; or
(b) a direct injection $\psi : \mathcal{G} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{F}(x)$ for some $x$.
Existence of (b) is exactly the conjecture; the pairing argument delivers only $\psi : \mathcal{G} \hookrightarrow \mathcal{F} \setminus \mathcal{G}$. Closing the gap means replacing the *global* pairing (which knows nothing about any element $x$) by a *localised* injection into a single star. No current technique selects the correct $x$ from $\mathcal{F}$ without a symmetry assumption.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Correlation-inequality programme.** Following Friedgut–Kahn–Kalai–Keller, groups at the Hebrew University, Bar-Ilan and Rutgers pursue conditional Harris–Kleitman inequalities strong enough to imply the conjecture, and search for the minimal correlation statement that is both true and sufficient.
- **Biased-measure attack.** Proving the $\mu_p$ version for all $p \le 1/2$ and all ideals would imply the counting case at $p=1/2$; partial ranges of $p$ are known, and extending the range is an active target. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Junta / spread approximation.** Keller, Lifshitz and collaborators' spread-approximation framework has resolved several long-standing intersection problems in the non-uniform setting; applying it to ideals of bounded rank (rank $4$, $5$) is the most concrete near-term goal. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Computational search.** SAT/ILP encodings of "ideal $\mathcal{F}$ on $[n]$ with $\nu(\mathcal{F}) > \Delta(\mathcal{F})$" have been run for $n \le 7$–$8$ with structural symmetry breaking; no counterexample. Full enumeration is blocked by the Dedekind numbers ($D(8) \approx 5.6 \times 10^{22}$). *(frontier — verify)*
- **Variants.** $t$-intersecting, cross-intersecting and matroid-restricted versions (Borg's line) continue to produce theorems; the $t \ge 2$ analogues are known to require extra hypotheses.

## 8. Future Work

- Design a compression that acts on the *pair* $(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{G})$ — e.g. shifting along a linear order chosen adaptively from the degree sequence of $\mathcal{F}$ — and show it is monotone in $|\mathcal{G}|$.
- Settle rank $4$: the smallest rank for which no proof exists, and a plausible target for finite case analysis plus spread approximation.
- Prove the $\mu_p$ statement for all $p \le 1/2$; Kalai has repeatedly flagged the measure version as the more tractable formulation.
- Establish a *fractional relaxation*: LP/entropy duality bounding $\nu(\mathcal{F})$ by a weighted maximum degree $\max_x \sum_{A \ni x} w_A$, then round.
- Identify the extremal structure: classify all ideals with $\nu(\mathcal{F}) = \lfloor |\mathcal{F}|/2 \rfloor$ (the pairing bound is tight only for very restricted $\mathcal{F}$); a classification would show most ideals have slack and localise the hard cases.
- Counterexample search guided by ideals with flat degree sequences and large rank, where stars are weakest relative to $|\mathcal{F}|/2$.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**(a) Full power set, $n = 3$.** $\mathcal{F} = 2^{[3]}$, $|\mathcal{F}| = 8$. Degrees: $d(1)=d(2)=d(3)=4$, so $\Delta(\mathcal{F}) = 4$. Pairing bound: $\nu \le \lfloor 8/2 \rfloor = 4$. Hence $\nu = 4$ and the conjecture holds, the star
$$\mathcal{F}(1) = \{\{1\},\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{1,2,3\}\}$$
being extremal. Note the **non-star** maximum
$$\mathcal{G}^\ast = \{\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{2,3\},\{1,2,3\}\},$$
also of size $4$ and pairwise intersecting. This is why the conjecture says *some* maximum is a star, not *every* one.

**(b) Two disjoint generators — pairing bound is not tight.** $X = [4]$, $\mathcal{M} = \{\{1,2\},\{3,4\}\}$, so
$$\mathcal{F} = \{\emptyset,\{1\},\{2\},\{3\},\{4\},\{1,2\},\{3,4\}\}, \quad |\mathcal{F}| = 7 .$$
Degrees: $d(x) = 2$ for every $x$, so $\Delta(\mathcal{F}) = 2$. The pairing bound gives only $\nu \le 3$. Direct check: any intersecting $\mathcal{G}$ excludes $\emptyset$; if $\mathcal{G}$ contains a singleton $\{x\}$ then every member contains $x$, giving $|\mathcal{G}| \le d(x) = 2$; if $\mathcal{G}$ contains no singleton then $\mathcal{G} \subseteq \{\{1,2\},\{3,4\}\}$, which is not intersecting, so $|\mathcal{G}| \le 1$. Hence $\nu(\mathcal{F}) = 2 = \Delta(\mathcal{F})$: the conjecture holds, and the slack against $\lfloor|\mathcal{F}|/2\rfloor$ is $1$.

**(c) Slack grows.** Generalise (b) to $m$ disjoint pairs $\{2i-1,2i\}$, $i \le m$. Then $\mathcal{F} = \prod_{i=1}^m \{\emptyset,\{2i-1\},\{2i\},\{2i-1,2i\}\}$ restricted to members of the product ideal has $|\mathcal{F}| = 4^m$ and $\Delta(\mathcal{F}) = 4^m/2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{1}$… concretely $d(x) = 2 \cdot 4^{m-1}$, while $\lfloor|\mathcal{F}|/2\rfloor = 4^m/2 = 2\cdot 4^{m-1}$: here the two bounds coincide and the conjecture is tight. Replacing pairs by triples $\{3i-2,3i-1,3i\}$ gives $|\mathcal{F}| = 8^m$, $d(x) = 4 \cdot 8^{m-1} = |\mathcal{F}|/2$ — still tight. To see genuine slack, take $\mathcal{M} = \{\{1,2\},\{3,4\},\dots,\{2m-1,2m\}\}$ as in (b) with no products: $|\mathcal{F}| = 2m+m+1 = 3m+1$, $\Delta(\mathcal{F}) = 2$, so the pairing bound $\lfloor (3m+1)/2 \rfloor$ overshoots the truth by a factor $\Theta(m)$. Chvátal's conjecture asserts the star value $2$ is correct for every such ideal — verified here by the same singleton case analysis as in (b), but with no general argument available once the generators overlap arbitrarily.

**(d) Where compression enters.** In (a) $\mathcal{F} = 2^{[3]}$ is left-compressed, so Chvátal's 1974 theorem applies directly. In (b) $\mathcal{F}$ is *not* left-compressed: $S_{13}(\{3,4\}) = \{1,4\} \notin \mathcal{F}$. Shifting repairs this by replacing $\mathcal{F}$ with the compressed ideal generated by $\{1,2\},\{1,3\}$ — a *different* family of the same size, with $\Delta = 3$. The verified case therefore says nothing about the original $\mathcal{F}$, illustrating in miniature the transfer failure described in Section 6.
