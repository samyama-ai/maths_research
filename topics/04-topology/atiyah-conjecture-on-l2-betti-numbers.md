# Atiyah Conjecture on L2-Betti Numbers

> **Topic:** Topology & Knot Theory · **ID:** `04-topology/atiyah-conjecture-on-l2-betti-numbers` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Atiyah (1976) introduced $L^2$-Betti numbers $b_i^{(2)}(\widetilde X)$ of a compact manifold or finite CW complex $X$ with fundamental group $G$, as von Neumann dimensions of the reduced $L^2$-homology of the universal cover. They are non-negative reals, not a priori integers. Atiyah asked which values occur.

**Strong Atiyah Conjecture (SAC).** Let $G$ be a countable group and let
$$\mathrm{lcm}(G) := \mathrm{lcm}\{\,|H| : H \le G \text{ finite}\,\} \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}.$$
If $\mathrm{lcm}(G) < \infty$, then for every $A \in M_{m\times n}(\mathbb{Q}G)$,
$$\dim_{\mathcal N(G)} \ker\big(A : \ell^2(G)^n \to \ell^2(G)^m\big) \in \tfrac{1}{\mathrm{lcm}(G)}\,\mathbb{Z}.$$
Equivalently, for every finite free $G$-CW complex $X$, $\mathrm{lcm}(G)\cdot b_i^{(2)}(X;\mathcal N(G)) \in \mathbb{Z}$.

**Torsion-free case.** If $G$ is torsion-free, $\mathrm{lcm}(G)=1$ and the claim is $b_i^{(2)} \in \mathbb{Z}$. This is the case that remains open and is the one of interest in topology.

Variants: the *weak* (rational) form asks only $b_i^{(2)} \in \mathbb{Q}$; the coefficient field may be enlarged from $\mathbb{Q}$ to $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$ or $\mathbb{C}$. A complete resolution means either a proof of SAC for all torsion-free groups (equivalently, for all groups with bounded finite subgroup orders), or a torsion-free counterexample group $G$ and matrix $A \in M_n(\mathbb{Q}G)$ with $\dim_{\mathcal N(G)}\ker A \notin \mathbb{Z}$.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Let $G$ be countable discrete, $\ell^2(G)$ the Hilbert space with orthonormal basis $\{\delta_g\}$. The **group von Neumann algebra** is
$$\mathcal N(G) := \mathcal B(\ell^2 G)^G = \{\text{bounded operators commuting with the right }G\text{-action}\} = \lambda(\mathbb{C}G)'' ,$$
a finite von Neumann algebra with faithful normal trace
$$\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal N(G)}(a) = \langle a\,\delta_e, \delta_e\rangle_{\ell^2 G}, \qquad \mathrm{tr}(1)=1 .$$
For $A = (a_{ij}) \in M_n(\mathcal N(G))$ set $\mathrm{tr}(A) = \sum_i \mathrm{tr}(a_{ii})$. A closed $G$-invariant subspace $V \subseteq \ell^2(G)^n$ has
$$\dim_{\mathcal N(G)} V := \mathrm{tr}\big(p_V\big) \in [0,n],$$
$p_V$ the orthogonal projection. This dimension is additive, monotone, and continuous under increasing unions.

For a finite free $G$-CW complex $X$ with cellular chain complex $C_*(X)$ of free $\mathbb{Z}G$-modules, the $L^2$-chain complex is $C_*^{(2)} = \ell^2(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}G} C_*(X)$, with differentials $c_i$ given by matrices over $\mathbb{Z}G$. Then
$$H_i^{(2)}(X) := \ker c_i / \overline{\mathrm{im}\, c_{i+1}}, \qquad b_i^{(2)}(X) := \dim_{\mathcal N(G)} H_i^{(2)}(X) .$$
Because $\overline{\mathrm{im}\,c_{i+1}}^{\perp} \cap \ker c_i \cong \ker \Delta_i$ with $\Delta_i = c_i^*c_i + c_{i+1}c_{i+1}^*$, all $L^2$-Betti numbers are kernel dimensions of matrices over $\mathbb{Z}G$; conversely every such kernel dimension is realized by a complex. This is why SAC is equivalently a statement about matrices.

Key structural facts:

- **Euler characteristic.** $\sum_i (-1)^i b_i^{(2)}(X) = \chi(X)$ (Atiyah's $L^2$-index theorem).
- **Spectral formulation.** For self-adjoint $A$, $\dim\ker A = \mu_A(\{0\})$, the atom at $0$ of the spectral measure $\mu_A(S) = \mathrm{tr}\,\chi_S(A)$.
- **Lück approximation.** If $G$ is residually finite with a chain $G \rhd G_1 \rhd G_2 \rhd \cdots$, $\bigcap G_n = 1$, and $X$ has a finite model, then $b_i^{(2)}(X) = \lim_{n} b_i(X/G_n)/[G:G_n]$ (Lück 1994). So $b_i^{(2)}$ is a limit of rationals with denominators $[G:G_n]$ — but limits of rationals need not be rational.
- **Division-ring reformulation.** For torsion-free $G$, SAC over a field $K$ holds iff $KG$ embeds in a division ring $\mathcal D$ with $\mathcal D \subseteq \mathcal U(G)$ (the algebra of affiliated operators) such that $\mathcal D$ is division closure of $KG$; then $\dim\ker A = n - \mathrm{rk}_{\mathcal D}(A) \in \mathbb{Z}$. SAC for torsion-free $G$ therefore implies the **Kaplansky zero-divisor conjecture** for $KG$.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1976.** Atiyah, *Elliptic operators, discrete groups and von Neumann algebras*, defines $L^2$-Betti numbers via the $\Gamma$-index theorem and raises the rationality question.
- **1993.** Linnell proves SAC for the class $\mathcal C$ generated by free groups under directed unions and extensions with elementary amenable quotients, assuming a bound on finite subgroup orders — the first substantial positive result, via the division ring $\mathcal D(G)$.
- **2000.** Grigorchuk–Linnell–Schick–Żuk exhibit a closed $7$-manifold whose $L^2$-Betti number is $1/3$ while the fundamental group has only $2$-power torsion: the *strong* form fails for groups with unbounded finite subgroups.
- **2002.** Dicks–Schick compute the full spectral measure of natural elements of $\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}/2 \wr \mathbb{Z}]$; Grigorchuk–Żuk's lamplighter spectrum (2001) is the engine.
- **2010–2015.** Austin produces the first **irrational** $L^2$-Betti number of a finitely generated group. Grabowski shows every non-negative $\Sigma_2$-real (in particular every algebraic and many transcendental numbers) is an $L^2$-Betti number of a f.g. group; Pichot–Schick–Żuk realize transcendental values by closed manifolds. All these groups have unbounded torsion. The **rational** form is thus dead in general; the torsion-free form is untouched by them.
- **2019–2020.** Jaikin-Zapirain's base-change theorem, and Jaikin-Zapirain–López-Álvarez's proof of SAC for **locally indicable** groups (hence all torsion-free one-relator groups) — the current high-water mark.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

SAC is a **theorem** for:

1. **Linnell's class $\mathcal C$** with $\mathrm{lcm}(G)<\infty$: free groups $F_n$, virtually free groups, free products, extensions $1 \to F \to G \to A \to 1$ with $A$ elementary amenable (Linnell 1993).
2. **Elementary amenable groups** with bounded finite subgroups, and all **amenable** groups in $\mathcal C$.
3. **Residually (torsion-free elementary amenable)** groups — Schick 2000; includes free nilpotent, free solvable, pure braid groups, and residually torsion-free nilpotent groups such as $F_n$ and right-angled Artin groups.
4. **Finite extensions**: if $G$ satisfies SAC and $G \le H$ with $[H:G]<\infty$, then $H$ does, under hypotheses of Linnell–Schick (2007), extending the class beyond torsion-free.
5. **Locally indicable groups** (every non-trivial f.g. subgroup surjects $\mathbb{Z}$): SAC over $\mathbb{Q}$, indeed the Hughes-free division ring exists — Jaikin-Zapirain–López-Álvarez (2020). Consequences: torsion-free **one-relator groups**, free-by-cyclic groups, torsion-free compact orientable irreducible $3$-manifold groups (which are locally indicable by Howie / Boyer–Rolfsen–Wiest), braid groups' pure parts, orderable groups arising in knot theory.
6. **Knot groups.** $\pi_1(S^3 \setminus K)$ for any knot $K$ is torsion-free and locally indicable, so SAC holds; here all $b_i^{(2)}$ vanish for $i\ge 1$ in the compact aspherical case and $b_0^{(2)} = 0$ for infinite $G$ — integrality is realized trivially, but the division ring $\mathcal D(\mathbb{Q}G)$ this produces is the natural home of $L^2$-Alexander invariants.
7. **Sofic/approximation regimes.** Elek–Szabó and Lück approximation give integrality whenever the approximating finite quotients have uniformly bounded Betti-number denominators.

Counterexample classes (bounded torsion hypothesis is essential): $\mathbb{Z}/2 \wr \mathbb{Z}$ and relatives give $1/3$; Austin/Grabowski/Pichot–Schick–Żuk give irrational and transcendental values for f.g. (even f.p.) groups with unbounded torsion.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No algebraic handle on $\ker$.** $\dim_{\mathcal N(G)}\ker A$ is a *spectral* quantity: $\mu_A(\{0\})$. Nothing in the group ring forces an atom at $0$ to have rational mass; the counterexamples are built precisely by engineering atoms of prescribed irrational mass out of infinite-order-limit constructions.
- **Approximation is not enough.** Lück approximation writes $b^{(2)}$ as $\lim b_i(X/G_n)/[G:G_n]$, a limit of rationals with exploding denominators. Controlling the limit needs a uniform spectral gap near $0$ (a Novikov–Shubin-type lower bound), which is not available and is false in general.
- **Failure of the division-ring method beyond local indicability.** Linnell's and Hughes' machinery needs an ordering-like structure (local indicability, or an induction over an elementary amenable hierarchy) to prove the division closure of $KG$ in $\mathcal U(G)$ is a division ring. For a torsion-free group with property (T), no infinite cyclic quotients, and no hierarchy — e.g. a torsion-free hyperbolic group with property (T), or Higman's group — there is no known induction to run.
- **Universality of $L^2$-invariants.** Grabowski's Turing-machine encoding shows $L^2$-Betti numbers can simulate arbitrary computably-enumerable-real behaviour. Any proof of the torsion-free case must use torsion-freeness in an essential way, since the general problem is provably wild.
- **Kaplansky barrier.** SAC for torsion-free $G$ implies the zero-divisor conjecture, open since 1957. Any full proof would resolve it, so no "soft" argument can work.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: SAC for groups admitting a hierarchy of the Linnell/Hughes type (locally indicable, residually torsion-free elementary amenable, plus finite extensions). Disproven: the rational/strong statement for groups with $\mathrm{lcm}(G)=\infty$.

The gap is exactly the class of torsion-free groups with **no indicability and no hierarchy**: torsion-free hyperbolic groups with property (T), torsion-free lattices in higher-rank Lie groups (e.g. torsion-free finite-index subgroups of $\mathrm{SL}_3(\mathbb{Z})$), Burnside-adjacent torsion-free monsters, and simple torsion-free groups. Concretely, the missing step is: *produce, for every torsion-free $G$, a division ring $\mathcal D$ with $\mathbb{Q}G \subseteq \mathcal D \subseteq \mathcal U(G)$* — or equivalently show $\mathbb{Q}G$ has no zero divisors and its Ore-like localization is division closed. No candidate mechanism exists that is insensitive to indicability.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Madrid school (Jaikin-Zapirain, López-Álvarez, and collaborators, ICMAT/UAM).** Hughes-freeness, uniqueness of Hughes-free division rings, and base change between $\mathbb{Q}$, $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}$, and function fields; extension of SAC to fields of positive characteristic and links with the Lück approximation conjecture.
- **Bonn/Oxford (Lück, Kielak, and coauthors).** Agrarian invariants and $\ell^2$-torsion for free-by-cyclic and RFRS groups; fibring criteria that convert integrality into topological fibring statements. *(frontier — verify)* Ongoing work reducing SAC for $3$-manifold groups with boundary to Thurston-norm computations.
- **Sofic and approximation programme.** Quantitative Lück approximation, character-variety and mod-$p$ approximations (Bergeron–Linnell–Lück–Sauer style) probing whether denominators stabilize.
- **Counterexample engineering.** Grabowski-style Turing-dynamical encodings pushed toward *smaller* torsion; the open question is whether any bounded-torsion or torsion-free encoding is possible. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Operator-algebraic side.** Atomicity of spectral measures of random walk operators on non-amenable groups; connections to the Connes embedding problem's demise (MIP*=RE) and its consequences for approximation of von Neumann dimensions.

## 8. Future Work

- Prove SAC for torsion-free groups acting properly cocompactly on CAT(0) cube complexes without assuming local indicability, using Wise's hierarchy as the induction.
- Settle SAC for a single torsion-free property (T) group — the smallest test case that defeats all present methods.
- Develop a Novikov–Shubin-type uniform spectral gap: show that for $A \in M_n(\mathbb{Z}G)$ with $G$ torsion-free, $\mu_A((0,\varepsilon))\to 0$ with a rate depending only on $n$ and $\|A\|$.
- Decide whether SAC over $\mathbb{Q}$ and over $\mathbb{C}$ are equivalent for torsion-free groups (base change).
- Attack Kaplansky's zero-divisor conjecture directly for orderable-adjacent classes; it is the weakest consequence and thus the natural first target.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**(a) A finite group: the denominator is exactly $\mathrm{lcm}(G)$.** Let $G = \mathbb{Z}/2 = \{e,s\}$. Then $\ell^2(G) = \mathbb{C}^2$, $\mathcal N(G) = \mathbb{C}G$, and $\mathrm{tr}$ is the normalized trace $\mathrm{tr}(\alpha e + \beta s) = \alpha$. Take $A = 1+s$. Acting on $\mathbb{C}^2$ in the basis $\delta_e,\delta_s$, $A = \begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$, so $\ker A = \mathbb{C}(\delta_e - \delta_s)$ and $p_{\ker A} = \tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{pmatrix}$. Hence
$$\dim_{\mathcal N(G)}\ker A = \mathrm{tr}(p) = \tfrac12 \in \tfrac{1}{\mathrm{lcm}(G)}\mathbb{Z}, \quad \mathrm{lcm}(G)=2 .$$
So $1/2$ occurs and the bound is sharp.

**(b) A torsion-free case: the free group.** Let $X = S^1 \vee S^1$, $G = F_2 = \langle a,b\rangle$, $\widetilde X$ the $4$-valent tree. The $L^2$-chain complex is
$$0 \to \ell^2(G)^2 \xrightarrow{\;c_1\;} \ell^2(G) \to 0, \qquad c_1 = \big(a-1,\; b-1\big).$$
$c_1$ is surjective onto a dense subspace and $H_0^{(2)} = \ell^2(G)/\overline{\mathrm{im}\,c_1} = 0$ since $G$ is infinite (indeed $b_0^{(2)}(G) = 1/|G| = 0$). Additivity gives
$$b_1^{(2)} = \dim\ker c_1 = 2 - \dim\overline{\mathrm{im}\,c_1} = 2 - 1 = 1 .$$
Check: $\chi(X) = -1 = b_0^{(2)} - b_1^{(2)} = 0 - 1$. Both values are integers, as SAC demands for the torsion-free group $F_2$ — and this is exactly the case Linnell's theorem generalizes.

**(c) Where it breaks: the lamplighter.** Let $\Gamma = \mathbb{Z}/2 \wr \mathbb{Z} = \big(\bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/2\big) \rtimes \mathbb{Z}$, generated by the shift $t$ and the lamp $s$ at the origin. Every finite subgroup is an elementary abelian $2$-group, so $\mathrm{lcm}(\Gamma) = \infty$, but every *finite* subgroup has $2$-power order. Grigorchuk–Żuk computed the spectral measure of the Markov operator on $\Gamma$ (equivalently on the Diestel–Leader graph $DL(2,2)$): it is purely atomic, with atoms at $\cos(p\pi/q)$ of mass computable from Euler's totient. Dicks–Schick used this to exhibit $A = A^* \in \mathbb{Z}[\Gamma]$ with
$$\dim_{\mathcal N(\Gamma)} \ker A = \mu_A(\{0\}) = \tfrac13 .$$
Since $1/3 \notin \tfrac{1}{2^k}\mathbb{Z}$ for any $k$, no $2$-power denominator can accommodate it. Grigorchuk–Linnell–Schick–Żuk then realized $1/3$ as an honest $L^2$-Betti number of a closed $7$-manifold with fundamental group a suitable extension containing $\Gamma$. The mechanism — an infinite ascending family of finite subgroups producing atoms whose masses converge to a value with a new prime in the denominator — is exactly what torsion-freeness is supposed to forbid, and exactly what no current technique can rule out abstractly.
