# Nilpotence Theorem

> **Topic:** Topology & Knot Theory · **ID:** `04-topology/nilpotence-theorem` · **Status:** solved-recently

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Ravenel's nilpotence conjecture (1984), proved by Devinatz, Hopkins and Smith (1988), asserts that **complex cobordism $MU$ detects all nilpotence in the stable homotopy category**. Three equivalent forms:

- **Ring-spectrum form.** Let $R$ be a ring spectrum and $h\colon \pi_*R \to MU_*R = \pi_*(MU \wedge R)$ the Hurewicz map. If $\alpha \in \pi_* R$ satisfies $h(\alpha) = 0$, then $\alpha$ is nilpotent: $\alpha^n = 0$ for some $n$.
- **Self-map form.** Let $F$ be a finite $p$-local spectrum and $f\colon \Sigma^d F \to F$. If $MU_*(f)$ is nilpotent, then $f$ is nilpotent under composition; equivalently, $f$ is nilpotent iff $K(n)_*(f)$ is nilpotent for all $0 \le n \le \infty$.
- **Smash-product form.** Let $F$ be finite and $f\colon F \to X$ arbitrary. If $MU \wedge f \simeq *$, then the $n$-fold smash power $f^{(n)}\colon F^{(n)} \to X^{(n)}$ is null for $n \gg 0$.

A complete proof must produce, from the vanishing of an $MU$-theoretic invariant, an actual null-homotopy after finitely many iterations — a passage from algebra to homotopy that no Adams-spectral-sequence bookkeeping supplies directly.

The theorem is **proved**. What remains open are its generalisations: the exact analogue in equivariant, motivic, and general tensor-triangulated settings, i.e. the classification of the Balmer spectrum outside the classical case.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Work in the stable homotopy category $\mathcal{SH}$: a symmetric monoidal triangulated category with unit the sphere spectrum $S$, product $\wedge$, and $\pi_*X = [S^*, X]$. The subcategory $\mathcal{SH}^{fin}$ of finite spectra is the thick subcategory generated by $S$.

**Complex cobordism.** $MU$ is the Thom spectrum of the universal bundle over $BU$, with
$$MU_* \;=\; \pi_*MU \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}[x_1, x_2, x_3, \dots], \qquad |x_i| = 2i,$$
a polynomial ring concentrated in even degrees. By Quillen's theorem, $(MU_*, MU_*MU)$ carries the universal formal group law, so $\mathrm{Spec}\,MU_*$ is the moduli of formal group laws and $\mathrm{Spec}\,MU_*/MU_*MU \simeq \mathcal{M}_{fg}$, the moduli stack of formal groups.

**Morava K-theories.** For a prime $p$ and $1 \le n < \infty$, $K(n)$ is the complex-oriented spectrum with
$$K(n)_* = \mathbb{F}_p[v_n^{\pm 1}], \qquad |v_n| = 2(p^n - 1),$$
whose formal group law is the Honda formal group of height $n$, with $p$-series $[p](x) = v_n x^{p^n}$. Set $K(0) = H\mathbb{Q}$ and $K(\infty) = H\mathbb{F}_p$. Morava K-theories are fields in $\mathcal{SH}$: every module splits as a wedge of suspensions, and
$$K(n)_*(X \wedge Y) \cong K(n)_*(X) \otimes_{K(n)_*} K(n)_*(Y).$$

**Type and thick subcategories.** A thick subcategory $\mathcal{C} \subseteq \mathcal{SH}^{fin}_{(p)}$ is closed under cofibres, retracts and suspension. Put
$$\mathcal{C}_n = \{X \in \mathcal{SH}^{fin}_{(p)} : K(n-1)_*X = 0\}, \qquad \mathcal{SH}^{fin}_{(p)} = \mathcal{C}_0 \supseteq \mathcal{C}_1 \supseteq \cdots$$
A finite $p$-local $X \ne *$ has **type $n$** if $K(i)_*X = 0$ for $i < n$ and $K(n)_*X \ne 0$; the vanishing is monotone by the Landweber filtration theorem.

**Consequences (Hopkins–Smith).**
- *Thick subcategory theorem:* every thick subcategory of $\mathcal{SH}^{fin}_{(p)}$ equals some $\mathcal{C}_n$.
- *Periodicity theorem:* every type-$n$ finite $X$ admits a $v_n$-self map $v\colon \Sigma^d X \to X$ with $K(n)_*(v)$ an isomorphism (multiplication by a power of $v_n$) and $K(m)_*(v) = 0$ for $m \ne n$.
- *Balmer spectrum:* $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{SH}^{fin})$ is the union over $p$ of the chains $\mathcal{C}_1 \supset \mathcal{C}_2 \supset \cdots$, whose closure is $\mathcal{M}_{fg}$-shaped — a "Zariski space" of the chromatic filtration.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1973.** Nishida proves every element of positive degree in $\pi_*S$ is nilpotent, using extended power constructions and the Kahn–Priddy theorem. The bound on the nilpotence order is astronomically large and non-explicit.
- **1984.** Ravenel, in *Localization with respect to certain periodic homology theories*, formulates seven conjectures organising stable homotopy chromatically; Conjecture 1 is the nilpotence conjecture.
- **1985–87.** Hopkins announces the proof (Durham, LMS Lecture Notes 117, 1987), reducing periodicity phenomena to nilpotence via the thick subcategory formalism.
- **1988.** Devinatz–Hopkins–Smith, *Nilpotence and stable homotopy theory I* (Annals 128), prove the smash-product and ring-spectrum forms. Key inputs: the vanishing line/Adams spectral sequence estimates for $X(n)$, the Thom spectra over $\Omega SU(n)$ interpolating between $S$ and $MU$, and a "self-map" induction with the $F_k(n)$ filtration.
- **1998.** Hopkins–Smith, *Nilpotence and stable homotopy theory II* (Annals 148), prove the self-map (type/periodicity) form, the thick subcategory theorem, and the periodicity theorem.
- **1992.** Ravenel's *Nilpotence and Periodicity in Stable Homotopy Theory* (the "orange book") gives the definitive exposition.
- **2005–2020.** Balmer's tensor-triangular geometry recasts the theorem as a computation of $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{SH}^{fin})$; Balmer–Sanders (Invent. Math. 2017) and Barthel–Greenlees–Hausmann (Compositio 2020) push this into equivariant homotopy theory.
- **2023.** Burklund–Hahn–Levy–Schlank disprove Ravenel's *telescope* conjecture, showing that the chromatic package around the nilpotence theorem is not uniformly true.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **Nishida (1973):** all of $\pi_{>0}S$ — the $p=2$ case $\eta^4 = 0$, and $\alpha^{p} $-type bounds for $p$ odd — verified before the general theorem.
- **Connective ring spectra of finite type:** the ring-spectrum form is fully proved (DHS I, Thm. 1); no restriction on associativity or commutativity is needed.
- **Finite $p$-local spectra of every type $n \ge 0$:** self-map form proved (Hopkins–Smith II). Explicit $v_n$-self maps are known only for small $n$: $v_1^4$ on $S/2$ (Adams, 1966), $v_1$ on $S/p$ for $p \ge 3$, $v_2^{32}$ on $M(2, v_1^4)$ at $p=2$ (Behrens–Hill–Hopkins–Mahowald), $v_2$-self maps at $p \ge 5$ on $M(p,v_1)$ (Smith).
- **Equivariant:** for a finite group $G$, $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{SH}^{fin}_G)$ is determined by Balmer–Sanders (2017) up to a "blue-shift" ambiguity, resolved for abelian $G$ by Barthel–Hausmann–Naumann–Nikolaus–Noel–Stapleton (2019). Compact Lie $G$: partial (Barthel–Greenlees–Hausmann, 2020).
- **Descent settings:** Mathew–Naumann–Noel (Adv. Math. 2017) prove nilpotence for $G$-equivariant ring spectra detected by geometric fixed points on the family of elementary abelian subgroups.
- **Algebraic analogues:** Hopkins–Neeman for $D^{perf}(R)$, $R$ Noetherian commutative; Benson–Carlson–Rickard for $\mathrm{stab}(kG)$; Thomason for perfect complexes on schemes.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

The theorem is settled; the obstructions concern its extensions and its effectivity.

- **No effective bounds.** The DHS proof is an induction over the $X(n)$ tower and gives no computable $N$ with $\alpha^N = 0$. Even for $\pi_*S$, the nilpotence order of a given element is not extractable from the proof; determining it requires separate spectral-sequence work.
- **$MU$ has no motivic/equivariant surrogate of the same strength.** In $\mathcal{SH}(k)$ the algebraic cobordism spectrum $MGL$ does *not* detect nilpotence for general base fields: $\rho \in \pi_{-1,-1}\mathbf{1}$ over $\mathbb{R}$ is non-nilpotent yet invisible to naive height arguments, and the Balmer spectrum of $\mathcal{SH}(k)^{fin}$ is unknown beyond low-rank cases.
- **Failure of the Landweber filtration input.** The classification $\mathcal{C}_n$ rests on the fact that $BP_*BP$-comodules that are finitely presented admit a filtration by $BP_*/I_n$. No analogue holds for $MGL_*MGL$ or for genuine $G$-spectra with non-abelian $G$, so the thick subcategory step breaks.
- **Chromatic localisations are not finitely generated.** The telescope counterexamples (2023) show $L_n^f \ne L_n$, i.e. the passage from finite to localised objects, which the nilpotence theorem does *not* control, genuinely diverges.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{SH}^{fin})$ is exactly the chromatic chain, and $MU$ detects nilpotence in $\mathcal{SH}$.

Not proven:
1. **Effective nilpotence.** No function $N(d, |\alpha|)$ bounding nilpotence order in $\pi_*S$ is known; Nishida-type bounds are exponential towers.
2. **Motivic nilpotence.** Is there a set of "motivic Morava K-theories" $\{K(n)^{mot}, KW, \dots\}$ jointly detecting nilpotence in $\mathcal{SH}(k)^{fin}$ for every field $k$? Known only for $k$ algebraically closed of characteristic $0$ in restricted ranges.
3. **Compact Lie equivariance.** The blue-shift numbers governing $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{SH}^{fin}_G)$ for non-abelian compact Lie $G$ are undetermined.

The exact step to be crossed for (2)–(3): a substitute for the $X(n)$-tower induction in categories where the unit is not generated by a single compact object of "height-monotone" type.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Chromatic Nullstellensatz.** Burklund–Schlank–Yuan (*The Chromatic Nullstellensatz*, Annals of Mathematics, 2025) show every non-zero $E_\infty$-ring under $E_n$ admits a map to an algebraically closed Lubin–Tate theory — a "points" theorem giving new nilpotence-detection statements. Active follow-up on Galois-theoretic descent forms. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Post-telescope chromatic geometry.** Following Burklund–Hahn–Levy–Schlank, groups at Copenhagen (Barthel, Schlank's students), MIT and Bonn are recomputing $\mathrm{Spec}$ of localised categories where $\mathcal{C}_n$-style classification fails. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Equivariant/global.** Barthel–Greenlees–Hausmann and collaborators continue the compact Lie Balmer spectrum programme; global homotopy theory (Schwede's school, Bonn) supplies the "$\mathcal{M}_{fg}$ over all groups" picture.
- **Motivic and synthetic.** Bachmann, Isaksen, Wang–Xu use synthetic and $\mathbb{C}$-motivic deformations to compute nilpotence orders in $\pi_{*,*}\mathbf{1}$ far beyond the classical stem range.
- **Higher algebra reformulation.** Mathew–Naumann–Noel descent methods are being extended to nilpotence detection in categories of modules over $E_\infty$-rings with Galois descent.

## 8. Future Work

- Extract an explicit nilpotence bound from the $X(n)$ induction, even a tower-of-exponentials function of degree; Ravenel has repeatedly flagged this as the main defect of the proof.
- Construct $v_n$-self maps of minimal periodicity for $n \ge 3$; currently no explicit $v_3$-self map is known at any prime.
- Determine $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{SH}(k)^{fin})$ for $k = \mathbb{R}$ and for finite fields; this would fix the motivic analogue of the thick subcategory theorem.
- Settle the chromatic splitting conjecture, which conditions the reassembly step that nilpotence/periodicity leaves open.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Claim.** $\eta \in \pi_1 S$, the stable Hopf map, is nilpotent — read straight off the nilpotence theorem.

*Step 1.* $S$ is a ring spectrum, so the theorem applies with $R = S$ and $\pi_*R = \pi_*S$.

*Step 2.* Compute the Hurewicz image. $MU_*S = MU_* = \mathbb{Z}[x_1,x_2,\dots]$ with $|x_i| = 2i$, so $MU_*$ is concentrated in **even** degrees and is torsion-free. Since $|\eta| = 1$,
$$h(\eta) \in MU_1 = 0 \implies h(\eta) = 0.$$

*Step 3.* By the ring-spectrum form, $\eta$ is nilpotent. (The classical computation sharpens this: $\eta^3 = 4\nu \ne 0$ in $\pi_3 S = \mathbb{Z}/24$, while $\eta^4 = 4\nu\eta = 0$ because $\nu\eta = 0$. So the exact order is $4$ — a fact the theorem cannot supply, illustrating the effectivity gap of §6.)

*Step 4 — the contrast that shows the theorem is sharp.* Let $S/2$ be the mod-$2$ Moore spectrum, a type-$1$ finite spectrum, and let
$$A\colon \Sigma^8 S/2 \longrightarrow S/2$$
be Adams' $v_1^4$-self map. Here $K(1)_*(A)$ is multiplication by $v_1^4$, a **unit** in $K(1)_* = \mathbb{F}_2[v_1^{\pm 1}]$. So $MU_*(A)$ is not nilpotent, and the self-map form predicts $A$ is not nilpotent: indeed $A^{\circ k}\colon \Sigma^{8k}S/2 \to S/2$ is essential for every $k \ge 1$. Telescoping gives $v_1^{-1}S/2$, whose homotopy is $\mathbb{F}_2[v_1^{\pm1}]\otimes E(\text{class in degree } 0,\,\text{plus } \eta\text{-multiples})$ — visibly non-trivial in arbitrarily high degrees.

The pair $(\eta, A)$ is the smallest complete illustration: same category, same $MU$-detection criterion, opposite verdicts, both correct.
