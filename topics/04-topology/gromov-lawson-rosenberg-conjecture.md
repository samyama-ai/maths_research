# Bordism of Positive Scalar Curvature Metrics (Gromov–Lawson–Rosenberg Conjecture)

> **Topic:** Topology & Knot Theory · **ID:** `04-topology/gromov-lawson-rosenberg-conjecture` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $M$ be a closed connected spin manifold of dimension $n \ge 5$ with fundamental group $\pi$, and let $u : M \to B\pi$ classify the universal cover. The **Gromov–Lawson–Rosenberg (GLR) conjecture** asserts:

> $M$ admits a Riemannian metric of positive scalar curvature (PSC) if and only if the Rosenberg index
> $$\alpha_\pi(M,u) \;=\; \operatorname{ind}_{C^*_r\pi}\big(\slashed{D}_{\mathcal L}\big) \;\in\; KO_n\!\left(C^*_r\pi\right)$$
> vanishes.

The "only if" direction is a theorem (Rosenberg 1983, extending Lichnerowicz and Hitchin). The conjectural content is the "if" direction: vanishing of a single $K$-theoretic index is sufficient for PSC.

The conjecture is **false as stated**. Schick (1998) produced a closed spin $5$-manifold with $\pi = \mathbb{Z}/3 \times \mathbb{Z}^4$, $\alpha_\pi = 0$, and no PSC metric. What remains open, and is the live problem, is:

1. **(Class problem)** For which groups $\pi$ does the unstable statement hold? A complete characterization is unknown.
2. **(Stable GLR)** Does $\alpha_\pi(M,u) = 0$ imply that $M \times B^{8k}$ admits PSC for some $k$, where $B^8$ is the Bott manifold (a simply connected spin $8$-manifold with $\hat{A}(B) = 1$)? Open in general; known to follow from injectivity of the Baum–Connes assembly map.

A resolution means either a proof of stable GLR for all groups, a counterexample to it, or a structural theorem delimiting exactly which $\pi$ satisfy the unstable form.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Lichnerowicz formula.** On a spin manifold with spinor bundle $S$ and scalar curvature $\kappa$,
$$\slashed{D}^2 \;=\; \nabla^*\nabla \;+\; \tfrac{1}{4}\kappa .$$
If $\kappa > 0$ then $\ker \slashed{D} = 0$, so all indices of $\slashed{D}$ vanish.

**Atiyah–Bott–Shapiro / $\alpha$-invariant.** The ABS orientation gives ring maps
$$\Omega^{\mathrm{spin}}_n \xrightarrow{\ \alpha\ } KO_n(\mathrm{pt}), \qquad KO_*(\mathrm{pt}) = \mathbb{Z},\ \mathbb{Z}/2,\ \mathbb{Z}/2,\ 0,\ \mathbb{Z},\ 0,\ 0,\ 0 \ (\ast \equiv 0,\dots,7 \bmod 8),$$
with $\alpha(M) = \hat{A}(M)$ for $n \equiv 0 \pmod 4$ (up to a factor $2$ in dimensions $\equiv 4 \bmod 8$) and $\mathbb{Z}/2$-valued mod-$2$ indices in dimensions $\equiv 1,2 \pmod 8$.

**Rosenberg index.** Let $\mathcal L = \tilde M \times_\pi C^*_r\pi$ be the Mishchenko–Fomenko bundle of flat $C^*_r\pi$-modules. Twisting the Dirac operator gives a class in the $KO$-theory of the real reduced group $C^*$-algebra, and the construction factors through the assembly map:
$$\Omega^{\mathrm{spin}}_n(B\pi) \xrightarrow{\ D\ } ko_n(B\pi) \xrightarrow{\ A\ } KO_n(C^*_r\pi), \qquad \alpha_\pi = A \circ D .$$
Here $D$ is the ABS transformation from spin bordism to connective real $K$-homology; $D$ is surjective for all $n$.

**Surgery / bordism principle.** Gromov–Lawson (1980) and Schoen–Yau (1979): PSC is preserved under surgeries of codimension $\ge 3$. Consequently PSC depends only on the class $[M,u] \in \Omega^{\mathrm{spin}}_n(B\pi)$ modulo the subgroup
$$\Omega^{\mathrm{spin},+}_n(B\pi) \;=\; \{\,[M,u] : M \text{ admits PSC}\,\} \subseteq \Omega^{\mathrm{spin}}_n(B\pi), \qquad n \ge 5 .$$

**Stolz's Bordism Theorem (1992).** Let $T_n(B\pi) \subseteq \Omega^{\mathrm{spin}}_n(B\pi)$ be the subgroup generated by total spaces of $\mathbb{HP}^2$-bundles with structure group $\mathrm{PSp}(3)$. Then
$$\Omega^{\mathrm{spin},+}_n(B\pi) \;=\; T_n(B\pi) \quad (n\ge 5), \qquad \Omega^{\mathrm{spin}}_n(B\pi)/T_n(B\pi) \;\cong\; ko_n(B\pi).$$
Hence:
$$\textbf{GLR holds for } \pi \text{ in all dimensions } n \ge 5 \iff A : ko_n(B\pi) \to KO_n(C^*_r\pi) \text{ is injective for } n \ge 5 .$$
This is the reformulation that drives all modern work: the geometry is fully absorbed by Stolz's theorem, and the conjecture becomes a purely homotopy-theoretic/operator-algebraic injectivity question.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1963** Lichnerowicz: $\hat{A}(M) = 0$ for spin $M^{4k}$ with PSC.
- **1974** Hitchin: refines to $\alpha(M) \in KO_n(\mathrm{pt})$, giving obstructions in dimensions $n \equiv 1, 2 \bmod 8$; exotic spheres carry no PSC metric in these dimensions.
- **1979–1980** Schoen–Yau (minimal hypersurfaces) and Gromov–Lawson (surgery) establish the two independent engines: an obstruction valid for $n \le 7$ without spin hypotheses, and the surgery/bordism construction.
- **1983** Rosenberg introduces $\alpha_\pi$ via $C^*$-algebras and proves it obstructs PSC; he conjectures the converse, connecting PSC to the Novikov conjecture.
- **1992** Stolz proves the simply connected case ($\pi = 1$, $n\ge 5$): PSC $\iff \alpha(M)=0$ — the sharpest positive result in the field.
- **1995–1998** Rosenberg–Stolz formulate the stable version; Stolz shows stable GLR follows from injectivity of Baum–Connes assembly.
- **1998** Schick disproves the unstable conjecture.
- **2003** Dwyer–Schick–Stolz sharpen and propagate the counterexamples.
- **2015–2026** The focus shifts to (i) secondary invariants and the space $\mathcal{R}^+(M)$ of PSC metrics (Botvinnik–Ebert–Randal-Williams), (ii) codimension-two/enlargeability obstructions (Hanke–Pape–Schick), (iii) non-spin and low-dimensional aspherical cases (Chodosh–Li).

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Class of $\pi$ | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| $\pi = 1$, $n \ge 5$ | **Proved** | Stolz, *Ann. of Math.* 136 (1992) |
| Finite groups with periodic cohomology (cyclic, quaternion, etc.), $n \ge 5$ | **Proved** | Botvinnik–Gilkey–Stolz (1997) |
| Free abelian $\pi = \mathbb{Z}^k$; free groups; fundamental groups of surfaces | **Proved** ($A$ injective by Künneth/Baum–Connes) | Rosenberg (1986); Rosenberg–Stolz survey (2001) |
| $\pi$ with Baum–Connes assembly injective (a-T-menable, hyperbolic, linear groups) | **Stable GLR proved** | Stolz; Higson–Kasparov, *Invent. Math.* 144 (2001) |
| $\pi = \mathbb{Z}/3 \times \mathbb{Z}^4$, $n = 5$ | **Counterexample** | Schick, *Topology* 37 (1998) |
| Further groups, all $n \ge 5$ | **Counterexamples** | Dwyer–Schick–Stolz (2003); Joachim–Schick (2000) |
| Non-spin simply connected $M^n$, $n \ge 5$ | PSC always exists | Gromov–Lawson (1980) |
| Aspherical $M^n$, $n = 4,5$: no PSC | **Proved** (supports the conjecture's obstruction side) | Chodosh–Li, arXiv:2008.11888 |

Dimensional caveat: everything above requires $n \ge 5$ for surgery. Dimension $4$ is governed by Seiberg–Witten theory, not index theory, and is excluded; dimension $3$ is settled by geometrization.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **The index map loses information.** $KO_n(C^*_r\pi)$ is $8$-periodic, while $ko_n(B\pi)$ is connective. Torsion classes in $\widetilde{ko}_*(B\pi)$ built from $\eta \in ko_1(\mathrm{pt})$ and from torsion in $H_*(B\pi)$ can die under $A$ purely for periodicity reasons, without any geometric PSC metric appearing. This is exactly Schick's mechanism, and it is not a defect that can be repaired by a better analytic index in $KO_*(C^*_r\pi)$.
- **No known complete obstruction.** Codimension-one/two descent (Schoen–Yau, Hanke–Pape–Schick), enlargeability (Gromov–Lawson, Hanke–Schick), and $\mathbb{Z}/k$-indices each detect classes the Rosenberg index misses, but no single invariant is known to be a complete obstruction for any infinite family of groups with torsion.
- **Minimal-hypersurface methods stop at $n \le 7$** (singularities of area-minimizers); generic-regularity and $\mu$-bubble refinements have pushed low-dimensional statements but do not reach the surgery range uniformly.
- **Computability.** $ko_*(B\pi)$ for $\pi$ with torsion is hard: Adams-spectral-sequence computations exist only for small groups, and $KO_*(C^*_r\pi)$ requires Baum–Connes input which is unavailable for groups with property (T) plus torsion.
- **Non-spin case.** For totally non-spin manifolds there is no Dirac operator at all; the natural analogue must be phrased in $ko\langle$ or $\mathrm{ko}$-orientations of the universal cover, and the correct general statement is not agreed upon.

## 6. The Gap

Section 4 gives injectivity of $A : ko_n(B\pi) \to KO_n(C^*_r\pi)$ for groups whose classifying spaces have $K$-theoretically transparent homology (trivial, periodic-cohomology, torsion-free with Baum–Connes). Section 1 asks for all $\pi$. The gap is precisely:

$$\ker\Big( A : ko_n(B\pi) \to KO_n(C^*_r\pi) \Big) \ \ne\ 0 \quad \Longrightarrow\ ?$$

Non-vanishing of this kernel is now known to occur (Schick). The remaining question is whether every nonzero kernel element is *geometrically* invisible after stabilization — i.e. whether
$$\ker(A) \subseteq \{\text{classes killed by multiplication with the Bott class } \beta \in ko_8(\mathrm{pt})\}.$$
Stable GLR is exactly the statement that $\ker(A)$ consists of $\beta$-torsion, so that $\ker\big(ko_n(B\pi)[\beta^{-1}] \to KO_n(C^*_r\pi)\big) = 0$. Crossing this gap requires either a proof of rational/integral injectivity of Baum–Connes assembly for arbitrary $\pi$ — the Novikov-type barrier — or a group where $\beta$-periodic kernel classes exist *and* an independent geometric obstruction certifies no PSC after stabilization.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Spaces of PSC metrics.** Botvinnik–Ebert–Randal-Williams (*Invent. Math.* 2017) show $\mathcal{R}^+(M)$ has nontrivial higher homotopy detected by the Atiyah–Singer family index; the emphasis has shifted from existence to the homotopy type of $\mathcal{R}^+(M)$ and to secondary $\rho$-invariants. Groups at Münster, Göttingen (Schick), Augsburg (Hanke), Copenhagen and Oxford drive this.
- **Codimension-two and submanifold obstructions.** Hanke–Pape–Schick (*Ann. Inst. Fourier* 2015) and Zeidler's index-theoretic descent produce obstructions independent of $\alpha_\pi$; ongoing work asks whether these suffice for $\pi = \mathbb{Z}/p \times \mathbb{Z}^k$.
- **$\mu$-bubbles and low dimensions.** Following Gromov's programme and Chodosh–Li, work continues on dimension $\le 7$ aspherical manifolds without spin hypotheses; extensions to bandwidth and macroscopic-dimension statements are active. *(frontier — verify)* Attempts to combine $\mu$-bubble slicing with $C^*$-index descent to rule out PSC for classes in $\ker(A)$ are circulating as preprints.
- **Baum–Connes injectivity.** Any new injectivity theorem for a class of groups immediately yields stable GLR for that class; the coarse-geometric (Yu, Guentner–Tessera–Yu) route remains the main supply line.

## 8. Future Work

- Determine whether stable GLR can fail: construct $\pi$ and $M$ with $\alpha_\pi(M)=0$ but $M \times B^{8k}$ never PSC. This would decouple PSC from $K$-theory entirely.
- Classify groups for which $A$ is injective on $ko_*$ — a homotopy-theoretic problem in the Adams spectral sequence for $ko \wedge B\pi_+$.
- Develop an obstruction theory valued in $ko_n(B\pi)$ itself rather than its $KO$-periodization; Stolz's unfinished programme on concordance classes of PSC metrics points this way.
- Formulate and test the correct non-spin analogue for totally non-spin manifolds with infinite $\pi_1$.
- Push minimal-surface/$\mu$-bubble obstructions past $n = 7$ using generic regularity.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Free abelian fundamental group: GLR holds, and it recovers the torus theorem.**

Take $\pi = \mathbb{Z}^k$, so $B\pi = \mathbb{T}^k$ and $C^*_r\pi \cong C(\mathbb{T}^k)$ (real form).

*Step 1 — split the classifying space.* Stably, $\mathbb{T}^k_+ \simeq \bigvee_{j=0}^{k} \big(S^j\big)^{\vee \binom{k}{j}}$. Smashing with the $ko$ and $KO$ spectra gives
$$ko_n(\mathbb{T}^k) \cong \bigoplus_{j=0}^{k} \binom{k}{j} ko_{n-j}(\mathrm{pt}), \qquad KO_n(C(\mathbb{T}^k)) \cong \bigoplus_{j=0}^{k} \binom{k}{j} KO_{n-j}(\mathrm{pt}).$$

*Step 2 — check injectivity.* The assembly map $A$ respects the splitting and on each summand is the canonical map $ko_{m}(\mathrm{pt}) \to KO_{m}(\mathrm{pt})$, which is an isomorphism for $m \ge 0$ and has source $0$ for $m < 0$. Hence $A$ is injective, and by Stolz's Bordism Theorem GLR holds for every $\mathbb{Z}^k$ in dimensions $n \ge 5$.

*Step 3 — evaluate on $M = \mathbb{T}^n$.* Here $u = \mathrm{id}$, and the top summand $j = n$ contributes $ko_0(\mathrm{pt}) = \mathbb{Z}$. The class $[\mathbb{T}^n, \mathrm{id}]$ maps to the generator $1 \in KO_0(\mathrm{pt}) \subset KO_n(C(\mathbb{T}^n))$, detected by the flat line-bundle pairing (the top exterior class of $H^1(\mathbb{T}^n)$). So
$$\alpha_{\mathbb{Z}^n}(\mathbb{T}^n) \neq 0 \implies \mathbb{T}^n \text{ admits no PSC metric},$$
recovering the Gromov–Lawson–Schoen–Yau torus theorem from pure index theory.

*Step 4 — contrast with the counterexample.* Now let $\pi = \mathbb{Z}/3 \times \mathbb{Z}^4$ and $n = 5$. Künneth gives
$$ko_5\big(B\mathbb{Z}/3 \times \mathbb{T}^4\big) \supseteq \bigoplus_{j} \binom{4}{j}\, \widetilde{ko}_{5-j}(B\mathbb{Z}/3),$$
and $\widetilde{ko}_*(B\mathbb{Z}/3)$ is all $3$-torsion concentrated in degrees $\equiv 3 \bmod 4$. Some of these torsion classes are connective phenomena: they are annihilated by the Bott class and therefore die in the $8$-periodic target $KO_5(C^*_r(\mathbb{Z}/3 \times \mathbb{Z}^4))$. Schick's manifold realizes such a class: $\alpha_\pi(M) = 0$, yet $[M,u] \notin T_5(B\pi)$, so no PSC metric exists. The difference between the two examples is exactly the difference between $ko$ and $KO$ — connectivity versus periodicity — and that is the whole content of the gap in Section 6.
