# Nakanishi's 4-move Conjecture

> **Topic:** Topology & Knot Theory · **ID:** `04-topology/nakanishis-4-move-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

A **4-move** is a local change on a link diagram that replaces two parallel strands by the same two strands with four extra half-twists (two full twists), in either direction, leaving the rest of the diagram fixed. The move is not an ambient isotopy: it changes the knot type in general.

**Conjecture (Nakanishi, 1979).** Every knot $K \subset S^3$ can be transformed into the trivial knot by a finite sequence of 4-moves.

Equivalently: the 4-move is an *unknotting operation* on knots. A proof requires an algorithm or existence argument producing, for each knot, a finite 4-move sequence to the unknot; a disproof requires an invariant of knots that is unchanged by 4-moves and separates some knot from the unknot.

Two points fix the scope.

- The statement is about **knots**, not links. For links the naive analogue is false: a 4-move between strands of two different components changes their linking number by $\pm 2$, so $\mathrm{lk}(L_i,L_j) \bmod 2$ is a 4-move invariant, and the Hopf link is not 4-move equivalent to the 2-component trivial link.
- The link version, restricted so that the obstruction above vanishes, is **Kawauchi's question**: is every 2-component link 4-move equivalent to the trivial 2-component link or to the Hopf link? This is also open.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**$n$-moves.** For $n \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$, an $n$-move replaces the trivial $2$-tangle by the $n$-twisted tangle:
$$T_0 \;\longleftrightarrow\; T_n , \qquad T_n = \text{$n$ positive half-twists on two strands}.$$
In braid notation the local replacement is $1 \leftrightarrow \sigma_i^{\,n}$. The relation "$L \sim_n L'$" generated by $n$-moves and isotopy is an equivalence relation on links, since $T_n \to T_0$ is the inverse move. The case $n=2$ is trivial (all knots become unknotted, $2$-moves change crossings). The case $n=3$ is the **Montesinos–Nakanishi 3-move conjecture**; $n=4$ is the present problem.

**Classical obstruction: the double branched cover.** Let $M_L^{(2)}$ be the double cover of $S^3$ branched along $L$. An $n$-move on $L$ changes the Goeritz/Seifert presentation matrix of $H_1(M_L^{(2)};\mathbb{Z})$ by adding $\pm n$ to one diagonal entry, so
$$H_1\!\left(M_L^{(2)};\mathbb{Z}_n\right) \quad\text{is an invariant of $n$-move equivalence.}$$
For a **knot** $K$, $|H_1(M_K^{(2)})| = |\det(K)| = |\Delta_K(-1)|$ is **odd**, hence $H_1(M_K^{(2)};\mathbb{Z}_4) = H_1 \otimes \mathbb{Z}_4 = 0$: the classical obstruction vanishes identically on knots. This is the structural reason the conjecture is plausible and simultaneously hard to attack by homology.

**Fox colorings.** An $n$-move preserves the set of Fox $n$-colorings, $\mathrm{Col}_n(L) = \mathrm{Hom}(\pi_1(S^3\setminus L), \mathrm{Dih}_n)$-type count, and more generally $\mathrm{Col}_p$ for $p \mid n$. For $n = 4$ the only relevant prime is $p = 2$, and $\mathrm{Col}_2$ is determined by the number of components. Again: no obstruction on knots.

**Burnside groups of links.** Dąbkowski and Przytycki refined the homological invariant to a non-abelian one. For a link $L$ set
$$\mathcal{B}_L(n) \;=\; \pi_1\!\left(M_L^{(2)}\right) \Big/ \big\langle\!\big\langle\, x^{n},\; \gamma_k(\pi_1) \,\big\rangle\!\big\rangle ,$$
the $n$-th Burnside-type quotient (exponent-$n$ quotient of a nilpotent stage of the group of the double branched cover). Theorem (Dąbkowski–Przytycki 2002): $\mathcal{B}_L(n)$ is invariant under $n$-moves. This is the tool that killed the 3-move conjecture.

**Braid quotients.** A closed $m$-braid can be 4-moved by replacing $\sigma_i^{\pm 1}$ with $\sigma_i^{\mp 3}$, so 4-move classes of closed $m$-braids are controlled by the quotient $B_m / \langle\!\langle \sigma_i^4 \rangle\!\rangle$. **Coxeter's criterion:** $B_m/\langle\!\langle \sigma_i^k \rangle\!\rangle$ is finite if and only if
$$\frac{1}{k} + \frac{1}{m} > \frac{1}{2}.$$
For $k = 4$ this gives $m \le 3$ finite, and $m = 4$ exactly on the boundary ($\tfrac14+\tfrac14=\tfrac12$, infinite). The conjecture is therefore decidable by finite group theory for closed 3-braids and immediately escapes that method at 4 strands.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1958.** Fox introduces congruence classes of knots, the setting in which "adding $n$ full twists" first appears as an equivalence.
- **1979–1985.** Y. Nakanishi poses the 4-move problem; it circulates through Kawauchi's problem collections and appears in Kirby's *Problems in low-dimensional topology* (Problem 1.59) and in Kawauchi's *A Survey of Knot Theory*.
- **1981.** Montesinos, in work on 3-fold branched coverings, raises the 3-move question; combined with Nakanishi's version it becomes the Montesinos–Nakanishi 3-move conjecture — the $n=3$ sibling.
- **1994.** Nakanishi, *On generalized unknotting operations*, records the positive cases (rational knots, small crossing numbers) and the linking-number obstruction for links.
- **2001.** Przytycki and Tsukamoto develop the **fourth skein module** $\mathcal{S}_4$, the quotient of the free module on links by the relation $L_{T_0} = L_{T_4}$, and prove the $n$-move conjectures for the class of **3-algebraic links**.
- **2002–2004.** Dąbkowski and Przytycki disprove the **3-move** conjecture using Burnside groups: the 2-parallel of the Borromean rings (a closed 4-braid built from $(\sigma_1\sigma_2^{-1})$-type words) is not 3-move equivalent to a trivial link. The same machinery, applied at $n=4$, has **not** produced a counterexample for knots.
- **Present.** The 4-move conjecture stands: no counterexample, no proof, no invariant known to obstruct it on knots. It is the last of the small-$n$ move conjectures still fully open.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **Rational (2-bridge) knots — TRUE.** Every 2-bridge knot $K(p/q)$, $p$ odd, is 4-move equivalent to the unknot (Nakanishi). Proof idea: 4-moves change a continued-fraction entry by $\pm 4$, so one reduces every entry of $p/q = [a_1,\dots,a_k]$ modulo 4 into $\{0,\pm 1, 2\}$ and cancels twist regions. Correspondingly, 2-bridge **links** reduce to the trivial 2-component link or the Hopf link, matching the $\mathrm{lk} \bmod 2$ invariant.
- **Closed 3-braids — TRUE.** The quotient $B_3/\langle\!\langle \sigma_i^4\rangle\!\rangle$ is finite by Coxeter's criterion; enumerating its conjugacy classes and their closures gives the unknot for every knot class.
- **3-algebraic links.** Przytycki–Tsukamoto establish the 4-move statement (in the appropriate link-corrected form) for 3-algebraic links, a class built from 3-tangles by algebraic operations; it contains all rational and Montesinos links and all links of small crossing number. *(frontier — verify the exact class and crossing threshold in the 2001 JKTR paper.)*
- **Small crossing numbers.** All knots in the standard tables through at least 12 crossings have been 4-move trivialized by explicit sequences; no tabulated knot resists. *(frontier — verify the published upper crossing bound.)*
- **Torus knots $T(2,n)$.** Immediate: the closure of $\sigma_1^n$ becomes the closure of $\sigma_1^{n \bmod 4}$.
- **Obstruction-side results.** Every $\mathbb{Z}_4$-homological and Fox-coloring obstruction is proven to vanish on knots (Section 2), so the conjecture cannot fail for classical reasons.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

1. **All cheap invariants vanish.** $\det(K)$ odd $\Rightarrow H_1(M_K^{(2)};\mathbb{Z}_4) = 0$; Fox 2-colorings are trivial for knots. There is no linear-algebra obstruction left to test.
2. **Polynomial invariants are not 4-move invariants.** Jones, HOMFLYPT and Alexander polynomials all change under a 4-move, and no known specialization is preserved. Skein-theoretic control is lost: the fourth skein module $\mathcal{S}_4$ of $S^3$ is exactly what the conjecture computes, so it cannot be used as input — arguing with it is circular unless one has an independent presentation.
3. **The braid quotient goes infinite at 4 strands.** Coxeter's inequality $\tfrac1k+\tfrac1m>\tfrac12$ fails at $(k,m)=(4,4)$. The finite-group enumeration that settles 3-braids has no analogue, and $B_4/\langle\!\langle\sigma_i^4\rangle\!\rangle$ is infinite, so exhaustive search over the quotient is impossible.
4. **Burnside obstructions are computationally out of reach.** By Sanov's theorem the free Burnside group $B(m,4)$ is finite, but its order is astronomical ($|B(2,4)| = 2^{12}$, $|B(3,4)| = 2^{69}$, and it grows super-exponentially), so the invariant $\mathcal{B}_L(4)$ that would be the natural counterexample detector cannot be evaluated for interesting links. The successful $n=3$ attack used $B(m,3)$, whose order is $3^{m + \binom{m}{2} + \binom{m}{3}}$ — small enough to compute.
5. **No monotone complexity.** 4-moves can increase crossing number arbitrarily before decreasing it; there is no known bounded search space, so even verification for a given knot is not a terminating algorithm.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: the conjecture for links whose diagrams decompose into 2- or 3-strand pieces (rational, Montesinos, 3-algebraic, closed 3-braids) and for tabulated knots up to moderate crossing number. General statement: all knots.

The precise barrier is the passage from **3-strand to 4-strand tangle complexity**. Every positive proof to date reduces a diagram to a 2- or 3-tangle and finishes inside a *finite* quotient of $B_2$ or $B_3$ modulo fourth powers. At 4 strands that quotient is infinite, so the induction that closes the 3-algebraic case has no termination argument. Crossing the gap requires either

- a normal form for links modulo 4-moves that reduces braid index (i.e. shows every knot is 4-move equivalent to a closed 3-braid), **or**
- a computable non-abelian invariant valued in a quotient of $B(m,4)$ that is provably nontrivial on some knot.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Burnside/nilpotent quotients at $n=4$.** The Dąbkowski–Przytycki program continues at George Washington University and UT Dallas, replacing the full Burnside group with metabelian or class-$\le 3$ exponent-4 quotients that are computationally tractable. No knot-separating obstruction found. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Skein module computations.** Work on $\mathcal{S}_4$ and on Khovanov-type homology of $n$-move quotients seeks structural presentations of the fourth skein module of $S^3$. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Computer search.** SAT/rewriting searches over 4-move sequences with bounded diagram size, extending verification into the 13–16 crossing tables, and searching for 4-move-hard candidates among links with even linking numbers. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Related move conjectures.** The $(2,2)$-move conjecture and 5-move classification results (Dąbkowski, Ishiwata, Przytycki) are used as testing grounds for techniques intended for $n=4$.

## 8. Future Work

- **Reduce braid index modulo 4-moves.** Show every knot is 4-move equivalent to a closed 3-braid; combined with the finiteness of $B_3/\langle\!\langle\sigma_i^4\rangle\!\rangle$ this proves the conjecture outright. This is the most direct stated route.
- **Find a computable exponent-4 invariant.** Identify a finite quotient of $\mathcal{B}_L(4)$ (e.g. a specific nilpotency class) whose evaluation on 2-parallels of Borromean-type links can be machine-checked, mirroring the $n=3$ disproof.
- **Attack Kawauchi's question first.** Settling the 2-component link case ("trivial or Hopf") is strictly weaker in obstruction content and would isolate whether the difficulty lies in the knot case or in linking data.
- **Bound the search.** Prove a crossing-number bound $c(K') \le f(c(K))$ along some 4-move trivializing sequence, making the conjecture algorithmically decidable per knot.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**The $(2,n)$ torus family.** Let $L_n$ be the closure of $\sigma_1^{\,n} \in B_2$: $L_1$ = unknot, $L_2$ = Hopf link, $L_3$ = trefoil, $L_4 = $ the $(2,4)$ torus link, and so on.

A 4-move applied to the single twist region sends $\sigma_1^{\,n} \mapsto \sigma_1^{\,n \pm 4}$. Hence
$$L_n \;\sim_4\; L_{n \bmod 4}, \qquad n \bmod 4 \in \{0,1,2,3\}.$$

- $n \equiv 1, 3 \pmod 4$ (knots): $L_n \sim_4 L_1$ or $L_3$. And $L_3 \sim_4 L_{-1}$, whose closure is the unknot. **Every $(2,n)$ torus knot is 4-move trivial in one move.** Explicitly, the trefoil $= \widehat{\sigma_1^3} \to \widehat{\sigma_1^{-1}} = $ unknot.
- $n \equiv 0$: $L_n \sim_4$ trivial 2-component link.
- $n \equiv 2$: $L_n \sim_4$ Hopf link.

**Why the last two classes do not merge.** Compute the invariant of Section 2. For the trivial 2-component link $U_2$, $M_{U_2}^{(2)} = S^1\times S^2$, so $H_1(M^{(2)};\mathbb{Z}_4) = \mathbb{Z}_4$. For the Hopf link $H$, $M_H^{(2)} = \mathbb{RP}^3$, so $H_1(M^{(2)};\mathbb{Z}_4) = \mathbb{Z}_2$. Since $\mathbb{Z}_4 \not\cong \mathbb{Z}_2$, the Hopf link is **not** 4-move equivalent to $U_2$ — equivalently $\mathrm{lk} = 1 \not\equiv 0 \pmod 2$.

**Contrast with knots.** For the trefoil, $\det = 3$ and $H_1(M^{(2)};\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}_3$, so $H_1(M^{(2)};\mathbb{Z}_4) = \mathbb{Z}_3\otimes\mathbb{Z}_4 = 0$, the same value as for the unknot — consistent with the one-move trivialization above. This is the general phenomenon: knot determinants are odd, the invariant is always $0$, and the conjecture has no classical obstruction. The open problem is whether that absence of obstruction reflects the truth, or merely the weakness of every invariant currently computable.
