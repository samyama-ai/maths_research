# Cobordism Hypothesis

> **Topic:** Topology & Knot Theory · **ID:** `04-topology/cobordism-hypothesis` · **Status:** solved-recently

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $n \geq 1$ and let $\mathcal{C}$ be a symmetric monoidal $(\infty,n)$-category. Let $\mathrm{Bord}_n^{\mathrm{fr}}$ be the symmetric monoidal $(\infty,n)$-category whose objects are framed $0$-manifolds, whose $k$-morphisms ($1 \le k \le n$) are framed $k$-dimensional bordisms, and whose $(n+j)$-morphisms are diffeomorphisms and isotopies between them.

**Cobordism Hypothesis (Baez–Dolan 1995; Lurie 2009).** Evaluation at the positively framed point $\mathrm{pt}^+$ induces an equivalence of $\infty$-groupoids
$$
\mathrm{ev}_{\mathrm{pt}^+} \colon \operatorname{Fun}^{\otimes}\!\left(\mathrm{Bord}_n^{\mathrm{fr}}, \mathcal{C}\right) \xrightarrow{\ \simeq\ } \left(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{fd}}\right)^{\simeq},
$$
where $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{fd}}$ is the full subcategory of **fully dualizable** objects and $(-)^\simeq$ denotes the maximal sub-$\infty$-groupoid. Equivalently: $\mathrm{Bord}_n^{\mathrm{fr}}$ is the free symmetric monoidal $(\infty,n)$-category with duals generated by a single object.

**Tangential-structure version.** For a topological group $G$ with map $G \to O(n)$ and associated $G$-structure, $O(n)$ acts on the space $(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{fd}})^\simeq$, and
$$
\operatorname{Fun}^{\otimes}\!\left(\mathrm{Bord}_n^{G}, \mathcal{C}\right) \simeq \left(\left(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{fd}}\right)^{\simeq}\right)^{hG}.
$$

A complete resolution requires: (i) a rigorous construction of $\mathrm{Bord}_n^{\mathrm{fr}}$ as a symmetric monoidal $(\infty,n)$-category; (ii) a proof that the evaluation map is an equivalence, for arbitrary target $\mathcal{C}$ and arbitrary $n$; (iii) full written details, not a sketch. Items (i) and (ii) are now available in the literature; the historically dominant argument (Lurie's) remains a detailed sketch, which is why the status is *solved-recently* rather than *solved-classically*.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Duals and adjoints.** In a symmetric monoidal category $(\mathcal{C}, \otimes, \mathbf{1})$, an object $X$ is *dualizable* if there exist $X^\vee$ and morphisms
$$
\mathrm{coev}\colon \mathbf{1} \to X \otimes X^\vee, \qquad \mathrm{ev}\colon X^\vee \otimes X \to \mathbf{1}
$$
satisfying the zig-zag (triangle) identities
$$
(\mathrm{id}_X \otimes \mathrm{ev}) \circ (\mathrm{coev} \otimes \mathrm{id}_X) = \mathrm{id}_X, \qquad (\mathrm{ev} \otimes \mathrm{id}_{X^\vee}) \circ (\mathrm{id}_{X^\vee} \otimes \mathrm{coev}) = \mathrm{id}_{X^\vee}.
$$
In an $(\infty,n)$-category, a $1$-morphism $f \colon X \to Y$ has a right adjoint $f^R$ if there are $2$-morphisms $u \colon \mathrm{id}_X \Rightarrow f^R f$, $c \colon f f^R \Rightarrow \mathrm{id}_Y$ satisfying the triangle identities up to invertible higher cells.

**Full dualizability.** $X \in \mathcal{C}$ is *$n$-dualizable* (fully dualizable when $n$ is the bordism dimension) if $X$ is dualizable, the morphisms $\mathrm{ev}, \mathrm{coev}$ admit both adjoints, their unit/counit $2$-morphisms admit both adjoints, and so on through level $n-1$. This is a *property*, not extra structure: $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{fd}} \subseteq \mathcal{C}$ is a full subcategory closed under symmetric monoidal functors.

**Bordism category.** Objects: finite framed $0$-manifolds. A $k$-morphism is a $k$-manifold $M$ with corners, with a chosen $n$-framing, i.e. a trivialization $TM \oplus \underline{\mathbb{R}}^{\,n-k} \cong \underline{\mathbb{R}}^{\,n}$. Composition is gluing along boundary strata. Rigorous models: Lurie's $\mathrm{PBord}_n$ built from sheaves of $n$-fold Segal spaces; Calaque–Schommer-Pries' complete $n$-fold Segal space model; Grady–Pavlov's model via smooth $\infty$-stacks of embedded bordisms.

**Serre automorphism.** The $O(n)$-action produces, for $n \ge 2$, the *Serre automorphism* $S_X \in \pi_1\!\left((\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{fd}})^\simeq, X\right)$, image of the generator of $\pi_1 SO(n) \cong \mathbb{Z}/2$ ($n\ge 3$) or $\mathbb{Z}$ ($n = 2$). Orientability of a TFT amounts to a trivialization of $S_X$ together with higher coherence.

**Homotopy type of $\mathrm{Bord}$.** For the $1$-category-level bordism category, Galatius–Madsen–Tillmann–Weiss prove
$$
B\,\mathcal{C}\mathrm{ob}_d \simeq \Omega^{\infty - 1} MTO(d),
$$
the Madsen–Tillmann spectrum; the cobordism hypothesis is the $(\infty,n)$-categorical strengthening whose $n=0$/groupoid shadow this is.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1988.** Atiyah axiomatizes TQFT as a symmetric monoidal functor $\mathrm{Bord}_n \to \mathrm{Vect}_k$.
- **1989–1996.** Two-dimensional (non-extended) TQFTs are classified by commutative Frobenius algebras (Dijkgraaf's thesis; Abrams, *J. Knot Theory Ramifications*, 1996).
- **1995.** Baez and Dolan state the cobordism hypothesis in *Higher-dimensional algebra and topological quantum field theory* (J. Math. Phys. **36**, 6073–6105), conjecturing that $n\mathrm{Cob}$ is the free stable weak $n$-category with duals on one object. Their framework (weak $n$-categories with duals) was never fully developed.
- **2000s.** Costello (2007) proves the $2$-dimensional open-string/Calabi–Yau case; Hopkins–Lurie develop the ambidexterity technology used for the $1$-dimensional case.
- **2009.** Lurie, *On the classification of topological field theories* (Current Developments in Mathematics 2008, Int. Press, 2009, pp. 129–280), reformulates the hypothesis using $(\infty,n)$-categories and gives a detailed proof sketch via Morse theory and an induction on handle index. This is the canonical reference and is widely accepted, but is explicitly not a complete write-up.
- **2009–2013.** Schommer-Pries proves the $n=2$ case for symmetric monoidal bicategories by generators-and-relations for the bordism bicategory.
- **2017.** Ayala–Francis, *The cobordism hypothesis* (arXiv:1705.02240), give a proof for all $n$ using factorization homology and the theory of *striated/conically smooth* spaces, deducing it from a "$\mathrm{Bord}_n$ is a classifying object" statement.
- **2020–2024.** Grady–Pavlov, *The geometric cobordism hypothesis* (arXiv:2111.01095) prove a strictly stronger *geometric* statement: bordism categories with arbitrary geometric structure (Riemannian, conformal, symplectic, $G$-structures, fields of a sigma-model) classify field theories, recovering the topological case. Their earlier *Extended field theories are local and have classifying spaces* supplies the locality input.

Consensus as of 2026: the topological cobordism hypothesis is a theorem, with at least two independent complete proofs plus Lurie's sketch; the outstanding work is verification, simplification, and extension.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Constructing $\mathrm{Bord}_n$ at all.** Manifolds with corners do not compose strictly; gluing is only defined up to a contractible choice of collar. Every rigorous model (Segal spaces, conically smooth stratified spaces, smooth $\infty$-stacks) trades one hard technical apparatus for another, and comparing models is itself nontrivial.
- **Morse theory is not enough.** Lurie's induction decomposes a bordism by handle attachments, but a handle decomposition is not canonical; controlling the space of decompositions requires the contractibility of spaces of "framed Morse functions", the step where the 2009 sketch is thinnest.
- **Higher-categorical coherence.** Traditional algebraic topology proves statements about spaces up to homotopy; here one needs coherence data at every level $\le n$ simultaneously, for which no small generators-and-relations presentation exists beyond $n = 2$. Schommer-Pries' $n=2$ proof is a finite list of generators and relations; the list grows without control at $n=3$.
- **Duality is a limit condition.** Full dualizability is defined by an infinite regress of adjoints; there is no finite criterion in a general target, so one cannot verify the hypothesis "objectwise".
- **Smooth vs. topological.** The statement is about smooth manifolds; the analogous topological-manifold statement fails to have an obvious proof since Morse theory and handle decompositions are unavailable in dimension $4$.

## 6. The Gap

What is proved: the framed and $G$-structured statements for all $n$ (Ayala–Francis; Grady–Pavlov), with independently checked low-dimensional cases $n \le 3$. What remains open:

1. **Verification consensus.** Neither of the two complete proofs has been absorbed by the community to the degree that a short, checkable account exists; both run to hundreds of pages of new foundational machinery. Lurie's proof remains unpublished in complete form. The gap is sociological-plus-technical: no one disputes the theorem; independent verification of each argument is incomplete.
2. **Non-invertible targets with genuine $n$-categorical subtlety.** Explicit identification of $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{fd}}$ for $n \ge 4$ (e.g. $4$-dimensional TFTs from braided fusion $2$-categories) is open in most cases.
3. **Beyond duals.** Extensions to non-compact bordisms, boundary conditions, and unoriented/defect settings are formulated but not uniformly proved.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Verification and exposition of Grady–Pavlov.** Ongoing effort (Pavlov's group, Oregon State; homotopy-theory seminars) to extract the topological case as a self-contained argument. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Higher fusion categories and $4$-d TFTs.** Johnson-Freyd, Scheimbauer, Douglas, and collaborators classify fully dualizable objects in $4$-categories of braided tensor categories; connections to the Crane–Yetter and Walker–Wang models.
- **Factorization homology.** Ayala–Francis and collaborators extend the method to stratified bordisms, defects, and TFTs with singularities.
- **Geometric/nontopological field theories.** Stolz–Teichner's program relating $2|1$-dimensional Euclidean field theories to $\mathrm{TMF}$ is now being reconsidered in the light of the geometric cobordism hypothesis. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Formalization.** Preliminary work on formalizing $(\infty,n)$-categories in Lean/Rzk; no formal statement of the cobordism hypothesis yet exists. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Future Work

- Produce a proof of the framed case for general $n$ that does not require rebuilding the theory of stratified spaces — the analogue of a "second-generation" proof.
- Compute $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{fd}}$ explicitly in $n = 4, 5$ for algebraically presented targets, giving new $4$-manifold invariants.
- Settle whether a topological-manifold cobordism hypothesis holds in dimension $4$.
- Develop the "cobordism hypothesis with singularities" to give an axiomatic account of TFTs with defects and boundary conditions, as advocated by Lurie (2009, §4.3).

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Take $n = 1$, $\mathcal{C} = \mathrm{Vect}_k$** (ordinary vector spaces, $\otimes_k$, unit $k$).

*Step 1 — identify $\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{fd}}$.* For $n=1$, full dualizability is just dualizability. A vector space $V$ is dualizable iff $\dim_k V < \infty$: given $\mathrm{coev}\colon k \to V \otimes V^*$ and $\mathrm{ev}\colon V^* \otimes V \to k$, choose a basis $\{e_i\}$ with dual $\{e^i\}$; then $\mathrm{coev}(1) = \sum_{i=1}^{d} e_i \otimes e^i$ is a finite sum, which forces $d < \infty$. The zig-zag identity reads, for $v \in V$,
$$
v \;\longmapsto\; \sum_i e_i \otimes e^i \otimes v \;\longmapsto\; \sum_i e_i \,\langle e^i, v\rangle \;=\; v. \quad\checkmark
$$

*Step 2 — the bordism side.* $\mathrm{Bord}_1^{\mathrm{fr}}$ has objects finite sets of signed points; the generating $1$-morphisms are the right elbow $\emptyset \to \{+,-\}$ and the left elbow $\{-,+\} \to \emptyset$. The only relations, imposed by isotopy of framed arcs in $\mathbb{R}^2$, are exactly the two zig-zag identities (straightening an S-shaped arc). Hence a symmetric monoidal functor $Z$ out of $\mathrm{Bord}_1^{\mathrm{fr}}$ is precisely: a vector space $V = Z(\mathrm{pt}^+)$, plus duality data $\mathrm{ev},\mathrm{coev}$, satisfying the zig-zags. So $Z \mapsto Z(\mathrm{pt}^+)$ lands in finite-dimensional spaces, and the assignment is an equivalence — the cobordism hypothesis for $n=1$.

*Step 3 — compute the circle.* Decompose $S^1$ (bounding framing) as
$$
S^1 \;=\; \emptyset \xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{coev}\ } \{+,-\} \xrightarrow{\ \sigma\ } \{-,+\} \xrightarrow{\ \mathrm{ev}\ } \emptyset .
$$
Applying $Z$:
$$
Z(S^1)(1) \;=\; \mathrm{ev}\!\left(\sum_i e^i \otimes e_i\right) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{d} \langle e^i, e_i \rangle \;=\; d \;=\; \dim_k V .
$$
So $Z(S^1) = \dim_k V \in k$, the categorical trace of $\mathrm{id}_V$. For $V = k^3$, $Z(S^1) = 3$.

*Step 4 — orientations and the Serre automorphism.* For $n=1$, $O(1) = \mathbb{Z}/2$ acts on $(\mathrm{Vect}_k^{\mathrm{fd}})^\simeq$ by $V \mapsto V^*$. Homotopy fixed points are pairs $(V, \beta)$ with $\beta \colon V \xrightarrow{\sim} V^*$ symmetric — i.e. a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form. This matches the classical statement that a $1$-dimensional oriented TFT is a finite-dimensional space with a nondegenerate pairing, and shows how the tangential-structure form of the hypothesis produces classical algebraic data from pure homotopy fixed points.
