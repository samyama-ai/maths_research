# Przytycki 3-Move Conjecture

> **Topic:** Topology & Knot Theory · **ID:** `04-topology/przytycki-3-move-conjecture` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

A **3-move** on a link diagram replaces a 2-string tangle consisting of two parallel arcs by the same two arcs with three half-twists (or the reverse). The move is local: everything outside a small disk is fixed.

**Montesinos–Nakanishi 3-move conjecture (Nakanishi 1981; Kirby's problem list 1.59(1)).** Every link $L \subset S^3$ can be transformed, by a finite sequence of 3-moves and ambient isotopies, into a trivial link (an unlink of some number of components).

Przytycki is the problem's principal custodian: he formulated the surrounding programme of **$n$-move** and **$(m,q)$-move** conjectures, popularised the 3-move version, and — with Dąbkowski — settled it. The conjecture as literally stated is **false**: Dąbkowski and Przytycki (2002) exhibited a link that is not 3-move equivalent to any trivial link. The name "Przytycki 3-move conjecture" is used in the catalog for the surviving cluster of questions:

1. **Classification problem (open).** Describe the equivalence classes of links under 3-moves. Is the Burnside obstruction of Section 2 complete for some natural family?
2. **Related open moves.** The **Nakanishi 4-move conjecture** ("every knot is 4-move equivalent to the unknot") and **Kawauchi's question** on 5-moves for knots remain open.

A complete resolution of (1) means an algorithmically checkable invariant $I$ with $I(L) = I(L')$ if and only if $L$ and $L'$ are 3-move equivalent.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**$n$-moves.** Let $t_k$ denote the 2-string tangle with $k$ half-twists, so $t_0$ is the trivial (identity) tangle and $t_k$ is the closure-free braid word $\sigma_1^{k}$ in $B_2$. An **$n$-move** is the local replacement
$$ t_k \;\longleftrightarrow\; t_{k+n}, \qquad n \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}, $$
performed inside a ball meeting the link in two unknotted, unlinked arcs. For $n=3$ this changes the crossing number by $\pm 3$ and can change the number of components by $\pm 1$.

**Fox 3-colorings.** For a diagram $D$ with arcs $a_1,\dots,a_m$, a Fox 3-coloring is a map $c:\{a_i\}\to \mathbb{Z}_3$ with, at every crossing of over-arc $a_o$ and under-arcs $a_u,a_{u'}$,
$$ 2\,c(a_o) - c(a_u) - c(a_{u'}) \equiv 0 \pmod 3 .$$
The set $\mathrm{Col}_3(L)$ is a $\mathbb{Z}_3$-vector space, $|\mathrm{Col}_3(L)| = 3^{\,d(L)+1}$, and $d(L) \ge 1$ iff $3 \mid \det(L)$, where $\det(L)=|\Delta_L(-1)|$. **3-moves preserve $\mathrm{Col}_3(L)$**: the twist relations force the two strands to carry equal colors on both sides. This is the classical first obstruction, but it is too weak — it does not obstruct anything, since every trivial link of $k$ components has $3^k$ colorings and $k$ is unconstrained.

**Burnside groups of links (Dąbkowski–Przytycki).** Let $M_L^{(2)}$ be the double branched cover of $S^3$ along $L$. Define the **$n$-th Burnside group of the link**
$$ B_L(n) \;=\; \pi_1\!\left(M_L^{(2)}\right) \big/ \big\langle\!\big\langle\, w^{\,n} : w \in \pi_1(M_L^{(2)}) \,\big\rangle\!\big\rangle , $$
the quotient by the normal subgroup generated by all $n$-th powers. The key lemma: an $n$-move on $L$ changes $\pi_1(M_L^{(2)})$ by inserting or deleting a relation that becomes trivial after imposing exponent $n$; hence

> **Theorem (Dąbkowski–Przytycki 2002).** $B_L(n)$ is an invariant of $n$-move equivalence.

For the trivial link $T_k$ of $k$ components, $M_{T_k}^{(2)} = \\#^{\,k-1}(S^1\times S^2)$, so $\pi_1$ is free of rank $k-1$ and
$$ B_{T_k}(3) \;\cong\; B(k-1,3), $$
the free Burnside group of exponent 3 and rank $k-1$. By Burnside and Levi–van der Waerden, $B(r,3)$ is finite, nilpotent of class $\le 3$, of order
$$ |B(r,3)| \;=\; 3^{\,r + \binom{r}{2} + \binom{r}{3}} .$$
So the conjecture predicts that $B_L(3)$ is always isomorphic to some $B(r,3)$ — a very rigid prediction, and the one that breaks.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1975.** Montesinos, studying 3-fold branched covers and surgery presentations, isolates $3$-moves as the natural relation on links whose quotient controls his covering constructions (*Surgery on links and double branched covers of $S^3$*, Annals of Math. Studies 84).
- **1981.** Nakanishi asks whether the 3-move is an unknotting operation in the above sense. The question enters **Kirby's problem list as Problem 1.59(1)**, where it is called the Montesinos–Nakanishi 3-move conjecture.
- **1980s–1990s.** Verification for rational (2-bridge) links, then algebraic (Conway) links; Chen settles closed 5-braids (2000); Przytycki–Tsukamoto settle 4-braids and analyse the associated Fibonacci-type groups (2001). Skein-theoretic reformulations via the Kauffman bracket and $q$-analogues are developed by Przytycki as part of the skein module programme.
- **2002.** **Dąbkowski–Przytycki**, *Burnside obstructions to the Montesinos–Nakanishi 3-move conjecture*, Geom. Topol. 6, 355–360: the conjecture is **false**. The $2$-parallel (2-cable) of the Borromean rings has $B_L(3)$ not isomorphic to any $B(r,3)$, hence is not 3-move equivalent to a trivial link.
- **2004.** Dąbkowski–Przytycki extend the machinery in PNAS 101, disproving the **$(2,2)$-move conjecture** for links and producing an infinite family of 3-move-irreducible links.
- **2006–present.** The programme shifts from "is every link reducible?" to "what are the 3-move classes?", and to the still-open **4-move** and **5-move** questions for knots.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

Positive results — classes where every link *is* 3-move reducible to a trivial link:

| Class | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Rational (2-bridge) links | Reducible; explicit continued-fraction algorithm | folklore / Nakanishi |
| Algebraic (Conway/arborescent) links | Reducible | Nakanishi; Przytycki |
| Closed 3-braids | Reducible | Nakanishi 1981 |
| Closed 4-braids | Reducible | Przytycki–Tsukamoto, JKTR 10 (2001) |
| Closed 5-braids | Reducible | Q. Chen, *Knots in Hellas '98*, 2000 |
| All links of $\le 9$ crossings | Reducible (verified diagrammatically) | Nakanishi; Przytycki |
| Closed 6-braids | **Open in general** | — |

Negative results: the $2$-parallel of the Borromean rings (a 6-component link, $24$ crossings, braid index $6$) is **not** 3-move equivalent to a trivial link; the same Burnside method yields infinite families, including $2$-parallels of other Brunnian links. Consequently any minimal counterexample has braid index $\ge 6$, since braid index $\le 5$ is settled.

Also proven: for $n=2$ the analogous conjecture is trivially true; for $n \ge 6$ the corresponding $n$-move conjectures were known to fail early (the Jones/Kauffman polynomial obstructs), so $n \in \{3,4,5\}$ was the interesting window — $3$ is now closed, $4$ and $5$ are not.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Polynomial invariants are blind.** A 3-move changes the Jones, HOMFLYPT and Kauffman polynomials in an uncontrolled way; only $\mathbb{Z}_3$-homological data (3-colorings, $\det \bmod 3$, the Goeritz form mod 3) survives, and that data cannot distinguish trivial links of different component numbers from a general link. This is why the conjecture resisted for two decades: the standard toolkit has essentially *no* invariants of 3-move equivalence.
- **Component number is unbounded.** Because a 3-move can split a component off, one cannot argue by induction on components or crossings; there is no monotone complexity that a 3-move decreases.
- **Burnside groups are hard to compute.** $B(r,n)$ is finite only for $n=2,3,4,6$ (and $r$ arbitrary); the exponent-3 case is tractable because of Levi–van der Waerden, but for exponent 4 the groups are finite yet astronomically large, and for exponent 5 finiteness is unknown. This is exactly why the same trick has not resolved the 4- and 5-move conjectures.
- **The obstruction is not known to be complete.** $B_L(3)$ detects irreducibility but gives no reduction algorithm when it vanishes as an obstruction; there is no known converse.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: reducibility for braid index $\le 5$, algebraic links, and all links to 9 crossings; irreducibility for an explicit infinite family via $B_L(3)$.

Unproven, and the exact boundary:

1. **Completeness.** Is $L$ 3-move equivalent to a trivial link **iff** $B_L(3) \cong B(r,3)$ for some $r$? Nothing is known in the "if" direction beyond the small cases above.
2. **Braid index 6.** Are all closed 6-braids classified? Chen's 5-braid argument uses a finite reduction of the 5-strand tangle monoid modulo 3-moves; the analogous 6-strand computation has not been completed.
3. **Exponent 4 and 5.** The Nakanishi 4-move conjecture for knots and Kawauchi's 5-move question remain open; $B_L(4)$ has not produced a counterexample, and $B_L(5)$ is not known to be finite for trivial links, so the entire method degenerates.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **The George Washington University school** (Przytycki and collaborators) continues the "Burnside groups from knot theory" programme: refinements of $B_L(n)$ via lower central series quotients, and Yang–Baxter/skein-module reformulations of $n$-move equivalence. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Dąbkowski and coauthors** work on higher-order obstructions extending the $(2,2)$-move disproof to more general rational moves $t_k \to t_{k+q/p}$. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Quandle and rack colorings mod 3** as candidate finer invariants of 3-move classes; the relevant statement is that any invariant of 3-move equivalence must factor through the exponent-3 quotient of $\pi_1(M_L^{(2)})$-type data.
- **Computational search** for a minimal-crossing 3-move-irreducible link; the Borromean 2-parallel at 24 crossings is the record upper bound, with nothing ruled out between 10 and 23 crossings. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Future Work

- Complete the braid-index-6 case, either by extending Chen's tangle-monoid reduction or by showing the 6-strand monoid modulo 3-moves is infinite.
- Determine whether $B_L(3)$ is a complete invariant of 3-move equivalence for links with fewer than $k$ components, starting with $k = 3$.
- Find the minimal-crossing counterexample; Przytycki has repeatedly posed this as the concrete next target.
- Attack the 4-move conjecture with $B_L(4)$ plus the extra relations coming from the Burnside group $B(r,4)$'s known nilpotency-class bounds (Sanov), and decide whether the method can fail for principled reasons.
- Develop skein-module analogues: 3-move equivalence should correspond to a specialisation of the Kauffman bracket skein module at a third root of unity, giving a functorial obstruction.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**(a) Rational links reduce, and the mod-3 determinant is the bookkeeping.**

Take the trefoil $3_1$, the closure of $\sigma_1^3 = t_3$. A single 3-move replaces $t_3$ by $t_0$, whose closure is the 2-component unlink. So $3_1$ is 3-move equivalent to $T_2$, in one move. Check the invariant: $\det(3_1) = 3 \equiv 0 \pmod 3$, and $\det(T_2)=0$. Consistent.

Now the figure-eight knot $4_1$, the rational link $C(2,2)$ with fraction
$$ \frac{p}{q} = 2 + \frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{2}, \qquad \det(4_1) = |p| = 5 .$$
A 3-move on the first twist region sends $2 \mapsto -1$, giving
$$ -1 + \frac{1}{2} = -\frac{1}{2}, \qquad |p'| = 1 ,$$
the unknot. So $4_1 \to$ unknot in one 3-move. Note $5 \equiv 2$ and $-1 \equiv 2 \pmod 3$: on rational links a 3-move changes the fraction $p/q$ by $p/q \mapsto p/(q+3p)$ or $p \mapsto p+3q$, so $p \bmod 3$ is preserved up to sign — matching $|\mathrm{Col}_3|$ invariance. Euclid's algorithm mod 3 then always terminates at $p' \in \{0,\pm 1\}$, i.e. at a trivial link. This proves the rational case in full.

**(b) Why the Borromean 2-parallel fails.**

Let $L$ be the 2-parallel of the Borromean rings $B$: replace each of the 3 components by two parallel copies (blackboard framing), giving 6 components and 24 crossings. Suppose $L$ were 3-move equivalent to $T_k$. Then
$$ B_L(3) \;\cong\; B_{T_k}(3) \;=\; B(k-1,3), $$
a finite group of order $3^{\,r+\binom r2+\binom r3}$ with $r=k-1$. Dąbkowski and Przytycki compute a presentation of $\pi_1(M_L^{(2)})$ from the Borromean structure and show the exponent-3 quotient is **not** isomorphic to any free Burnside group $B(r,3)$ — the nilpotency-class-3 relations forced by freeness fail, detected by counting elements of order 3 in the quotient by the lower central series term $\gamma_3$. Hence no such $k$ exists, and $L$ is not 3-move reducible. The Fox 3-coloring invariant, by contrast, gives $|\mathrm{Col}_3(L)| = 3^{6}$ — exactly what a 6-component trivial link would give, which is precisely why the classical invariant saw nothing.
