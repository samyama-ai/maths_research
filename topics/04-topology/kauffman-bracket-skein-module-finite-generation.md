# Kauffman Bracket Skein Module Finite Generation

> **Topic:** Topology & Knot Theory · **ID:** `04-topology/kauffman-bracket-skein-module-finite-generation` · **Status:** solved-recently

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

For a compact oriented 3-manifold $M$, the Kauffman bracket skein module $K(M)$ is the quotient of the free module on framed links in $M$ by the Kauffman relations. The finiteness question has two distinct forms, separated by the coefficient ring.

**(A) Witten's finiteness conjecture (generic coefficients).** For $M$ closed and oriented, $K(M)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm1}]}\mathbb{Q}(A)$ is a finite-dimensional $\mathbb{Q}(A)$-vector space. **Status: proved** by Gunningham, Jordan and Safronov (*Invent. Math.* 232, 2023). A complete proof required showing $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}(A)}K(M)<\infty$ for every closed oriented $M$, with no hypothesis on geometry, Heegaard genus, or fundamental group.

**(B) Integral finite generation (open).** Is $K(M)$ finitely generated as a $\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm1}]$-module for $M$ compact? Here the answer is **no in general** — $K(S^1\times S^2)$ is not finitely generated — and the open problem is the *characterization*: Przytycki's conjecture that $K(M)$ is finitely generated over $\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm1}]$ exactly when $M$ contains no closed incompressible surface that is not boundary-parallel, and that all $\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm1}]$-torsion in $K(M)$ originates from such surfaces (in particular from incompressible tori and non-separating spheres). A disproof requires an atoroidal, boundary-incompressible $M$ with $K(M)$ infinitely generated, or torsion in a manifold with no incompressible surface.

The relative version, for $M$ with boundary and for the skein *algebra* $K(\Sigma\times I)$, is a third strand: finite generation as an algebra is known, finite generation as a module is false for any $\Sigma$ of positive genus.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Let $R=\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm1}]$ and let $M$ be an oriented 3-manifold. Let $\mathcal{L}(M)$ be the set of isotopy classes of framed unoriented links in $M$, including the empty link $\emptyset$. Let $R\mathcal{L}(M)$ be the free $R$-module on $\mathcal{L}(M)$, and let $S(M)\subset R\mathcal{L}(M)$ be the submodule generated by all expressions

$$L_{\times}-A\,L_{0}-A^{-1}L_{\infty}, \qquad L\sqcup \bigcirc + (A^{2}+A^{-2})\,L,$$

where $L_\times, L_0, L_\infty$ agree outside a ball in which they appear as the crossing, the $0$-smoothing and the $\infty$-smoothing, and $\bigcirc$ is a framed unknot bounding a disk disjoint from $L$.

**Definition.** The Kauffman bracket skein module is
$$K(M)\;=\;R\mathcal{L}(M)\big/ S(M).$$

Functoriality: an orientation-preserving embedding $M\hookrightarrow N$ induces $K(M)\to K(N)$. For a surface $\Sigma$, stacking in the $I$ direction makes $K(\Sigma\times I)$ an $R$-algebra, the **Kauffman bracket skein algebra** $\mathcal{S}_A(\Sigma)$, and $K(M)$ is a module over $\mathcal{S}_A(\Sigma)$ whenever $\Sigma\subset\partial M$ or $\Sigma$ is a Heegaard surface.

**Classical limit.** At $A=-1$ the algebra $\mathcal{S}_{-1}(\Sigma)$ is commutative, and Bullock's theorem (1997) together with Przytycki–Sikora (2000) gives
$$\mathcal{S}_{-1}(\Sigma)/\sqrt{0}\;\cong\;\mathbb{C}[X_{SL_2(\mathbb{C})}(\Sigma)],\qquad [\gamma]\longmapsto -\operatorname{tr}\rho(\gamma),$$
the coordinate ring of the $SL_2(\mathbb{C})$-character variety; Przytycki–Sikora later showed the nilradical vanishes. So $K(M)$ is a quantization of $\mathbb{C}[X_{SL_2(\mathbb{C})}(\pi_1 M)]$, and finite dimensionality at generic $A$ is a quantum shadow of the fact that a Heegaard splitting $M=H_1\cup_\Sigma H_2$ presents $X(M)$ as an intersection
$$X(M)\;=\;X(H_1)\times_{X(\Sigma)}X(H_2)$$
of two **Lagrangian** subvarieties in the symplectic (Atiyah–Bott–Goldman) smooth locus of $X(\Sigma)$, of dimension $3g-3$ each inside a $6g-6$-dimensional space.

**Known basic computations.**
- $K(S^3)=R$, generated by $\emptyset$; the class of a link is its Kauffman bracket.
- $K(\text{handlebody } H_g)$ is free on isotopy classes of multicurves in $\Sigma_g$; for $g=1$, $K(S^1\times D^2)=R[z]$, free with basis $\{z^n\}_{n\ge0}$, $z$ the core.
- $\mathcal{S}_A(T^2)$: Frohman–Gelca's product-to-sum formula, with $(p,q)_T$ the Chebyshev-normalized $(p,q)$-curve,
$$(p,q)_T\cdot(r,s)_T \;=\; A^{\,ps-qr}\,(p+r,q+s)_T \;+\; A^{-(ps-qr)}\,(p-r,q-s)_T .$$

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

Skein modules were introduced independently by **Józef Przytycki** (1987, published as *Skein modules of 3-manifolds*, Bull. Polish Acad. Sci. 1991) and **Vladimir Turaev** (1988), as a way to extend the Jones polynomial from $S^3$ to arbitrary 3-manifolds and to quantize the ring of $SL_2$ trace functions. Przytycki's 1999 Kobe survey *Fundamentals of Kauffman bracket skein modules* set the research programme: compute $K(M)$, decide freeness, locate torsion, decide finite generation.

Hoste and Przytycki computed the first non-trivial closed examples: $K(S^1\times S^2)$ (1995) and $K(L(p,q))$ (1993). Bullock (1997, 1999) established the character-variety limit and proved that $K(M)$ is finitely generated over $\mathbb{Q}(A)$ modulo a conjecture on the classical limit — in effect reducing finiteness to a statement about $X(M)$.

**Witten** conjectured the generic-coefficient statement in *Fivebranes and knots* (Quantum Topology, 2012), motivated by the expectation that skein modules compute the space of states of a 4d gauge theory on $M\times\mathbb{R}$, which physics requires to be finite dimensional.

**SOTA:** Gunningham–Jordan–Safronov (Inventiones 2023) proved Witten's conjecture. Their method: realize $K(M)\otimes\mathbb{Q}(A)$ as factorization homology of $M$ with coefficients in the ribbon category $\mathrm{Rep}_q(SL_2)$ (Ben-Zvi–Brochier–Jordan), filter it so the associated graded is a coherent sheaf on the character stack supported in the intersection $X(H_1)\cap X(H_2)$, and use that the intersection of two Lagrangians in a symplectic stack, combined with a **holonomicity** argument for the resulting module over the quantized character variety, forces finite support and hence finite rank. The argument is uniform in the gauge group and yields finiteness for $\mathfrak{g}$-skein modules generally.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

Explicit computations, all over $R=\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm1}]$ unless stated:

| Manifold | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| $S^3$ | $K=R$, rank 1, free | Kauffman / Przytycki |
| $S^1\times D^2$ | free, basis $\{z^n\}_{n\ge0}$ | Przytycki 1991 |
| $\Sigma_g\times I$ | free on multicurves | Przytycki 1999 |
| $L(p,q)$, $p\ge1$ | free of rank $\lfloor p/2\rfloor+1$ | Hoste–Przytycki 1993 |
| $S^1\times S^2$ | $R\oplus\bigoplus_{i\ge1}R/(1-A^{2i+4})$ — infinitely generated, all torsion | Hoste–Przytycki 1995 |
| $T^3$ | $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}(A)}K=9$ | Carrega 2017; Gilmer 2018 |
| $\Sigma_g\times S^1$ | $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}(A)}K=2^{2g+1}+2g-1$ | Detcherry–Wolff 2021 |
| prism manifolds, $\mathbb{RP}^3\\#\mathbb{RP}^3$, twisted $I$-bundles | explicit bases with torsion | Mroczkowski (2011–2019) |
| all closed oriented $M$ | $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}(A)}K(M)<\infty$ | Gunningham–Jordan–Safronov 2023 |

Structural results: Przytycki proved $K(M_1\\# M_2)\cong K(M_1)\otimes_R K(M_2)$ when the connected sum is along a sphere, and that $K$ of a manifold with a compressible boundary splits along the compression. Bullock's *A finite set of generators for the Kauffman bracket skein algebra* (Math. Z. 231, 1999) shows $\mathcal{S}_A(\Sigma)$ is finitely generated **as an algebra**; Przytycki–Sikora and Bullock–Przytycki give explicit generators for genus $\le2$.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No diagrammatic finiteness engine.** Over $\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm1}]$ one reduces links by resolving crossings and eliminating trivial components, but in a general $M$ there is no bound on the complexity of a link needed to represent a given class: essential surfaces let links wind arbitrarily, and each winding number produces a genuinely new generator (this is exactly the $S^1\times S^2$ phenomenon).
- **Torsion is invisible generically and hard integrally.** Elements killed by $1-A^{2k}$ vanish over $\mathbb{Q}(A)$. GJS's proof therefore says nothing about $R$-module structure; the cyclotomic factors that appear in every known computation have no conceptual explanation.
- **Character varieties are singular and non-reduced in families.** The Lagrangian-intersection heuristic requires smooth loci; deformations of reducible representations, and the failure of $X(M)$ to be reduced, break naive dimension counts.
- **Localization loses information.** Factorization homology and quantum-group techniques work over $\mathbb{C}(q)$ or at roots of unity; they do not descend to $\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm1}]$ because the relevant categories are not defined integrally with the needed finiteness (the quantum $SL_2$ Verlinde-type bounds require invertibility of quantum integers).
- **No effective bound.** The GJS proof is non-constructive: it gives $\dim K(M)<\infty$ without an algorithm or an explicit bound in terms of Heegaard genus.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: finite dimensionality over $\mathbb{Q}(A)$ for closed $M$ (all cases). Open, and the precise boundary:

1. **Integral characterization.** Prove $K(M)$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm1}]$-module when $M$ is compact and contains no closed non-boundary-parallel incompressible surface (equivalently, prove Przytycki's torsion conjecture). No hyperbolic $M$ has had $K(M)$ computed integrally.
2. **Effectivity.** Convert the Lagrangian-support argument into a bound $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}(A)}K(M)\le f(g)$ for Heegaard genus $g$. Even $f$ existing is unknown.
3. **The dimension formula.** Detcherry's conjecture, that for $M$ with finitely many $SL_2(\mathbb{C})$ characters $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}(A)}K(M)=\\#X_{SL_2(\mathbb{C})}(M)$ counted suitably, is verified for lens spaces ($\lfloor p/2\rfloor+1$ characters, matching) and $\Sigma_g\times S^1$, but is open in general.

The missing step for (1) is a JSJ-type decomposition theorem for skein modules: a proof that cutting along an incompressible torus produces a Mayer–Vietoris sequence whose failure to be exact is precisely the torsion.

## 8. Future Work

- Build a **skein-theoretic JSJ / gluing formula**: express $K(M)$ for $M=M_1\cup_T M_2$ in terms of the $\mathcal{S}_A(T^2)$-module structures via a derived tensor product, and read torsion off the $\mathrm{Tor}$ terms.
- Make the Lagrangian-intersection argument **quantitative**, producing $\dim K(M)$ bounds from Heegaard genus or from Kneser–Haken-type surface counts.
- Prove or refute the **dimension = character count** conjecture for hyperbolic manifolds with finitely many characters, the first family where the answer is not forced by an explicit basis.
- Develop **integral factorization homology** for $\mathrm{Rep}_q(SL_2)$ over $\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm1}]$, the natural home of Przytycki's conjecture.
- Compute $K(M)$ integrally for a single closed hyperbolic $M$ (e.g. the Weeks manifold) — currently no example exists.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**$M=S^1\times S^2$: finite dimensional generically, infinitely generated integrally.**

Present $S^1\times S^2$ as $0$-framed surgery on the unknot $U\subset S^3$. Links in $S^1\times S^2$ are represented by links in the complement of $U$, i.e. links in the solid torus $V=S^1\times D^2$, modulo *handle-slide* over the surgery $2$-handle.

Let $x_i\in K(V)=R[z]$ be the class of $i$ parallel copies of the core, so $\{x_i\}_{i\ge0}$ ($x_0=\emptyset$) is a free basis of $K(V)$. The surjection $K(V)\twoheadrightarrow K(S^1\times S^2)$ has kernel generated by the differences $L-\mathrm{sl}(L)$, where $\mathrm{sl}$ slides a strand over the $2$-handle. Computing the slide of the $i$-fold core in the Kauffman bracket skein of the solid torus, and expanding in the Chebyshev-type basis, Hoste and Przytycki obtain exactly the relations

$$(1-A^{2i+4})\,x_i \;=\; 0, \qquad i\ge1,$$

with $x_0=\emptyset$ unconstrained. Hence

$$K(S^1\times S^2)\;\cong\;\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm1}]\;\oplus\;\bigoplus_{i\ge1}\;\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm1}]\big/(1-A^{2i+4}).$$

Two consequences, which delimit exactly the solved and open halves of this entry:

1. **Integrally infinite.** The summands $R/(1-A^{2i+4})$ are non-zero for every $i\ge1$ (e.g. $x_1$ is killed by $1-A^{6}$ but not by any unit, so $x_1\ne0$), and no finite subset of $\{x_i\}$ generates them all. So $K(S^1\times S^2)$ is **not** a finitely generated $\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm1}]$-module. This is consistent with Przytycki's conjecture: $S^1\times S^2$ contains a non-separating incompressible sphere, the predicted source of torsion.
2. **Generically 1-dimensional.** Over $\mathbb{Q}(A)$ the elements $1-A^{2i+4}$ are invertible, so $x_i=0$ for all $i\ge1$ and
$$\dim_{\mathbb{Q}(A)} K(S^1\times S^2)\otimes\mathbb{Q}(A)=1,$$
spanned by $\emptyset$. Cross-check with the character variety: $\pi_1(S^1\times S^2)=\mathbb{Z}$, whose $SL_2(\mathbb{C})$-character variety is $\mathbb{C}$ — infinite, yet the *quantum* count collapses to $1$, showing that the naive "dimension = number of characters" heuristic needs the finiteness hypothesis of Section 6(3).

Contrast $L(5,1)$: $\pi_1=\mathbb{Z}/5$ has $\lfloor 5/2\rfloor+1=3$ characters, and $K(L(5,1))$ is free of rank $3$ over $\mathbb{Z}[A^{\pm1}]$ — no torsion, matching the absence of incompressible surfaces in lens spaces, and dimension equal to the character count.
