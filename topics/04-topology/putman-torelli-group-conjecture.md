# Putman Torelli Group Conjecture

> **Topic:** Topology & Knot Theory · **ID:** `04-topology/putman-torelli-group-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $\Sigma_g$ be a closed orientable surface of genus $g$, $\mathrm{Mod}(\Sigma_g)$ its mapping class group, and $\mathcal{I}_g \le \mathrm{Mod}(\Sigma_g)$ the **Torelli group** — the subgroup acting trivially on $H_1(\Sigma_g;\mathbb{Z})$.

**Conjecture (Putman).** For every $k \ge 0$ there is a genus bound $G(k)$ such that for all $g \ge G(k)$ the homology group $H_k(\mathcal{I}_g;\mathbb{Z})$ is finitely generated. Equivalently, $\mathcal{I}_g$ has finiteness type $F_k$ once the genus is large relative to the homological degree.

**Strong (representation-stable) form.** For $g \ge G(k)$, the $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})$-representation $H_k(\mathcal{I}_g;\mathbb{Q})$ is *algebraic*: it is the restriction of a finite-dimensional rational representation of the algebraic group $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}$, and its decomposition into irreducibles is independent of $g$ when highest weights are written in the stable coordinates.

This is the conjecture Putman formulates in his PCMI lecture notes on the Torelli group and develops in the "partial Torelli group" program. The case $k = 2$ contains the classical question of Mess and Farb: **is $\mathcal{I}_g$ finitely presented for $g \ge 3$?**

A complete proof must produce, for each $k$, an explicit $G(k)$ and a finiteness argument valid for all $g \ge G(k)$. A disproof requires exhibiting some $k$ and an infinite sequence of genera $g \to \infty$ with $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}} H_k(\mathcal{I}_g;\mathbb{Q}) = \infty$ — which would be a strong negative statement, since it would show no stable range exists at all.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Write $H = H_1(\Sigma_g;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{2g}$, equipped with the algebraic intersection form
$$\hat{\imath} : H \times H \longrightarrow \mathbb{Z}, \qquad \hat{\imath}(a,b) = -\hat{\imath}(b,a),$$
a unimodular symplectic pairing. The action on $H$ gives the **symplectic representation**
$$1 \longrightarrow \mathcal{I}_g \longrightarrow \mathrm{Mod}(\Sigma_g) \xrightarrow{\ \sigma\ } \mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}) \longrightarrow 1,$$
with $\sigma$ surjective (a classical consequence of the action on $H$ by transvections). Since $\mathcal{I}_g \trianglelefteq \mathrm{Mod}(\Sigma_g)$, conjugation makes each $H_k(\mathcal{I}_g;\mathbb{Z})$ a module over $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})$: this is the extra structure the conjecture exploits.

**Johnson homomorphism.** Contracting with the symplectic form gives an $\mathrm{Sp}$-equivariant surjection $\wedge^3 H \to H$, and Johnson's homomorphism is
$$\tau : \mathcal{I}_g \longrightarrow \wedge^3 H / H, \qquad \ker \tau =: \mathcal{K}_g,$$
the **Johnson kernel**, generated by Dehn twists about separating curves for $g \ge 3$. Rationally,
$$H_1(\mathcal{I}_g;\mathbb{Q}) \cong \left(\wedge^3 H_{\mathbb{Q}}\right)/H_{\mathbb{Q}} \cong V_{\omega_3}, \qquad \dim = \binom{2g}{3} - 2g,$$
where $V_{\omega_3}$ is the irreducible $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}$-representation of highest weight $\omega_3$. This is the $k=1$ case of the strong form: one irreducible, constant in $g$.

**Johnson filtration.** Let $\pi = \pi_1(\Sigma_g)$ with lower central series $\pi = \gamma_1 \supseteq \gamma_2 \supseteq \cdots$. Set
$$\mathcal{I}_g(k) = \ker\!\big(\mathrm{Mod}(\Sigma_g) \to \mathrm{Aut}(\pi/\gamma_{k+1})\big),$$
so $\mathcal{I}_g(1) = \mathcal{I}_g$ and $\mathcal{I}_g(2) = \mathcal{K}_g$. The associated graded $\bigoplus_k \mathcal{I}_g(k)/\mathcal{I}_g(k+1) \otimes \mathbb{Q}$ embeds in the free Lie algebra $\mathrm{Der}(\mathbb{L}(H_\mathbb{Q}))$ (Morita, Hain).

**Cohomological dimension.** Bestvina–Bux–Margalit: $\mathrm{cd}(\mathcal{I}_g) = 3g-5$ for $g \ge 2$. Hence the conjecture's bound must satisfy $G(k) > (k+5)/3$, and any $G(k)$ growing at least linearly in $k$ is forced: $H_{3g-5}(\mathcal{I}_g;\mathbb{Z})$ is *infinitely* generated.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1969–1980, Birman, Powell, Johnson.** Torelli groups introduced as the kernel of $\sigma$; Powell (1978) shows $\mathcal{I}_g$ is generated by twists about separating curves and bounding pairs for $g \ge 3$.
- **1983, Johnson.** $\mathcal{I}_g$ is finitely generated for $g \ge 3$ (Annals). This is the $k=0,1$ case.
- **1985, Johnson.** Computes $H_1(\mathcal{I}_g;\mathbb{Z}) = (\wedge^3 H/H) \oplus (\text{2-torsion})$ for $g \ge 3$.
- **1992, Mess.** $\mathcal{I}_2$ is a free group of infinite rank — the base case where every finiteness statement fails.
- **1997, Hain.** Presents the Malcev Lie algebra of $\mathcal{I}_g$ by quadratic relations for $g \ge 6$; the "infinitesimal" analogue of the conjecture holds.
- **2010, Bestvina–Bux–Margalit.** $\mathrm{cd}(\mathcal{I}_g) = 3g-5$; top homology infinitely generated. Establishes that any finiteness must be a *range* statement.
- **2013, Church–Farb.** Formulate representation stability; conjecture $H_k(\mathcal{I}_g;\mathbb{Q})$ is uniformly representation stable — the strong form's antecedent.
- **2013, Dimca–Papadima.** $\mathcal{K}_3$ is not finitely generated, so $H_1(\mathcal{K}_3;\mathbb{Q})$ is infinite-dimensional: low genus genuinely misbehaves.
- **2020, Kassabov–Putman.** $\mathcal{I}_g$ is finitely presented *as an $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})$-group*; hence $H_2(\mathcal{I}_g;\mathbb{Z})$ is a finitely generated $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})$-module for $g \ge 3$. This is the decisive structural reduction.
- **2022, Church–Ershov–Putman.** Every term $\mathcal{I}_g(k)$ of the Johnson filtration is finitely generated for $g$ large relative to $k$ — the conjecture's group-theoretic shadow.
- **2023–2025, Minahan.** $H_2(\mathcal{I}_g;\mathbb{Q})$ is finite-dimensional for $g$ sufficiently large. *(frontier — verify)*

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Case | Status |
|---|---|
| $k = 0$, all $g$ | Trivially true. |
| $k = 1$, $g \ge 3$ | **Proved** (Johnson 1983/85); $G(1) = 3$ is sharp since $H_1(\mathcal{I}_2;\mathbb{Z})$ is infinitely generated. |
| $k = 1$, strong form | **Proved**: $H_1(\mathcal{I}_g;\mathbb{Q}) = V_{\omega_3}$, one irreducible, stable for $g \ge 3$. |
| $k = 2$, $\mathrm{Sp}$-module version | **Proved** (Kassabov–Putman 2020): $H_2(\mathcal{I}_g;\mathbb{Z})$ finitely generated over $\mathbb{Z}[\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})]$. |
| $k = 2$, rational, $g \gg 0$ | **Proved** (Minahan) *(frontier — verify)*; explicit genus bound large (announced in the range $g \ge 51$, later improved). |
| $k = 3g-5$ | **Refuted for the group itself**: $H_{3g-5}(\mathcal{I}_g;\mathbb{Z})$ infinitely generated (BBM 2010), consistent only because $g < G(3g-5)$. |
| Genus $2$, all $k \ge 1$ | **False**: $\mathcal{I}_2$ free of infinite rank. |
| Johnson filtration $\mathcal{I}_g(k)$, $g \gtrsim 2k+4$ | **Finitely generated** (Church–Ershov–Putman 2022; Ershov–He 2018 for $\mathcal{K}_g$, $g \ge 12$). |
| Genus $3$, $k=1$ on $\mathcal{K}_3$ | **Infinite-dimensional** (Dimca–Papadima 2013). |
| Malcev/infinitesimal analogue, $g \ge 6$ | **Proved** (Hain 1997): quadratic presentation of $\mathfrak{t}_g$. |

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No classifying space of finite type is known.** Standard proofs of homological finiteness use a cocompact action on a contractible complex. $\mathcal{I}_g$ acts on Teichmüller space and on the complex of curves, but neither quotient is cocompact for $\mathcal{I}_g$; BBM's proof that $\mathrm{cd} = 3g-5$ shows genuine non-cocompactness at the top.
- **Homological stability machinery does not apply directly.** Harer-type stability for $\mathrm{Mod}(\Sigma_{g,1})$ relies on high connectivity of arc/tether complexes together with transitivity of the group action on simplices. $\mathcal{I}_g$ does not act transitively on isotopy classes of non-separating simple closed curves with a fixed homology class boundary data — the stabilizer bookkeeping breaks. Putman's *partial Torelli groups* were introduced precisely to restore a transitive action, at the cost of tracking a partial-basis parameter.
- **Infinite-index, non-arithmetic subgroup.** $\mathcal{I}_g$ is not a lattice, has no known finite-index-torsion-free arithmetic model, and Margulis-type rigidity, property (T) arguments, and Borel–Serre compactifications are unavailable. $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})$ has property (T) for $g \ge 2$, but this transfers no finiteness to the kernel.
- **The $\mathrm{Sp}$-module reduction is not enough.** Kassabov–Putman give finite generation *over the group ring*; converting to absolute finite generation requires the stabilizers of generators to have finite-index-controlled orbits, and the relevant stabilizers are themselves poorly understood subgroups of $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})$ with non-trivial $H_1$.
- **Low-genus counterexamples poison induction.** Any induction on $g$ must have a base case; $\mathcal{I}_2$ and $\mathcal{K}_3$ both fail finiteness, so the induction must start above the failure locus and the spectral sequences relating genus $g$ to genus $g-1$ carry infinitely generated terms until well into the range.

## 6. The Gap

Proved: (i) $k = 1$ absolutely, all $g \ge 3$; (ii) $k = 2$ as an $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})$-module for all $g \ge 3$, and rationally for $g \gg 0$. Conjectured: absolute finite generation of $H_k$ for *every* $k$ once $g \ge G(k)$.

The precise barrier is the passage from **relative** finiteness (finitely generated as a module over $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})$, or over a suitable $\mathrm{FI}$-type category) to **absolute** finiteness (finite-dimensional over $\mathbb{Q}$). Concretely: one needs a Noetherianity theorem for the category of $\mathrm{Sp}$-equivariant Torelli homology modules — an analogue of the $\mathrm{FI}$-module Noetherianity of Church–Ellenberg–Farb — plus vanishing of the higher $\mathrm{Sp}$-equivariant obstruction groups $H_i(\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}); H_j(\mathcal{I}_g))$ in the relevant bidegrees of the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence
$$E_2^{p,q} = H_p\big(\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z});\, H_q(\mathcal{I}_g;\mathbb{Q})\big) \Longrightarrow H_{p+q}(\mathrm{Mod}(\Sigma_g);\mathbb{Q}).$$
The right-hand side is known (Madsen–Weiss) in the stable range; the conjecture is effectively the statement that the $E_2$-page has finite-dimensional entries in that range, and no current method controls $q \ge 3$.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Notre Dame (Putman and collaborators).** The partial Torelli group program: replacing $\mathcal{I}_g$ by groups $\mathcal{I}(\Sigma, \text{partial basis})$ that admit transitive actions on complexes of curves, yielding homological stability statements that feed back to $\mathcal{I}_g$.
- **Georgia Tech / Vanderbilt (Margalit school).** Minahan's work on $H_2(\mathcal{I}_g;\mathbb{Q})$ via the "double Johnson filtration" and Bieri–Eckmann duality-style arguments; extension attempts to $H_3$ are the current frontier. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Ershov, He, Church.** Finite generation of Johnson filtration terms, with ongoing effort to make genus bounds linear in $k$ and to upgrade generation to presentation.
- **Hain, Dimca, Papadima school (Hodge theory / rational homotopy).** Mixed Hodge structures on $\mathcal{I}_g$; the weight filtration constrains which $\mathrm{Sp}$-irreducibles can appear in $H_k$, ruling out large families a priori.
- **Chan–Galatius–Payne (tropical/graph-complex).** Computations of $H_*(\mathcal{M}_g;\mathbb{Q})$ in top weight give lower bounds on $\mathrm{Mod}(\Sigma_g)$ homology that constrain the abutment of the spectral sequence above.
- **Related conjecture kept adjacent.** The Putman–Wieland conjecture on higher Prym representations (equivalent to Ivanov's conjecture $H^1(\Gamma;\mathbb{Q}) = 0$ for finite-index $\Gamma \le \mathrm{Mod}(\Sigma_g)$, $g \ge 3$) is a separate, also-open Putman conjecture about Torelli-type subgroups; recent work by Landesman–Litt gives strong evidence in the large-genus regime.

## 8. Future Work

1. **Build an $\mathrm{FI}$-like category for symplectic/Torelli data** and prove it is Noetherian; this would convert Kassabov–Putman-style module finiteness into absolute finiteness in one step.
2. **Explicit genus bounds.** Reduce Minahan's $k=2$ bound toward the conjecturally optimal $G(2) = 4$ or $5$, and compute $H_2(\mathcal{I}_4;\mathbb{Q})$ directly.
3. **Settle finite presentability of $\mathcal{I}_g$**, $g \ge 4$ — the headline consequence, listed as Problem 5.1 in Farb's problem list.
4. **Determine the exact failure locus.** For each $k$, find the largest $g$ with $H_k(\mathcal{I}_g;\mathbb{Z})$ infinitely generated; BBM gives $k = 3g-5$, and interpolating between $k=1$ (fails only at $g=2$) and the top degree is wide open.
5. **Compute the $\mathrm{Sp}$-decomposition of $H_2(\mathcal{I}_g;\mathbb{Q})$** stably and compare with Hain's quadratic presentation, which predicts a specific list of irreducibles.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Case $k = 1$: the conjecture is a theorem, and the genus bound is sharp.**

Take $H_\mathbb{Q} = \mathbb{Q}^{2g}$ with symplectic basis $a_1,b_1,\dots,a_g,b_g$, $\hat{\imath}(a_i,b_i)=1$. The contraction $C: \wedge^3 H_\mathbb{Q} \to H_\mathbb{Q}$,
$$C(x \wedge y \wedge z) = \hat{\imath}(x,y)\,z + \hat{\imath}(y,z)\,x + \hat{\imath}(z,x)\,y,$$
is $\mathrm{Sp}$-equivariant and surjective for $g \ge 2$. Hence
$$\dim_\mathbb{Q} \big(\wedge^3 H_\mathbb{Q}/H_\mathbb{Q}\big) = \binom{2g}{3} - 2g.$$

*Genus 3.* $\binom{6}{3} - 6 = 20 - 6 = 14$. Johnson's theorem gives $H_1(\mathcal{I}_3;\mathbb{Q}) \cong V_{\omega_3}$, a $14$-dimensional irreducible $\mathrm{Sp}_6$-representation. Finitely generated ✓, algebraic ✓, one irreducible ✓ — the strong form holds.

*Genus 4.* $\binom{8}{3} - 8 = 56 - 8 = 48$, again exactly $\dim V_{\omega_3}$ for $\mathrm{Sp}_8$. The multiplicity pattern "$V_{\omega_3}$ with multiplicity 1" is unchanged: this is precisely what representation stability asserts.

*Genus 2 — the failure.* $\binom{4}{3} - 4 = 4 - 4 = 0$. The Johnson homomorphism is identically zero, so it detects nothing. And indeed Mess proved $\mathcal{I}_2$ is a free group of infinite rank, generated by the Dehn twists about separating curves, so
$$H_1(\mathcal{I}_2;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \bigoplus_{\infty} \mathbb{Z}$$
is infinitely generated. So $G(1) = 3$ exactly.

*Reading the general conjecture off this case.* The vanishing at $g=2$ is not an accident of small numbers: it says that when the genus is too small relative to the homological degree, the $\mathrm{Sp}$-representation that "should" carry the homology has no room to exist, and the group's homology escapes into an infinitely generated free part. The conjecture asserts this is the only mechanism — that for each $k$ there is a threshold $G(k)$ past which the homology is captured entirely by finitely many algebraic $\mathrm{Sp}_{2g}$-representations. Bestvina–Bux–Margalit's $\mathrm{cd}(\mathcal{I}_g) = 3g-5$ pins the other end: at $k = 3g-5$ the escape always happens, so $G(k) \gtrsim (k+5)/3$.
