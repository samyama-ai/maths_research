# Kronheimer-Mrowka P-property

> **Topic:** Topology & Knot Theory · **ID:** `04-topology/kronheimer-mrowka-p-property` · **Status:** solved-recently

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

**Property P (classical form).** Let $K \subset S^3$ be a nontrivial knot and let $S^3_r(K)$ denote the closed $3$-manifold obtained by Dehn surgery along $K$ with slope $r \in \mathbb{Q} \cup \{\infty\}$. Property P asserts:
$$\pi_1\big(S^3_r(K)\big) \neq 1 \quad \text{for every } r \neq \infty .$$
Equivalently: no surgery on a nontrivial knot produces a homotopy $3$-sphere. Since $H_1(S^3_{p/q}(K)) \cong \mathbb{Z}/p$, the only candidate slopes are $r = 1/n$, $n \in \mathbb{Z}$.

**Kronheimer–Mrowka theorem (2004).** For $K$ nontrivial and $r = \pm 1$, the group $\pi_1(S^3_r(K))$ admits a homomorphism to $SU(2)$ with non-cyclic image. Non-cyclic image forces $\pi_1 \neq 1$, so Property P holds. This is strictly stronger than Property P: it produces an *irreducible* $SU(2)$ representation, not merely nontriviality.

**The residual open problem (the "P-property" in its live form).** For which slopes $r$ does every nontrivial knot satisfy
$$\exists\, \rho : \pi_1(S^3_r(K)) \to SU(2), \quad \operatorname{im}\rho \text{ non-cyclic}\,?$$
Call $r$ an *SU(2)-cyclic slope* for $K$ when no such $\rho$ exists. Proven for $|r| \le 2$; **open** for general $r$. A complete resolution means either (i) a proof that the SU(2)-cyclic slopes of a nontrivial knot are constrained to an explicitly classified list (conjecturally: only lens-space and small-Seifert-fibered surgeries on torus knots and a short list of exceptions), or (ii) a counterexample knot with an unexpected SU(2)-cyclic slope.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Dehn surgery.** For $K \subset S^3$ with exterior $E_K = S^3 \setminus \nu(K)$ and meridian–longitude basis $(\mu,\lambda)$ of $H_1(\partial E_K)$,
$$S^3_{p/q}(K) \;=\; E_K \cup_\varphi (S^1 \times D^2), \qquad \varphi(\partial D^2) \simeq p\mu + q\lambda .$$

**Character variety.** For a closed $3$-manifold $Y$,
$$R(Y) = \operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1 Y, SU(2)), \qquad X(Y) = R(Y)/\!/ SU(2).$$
$Y$ is *SU(2)-cyclic* if every $\rho \in R(Y)$ has cyclic (equivalently abelian, since $SU(2)$ abelian subgroups are cyclic or conjugate into a maximal torus) image. Irreducible $\rho$ correspond to flat non-central $SU(2)$ connections, i.e. critical points of the Chern–Simons functional
$$CS(A) = \frac{1}{8\pi^2}\int_Y \operatorname{tr}\!\left(A \wedge dA + \tfrac{2}{3} A\wedge A \wedge A\right).$$

**Instanton Floer homology.** For an integer homology sphere $Y$, $I_*(Y)$ is the $\mathbb{Z}/8$-graded Morse homology of $CS$ generated by irreducible flat connections; $I_*(Y) \neq 0 \Rightarrow R(Y)$ contains an irreducible. For admissible $SO(3)$ bundles one has $I_*(Y,w)$ with $w \cdot \Sigma$ odd. Floer's surgery exact triangle relates the $0$-, $1$-, and $\infty$-surgeries:
$$\cdots \to I_*(S^3_0(K), w) \to I_*(S^3_1(K)) \to I_*(S^3) \to \cdots$$

**Casson invariant.** For $\Sigma$ an integer homology sphere, $\lambda(\Sigma)$ is a signed count of irreducible $SU(2)$ characters, with surgery formula
$$\lambda\big(S^3_{1/n}(K)\big) = \frac{n}{2}\,\Delta_K''(1),$$
$\Delta_K$ the symmetrized Alexander polynomial normalized by $\Delta_K(1)=1$.

**Ingredients of the 2004 proof.** (a) Gabai: for $K$ nontrivial, $S^3_0(K)$ carries a taut foliation with a closed leaf of genus $g(K) \ge 1$. (b) Eliashberg–Thurston: perturb the taut foliation to a weakly symplectically fillable contact structure. (c) Eliashberg: cap the weak filling to a closed symplectic $4$-manifold $X$ with $b^+ \ge 2$. (d) Taubes: $SW_X \neq 0$. (e) Feehan–Leness / Witten's conjecture: nonvanishing $SW$ implies nonvanishing Donaldson invariants, hence $I_*(S^3_0(K), w) \neq 0$. (f) Floer's triangle then forces irreducible flat connections on $S^3_{\pm 1}(K)$.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1971** — Bing and Martin, *Cubes with knotted holes*, name Property P and verify it for large families.
- **1987** — Culler–Gordon–Luecke–Shalen prove the Cyclic Surgery Theorem: cyclic-$\pi_1$ slopes on a non-torus knot exterior sit at mutual distance $\le 1$. Consequence: Property P reduces to $r = \pm 1$; all $1/n$ with $|n| \ge 2$ are settled.
- **1989** — Gordon–Luecke: knots are determined by their complements; no nontrivial surgery gives $S^3$ itself.
- **1987–90** — Gabai's sutured manifold hierarchy: taut foliations on $S^3_0(K)$ for nontrivial $K$; Property R.
- **2004** — Kronheimer–Mrowka, *Witten's conjecture and Property P* (Geom. Topol. 8): full proof for $r = \pm 1$.
- **2004** — Kronheimer–Mrowka, *Dehn surgery, knots and SU(2)* (Math. Res. Lett. 11): irreducible $SU(2)$ representations for all rational $r$ with $|r| \le 2$.
- **2010–2011** — Kronheimer–Mrowka's sutured instanton homology $KHI$ and the reduced instanton homology $I^\natural$: $\dim I^\natural(K) = 1 \iff K$ unknotted; Khovanov homology detects the unknot.
- **2018** — Zentner: every closed integer homology $3$-sphere other than $S^3$ admits an irreducible $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ representation.
- **2020s** — Baldwin–Sivek and Sivek–Zentner develop instanton and pillowcase-holonomy methods classifying SU(2)-cyclic surgeries.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Class / parameter range | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| $r = 1/n$, $|n| \ge 2$, $K$ non-torus | Property P proven | CGLS 1987 |
| $K$ a torus knot $T_{p,q}$ | All surgeries explicit Seifert fibered; $\pi_1 \ne 1$ | classical |
| $\Delta_K''(1) \neq 0$ (e.g. all knots with $\lambda \ne 0$) | $\lambda(S^3_{1/n}(K)) \ne 0$ $\Rightarrow$ irreducible $SU(2)$ rep | Casson surgery formula |
| $K$ satellite, or $K$ with an essential surface constraint | Property P | Gabai; Gordon 1980s |
| $r = \pm 1$, all nontrivial $K$ | Property P + irreducible $SU(2)$ rep | Kronheimer–Mrowka 2004 |
| all $r \in \mathbb{Q}$, $|r| \le 2$ | irreducible $SU(2)$ rep | Kronheimer–Mrowka, MRL 2004 |
| $r$ a slope with $S^3_r(K)$ an instanton L-space | strong structural restrictions; $K$ fibered, $r \ge 2g(K)-1$ | Baldwin–Sivek 2022 |
| $Y$ any homology sphere $\ne S^3$ | irreducible $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ rep | Zentner 2018 |

Known SU(2)-cyclic examples exist and bound the possible theorem: $S^3_{5}(T_{2,3}) = L(5,4)$ has cyclic $\pi_1 = \mathbb{Z}/5$, so the hypothesis $|r| \le 2$ cannot be dropped naively.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Gauge theory is not a slope-uniform tool.** Floer's exact triangle links $\{0, 1, \infty\}$-surgeries; the $|r| \le 2$ extension needs a delicate handle on $I^\natural$ and holonomy perturbations. For $|r| > 2$ the triangle no longer separates the reducible from the irreducible locus, and the algebra loses the needed nonvanishing.
- **Genuine counterexamples at large slopes.** Lens space surgeries on torus knots are SU(2)-cyclic, so any general statement must first classify these — a classification problem (which knots admit lens space surgeries: Berge's list, still conjecturally complete) that is itself open.
- **Reducibles dominate.** For $|r|$ large, $H_1 = \mathbb{Z}/p$ is big and abelian representations proliferate; separating an irreducible from the reducible stratum requires transversality on a singular character variety where $SU(2)$ stabilizers jump.
- **Classical $3$-manifold topology is insufficient.** CGLS-style essential surface arguments produce *cyclic* obstructions, not $SU(2)$-representation obstructions; a group can be non-cyclic, even infinite, and admit only cyclic $SU(2)$ images (e.g. some graph manifold groups).
- **Witten's conjecture input is heavy.** The 2004 proof depends on the $SW$–Donaldson correspondence (Feehan–Leness), whose hypotheses (simple type, $b^+ \ge 2$) do not survive naive generalization; no self-contained instanton-only proof of Property P is known.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: irreducibility for all $|r| \le 2$. General target: a classification of SU(2)-cyclic slopes for arbitrary nontrivial $K$. The precise unbridged step is the passage from the surgery exact triangle at distance-$1$ slopes to *arbitrary* rational slopes. Concretely, one wants an instanton-theoretic invariant $\mathcal{I}(K,r)$ whose nonvanishing certifies an irreducible $SU(2)$ character and which is computable for all $r$, in the way $\widehat{HF}$ rank behaves under rational surgery. The Heegaard Floer surgery formula supplies exactly this bookkeeping but sees only abelian information; instanton theory sees $SU(2)$ but lacks the formula.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Instanton knot homology and detection** (Kronheimer–Mrowka; Baldwin–Sivek, Boston College / Imperial): $KHI$, $I^\natural$, and detection results for the trefoils, figure-eight, cinquefoil. The technology that proves detection is the same that constrains SU(2)-cyclic surgeries.
- **Pillowcase / holonomy perturbation methods** (Sivek, Zentner, Hedden–Herald–Kirk): counting traceless $SU(2)$ representations of tangles via immersed curves in the pillowcase $\mathcal{P} = T^2/\pm 1$; used to bound the number of SU(2)-cyclic slopes of a nontrivial knot by a small constant. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Instanton L-space program** (Baldwin–Sivek): conjecture that instanton L-spaces coincide with Heegaard Floer L-spaces and with non-left-orderable / no-taut-foliation manifolds; would tie SU(2)-cyclicity to the L-space conjecture. *(frontier — verify)*
- **$SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ side** (Zentner, Regensburg): extending irreducibility from homology spheres to all rational homology spheres and to links.
- **Property P for links** remains open in general; partial results via sutured instanton homology of links. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Future Work

- Prove a rational-slope surgery formula for framed instanton homology $I^\sharp(S^3_{p/q}(K))$ matching the Ozsváth–Szabó mapping cone; this would extend irreducibility past $|r| = 2$ mechanically.
- Classify all SU(2)-cyclic $3$-manifolds; conjecturally these are precisely lens spaces and a bounded family of Seifert-fibered spaces.
- Remove the dependence on Witten's conjecture: give a direct proof that $I_*(S^3_0(K), w) \ne 0$ from a taut foliation, via contact instanton invariants.
- Extend to links: show that surgery on a nonsplit link with nontrivial exterior never yields a homotopy sphere.
- Determine whether the number of SU(2)-cyclic slopes of a nontrivial knot is bounded by $3$, matching the exceptional-surgery count.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take $K = T_{2,3}$, the right-handed trefoil, $\Delta_K(t) = t - 1 + t^{-1}$, $g(K) = 1$.

**Step 1 — Casson invariant.** $\Delta_K''(1) = 2$, so
$$\lambda\big(S^3_{1/n}(K)\big) = \frac{n}{2}\cdot 2 = n .$$
For every $n \neq 0$ this is nonzero, hence $S^3_{1/n}(K)$ has an irreducible $SU(2)$ character and $\pi_1 \neq 1$. Property P for the trefoil follows from Casson theory alone — no gauge theory needed.

**Step 2 — Identify the manifolds.** Classical Seifert-fibered identifications give
$$S^3_{1/n}(T_{2,3}) = \Sigma(2,3,6n-1), \qquad S^3_{-1/n}(T_{2,3}) = \Sigma(2,3,6n+1).$$
So $S^3_{+1}(T_{2,3}) = \Sigma(2,3,5)$, the Poincaré sphere, and $S^3_{-1}(T_{2,3}) = \Sigma(2,3,7)$.

**Step 3 — Explicit representation.** $\pi_1(\Sigma(2,3,5)) \cong 2I$, the binary icosahedral group, of order $120$, which *is* a finite subgroup of $SU(2)$. The inclusion $2I \hookrightarrow SU(2)$ is the required non-cyclic representation. Counting: $\Sigma(2,3,5)$ has exactly $2$ irreducible $SU(2)$ characters, consistent with $\lambda(\Sigma(2,3,5)) = -1$ and Fintushel–Stern's formula $\\#\{\text{irreducibles}\} = -2\lambda$. The same count holds for $\Sigma(2,3,7)$, whose $\pi_1$ is infinite.

**Step 4 — Where the general theorem is needed and where it stops.** At slope $r = 5$, $S^3_5(T_{2,3}) = L(5,4)$, with $\pi_1 = \mathbb{Z}/5$ cyclic: an SU(2)-cyclic slope. Since $|5| > 2$, this is outside the Kronheimer–Mrowka range and shows the range is not an artifact of proof technique — the truth changes. The open problem of Section 1 is exactly to say which knots have such slopes and how many.
