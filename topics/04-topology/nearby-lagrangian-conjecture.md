# Nearby Lagrangian Conjecture

> **Topic:** Topology & Knot Theory · **ID:** `04-topology/nearby-lagrangian-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $N$ be a closed, connected, smooth manifold of dimension $n$, and let $T^*N$ carry its canonical symplectic form. **Conjecture (Arnold).** Every closed exact Lagrangian submanifold $L \subset T^*N$ is Hamiltonian isotopic to the zero section $0_N$.

"Exact" means the restriction of the Liouville $1$-form to $L$ is exact: $\theta|_L = df$ for some $f \in C^\infty(L)$. A complete proof must produce, for an arbitrary such $L$, a compactly supported Hamiltonian $H_t$ whose time-$1$ flow carries $0_N$ to $L$. A disproof requires one closed exact $L \subset T^*N$ not in the Hamiltonian orbit of $0_N$ — for instance one that is not diffeomorphic to $N$, or one that is smoothly isotopic to $0_N$ but symplectically distinguishable.

Three progressively weaker statements are standard checkpoints:

- **(H)** the projection $\pi|_L : L \to N$ is a homotopy equivalence;
- **(D)** $L$ is diffeomorphic to $N$;
- **(L)** $L$ is Lagrangian isotopic (not necessarily Hamiltonian) to $0_N$.

(H) is a theorem; (D) and (L) are open in general, and the full conjecture is open for every $N$ with $\dim N \geq 3$.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Write local coordinates $(q_1,\dots,q_n,p_1,\dots,p_n)$ on $T^*N$. The tautological $1$-form and symplectic form are
$$\theta = \sum_{i=1}^n p_i\, dq_i, \qquad \omega = d\theta = \sum_{i=1}^n dp_i \wedge dq_i .$$
A submanifold $L^n \subset T^*N$ is **Lagrangian** if $\omega|_L = 0$, equivalently $d(\theta|_L) = 0$, so $[\theta|_L] \in H^1(L;\mathbb{R})$ is defined; $L$ is **exact** iff this class vanishes. If $H^1(L;\mathbb{R}) = 0$, exactness is automatic. The **Liouville flow** $\varphi_t(q,p) = (q, e^t p)$ satisfies $\varphi_t^*\theta = e^t\theta$; exactness is the condition preserved under Hamiltonian isotopy, and the class $[\theta|_L]$ is the obstruction that makes the non-exact case genuinely different (e.g. $S^1 \subset T^*\mathbb{R} = \mathbb{R}^2$ of enclosed area $\pi$).

**Maslov class.** A choice of almost complex structure compatible with $\omega$ and a Lagrangian splitting identifies the bundle of Lagrangian planes with $U(n)/O(n)$. The Gauss map $\mathcal{G}_L : L \to U(n)/O(n)$ sends $x \mapsto T_xL$; since $H^1(U(n)/O(n);\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$ generated by $\det^2$, one sets
$$\mu_L = \mathcal{G}_L^*(\det{}^2) \in H^1(L;\mathbb{Z}),$$
the **Maslov class**. Its stable version uses $\mathcal{G}_L^{st} : L \to U/O$, whose homotopy class is the relevant refined invariant.

**Floer-theoretic input.** For $L_0, L_1$ closed exact Lagrangians in a Liouville manifold, Floer cohomology $HF^*(L_0,L_1)$ is well defined (no disc bubbling, by exactness) and invariant under Hamiltonian isotopy, with $HF^*(L,L) \cong H^*(L)$ when $L$ is exact and displaceability fails. Two exact Lagrangians in $T^*N$ that are not isomorphic in the Fukaya category $\mathcal{F}(T^*N)$ cannot be Hamiltonian isotopic; the difficulty is the converse.

**Microlocal side.** Nadler–Zaslow and Guillermou relate $\mathcal{F}(T^*N)$ to the category $\mathrm{Sh}(N)$ of constructible sheaves via the singular support $SS(\mathcal{F}) \subset T^*N$; a nearby Lagrangian $L$ is "quantized" by a sheaf $\mathcal{F}_L$ with $SS(\mathcal{F}_L) = L$, which is a rank-one local system in the simply connected case.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

Arnold posed the question in the 1960s–80s; it appears in "First steps in symplectic topology" (1986) alongside the Arnold conjectures. Gromov's 1985 pseudo-holomorphic curve paper supplied the first hard constraint: there is **no** closed exact Lagrangian in $\mathbb{C}^n = T^*\mathbb{R}^n$, so nearby Lagrangians in $T^*N$ can never be contained in a Darboux ball. Lalonde–Sikorav (1991) gave the first systematic results for cotangent bundles of surfaces and showed exact Lagrangians cannot be displaced off the zero section.

Milestones: Hind (2004) resolved $T^*S^2$ up to Hamiltonian isotopy; Fukaya–Seidel–Smith (2008) and independently Nadler (2009) proved that a spin nearby Lagrangian with vanishing Maslov class in a simply connected $T^*N$ projects by a homology equivalence; Abouzaid (2012) upgraded this to a homotopy equivalence assuming $\mu_L = 0$; Kragh (2013) proved $\mu_L = 0$ unconditionally by a parametrized-stable-homotopy Viterbo transfer, closing statement **(H)** in general. Abouzaid–Kragh (2018) removed the Whitehead torsion: $\pi|_L$ is a *simple* homotopy equivalence.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **$n = 1$:** complete. Every closed exact curve in $T^*S^1$ is Hamiltonian isotopic to $0_{S^1}$ (elementary area argument; see §10).
- **$n = 2$:** $N = S^2$ complete (Hind 2004, via $S^2\times S^2$ and holomorphic foliations). $N = T^2$ complete (Dimitroglou Rizell–Goodman–Ivrii 2016: exact Lagrangian tori in $T^*T^2$ are Hamiltonian isotopic to the zero section). For genus $g \geq 2$, (H) plus surface classification gives (D); the Hamiltonian statement is not settled in the same generality.
- **All $N$ closed:** $\pi|_L : L \to N$ is a simple homotopy equivalence (Abouzaid–Kragh, *Acta Math.* 2018); in particular $\pi_1(L)\cong\pi_1(N)$, $\chi(L)=\chi(N)$, and $\pi|_L$ has degree $\pm 1$.
- **Maslov class:** $\mu_L = 0$ for every closed exact $L \subset T^*N$ (Kragh 2013).
- **Stable tangential data:** $\pi|_L$ is covered by a stable tangential equivalence, so $L$ and $N$ have the same stable normal bundle data (Abouzaid–Kragh, *J. Topol.* 2016). Hence the only remaining smooth obstruction lives in the surgery structure set $\mathcal{S}(N)$.
- **Spheres:** if an exotic $n$-sphere $\Sigma$ embeds as an exact Lagrangian in $T^*S^n$, then $\Sigma$ bounds a parallelizable manifold — for $n \equiv 1 \pmod 4$ this rules out infinitely many exotic spheres (Abouzaid, *Ann. of Math.* 2012). Ekholm–Kragh–Smith (2016) extend such restrictions using stable homotopy of the Gauss map.
- **$K(\pi,1)$ bases:** for $N$ aspherical with $\dim N \geq 5$ satisfying the Borel conjecture, (H) + simple homotopy equivalence $\Rightarrow$ (D) by topological rigidity, though smooth structures still require care.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Floer theory sees only the algebra.** $HF^*$, symplectic cohomology, and the Fukaya category detect $L$ up to quasi-isomorphism of objects. Knowing $L \cong 0_N$ in $\mathcal{F}(T^*N)$ does not produce a Hamiltonian isotopy — no "generation implies isotopy" mechanism exists. This is the structural reason results stop at (H).
- **No $h$-principle for exact Lagrangian isotopy.** Lagrangian *immersions* obey the Gromov–Lees $h$-principle, so all flexible-topology input is already consumed; embedded exact Lagrangians are rigid and outside the reach of $h$-principle techniques, except in the recent twisted-generating-function form.
- **Generating functions do not exist a priori.** For Lagrangians produced by Hamiltonian isotopy from $0_N$, Chaperon–Laudenbach–Sikorav give a generating function quadratic at infinity, and Viterbo's invariants then force the conclusion. For an abstract nearby $L$, existence of a (untwisted) generating function is unknown and is essentially equivalent to the hard part of the conjecture.
- **Surgery-theoretic gap.** Even granting simple homotopy equivalence and matching stable tangential structure, distinct smooth manifolds can share these data; ruling them out needs input from $\mathcal{S}(N)$ that symplectic topology has not yet supplied.
- **Non-simply-connected bases.** Local systems on $L$ that are not rank one over $\pi_1$-covers break the sheaf-quantization arguments' simplest form; Guillermou's machinery handles much but not the isotopy statement.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: $\pi|_L$ is a simple homotopy equivalence, $\mu_L = 0$, and the stable Gauss map is trivialized. Conjectured: a Hamiltonian isotopy. The exact missing step has two layers.

1. **From homotopy to diffeomorphism.** One needs $L \cong N$ as smooth manifolds, i.e. that the class of $\pi|_L$ in the smooth structure set $\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{Diff}}(N)$ is trivial. Currently no symplectic invariant obstructs a nontrivial class.
2. **From diffeomorphism to Hamiltonian isotopy.** Even given $L \cong N$ smoothly, one must construct the isotopy. The plausible route is: show $L$ admits a generating function quadratic at infinity (untwisted), then apply Viterbo's spectral-invariant uniqueness. Abouzaid–Courte–Guillermou–Kragh produce a *twisted* generating function; removing the twist — a stable-homotopy-theoretic $J$-homomorphism problem — is the concrete open barrier.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Twisted generating functions.** Abouzaid–Courte–Guillermou–Kragh (arXiv:2011.13178) show every nearby Lagrangian admits a twisted generating function, recovering and strengthening the Gauss-map results. Work on untwisting via $J$-homomorphism vanishing is the most direct current attack. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Microlocal sheaves.** Guillermou's programme (sheaf quantization of exact Lagrangians, *Astérisque*-length monograph, arXiv:1905.07341) plus Jin's "Microlocal sheaf categories and the $J$-homomorphism" (arXiv:2004.14270) recast the remaining obstruction as a question about $\mathrm{Pic}$-valued local systems and $U/O$-classifying maps. Groups: IMJ-PRG/Paris-Saclay (Guillermou, Viterbo, Courte), Northwestern (Nadler, Jin).
- **Stable homotopy / Floer homotopy.** Kragh (Copenhagen/Oslo) and Abouzaid (Stanford) continue Viterbo-transfer-of-spectra methods; the target is spectral refinement of the structure-set obstruction.
- **Low dimensions and holomorphic curves.** Dimitroglou Rizell, Evans, Hind and collaborators pursue $n=3$ cases ($T^*S^3$, $T^*T^3$) with neck-stretching and SFT. No $n=3$ case is closed. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Arborealization.** Álvarez-Gavela–Eliashberg–Nadler's normal forms for Lagrangian skeleta feed combinatorial models of $\mathcal{F}(T^*N)$ that may make isotopy statements accessible. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Future Work

- Prove the twist in the generating function is null, yielding a genuine generating function quadratic at infinity and hence, via Viterbo's uniqueness theory, the full conjecture for simply connected $N$.
- Construct a symplectic invariant that lands in $\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{Diff}}(N)$, closing the diffeomorphism gap directly rather than through homotopy theory.
- Settle $T^*S^3$ and $T^*T^3$: the first dimension where neither surface techniques nor surgery rigidity applies cleanly.
- Extend the Dimitroglou Rizell–Goodman–Ivrii Lagrangian-isotopy method (foliations by holomorphic discs after compactifying) beyond dimension two.
- Determine whether the conjecture can fail for non-orientable $N$ or for $N$ with large $\pi_1$, where the sheaf-theoretic dictionary is weakest.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**The conjecture for $N = S^1$.** Here $T^*S^1 = S^1 \times \mathbb{R}$ with coordinates $(q \bmod 2\pi, p)$, $\theta = p\,dq$, $\omega = dp\wedge dq$. A Lagrangian submanifold is any embedded closed curve $L$ (dimension $1 = \tfrac12\dim$, and $\omega|_L=0$ automatically).

*Step 1: $L$ is not null-homotopic.* Suppose $L$ bounds an embedded disc $D \subset S^1\times\mathbb{R}$. By Stokes,
$$\mathrm{Area}(D) = \int_D \omega = \oint_L \theta = \oint_L df = 0,$$
using exactness. But an embedded closed curve bounds a region of strictly positive area. Contradiction. (This is the $n=1$ shadow of Gromov's theorem that $\mathbb{C}^n$ contains no closed exact Lagrangian.)

*Step 2: degree.* An embedded non-contractible closed curve in the annulus is smoothly isotopic to the core circle and satisfies $\deg(\pi|_L) = \pm 1$ — matching the general theorem that $\pi|_L$ is a homotopy equivalence.

*Step 3: $L$ is a graph after isotopy, with zero net area.* Isotope $L$ to a graph $p = u(q)$ (possible in the annulus). Exactness gives
$$\oint_L \theta = \int_0^{2\pi} u(q)\,dq = 0 .$$
So the signed area between $L$ and $0_{S^1}$ vanishes.

*Step 4: Hamiltonian isotopy.* Let $U(q) = \int_0^q u$, well defined on $S^1$ precisely because $\int_0^{2\pi}u = 0$. Take $H_t(q,p) = -U(q)$, a genuine function on $T^*S^1$ (cut off outside a compact set). Its Hamiltonian flow is $(q,p)\mapsto (q, p + t\,u(q))$, which carries $0_{S^1}$ to $L$ at $t=1$. Hence $L$ is Hamiltonian isotopic to the zero section. $\square$

**Where this breaks down.** The whole argument rests on Step 3: a one-dimensional exact Lagrangian is automatically a graph, so its primitive $U$ *is* a generating function. For $n \geq 2$ no such statement is available — $L$ generally has caustics and is nowhere near a graph — and producing a generating function quadratic at infinity for an arbitrary nearby Lagrangian is exactly the open problem of §6. Compare also the non-exact case: the circle $p^2+q^2 = 1$ in $T^*\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{R}^2$ is Lagrangian with $\oint \theta = \pi \neq 0$ and Maslov class $\mu = 2$, so exactness (which forces $\mu = 0$ by Kragh's theorem) is not a cosmetic hypothesis.
