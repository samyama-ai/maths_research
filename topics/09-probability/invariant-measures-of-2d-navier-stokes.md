# Invariant Measures of 2D Navier-Stokes

> **Topic:** Probability & Stochastic Processes · **ID:** `09-probability/invariant-measures-of-2d-navier-stokes` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Consider the 2D Navier–Stokes equations on the torus $\mathbb{T}^2 = [0,2\pi]^2$ driven by a white-in-time random force. The Markov semigroup generated by the solution acts on probability measures on the phase space $H = \{u \in L^2(\mathbb{T}^2;\mathbb{R}^2) : \nabla\cdot u = 0,\ \int u = 0\}$. The central questions are:

1. **Existence.** Does an invariant probability measure $\mu$ exist for the transition semigroup $P_t$, i.e. $\mu P_t = \mu$ for all $t \ge 0$? *(Answered: yes, for any square-summable trace-class forcing.)*
2. **Uniqueness and ergodicity.** Is $\mu$ unique, and does $P_t^*\nu \to \mu$ weakly for every initial law $\nu$? In particular, is uniqueness true when the noise excites only **finitely many** Fourier modes — so that the noise is degenerate and the Markov process is not strong Feller?
3. **Quantitative structure.** What are the properties of $\mu$: rate of mixing, regularity/absolute continuity of finite-dimensional marginals, Lyapunov exponents, tail decay of the energy spectrum, and the behaviour of $\mu = \mu_\nu$ in the inviscid limit $\nu \to 0$?

A complete resolution requires: (a) unique ergodicity under a *minimal* and *sharp* hypothesis on the range of the noise covariance, (b) a description of the small-scale statistics of $\mu_\nu$ matching or refuting the Kraichnan dual-cascade picture, and (c) identification of the limit points of $\{\mu_\nu\}_{\nu>0}$ as $\nu\to 0$ (the "Kuksin measures") as invariant measures of the 2D Euler equations.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Write the equation in vorticity form. With $w = \nabla^\perp \cdot u = \partial_1 u_2 - \partial_2 u_1$ and mean-zero data,

$$ dw + \big( u\cdot\nabla w - \nu \Delta w \big)\, dt = \sum_{k \in \mathcal{Z}} q_k\, e_k \, dW_t^k, \qquad u = K * w, $$

where $K$ is the Biot–Savart kernel ($\hat{u}(k) = i k^\perp |k|^{-2} \hat{w}(k)$), $\{e_k\}$ is the real Fourier basis of $L^2_0(\mathbb{T}^2)$, $\{W^k\}$ are independent standard Brownian motions, $q_k \in \mathbb{R}$, and $\mathcal{Z} \subset \mathbb{Z}^2\setminus\{0\}$ is the set of **forced modes**. The noise covariance is $Q = \sum_k q_k^2 e_k \otimes e_k$; its energy and enstrophy injection rates are

$$ \mathcal{E}_0 = \sum_{k\in\mathcal{Z}} \frac{q_k^2}{|k|^2}, \qquad \mathcal{E}_1 = \sum_{k\in\mathcal{Z}} q_k^2 . $$

**Well-posedness and the Markov semigroup.** For $w_0 \in L^2$ and $\mathcal{E}_1 < \infty$, the equation has a unique global mild/probabilistically strong solution with continuous paths in $L^2$ and $w \in L^2_{loc}((0,\infty); H^1)$; the solution map defines a Feller Markov semigroup $(P_t\varphi)(w_0) = \mathbb{E}\,\varphi(w(t;w_0))$ on $C_b(L^2)$.

**Itô energy balance.** Applying Itô's formula to $\|w\|_{L^2}^2$ and using $\langle u\cdot\nabla w, w\rangle = 0$:

$$ d\|w\|^2_{L^2} = -2\nu\|\nabla w\|^2_{L^2}\,dt + \mathcal{E}_1\, dt + 2\sum_k q_k \langle w, e_k\rangle\, dW^k_t . $$

Since $\|\nabla w\| \ge \|w\|$ on $\mathbb{T}^2$ (lowest eigenvalue $1$), this gives the Lyapunov function bound and, in stationarity, the exact **enstrophy balance** $\;\mathbb{E}_\mu\, 2\nu\|\nabla w\|^2 = \mathcal{E}_1$, together with $\mathbb{E}_\mu\, 2\nu \|w\|^2 = \mathcal{E}_0$ (energy balance).

**Existence (Krylov–Bogoliubov).** The above bound gives $\sup_{t} \mathbb{E}\|w(t)\|^2 < \infty$ plus compactness of the embedding $H^1 \hookrightarrow L^2$, so time-averaged laws are tight and any weak limit is invariant.

**Uniqueness criteria.** Two structural tools dominate:
- *Asymptotic strong Feller* (Hairer–Mattingly): $P_t$ is ASF at $w_0$ if there exist $t_n\to\infty$, $\delta_n \to 0$ with $\|\nabla P_{t_n}\varphi(w)\| \le C(\|\varphi\|_\infty + \delta_n \|\nabla\varphi\|_\infty)$ locally uniformly. ASF plus topological irreducibility (every point is in the support of a common "accessible" set) implies at most one invariant measure.
- *Coupling / Foiaş–Prodi determining modes* (Kuksin–Shirikyan, E–Mattingly–Sinai, Mattingly, Bricmont–Kupiainen–Lefevere): the low modes are forced nondegenerately and the high modes are slaved, allowing a Markov coupling with geometric return times.

**Hörmander condition in infinite dimensions.** With $\mathcal{A}_0 = \mathrm{span}\{e_k : k\in\mathcal{Z}\}$ and the recursion $\mathcal{A}_{n+1} = \mathcal{A}_n + \mathrm{span}\{B(v,v') : v,v'\in\mathcal{A}_n\}$ ($B$ the symmetrized bilinear nonlinearity), the relevant nondegeneracy hypothesis is $\overline{\bigcup_n \mathcal{A}_n} = H$, an infinite-dimensional analogue of the parabolic Hörmander bracket condition.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1970s–80s.** Vishik–Fursikov and Foiaş studied statistical solutions and existence of invariant measures for randomly forced Navier–Stokes.
- **1995.** Flandoli–Maslowski prove existence *and uniqueness* of the invariant measure when the noise is nondegenerate in all modes with a precisely tuned covariance decay, using the strong Feller property plus irreducibility.
- **2000–2002.** The degenerate-noise era opens. Kuksin–Shirikyan (kick-forced and white-forced, *CMP* 2000 onwards) and Bricmont–Kupiainen–Lefevere develop coupling methods; E–Mattingly–Sinai (*CMP* 2001) and Mattingly (*CMP* 2002) prove exponential mixing when all modes below a viscosity-dependent cutoff are forced.
- **2006.** Hairer–Mattingly (*Annals of Mathematics* 164) prove unique ergodicity for **finitely many** forced modes satisfying the bracket condition — the landmark result. Follow-ups: spectral gaps in Wasserstein distance (*Ann. Probab.* 2008) and the general semilinear SPDE theory (*EJP* 2011, Malliavin calculus + ASF).
- **2012.** Kuksin–Shirikyan's monograph consolidates the coupling theory, including the inviscid ("Eulerian") limit and balance relations.
- **2014–2016.** Constantin–Glatt-Holtz–Vicol extend unique ergodicity to fractionally dissipated 2D Euler, $(-\Delta)^{\gamma}$ with $\gamma$ arbitrarily small.
- **2019–2024.** Bedrossian–Blumenthal–Punshon-Smith prove positivity of the top Lyapunov exponent (Lagrangian chaos), almost-sure exponential mixing of passive scalars, and the Batchelor spectrum $k^{-1}$ at fixed Reynolds number — the first rigorous turbulence-spectrum theorem in a stochastic fluid model.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Setting | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Nondegenerate trace-class noise, $Q$ with full range, suitable $Q^{-1}$ bounds | Existence + uniqueness + strong Feller | Flandoli–Maslowski 1995 |
| All modes $|k| \le N(\nu)$ forced, $N$ large in terms of $\mathcal{E}_1/\nu^2$ | Exponential mixing in total variation on bounded sets | E–Mattingly–Sinai 2001; Mattingly 2002; Bricmont–Kupiainen–Lefevere 2002 |
| Finite $\mathcal{Z}$ with $\mathcal{Z}$ generating $\mathbb{Z}^2$ under the bracket recursion — e.g. $\mathcal{Z} = \{\pm(1,0),\pm(1,1),\pm(2,0)\dots\}$; four real modes suffice on the square torus | Unique ergodicity, ASF, exponential mixing in a Wasserstein-type distance | Hairer–Mattingly 2006, 2008, 2011 |
| Fractional dissipation $\nu(-\Delta)^\gamma$, any $\gamma > 0$, degenerate noise | Unique ergodicity | Constantin–Glatt-Holtz–Vicol 2014 |
| Stationary $\mu_\nu$, generic finite-mode forcing | Top Lyapunov exponent $\lambda_1 > 0$ for the Lagrangian flow; a.s. exponential mixing | Bedrossian–Blumenthal–Punshon-Smith 2022 |

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No strong Feller property.** With finitely many forced modes, the transition probabilities from distinct initial points are mutually singular for all time; total-variation methods and Doeblin/Harris chains do not apply directly. ASF is a substitute, but it yields only weak-topology convergence and Wasserstein gaps, not TV.
- **Infinite-dimensional Hörmander theory is incomplete.** Malliavin matrix invertibility is only proved with *very weak* (non-uniform, "$\epsilon$-approximate") lower bounds; there is no infinite-dimensional analogue of the Hörmander regularity theorem, so smoothness/absolute continuity of $\mu$ on infinite-dimensional subspaces is unknown.
- **Irreducibility is not automatic.** Establishing that the support of $\mu$ is all of $H$ requires an approximate-controllability argument for the nonlinear system with finite-dimensional control; sharp results are only known for specific $\mathcal{Z}$.
- **No small-scale control uniform in $\nu$.** Estimates degrade like $e^{C/\nu}$ or $\nu^{-\alpha}$, so statements about $\mu_\nu$ in the turbulent regime (spectra, intermittency) cannot be extracted from the mixing proofs.
- **Nonlinearity is not perturbative.** The enstrophy balance controls only $\mathbb{E}_\mu\|\nabla w\|^2$; higher moments $\mathbb{E}_\mu\|w\|^p_{H^s}$ require exponential-moment estimates that are only available for $\|w\|_{L^2}$ with constants depending badly on $\nu$.
- **Euler limit has no dissipation mechanism.** Any limit of $\mu_\nu$ must be invariant for a PDE with infinitely many conserved quantities; selecting among the (presumably vast) set of Euler-invariant measures is a genuinely new problem.

## 6. The Gap

Three precise gaps separate proven results from the general statement:

1. **Sharpness of the bracket condition.** Hairer–Mattingly require $\mathcal{Z}$ to generate $H$ via Lie brackets. Whether unique ergodicity holds for *every* nonempty $\mathcal{Z}$ that is not obviously obstructed — say a single forced mode pair $\{\pm(1,0)\}$, where the bracket recursion stalls because $B(e_k,e_k) = 0$ — is open. For a single mode the deterministic dynamics may possess extra invariant structure, so the question is genuinely delicate.
2. **Regularity of $\mu$.** It is unknown whether $\mu$ is absolutely continuous with respect to any Gaussian measure, whether its finite-dimensional marginals have smooth densities, or whether $\mu$ charges $C^\infty$.
3. **Uniform-in-$\nu$ statistics.** No rigorous derivation exists of the Kraichnan enstrophy-cascade spectrum $E(k) \sim k^{-3}$ (or $k^{-3}(\log k)^{-1/3}$) for $\mu_\nu$; the Batchelor result is for a passive scalar at *fixed* Reynolds number, not for the velocity field at high Reynolds number.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Lyapunov exponents and chaos.** Bedrossian, Blumenthal, Punshon-Smith and collaborators (Maryland, Brown, Georgia Tech) continue the projective-Hörmander programme: quantitative lower bounds on $\lambda_1$, anomalous dissipation for passive scalars, and Eulerian (as opposed to Lagrangian) chaos. Extension of positive-$\lambda_1$ results to the Eulerian velocity field itself remains *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Minimal forcing sets.** Work on removing bracket hypotheses via control-theoretic irreducibility and refined Malliavin bounds; Shirikyan and coauthors (Cergy) study controllability of NSE with degenerate forcing on general domains and with boundary control.
- **Non-Gaussian and non-white forcing.** Ergodicity for bounded/Haar-type kicks, Lévy noise, and rough forcing; Kuksin–Nersesyan–Shirikyan results on exponential mixing for a wide class of driving processes.
- **Regularity structures and singular limits.** Techniques from Hairer's regularity-structures programme applied to space-time white noise forced 2D NSE (critical/borderline case), where the invariant measure is expected to be related to the enstrophy Gibbs measure *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Gibbsian/Euler invariant measures.** Renewed interest in invariant measures for 2D Euler (Albeverio–Cruzeiro-type white-noise vorticity measures, energy–enstrophy Gaussians) as candidate limits of $\mu_\nu$.

## 8. Future Work

- Prove or disprove unique ergodicity for forcing supported on a single Fourier mode pair; identify the sharp algebraic condition on $\mathcal{Z}$.
- Establish quantitative mixing rates with constants polynomial in $\nu^{-1}$, which would open the door to uniform-in-$\nu$ statistics.
- Derive rigorous upper and lower bounds on the stationary spectrum $\mathbb{E}_\mu |\hat{w}(k)|^2$ in the inertial range; even a one-sided bound of Kraichnan type would be a major advance.
- Characterize the Kuksin limit measures: are they unique, and do they coincide with a known Euler-invariant Gibbs measure?
- Transfer the 2D machinery to the 3D Navier–Stokes Markov selections (Da Prato–Debussche, Flandoli–Romito) and to geophysical models (Boussinesq, primitive equations), where partial ergodicity results already exist.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**The linear (Stokes) case, and why the nonlinearity destroys explicitness.**

Drop the nonlinear term. Each Fourier coefficient $w_k = \langle w, e_k\rangle$ then solves an independent Ornstein–Uhlenbeck equation

$$ dw_k = -\nu |k|^2 w_k\, dt + q_k\, dW^k_t . $$

Its unique stationary law is $\mathcal{N}\!\left(0, \frac{q_k^2}{2\nu|k|^2}\right)$, so the invariant measure of the Stokes system is the explicit Gaussian

$$ \mu^{\mathrm{Stokes}} = \bigotimes_{k} \mathcal{N}\!\left(0, \frac{q_k^2}{2\nu |k|^2}\right), \qquad \mathbb{E}_{\mu}\|w\|^2_{L^2} = \sum_k \frac{q_k^2}{2\nu|k|^2} = \frac{\mathcal{E}_0}{2\nu}. $$

Check against the general balance law of Section 2: $2\nu\,\mathbb{E}_\mu\|w\|^2 = \mathcal{E}_0$. ✓. Note that if $q_k = 0$ for some $k$, the corresponding coordinate decays to $0$ deterministically, and $\mu^{\mathrm{Stokes}}$ is supported on the finite-dimensional span of the forced modes. **Degenerate forcing in the linear problem gives a degenerate invariant measure.**

**How the nonlinearity spreads the noise.** Take $\mathcal{Z} = \{\pm(1,0), \pm(1,1)\}$ (four real modes, i.e. two complex conjugate pairs). The bilinear term in Fourier variables is

$$ (B(w,w))_k \;=\; \sum_{j + \ell = k} c(j,\ell)\, w_j w_\ell, \qquad c(j,\ell) = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{|\ell|^2} - \frac{1}{|j|^2}\right) (j^\perp\!\cdot \ell). $$

With $j = (1,0)$ and $\ell = (1,1)$ we get $j^\perp\!\cdot\ell = (0,1)\cdot(1,1) = 1$ and $|\ell|^2 = 2$, $|j|^2 = 1$, so $c = \tfrac12(\tfrac12 - 1) = -\tfrac14 \neq 0$. Hence the mode $k = (2,1)$ is directly excited at second order. Iterating, $(2,1)$ and $(1,0)$ generate $(3,1)$ with $c = \tfrac12(1 - \tfrac15)(0\cdot 3 + 1\cdot 1)\ne 0$, and the recursion sweeps out all of $\mathbb{Z}^2\setminus\{0\}$ — precisely the bracket condition $\overline{\bigcup_n \mathcal{A}_n} = H$ of Section 2. Hairer–Mattingly's theorem then gives: **a unique invariant measure $\mu$ on $L^2$, with $P_t^*\nu \to \mu$ exponentially fast in a Wasserstein-type distance, for every initial law $\nu$ with finite energy.**

**The obstructed case.** Take $\mathcal{Z} = \{\pm(1,0)\}$ only. Then $c((1,0),(1,0)) = \tfrac12(1-1)\cdot 0 = 0$ and $c((1,0),(-1,0)) = 0$ as well, so $\mathcal{A}_1 = \mathcal{A}_0$: the recursion stalls immediately. The forced modes are an invariant subspace for the truncated dynamics, and the Hairer–Mattingly hypothesis fails. Whether the full stochastic dynamics is still uniquely ergodic in this case is exactly the open Gap 1 above — and it is the smallest possible instance of the problem.
