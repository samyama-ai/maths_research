# Edgeworth Expansion for Markov Chains

> **Topic:** Probability & Stochastic Processes · **ID:** `09-probability/edgeworth-expansion-for-markov-chains` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

The central problem is to rigorously establish valid higher-order Edgeworth expansions for sums of functions of states of a general state-space Markov chain, matching the sharp $o(n^{-m/2})$ asymptotic error rates known for independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) sequences. 

Let $(X_n)_{n \ge 0}$ be a Markov chain and let $S_n = \sum_{k=1}^n f(X_k)$ be an additive functional for a zero-mean observable $f$. While the Central Limit Theorem (CLT) provides a Gaussian approximation for $S_n / \sqrt{n}$, an order-$m$ Edgeworth expansion refines this approximation by adding polynomial correction terms derived from the chain's asymptotic cumulants. The conjecture/problem is to prove that under minimal, optimal moment conditions on $f$ and a generalized Cramér-type non-lattice condition on the transition kernel, the uniform approximation error of the order-$m$ expansion over the real line decays precisely as $o(n^{-m/2})$. A complete resolution requires closing the "moment gap" (where Markov chain proofs currently demand strictly stronger moment assumptions than the i.i.d. case) and extending the theory to sub-geometrically ergodic (polynomially ergodic) chains lacking a spectral gap.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Let $(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{F})$ be a measurable space. Let $(X_n)_{n \ge 0}$ be a time-homogeneous Markov chain on $\mathcal{X}$ with transition kernel $P(x, A) = \mathbb{P}(X_1 \in A \mid X_0 = x)$ and a unique invariant probability measure $\pi$ such that $\pi P = \pi$.

Let $f: \mathcal{X} \to \mathbb{R}$ be a measurable observable satisfying $\pi(f) = \int_\mathcal{X} f(x) \pi(dx) = 0$. Define the additive functional $S_n = \sum_{j=1}^n f(X_j)$. 
Assume that $f \in L^2(\pi)$ and that the asymptotic variance $\sigma^2$ is strictly positive and finite:
$$ \sigma^2 = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} \mathbb{E}_\pi[S_n^2] = \mathbb{E}_\pi[f(X_0)^2] + 2 \sum_{k=1}^\infty \mathbb{E}_\pi[f(X_0)f(X_k)] \in (0, \infty) $$

Let $F_n(x) = \mathbb{P}_\pi \left( \frac{S_n}{\sigma \sqrt{n}} \le x \right)$ be the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of the normalized sum. The formal order-$m$ Edgeworth expansion is defined as:
$$ E_n^{(m)}(x) = \Phi(x) + \phi(x) \sum_{k=1}^m n^{-k/2} p_k(x) $$
where $\Phi(x)$ and $\phi(x)$ are the standard normal CDF and PDF, respectively. The polynomials $p_k(x)$ are generated by the asymptotic cumulants $\kappa_j$ of $S_n$. For example, utilizing probabilist's Hermite polynomials $He_k(x)$, the first two corrections are:
$$ p_1(x) = - \frac{\kappa_3}{6\sigma^3} He_2(x) = - \frac{\kappa_3}{6\sigma^3}(x^2 - 1) $$
$$ p_2(x) = - \frac{\kappa_4}{24\sigma^4} He_3(x) - \frac{\kappa_3^2}{72\sigma^6} He_5(x) $$

To evaluate $F_n(x)$ via Fourier inversion, one introduces the complex perturbed transition operator $P_t$ acting on a chosen Banach space $\mathcal{B} \subset L^\infty(\mathcal{X})$ (or a suitably weighted space):
$$ P_t h(x) = \mathbb{E}_x \left[ e^{itf(X_1)} h(X_1) \right] = \int_{\mathcal{X}} e^{itf(y)} h(y) P(x, dy) $$

If $P_0 = P$ has a spectral gap on $\mathcal{B}$, analytic perturbation theory dictates that for sufficiently small $|t|$, $P_t$ decomposes as:
$$ P_t = \lambda(t) \Pi_t + N_t $$
where $\lambda(t)$ is the isolated dominant eigenvalue ($\lambda(0) = 1$), $\Pi_t$ is the corresponding rank-one spectral projection, and $N_t$ operates on the complementary subspace with spectral radius $r(N_t) < |\lambda(t)|$. The cumulants are rigorously given by $\kappa_j = (-i)^j \left. \frac{d^j}{dt^j} \log \lambda(t) \right|_{t=0}$.

A valid Edgeworth expansion of order $m$ requires proving:
$$ \sup_{x \in \mathbb{R}} \left| F_n(x) - E_n^{(m)}(x) \right| = o(n^{-m/2}) $$
This typically requires a Cramér condition $(C)$ bounding the operator norm away from the origin to control high-frequency Fourier modes:
$$ \limsup_{|t| \to \infty} \| P_t^n \|_{\mathcal{B}} < 1 $$

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

The Edgeworth expansion was historically formalized by Chebyshev (1890), Edgeworth (1905), and Cramér (1928) for sequences of i.i.d. random variables. 

The extension to Markov chains began with Nagaev (1957), who utilized matrix perturbation theory to prove valid expansions for finite-state Markov chains. For over two decades, the continuous state space remained elusive until Götze and Hipp (1983) established expansions for sums of weakly dependent random vectors utilizing abstract mixing conditions. 

In a major structural shift, Malinovskii (1987) abandoned purely analytic perturbation for the *regeneration method* (Nummelin splitting), which slices a Harris recurrent Markov chain into independent blocks. This effectively reduced the problem back to the i.i.d. case, although it demanded heavy, often exponential, moment bounds on the return times to the recurrent atom.

The current State of the Art synthesized these approaches. Hervé and Pène (2010) successfully applied the Keller-Liverani perturbation theorem to the Nagaev-Guivarc'h method. This bypassed the need for $P$ to be a compact operator, requiring only that $P$ satisfies a Doeblin-Fortet (or Lasota-Yorke) inequality on a pair of adapted Banach spaces. Modern SOTA comfortably handles geometrically ergodic chains with bounded functions or unbounded functions that strictly satisfy heavily weighted drift conditions.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

The conjecture is currently resolved under the following specific structural regimes:

- **Finite State Spaces:** Completely solved by Nagaev. The expansion holds to any arbitrary order $m$ provided the function $f$ takes values on a non-lattice span relative to the chain's cycles.
- **Compact Manifolds:** Solved for uniformly ergodic chains on compact spaces where $f$ is bounded and Hölder continuous, utilizing the Banach space of bounded variation functions.
- **Geometrically Ergodic Chains (Strong Drift):** Verified for unbounded functions if there exists a Lyapunov function $V \ge 1$ such that $P V \le \alpha V + b \mathbf{1}_C$ (for $\alpha < 1$ and small set $C$), and the observable satisfies $|f|^{m+2+\epsilon} \le V$. 
- **Regenerative Chains:** Valid for order $m$ if the chain possesses an accessible atom, and the return time $\tau$ to this atom possesses finite moments of order strictly greater than $m+2$.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

Three distinct technical obstacles block the general resolution of the problem:

1. **The Spectral Gap Requirement (Failure on Sub-Geometric Chains):** Standard operator perturbation relies on an isolated dominant eigenvalue $\lambda(t)$. For polynomially ergodic Markov chains, the unperturbed operator $P$ lacks a spectral gap. The essential spectral radius touches $1$, meaning $\lambda(t)$ cannot be analytically isolated. The decomposition $P_t^n = \lambda(t)^n \Pi_t + N_t^n$ fails because $N_t^n$ does not decay exponentially, causing the truncation error to overwhelm the $o(n^{-m/2})$ Edgeworth polynomial corrections.
2. **The Operator Cramér Condition:** For i.i.d. variables, Cramér's condition simply requires that the characteristic function modulus is bounded away from $1$ for $|t| \ge \delta$. For Markov chains, one must prove $\sup_{\delta \le |t| \le c \sqrt{n}} r(P_t) < 1$. Verifying this spectral radius bound analytically for specific multidimensional, continuous state-space models (like those found in MCMC) is highly intractable.
3. **The Moment Gap:** In the i.i.d. regime, an order-$m$ expansion requires exactly $m+2$ moments. In the Markov setting, proving bounds on the higher derivatives of the operator $P_t$ entails integrating the kernel against higher powers of $f$. Due to the complex interplay between the Lyapunov function $V$ dictating the ergodicity rate and the polynomial growth of $f$, Markov chain proofs routinely demand strictly higher moments (e.g., $m+2+\epsilon$ or exponential moments), creating a gap between necessary and sufficient conditions.

## 6. The Gap

The exact mathematical barrier is two-fold. First, generalizing the continuous perturbation theory (Keller-Liverani) to fractional or continuous-spectrum operators to handle sub-geometrically ergodic chains. Second, formulating a refined smoothing lemma that disentangles the temporal dependence of the chain from the heavy tails of $f$, allowing one to recover the optimal $(m+2)$-moment condition without relying on globally dominating Lyapunov functions. Crossing this boundary would unite the i.i.d. theory and Markov theory under a single, optimal moment regime.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

Active schools of thought are currently exploring non-Fourier methods to circumvent the operator spectral gap entirely:

- **Stein's Method for Higher-Order Approximations:** Pioneered by Barbour, Chen, and Choi, applying the Stein-Tikhomirov method to Markov sequences allows for higher-order corrections without requiring Cramér's condition. However, this currently only yields bounds for smooth test functions; adapting it to indicator functions (required for bounding the CDF and the Kolmogorov distance $\sup_x |F_n(x) - E_n^{(m)}(x)|$) remains a highly active frontier.
- **Fractional Poisson Equations:** Researchers are investigating solutions to higher-order Poisson equations of the form $(I - P) g = f$ in weak function spaces to handle sub-geometric ergodicity.
- **Malliavin Calculus in Discrete Time:** Leveraging the Nourdin-Peccati approach, attempts are being made to express asymptotic cumulants directly via discrete Malliavin operators, bypassing the transfer operator $P_t$ entirely. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Future Work

Leading probabilists suggest three primary research strategies for the next decade:
1. **Computable Non-Asymptotic Bounds:** MCMC practitioners require explicit, computable constants for the $o(n^{-m/2})$ remainder term to construct rigorous finite-sample confidence intervals. Future work must extract explicit dependencies of these bounds on the spectral gap and mixing time.
2. **Hypoelliptic Diffusions:** Extending the discrete-time Edgeworth expansion to continuous-time non-reversible Markov processes and Langevin dynamics, which are increasingly used in machine learning optimization.
3. **High-Dimensional Scaling:** Investigating how the Edgeworth correction polynomials $p_k(x)$ behave when the state space dimension $d$ grows with $n$, particularly in interacting particle systems where the spectral gap deteriorates as $d \to \infty$.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Consider a continuous state-space autoregressive model AR(1), which neatly sidesteps the lattice issues inherent in finite-state chains. 
Let $X_n = \rho X_{n-1} + \epsilon_n$, where $|\rho| < 1$ and the innovations $\epsilon_n$ are i.i.d. random variables with a smooth continuous density. Assume $\mathbb{E}[\epsilon_n] = 0$, $\mathbb{E}[\epsilon_n^2] = 1$, and that the innovations possess a non-zero third cumulant (skewness) $\mathbb{E}[\epsilon_n^3] = \gamma_3 \neq 0$. 

Let the observable be $f(x) = x$. We seek the first-order Edgeworth expansion for $S_n = \sum_{j=1}^n X_j$. 
By recursively unwinding the AR(1) definition, the sum can be rewritten in terms of the innovations:
$$ S_n = \sum_{j=1}^n \sum_{k=0}^\infty \rho^k \epsilon_{j-k} \approx \frac{1}{1-\rho} \sum_{j=1}^n \epsilon_j $$
(where the approximation ignores negligible boundary terms as $n \to \infty$). 

Because the sequence is geometrically ergodic and the innovations possess a continuous density, Cramér's condition $(C)$ holds strictly for the perturbed operator $P_t$ acting on a Lyapunov-weighted space $V(x) = 1 + |x|^3$.

The asymptotic variance is obtained directly from the infinite moving-average representation:
$$ \sigma^2 = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} \mathbb{E}[S_n^2] = \frac{1}{(1-\rho)^2} $$
The asymptotic third cumulant $\kappa_3$, which dictates the first Edgeworth polynomial, scales similarly due to the linearity of the AR(1) process:
$$ \kappa_3 = \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{1}{n} \mathbb{E}[S_n^3] = \frac{\gamma_3}{(1-\rho)^3} $$
Applying the Nagaev-Guivarc'h spectral method yields the rigorous first-order Edgeworth expansion for the normalized sum:
$$ \mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1-\rho}{\sqrt{n}} S_n \le x \right) = \Phi(x) - \frac{\gamma_3}{6\sqrt{n}} (x^2 - 1)\phi(x) + o\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right) $$
This calculation concretely demonstrates how the spectral perturbation approach isolates the cumulants of the Markov sequence, capturing the temporal dependence structure (the $(1-\rho)$ scaling factors) while safely utilizing the intrinsic continuous noise of the system to satisfy the necessary operator norm bounds.
