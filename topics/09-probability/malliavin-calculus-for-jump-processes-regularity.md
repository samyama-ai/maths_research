# Malliavin Calculus for Jump Processes: Regularity of Densities

> **Topic:** Probability & Stochastic Processes · **ID:** `09-probability/malliavin-calculus-for-jump-processes-regularity` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

For diffusions driven by Brownian motion, Malliavin calculus delivers a complete and sharp answer to the regularity question: Hörmander's condition on the Lie algebra generated by the vector fields implies that the law of $X_t$ has a $C^\infty$ density for every $t>0$. No comparable theorem exists for jump processes.

**The problem.** Let $X$ solve the $\mathbb{R}^d$-valued SDE driven by a Poisson random measure $N$ with Lévy (intensity) measure $\nu$ on $\mathbb{R}^m_0 = \mathbb{R}^m\setminus\{0\}$,

$$dX_t = b(X_{t^-})\,dt + \int_{\mathbb{R}^m_0} \gamma(X_{t^-},z)\,\widetilde N(dt,dz),$$

with $\widetilde N = N - dt\,\nu(dz)$ the compensated measure. **Find checkable necessary-and-sufficient conditions on $(b,\gamma,\nu)$ under which the law of $X_t$ is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure, and under which its density is $C^\infty$ (or in $C^k$, or in a Besov class).**

A complete solution must (i) not assume $\nu$ has a density, nor that $\nu(\mathbb{R}^m_0)=\infty$ beyond what is genuinely needed; (ii) cover degenerate/hypoelliptic geometries where $\gamma(x,\cdot)$ spans a proper subspace at each $x$ and the missing directions are generated by iterated commutators or by the drift; (iii) handle coefficients of low regularity (Hölder, not $C^\infty$); (iv) give the correct sharp exponent linking the small-jump activity index of $\nu$ to the order of smoothness. Failure modes are known to be genuine: for finite-activity $\nu$ the law of $X_t$ always carries an atom, so no criterion purely in terms of the "jump vector fields" can suffice.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Poisson space.** Let $\Omega$ be the configuration space of point measures $\omega=\sum_i \delta_{(t_i,z_i)}$ on $[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^m_0$ with intensity $\mu = dt\otimes\nu$, $\nu$ a Lévy measure, $\int (1\wedge|z|^2)\nu(dz)<\infty$.

**Approach A — finite-difference (Nualart–Vives, Picard).** Define the add-a-point (difference) operator
$$D_{t,z}F(\omega) = F(\omega+\delta_{(t,z)}) - F(\omega),$$
with domain $\mathbb{D}^{1,2}=\{F: \mathbb{E}\int |D_{t,z}F|^2\,\mu(dt,dz)<\infty\}$. The dual (Skorokhod integral) $\delta$ satisfies the Mecke-type duality
$$\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^m_0} D_{t,z}F\; u(t,z)\,\mu(dt\,dz)\Big] = \mathbb{E}[F\,\delta(u)].$$
Chaos expansion: every $F\in L^2$ writes $F=\sum_{n\ge0} I_n(f_n)$ with multiple $\widetilde N$-integrals, and $D$ acts as $n$-fold contraction only in the *finite-difference* sense. Crucially $D$ is **not a derivation**: $D(FG)=F\,DG+G\,DF+DF\,DG$.

**Approach B — Bismut / Bichteler–Gravereaux–Jacod.** Assume $\nu(dz)=h(z)\,dz$ with $h$ smooth. Perturb jump *amplitudes* by a flow $z\mapsto \Phi_\varepsilon(z)$ preserving $\nu$ up to a Girsanov density; differentiating in $\varepsilon$ gives a genuine derivation operator
$$\mathcal{D}_v F = \int_0^T\!\!\int \partial_z F\cdot v(t,z)\,N(dt,dz),$$
and an integration-by-parts formula
$$\mathbb{E}[\partial_i \varphi(X_t)] = \mathbb{E}[\varphi(X_t)\,H_i],\qquad H_i \in \textstyle\bigcap_p L^p .$$

**Malliavin matrix and the criterion.** With either operator, set $\sigma_t = \big(\langle DX_t^i, DX_t^j\rangle_{L^2(\mu)}\big)_{i,j\le d}$. If $X_t\in\mathbb{D}^\infty$ and $\det\sigma_t^{-1}\in\bigcap_p L^p$, then $X_t$ has a $C^\infty$ density. The **non-degeneracy** hypothesis in the jump setting is that for small $r>0$,
$$\inf_{|\xi|=1}\ \int_0^t\!\!\int_{|z|\le r} \big|\xi^\top \partial_x\gamma\,\Xi\big|^2 \,\nu(dz)\,ds \ \gtrsim\ r^{2-\alpha}$$
where $\alpha=\inf\{\beta>0:\int_{|z|\le1}|z|^\beta\nu(dz)<\infty\}$ is the **Blumenthal–Getoor index**. The order-index tension — smoothness must be bought from small jumps whose activity is measured by $\alpha\in[0,2]$ — is the arithmetic core of the problem.

**Alternative criterion (Fourier / Picard).** $X_t$ has a $C^k$ density if $\int_{\mathbb{R}^d}|\xi|^k|\widehat{\mathcal{L}(X_t)}(\xi)|\,d\xi<\infty$; a Malliavin-type interpolation gives $|\mathbb{E}[e^{i\xi\cdot X_t}]|\le C|\xi|^{-p}$ for all $p$ under non-degeneracy.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1983–1987.** Bismut ("Calcul des variations stochastique et processus de sauts", *ZfW* 1983) introduces the jump-amplitude perturbation. Bichteler, Gravereaux and Jacod (*Malliavin Calculus for Processes with Jumps*, 1987) build the first systematic theory, requiring $\nu$ absolutely continuous with a smooth, non-vanishing density.
- **1988.** Léandre gives regularity results for degenerate jump processes; Norris develops an independent integration-by-parts for jump processes.
- **1990.** Carlen and Pardoux introduce a differential calculus on Poisson space; Nualart and Vives (1990) formalize the finite-difference operator and its chaos characterization.
- **1996.** Picard's "On the existence of smooth densities for jump processes" (*PTRF*) is the decisive structural advance: a purely finite-difference criterion using the *order* of $\nu$ near $0$, valid for singular $\nu$ (e.g. $\nu$ supported on a countable set of spheres).
- **2006–2009.** Ishikawa–Kunita (*SPA* 2006) treat canonical SDEs with jumps; Kulik obtains criteria for arbitrary Lévy measures via stratification; Cass (*SPA* 2009) proves smoothness for SDEs driven by Lévy processes under a Hörmander-type bracket condition on the drift and diffusion parts.
- **2010–2015.** Bouleau–Denis develop the Dirichlet-form ("lent particle") method, which requires no smoothness of $\nu$. Debussche–Fournier (*JFA* 2013) get existence of densities for stable-driven SDEs with merely Hölder coefficients. Zhang Xicheng (*Ann. Prob.* 2014) proves smooth densities for degenerate $\alpha$-stable-driven SDEs.
- **2015–present.** Sharp Besov-regularity statements, sub-Gaussian/stable heat-kernel two-sided bounds, and non-degenerate-in-a-cone criteria. The general hypoelliptic theorem remains absent.

|---|---|---|

| $\nu$ arbitrary (possibly singular) with $\liminf_{r\to0} r^{-\alpha}\nu(|z|\le r)>0$, $\alpha\in(0,2)$, ellipticity of $z\mapsto\gamma(x,z)$ near $0$ | $C^\infty$ density for $t>0$ | Picard 1996 |
| $\alpha$-stable-driven, $\alpha\in(0,2)$, coefficients $C^\infty_b$, uniformly elliptic $\sigma$ | $C^\infty$; two-sided heat-kernel bounds for $\alpha\in(0,2)$ | Kulik; Chen–Zhang |
| **Degenerate chain**: $\dot X^{(1)}=\text{stable noise}$, $\dot X^{(k)}=X^{(k-1)}$, $d=nd_1$, $\alpha\in(0,2)$ | $C^\infty$ density; Hörmander-type for linear-in-$x$ jump structure | Zhang 2014 |
| Hölder coefficients: $\gamma$ only $C^{\eta}$, $\eta>0$, $\alpha$-stable driver | **existence** of a density (no smoothness) | Debussche–Fournier 2013 |
| One-dimensional, $\nu$ finite plus drift | absolute continuity **fails**: atom of mass $e^{-\nu(\mathbb{R})t}$ | elementary |
| $\nu$ singular on a Cantor-type set, $\alpha$-order condition failing | counterexamples with singular law | Picard 1996 |
| Lévy driver with $\alpha=0$ (e.g. Gamma, log-activity) | density exists, smoothness only in $C^k$ for $k$ tied to $\log$-rate | Kulik 2006 |

## 5. Principal Obstacles

1. **No chain rule.** The finite-difference operator satisfies $D(\varphi(F)) \ne \varphi'(F)DF$. Every step of the Wiener-space proof — from $\mathbb{E}[\varphi'(X)] = \mathbb{E}[\varphi(X)H]$ to the iteration producing arbitrary order — must be rebuilt with error terms of size $|DF|^2$ that do not telescope.
2. **No Hörmander bracket calculus.** Jumps are non-local: $x\mapsto x+\gamma(x,z)$ is a *map*, not a vector field, so there is no Lie bracket. The natural substitute is the group/semigroup generated by the maps $\{\mathrm{id}+\gamma(\cdot,z)\}$, but iterated compositions do not generate the required non-degeneracy *quantitatively* at the right rate in $r$.
3. **Small-jump budget.** Smoothing comes only from jumps of size $\le r$, whose number is $\sim r^{-\alpha}$. For $\alpha$ small there is simply not enough randomness: the theory degenerates continuously to the finite-activity case where atoms appear. This is a hard information-theoretic obstruction, not a proof artefact.
4. **Singular Lévy measures.** Bismut-type perturbation requires differentiating in $z$, impossible when $\nu$ is carried by a lower-dimensional or fractal set. Picard's method covers some such $\nu$, but its non-degeneracy hypothesis is not stable under composition with the flow.
5. **Non-integrable Malliavin covariance inverse.** Even when $\sigma_t$ is a.s. invertible, $\det\sigma_t^{-1}\in L^p$ for all $p$ typically fails in degenerate cases; the Poisson-space concentration inequalities available (modified log-Sobolev, Poincaré with $D$) give only exponential, not Gaussian, tails.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: smoothness under *ellipticity of the small jumps* plus an $\alpha$-order lower bound on $\nu$, or under linear/triangular degeneracy structures. Conjectured: a **Hörmander theorem for jumps** — a purely geometric-algebraic condition on the family of maps $\{x\mapsto x+\gamma(x,z)\}_{z\in\mathrm{supp}\,\nu}$ together with the drift $b$, sufficient (and, modulo the atom obstruction, necessary) for $C^\infty$ density.

The precise missing step: convert a *qualitative* spanning statement — the smallest set of directions containing $\{\partial_z\gamma(x,z)\}$ and closed under $\partial_x$-transport by $b$ spans $\mathbb{R}^d$ — into a *quantitative* lower bound
$$\inf_{|\xi|=1}\ \xi^\top\sigma_t\,\xi \ \ge\ c\,t^{\kappa}\quad\text{with } \mathbb{P}(\text{failure})\ \le\ e^{-c\epsilon^{-\theta}},$$
for an explicit $\kappa(\alpha,\text{bracket depth})$. On Wiener space this is Norris's lemma; **no Norris lemma is known for Poisson martingales**, because the quadratic variation of a compensated jump integral is itself a jump process and cannot be bounded below by its predictable bracket on small time scales.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Stable-like and non-symmetric drivers.** X. Zhang, Z.-Q. Chen and collaborators (Beijing/Wuhan, Seattle) continue two-sided heat-kernel estimates and Schauder theory for $\alpha$-stable generators with drift, $\alpha\in(0,1]$ — the supercritical range where the drift dominates. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Dirichlet-forms / lent particle method.** The Bouleau–Denis school (Paris) pushes toward criteria requiring no regularity of $\nu$ at all; the trade-off is that only $H^1$-type (existence, not $C^\infty$) conclusions follow.
- **Interpolation / Bally–Caramellino programme.** Approximating non-smooth functionals by smooth ones and quantifying the error in total variation or Besov norms $B^{s}_{1,\infty}$; yields *quantitative* regularity where full smoothness is out of reach.
- **Rough-path and Lévy-rough-path interface.** Combining Cass-type Hörmander results with $p$-variation drivers for jump rough paths (Friz–Zhang, Chevyrev). *(frontier — verify)*
- **Numerics.** Multi-level Monte Carlo and unbiased simulation for stable-driven SDEs use precisely these density-regularity bounds to certify weak error rates.

## 8. Future Work

1. **Prove or refute a Norris-type lemma on Poisson space** — the single highest-value technical target.
2. **Classify the atom/absolute-continuity dichotomy** in terms of $\nu(|z|>r)$ as $r\to0$, sharply: conjecturally, absolute continuity of $X_t$ for all $t>0$ holds iff $\nu(\mathbb{R}^m_0)=\infty$ *and* an order condition on the direction distribution of small jumps.
3. **Hypoelliptic exponent.** Determine the exact $\kappa$ in $\|\partial^k p_t\|_\infty \lesssim t^{-\kappa(k)}$ for degenerate stable chains of depth $n$; the diffusion answer is $\kappa = (2n-1)$-scaled, the stable answer is conjectured $\alpha$-dependent.
4. **Low-regularity coefficients.** Upgrade Debussche–Fournier existence to $C^k$ smoothness for $\gamma\in C^{\eta}$, identifying the threshold $\eta_c(\alpha)$.
5. **Non-Markovian / infinite-dimensional.** Extend to SPDEs driven by Lévy space-time noise, where even absolute continuity is largely open.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Ornstein–Uhlenbeck driven by a Lévy process.** Take $d=1$ and
$$dX_t = -X_t\,dt + dZ_t,\qquad X_0=x,$$
so $X_t = xe^{-t} + \int_0^t e^{-(t-s)}dZ_s$. The characteristic function factorizes:
$$\mathbb{E}\big[e^{i\xi X_t}\big] = \exp\Big(i\xi x e^{-t} + \int_0^t \psi\big(\xi e^{-(t-s)}\big)\,ds\Big),$$
with $\psi$ the Lévy exponent of $Z$.

*Case 1: $Z$ compound Poisson, rate $\lambda$, jump law $\rho$.* Then $\alpha=0$ and $\nu(\mathbb{R})=\lambda<\infty$. On the event "no jump before $t$" (probability $e^{-\lambda t}>0$) we have $X_t = xe^{-t}$ exactly. Hence
$$\mathcal{L}(X_t)\ \ge\ e^{-\lambda t}\,\delta_{xe^{-t}},$$
an atom. **No non-degeneracy condition on $\gamma$ can repair this** — Malliavin calculus on Poisson space must produce a criterion that sees $\nu(\mathbb{R})=\infty$.

*Case 2: $Z$ symmetric $\alpha$-stable, $\psi(\xi) = -c|\xi|^\alpha$, $\alpha\in(0,2)$, $\nu(dz)=C_\alpha|z|^{-1-\alpha}dz$.* Then
$$\int_0^t \psi(\xi e^{-(t-s)})ds = -c|\xi|^\alpha \int_0^t e^{-\alpha(t-s)}ds = -\frac{c|\xi|^\alpha}{\alpha}\big(1-e^{-\alpha t}\big),$$
so
$$\big|\mathbb{E}[e^{i\xi X_t}]\big| = \exp\!\Big(-\tfrac{c}{\alpha}(1-e^{-\alpha t})|\xi|^\alpha\Big).$$
For every $t>0$ this decays faster than any polynomial, so $\int|\xi|^k|\widehat{\mathcal{L}(X_t)}|\,d\xi<\infty$ for all $k$: the density $p_t$ is $C^\infty$, with $\|p_t^{(k)}\|_\infty \le C_k\big(\tfrac{1-e^{-\alpha t}}{\alpha}\big)^{-(k+1)/\alpha} \sim C_k t^{-(k+1)/\alpha}$ as $t\downarrow0$.

**Where the difficulty enters.** The computation above is exact only because the coefficients are *linear* and the driver is *additive*. Replace $dZ_t$ by $\gamma(X_{t^-})\,dZ_t$ with $\gamma$ non-constant, or make the noise act only on the first coordinate of a chain $dX^{(1)}=dZ$, $dX^{(2)} = X^{(1)}dt$, and no explicit characteristic function exists. One must instead bound $\mathbb{E}[e^{i\xi X_t}]$ through the Malliavin covariance $\sigma_t$, which requires exactly the missing quantitative small-jump non-degeneracy — the content of Section 6. Zhang (2014) settles this specific two-step chain; depth $n\ge3$ with genuinely nonlinear $\gamma$ and $\alpha<1$ is open.
