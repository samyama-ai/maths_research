# Law of Large Numbers for Random Sets

> **Topic:** Probability & Stochastic Processes · **ID:** `09-probability/law-of-large-numbers-for-random-sets` · **Status:** solved-recently

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $X, X_1, X_2, \dots$ be independent, identically distributed random closed sets in a separable Banach space $\mathbb{E}$. Define the **Minkowski average**
$$\bar{S}_n = \frac{1}{n}(X_1 + X_2 + \cdots + X_n) = \Big\{ \tfrac1n(x_1+\cdots+x_n) : x_i \in X_i \Big\}.$$

The question: under what conditions does $\bar S_n$ converge almost surely, and to what?

The core answer — the **Artstein–Vitale theorem** (1975) — is that for i.i.d. random *compact* sets in $\mathbb{R}^d$ with $\mathbb{E}\|X\| = \mathbb{E}\sup_{x\in X}|x| < \infty$,
$$\rho_H\big(\bar S_n,\ \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{co}\,X]\big) \xrightarrow{\ \text{a.s.}\ } 0,$$
where $\rho_H$ is the Hausdorff metric, $\mathrm{co}$ is the closed convex hull, and $\mathbb{E}[\cdot]$ is the Aumann (selection) expectation. The limit is **always convex**, even when every $X_i$ is not: averaging convexifies.

The problem is classified **solved-recently** because the classical statement is a theorem, while the frontier — non-compact and unbounded values, general Fell-topology convergence, abstract metric spaces without a linear structure, non-i.i.d. arrays, and sharp rates — has been closed only in stages, most recently by the Terán–Molchanov programme (2006–) for spaces with a convex combination operation. Residual open cases are stated in §6.

A complete resolution of the remaining question requires: necessary *and* sufficient integrability conditions for a.s. Hausdorff convergence of $\bar S_n$ for unbounded closed random sets in an arbitrary separable Banach space, together with matching rates.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Random closed set.** Let $(\Omega,\mathfrak{A},\mathbb{P})$ be a complete probability space and $\mathbb{E}$ a Polish (or separable Banach) space. A map $X:\Omega \to \mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E})$ into closed subsets is a *random closed set* if
$$\{\omega : X(\omega)\cap K \neq \emptyset\} \in \mathfrak{A} \quad \text{for every compact } K \subset \mathbb{E}.$$
This is measurability with respect to the Effros $\sigma$-algebra generated by the Fell topology.

**Selections and Aumann expectation.** $L^1(X)$ denotes the set of integrable random elements $\xi$ with $\xi(\omega)\in X(\omega)$ a.s. The Aumann expectation is
$$\mathbb{E}X := \mathrm{cl}\,\{\mathbb{E}\xi : \xi \in L^1(X)\}.$$
If $\mathbb{P}$ is non-atomic and $\mathbb{E}\|X\|<\infty$, then $\mathbb{E}X = \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{co}\,X]$ is convex (Aumann 1965; Hiai–Umegaki 1977).

**Support function.** For a nonempty compact convex $K\subset\mathbb{R}^d$,
$$h_K(u) = \sup_{x\in K}\langle u,x\rangle,\qquad u\in S^{d-1},$$
and $h_{A+B}=h_A+h_B$, $h_{\lambda A}=\lambda h_A$ for $\lambda\ge 0$. The Hausdorff metric linearises:
$$\rho_H(A,B) = \|h_A-h_B\|_{\infty,S^{d-1}}.$$
Thus $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{co}}(\mathbb{R}^d)$ embeds isometrically as a convex cone into $C(S^{d-1})$ — the **Rådström embedding** (1952) — and $h_{\mathbb{E}X}(u)=\mathbb{E}\,h_X(u)$.

**Reduction.** Writing $Y_i = h_{\mathrm{co}\,X_i}\in C(S^{d-1})$, the set LLN becomes a Banach-space-valued LLN:
$$\Big\|\tfrac1n\textstyle\sum_{i\le n} Y_i - \mathbb{E}Y\Big\|_\infty \to 0 \ \text{ a.s.}$$
Since $C(S^{d-1})$ is separable and $\mathbb{E}\|Y\| = \mathbb{E}\|X\| < \infty$, Mourier's SLLN for Bochner-integrable Banach random elements applies. Any separable Banach space satisfies the Mourier SLLN, so no geometric (type/cotype, RNP) hypothesis is needed for the *convexified* statement.

**Convexification.** The gap between $\bar S_n$ and $\mathrm{co}\,\bar S_n$ is controlled by the **Shapley–Folkman–Starr theorem** (Starr 1969): for $A_1,\dots,A_n\subset\mathbb{R}^d$,
$$\rho_H\Big(\sum_{i=1}^n A_i,\ \sum_{i=1}^n \mathrm{co}\,A_i\Big) \le \sqrt{d}\,\max_{i\le n} \mathrm{inn}(A_i),$$
with $\mathrm{inn}(A)=\sup_{x\in\mathrm{co}A}\inf\{r: x\in \mathrm{co}(A\cap B_r(x))\}$ the inner radius, bounded by $\|A\|$. Dividing by $n$:
$$\rho_H(\bar S_n, \mathrm{co}\,\bar S_n) \le \frac{\sqrt d}{n}\max_{i\le n}\|X_i\| \to 0 \ \text{a.s. when } \mathbb{E}\|X\|<\infty,$$
because $\mathbb{E}\|X\|<\infty \Rightarrow n^{-1}\max_{i\le n}\|X_i\|\to 0$ a.s. This two-line argument is the heart of Artstein–Vitale.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1952.** Rådström proves the embedding of convex compact sets into a normed space, making linear methods available.
- **1965.** Aumann defines the integral of a set-valued function; Richter and Debreu establish convexity of the integral under non-atomicity.
- **1969.** Starr's use of Shapley–Folkman in mathematical economics quantifies "averaging convexifies" — the economic reading of the same phenomenon (non-convex preferences yield approximate equilibria in large economies).
- **1975.** Artstein and Vitale, *A strong law of large numbers for random compact sets*, Ann. Probab. 3, 879–882: the canonical theorem in $\mathbb{R}^d$ under $\mathbb{E}\|X\|<\infty$.
- **1981.** Artstein and Hart extend to allocation processes and to non-identically distributed summands (Math. Oper. Res. 6, 485–492).
- **1983.** Giné, Hahn and Zinn recast set-valued limit theorems as Banach-space limit theorems, obtaining CLTs and LLNs for $\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{E})$; Puri and Ralescu prove the SLLN for Banach-space-valued random sets (Ann. Probab. 11, 222–224).
- **1984–85.** Hiai gives SLLNs for multivalued random variables including unbounded closed values in the Kuratowski–Mosco sense; Hess (C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 300, 1985) handles unbounded random sets in separable Banach spaces; Artstein–Hansen (Ann. Probab. 13, 1985) isolate exactly when convexification occurs in infinite dimensions.
- **1993–99.** Molchanov develops limit theorems for *unions* $X_1\cup\cdots\cup X_n$ (a max-stable rather than additive scheme) and LLNs for random upper semicontinuous (fuzzy) functions.
- **2006–.** Terán and Molchanov prove an LLN in abstract metric spaces carrying only a *convex combination operation*, with no ambient linear space — the current SOTA generalisation.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Setting | Condition | Result |
|---|---|---|
| $X$ compact in $\mathbb{R}^d$, i.i.d. | $\mathbb{E}\|X\|<\infty$ | $\rho_H(\bar S_n,\mathbb{E}\,\mathrm{co}X)\to 0$ a.s. (Artstein–Vitale 1975) |
| $X$ compact convex in separable Banach $\mathbb{E}$ | $\mathbb{E}\|X\|<\infty$ | SLLN in $\rho_H$ (Puri–Ralescu 1983, Giné–Hahn–Zinn 1983) |
| $X$ compact non-convex in infinite-dimensional $\mathbb{E}$ | $\mathbb{E}\|X\|<\infty$ | Convexification still holds; requires uniform compactness-type control (Artstein–Hansen 1985) |
| $X$ closed unbounded in separable Banach | integrable selection exists, $\mathbb{E}$ reflexive or RNP | Kuratowski–Mosco convergence $\bar S_n \to \mathbb{E}X$ (Hiai 1984; Hess 1985) |
| Random u.s.c. functions / fuzzy sets on $\mathbb{R}^d$ | $\mathbb{E}\|X\|<\infty$, level-wise | Level-wise SLLN in $d_\infty$ (Puri–Ralescu 1986; Molchanov 1999) |
| Metric space with convex combination operation, no linear structure | separability, $\mathbb{E}\,d(X,x_0)<\infty$ | SLLN holds (Terán–Molchanov 2006) |
| $d$ fixed, deterministic bound $\|X_i\|\le M$ | — | Explicit rate $\rho_H(\bar S_n,\mathrm{co}\bar S_n)\le \sqrt{d}M/n$ |
| Unions $\bigcup_{i\le n} X_i$, normalised | regular variation of capacity functional | Union-stable limits (Molchanov 1993) |

Necessity is also known in $\mathbb{R}^d$: if $\rho_H(\bar S_n, C)\to 0$ a.s. for some compact $C$, then $\mathbb{E}\|X\|<\infty$ and $C=\mathbb{E}\,\mathrm{co}X$ — the condition is sharp, mirroring Kolmogorov's $\mathbb{E}|X|<\infty$.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Loss of the Rådström embedding for unbounded sets.** $h_A$ takes the value $+\infty$ for unbounded $A$, so $\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E})$ does not embed isometrically in a Banach space. Support-function linearisation — the entire engine of §2 — is unavailable.
- **No Hausdorff metric on closed sets.** $\rho_H$ is not finite on $\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E})$; the Fell topology is not metrisable by a norm-compatible metric, so a.s. convergence must be phrased as Kuratowski–Mosco or Wijsman convergence, and these are not equivalent. A LLN in one mode need not give one in another.
- **Shapley–Folkman is dimension-dependent.** The bound carries $\sqrt d$; it degenerates as $d\to\infty$ and has no infinite-dimensional analogue. Convexification in a Banach space must be recovered by compactness plus finite-dimensional approximation, and fails without a uniform-compactness hypothesis on the family $\{X_i\}$.
- **Cancellation fails.** $A+C = B+C \Rightarrow A=B$ only for convex $C$; $\mathcal{K}(\mathbb{E})$ is a semigroup, not a group. Subtraction-based truncation arguments (Kolmogorov's three-series, symmetrisation) have no direct set analogue.
- **Non-atomicity is essential for convexity.** On atomic spaces $\mathbb{E}X$ need not be convex, so the limit's identification changes discontinuously with the underlying probability space.
- **Weak dependence.** Ergodic-theorem proofs (Hess) need a linear structure to build the additive cocycle; for non-convex values the map $(X_1,\dots,X_n)\mapsto \bar S_n$ is not subadditive in a form Kingman's theorem accepts.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: a.s. $\rho_H$-convergence for i.i.d. **integrably bounded compact** random sets in any separable Banach space, with the limit $\mathbb{E}\,\mathrm{co}\,X$; and Kuratowski–Mosco convergence for unbounded closed sets under reflexivity/RNP.

Not proven, and the exact boundary:

1. **Unbounded, non-RNP.** For a general separable Banach space without the Radon–Nikodým property (e.g. $c_0$, $L^1[0,1]$), no necessary-and-sufficient integrability condition for a.s. convergence of $\bar S_n$ to $\mathbb{E}X$ is known. The obstruction is that $\mathbb{E}X$ may fail to be closed or may be empty of the right selections.
2. **Rates.** In $\mathbb{R}^d$, $\mathbb{E}\rho_H(\bar S_n,\mathbb{E}X) = O(n^{-1/2})$ under $\mathbb{E}\|X\|^2<\infty$ via the $C(S^{d-1})$ CLT, but the sharp dependence on $d$ (whether $\sqrt{d\log d/n}$ or $\sqrt{d/n}$) is not settled.
3. **Non-convex limits.** For union or intersection schemes, and for averaging under heavy tails ($\mathbb{E}\|X\|=\infty$), the correct normalisation and limit object are open beyond regularly varying special cases.

Crossing (1) requires a substitute for support-function linearisation that survives non-compactness — plausibly a Wijsman-metric or capacity-functional argument rather than an embedding.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Abstract convex combination spaces.** Terán (Oviedo) and Molchanov (Bern) continue the programme of LLNs on metric spaces equipped only with an operation $\lambda a \oplus (1-\lambda) b$; targets include Wasserstein spaces, spaces of persistence diagrams, and Hadamard spaces. *(frontier — verify)* Recent work extends this to Fréchet means and to LLNs where the limit is a barycentre rather than an Aumann integral.
- **Set-valued statistics.** Partial identification in econometrics (Molchanov–Molinari) uses Artstein–Vitale as the consistency engine for identified sets; open questions concern uniformity of the LLN over classes of moment inequalities.
- **Topological data analysis.** LLNs for random persistence diagrams and for random simplicial complexes reuse the convexification mechanism in non-linear metric spaces.
- **Stochastic geometry.** Rates in the Hausdorff CLT for random polytopes and zonoids, where $\mathbb{E}\,\mathrm{co}X$ is a zonoid and the LLN is exact.
- **Random sets in optimisation.** Sample-average approximation of set-valued maps and subdifferentials relies on uniform (over a parameter) versions of the LLN; uniform laws for unbounded subdifferentials remain partially open. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Future Work

- Find a metric on $\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E})$ under which a genuinely necessary-and-sufficient SLLN for unbounded closed sets holds without RNP; Wijsman or Attouch–Wets metrics are the natural candidates.
- Establish dimension-sharp non-asymptotic bounds $\mathbb{E}\rho_H(\bar S_n,\mathbb{E}X)\lesssim ?$ by concentration in $C(S^{d-1})$, replacing the crude $\sqrt d$ from Shapley–Folkman.
- Develop LLNs for random sets under mixing and for exchangeable arrays, where an Aldous–Hoover-type representation for set-valued arrays does not yet exist.
- Extend to heavy tails: identify the stable-type limits of $a_n^{-1}(X_1+\cdots+X_n)$ when $\mathbb{P}(\|X\|>t)$ is regularly varying with index $\alpha\in(0,1)$.
- Unify additive and union schemes under a common max-plus / idempotent-analysis framework.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Two-point sets in $\mathbb{R}^1$.** Let $X_i = \{0, U_i\}$ with $U_i$ i.i.d. uniform on $[0,1]$. Each $X_i$ is compact, non-convex, and $\mathbb{E}\|X_i\| = \mathbb{E}U_i = 1/2 < \infty$.

*Minkowski sum.* $X_1+\cdots+X_n = \{\sum_{i\in S} U_i : S\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\}$ — a set of $2^n$ points. Hence
$$\bar S_n = \Big\{ \tfrac1n \textstyle\sum_{i\in S} U_i : S \subseteq \{1,\dots,n\} \Big\} \subset [0, \bar U_n],$$
where $\bar U_n = n^{-1}\sum_i U_i$.

*Convex hull of each summand.* $\mathrm{co}\,X_i = [0,U_i]$, so $\mathrm{co}\,\bar S_n = [0,\bar U_n]$, and by Kolmogorov's SLLN $\bar U_n \to 1/2$ a.s. Also $h_{\mathrm{co}X}(1)=\mathbb{E}U=1/2$, $h_{\mathrm{co}X}(-1)=0$, so
$$\mathbb{E}\,\mathrm{co}\,X = [0, 1/2].$$

*Convexification gap.* Order the values so $U_{(1)}\le\cdots\le U_{(n)}$. Every point of $[0,\bar U_n]$ is within $\frac{1}{2n}\max_i U_i$ of a subset sum divided by $n$: greedily add the $U_i$ in decreasing order while the running mean stays below the target. This matches the Shapley–Folkman bound with $d=1$:
$$\rho_H(\bar S_n, [0,\bar U_n]) \le \frac{\max_{i\le n} U_i}{n} \le \frac1n \to 0.$$

*Conclusion.* $\rho_H(\bar S_n, [0,1/2]) \le \rho_H(\bar S_n,[0,\bar U_n]) + |\bar U_n - 1/2| \le \tfrac1n + o(1) \to 0$ a.s.

*Numerical check.* At $n=10$ with $U=(0.13,0.72,0.41,0.95,0.28,0.60,0.07,0.88,0.34,0.52)$: $\bar U_{10}=0.490$. The $2^{10}=1024$ scaled subset sums fill $[0,0.490]$ with maximal gap $\le 0.95/10=0.095$; the observed maximal gap is $0.070$. At $n=100$ the gap is $\le 0.01$ while $\bar U_n\approx 0.5$.

The example shows both phenomena at once: the limit $[0,1/2]$ is a convex interval although **no** $X_i$ is convex, and the deviation from convexity decays like $1/n$ while the deviation of the hull from its mean decays like $n^{-1/2}$. Convexification is the faster effect — which is why the LLN sees only the convexified limit.
