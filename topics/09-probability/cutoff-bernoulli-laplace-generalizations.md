# Cutoff for the Bernoulli-Laplace and Card Shuffling Generalizations

> **Topic:** Probability & Stochastic Processes · **ID:** `09-probability/cutoff-bernoulli-laplace-generalizations` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

The classical Bernoulli–Laplace urn chain (two urns of $n$ balls, $n$ red and $n$ black, one ball swapped per step) exhibits a **total-variation cutoff** at time $\frac{1}{4}n\log n$: mixing goes from "no mixing" to "essentially mixed" in a window of width $O(n)$ around that time. The open problem is the family of generalizations obtained by moving three parameters simultaneously:

1. **Batch size.** Swap $k$ balls at a time instead of one. Conjecturally there is cutoff at $t_k(n)=\frac{n}{4k}\log n$ whenever $k=o(n)$ — **proved** — and the open regime is $k=\theta n$ for fixed $\theta\in(0,1/2]$, where the mixing time is $O(1)$ and no cutoff can exist. There the question becomes: does the total-variation distance converge to an explicit **limit profile** $\Phi_\theta(t)$, and is that profile universal?
2. **Unequal urns / general exclusion.** With $N$ sites and $k$ indistinguishable particles (simple exclusion on the complete graph), is there cutoff at $\frac{1}{4}N\log\min(k,N-k)$ uniformly over all $k=k(N)$, including $k$ bounded?
3. **The symmetric-group analogue.** Random walk on $S_n$ driven by a conjugacy class with support size $k$: cutoff at $\frac{1}{k}n\log n$ is known for $k=o(n)$; the case $k=\theta n$ (e.g. the random walk generated by permutations with $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ non-fixed points) is open.

A complete solution means: for each parameter regime, identify $t_{\mathrm{mix}}$ with its window, prove or disprove cutoff, and in the non-cutoff regimes exhibit the limit profile $\lim_{n\to\infty}\|P^{\lfloor c\,t_{\mathrm{mix}}\rfloor}_{x_0}-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}$.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**State space and chain.** Let $\Omega_n=\{0,1,\dots,n\}$, where $x$ records the number of red balls in urn I. The single-swap chain has
$$P(x,x+1)=\Big(\tfrac{n-x}{n}\Big)^{2},\qquad P(x,x-1)=\Big(\tfrac{x}{n}\Big)^{2},\qquad P(x,x)=\tfrac{2x(n-x)}{n^{2}},$$
reversible with hypergeometric stationary law
$$\pi(x)=\binom{n}{x}\binom{n}{n-x}\Big/\binom{2n}{n}.$$

**Gelfand pair structure.** The chain is the projection to $\{0,\dots,n\}$ of a random walk on the homogeneous space $S_{2n}/(S_n\times S_n)$. The pair $(S_{2n},S_n\times S_n)$ is a **Gelfand pair**: the induced representation decomposes multiplicity-freely as $\bigoplus_{j=0}^{n}S^{(2n-j,j)}$, with $\dim S^{(2n-j,j)}=\binom{2n}{j}-\binom{2n}{j-1}$. The spherical functions are **dual Hahn polynomials**, and the eigenvalues are
$$\beta_j \;=\; 1-\frac{j(2n+1-j)}{n^{2}},\qquad j=0,1,\dots,n .$$

**Cutoff.** A sequence of chains has cutoff at $(t_n,w_n)$ with $w_n=o(t_n)$ if
$$\lim_{c\to\infty}\liminf_{n}\;d_n(t_n-cw_n)=1,\qquad \lim_{c\to\infty}\limsup_{n}\;d_n(t_n+cw_n)=0,$$
where $d_n(t)=\max_x\|P^t(x,\cdot)-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}$.

**Upper-bound machinery.** For a reversible chain started at a spherical point,
$$4\,\|P^{t}(x_0,\cdot)-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}^{2}\;\le\;\chi^{2}(t)\;=\;\sum_{j\ge 1} d_j\,\beta_j^{\,2t},$$
which for $\beta_1=1-\frac{2}{n}$, $d_1=2n-1$ and $t=\frac{1}{4}n(\log n+c)$ gives $\chi^2 \lesssim 2e^{-c}$ — the classical upper bound.

**$k$-swap generalization.** Exchange $k$ uniformly chosen balls from each urn. Eigenvalues are given by evaluating dual Hahn polynomials at the $k$-swap measure; the leading one satisfies $\beta_1^{(k)} = 1-\frac{2k}{n}+O(k^2/n^2)$, which is what produces the conjectured time $\frac{n}{4k}\log n$ and shows the degeneration when $k \asymp n$.

**Conjugacy-class walks.** For $\Gamma\subset S_n$ a conjugacy class with $k$ non-fixed points, the walk $\mu_\Gamma$ is central, so by Schur's lemma $\hat\mu_\Gamma(\rho)=\frac{\chi^\rho(\Gamma)}{\chi^\rho(1)}I$, and the Diaconis–Shahshahani upper bound reads
$$4\|\mu_\Gamma^{*t}-U\|_{\mathrm{TV}}^{2}\;\le\;\sum_{\rho\ne\mathrm{triv}}(\dim\rho)^{2}\Big(\tfrac{\chi^{\rho}(\Gamma)}{\dim\rho}\Big)^{2t}.$$

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1907.** Model introduced independently by P. and T. Ehrenfest and, in the two-urn two-colour form, attributed to Bernoulli and Laplace; used as a discrete model of diffusion and of the second law.
- **1981.** Diaconis and Shahshahani prove the first cutoff theorem for random transpositions on $S_n$ at $\frac12 n\log n$, introducing the representation-theoretic upper bound lemma.
- **1987.** Diaconis and Shahshahani, *Time to reach stationarity in the Bernoulli–Laplace diffusion model* (SIAM J. Math. Anal. 18), establish cutoff at $\frac14 n\log n$ with window $n$, using the dual Hahn spherical functions.
- **1992.** Bayer–Diaconis solve riffle shuffling exactly ($\frac32\log_2 n$ cutoff), establishing shuffling as the canonical laboratory for cutoff.
- **1994.** Donnelly, Lloyd and Sudbury extend to unequal urn sizes and general numbers of balls of each colour.
- **2011–2019.** Berestycki–Schramm–Zeitouni, Hough, and Berestycki–Şengül establish cutoff at $\frac{n}{k}\log n$ for random $k$-cycles and for general conjugacy classes with $k=o(n)$.
- **2016.** Lacoin–Leblond treat exclusion on the complete graph (unequal colour counts), obtaining cutoff at $\frac14 N\log\min(k,N-k)$.
- **2019–2020.** Nestoridi–White, then Eskenazis–Nestoridi, settle the $k$-swap Bernoulli–Laplace urn for all $k=o(n)$.
- **2020–2022.** Teyssier's **limit profile** method (characters + Fourier inversion) gives the exact profile for random transpositions; Nestoridi–Olesker-Taylor generalize it to reversible chains, including Bernoulli–Laplace.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Regime | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| $k=1$, equal urns, $n$ red / $n$ black | Cutoff at $\frac14 n\log n$, window $O(n)$ | Diaconis–Shahshahani 1987 |
| Unequal urn sizes, general colour counts | Mixing-time asymptotics, cutoff in the balanced regimes | Donnelly–Lloyd–Sudbury 1994 |
| Exclusion on $K_N$, $\min(k,N-k)\to\infty$ | Cutoff at $\frac14 N\log\min(k,N-k)$, window $N$ | Lacoin–Leblond 2016 |
| $k$-swap urn, $k=o(n)$ | Cutoff at $\frac{n}{4k}\log n$, window $n/k$ | Nestoridi–White 2019; Eskenazis–Nestoridi 2020 |
| $k$-cycles, $k$ fixed | Cutoff at $\frac{1}{k}n\log n$ | Berestycki–Schramm–Zeitouni 2011 |
| Conjugacy classes, support $k=o(n)$ | Cutoff at $\frac{1}{k}n\log n$ | Hough 2016; Berestycki–Şengül 2019 |
| Random transpositions, limit profile | $d(\tfrac12 n\log n + cn)\to \|\mathrm{Poi}(1+e^{-2c})-\mathrm{Poi}(1)\|_{\mathrm{TV}}$ | Teyssier 2020 |
| Bernoulli–Laplace limit profile, $k=1$ | Explicit profile in the cutoff window | Nestoridi–Olesker-Taylor 2022 |
| Separation / $L^\infty$ cutoff, $k=1$ | Separation cutoff at $\frac14 n\log n$ | Diaconis–Shahshahani 1987; strong stationary duality arguments |

Small cases ($n\le 10^3$) are verified numerically by direct diagonalization: the eigenvalue formula $\beta_j$ is exact, so $\chi^2(t)$ is computable to machine precision.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Loss of spectral separation.** For $k=\theta n$, $\beta_1^{(k)}$ is bounded away from $1$ (it is $\approx 1-2\theta$), so the relaxation time is $O(1)$ and the $\chi^2$ sum no longer has a dominant term. Cutoff proofs rely on one eigenvalue governing decay; here $\Theta(n)$ eigenvalues contribute comparably and the upper bound lemma diverges.
- **Character estimates degrade.** For conjugacy classes with support $\theta n$, the ratios $\chi^\rho(\Gamma)/\dim\rho$ are not controlled by the Frobenius/Roichman-type bounds that give $\big|\chi^\rho(\Gamma)/\dim\rho\big|\le (\text{const})^{k}$ uniformly; the effective bound becomes trivial once $k\asymp n$.
- **Coupling and strong stationary times are lossy.** Known couplings for exclusion contract at rate $\asymp k/N$ per step but incur an $O(1)$ additive error that is exactly the size of the quantity to be estimated in the $O(1)$-mixing regime.
- **No curvature route.** Salez's theorem (cutoff for non-negatively curved chains under the product condition) requires $t_{\mathrm{rel}}=o(t_{\mathrm{mix}})$; the product condition **fails** at $k=\theta n$, so the general theory correctly predicts nothing rather than failing technically.
- **Profile inversion needs uniform Fourier control.** Teyssier's method requires summing an inverse Fourier transform over all irreducibles with uniform error control; for $k=\theta n$ the dimensions $\dim\rho$ grow superpolynomially against character ratios that no longer decay, and the sum is not absolutely convergent term-by-term.

## 6. The Gap

Everything proven lives on the side $k=o(n)$ (equivalently, per-step spectral gap $\to 0$). The general statement demands the complementary regime $k\asymp n$, where the object of interest changes character: cutoff is impossible, and what must be produced is the **exact function**
$$\Phi_\theta(t)\;=\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\big\|P^{\lceil t\rceil}_{\theta n}(x_0,\cdot)-\pi_n\big\|_{\mathrm{TV}},\qquad t\in\mathbb{N},$$
including whether $\Phi_\theta$ is the same for the urn model, complete-graph exclusion, and conjugacy-class walks with matched $\theta$ (a universality claim). The precise missing step is a **uniform-in-$\rho$ bound on $\chi^\rho(\Gamma_{\theta n})/\dim\rho$** — or, on the urn side, a uniform asymptotic for dual Hahn polynomials $R_j$ at parameter scaling $k/n\to\theta$ across the full index range $j\le n$, not just $j=o(n)$.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Limit-profile programme.** Groups around Nestoridi (Stony Brook/Princeton), Olesker-Taylor (Warwick) and Teyssier (Vienna/Warwick) are extending profile computations from cutoff windows to $O(1)$-mixing regimes; the Bernoulli–Laplace $\theta$-regime is a named target. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Non-reversible and asymmetric variants.** Interchange-process and asymmetric exclusion analogues on $K_N$, where reversibility fails and the spectral method must be replaced by hydrodynamic or Bethe-ansatz input.
- **Curvature and entropy criteria.** Following Salez (JEMS 2024) and Salez–Bordenave-type entropic arguments, work on whether "non-negative curvature $\Rightarrow$ cutoff" can be quantified to yield profiles when the product condition fails. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Random-matrix and free-probability heuristics** for the $\theta$-regime spectrum of the $k$-swap generator, suggesting $\Phi_\theta$ is a Gaussian-type distance rather than a Poisson one. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Future Work

1. Prove or disprove existence of $\Phi_\theta$ for the $k=\theta n$ urn model, starting with $\theta=1/2$ where symmetry is maximal.
2. Obtain character-ratio bounds for conjugacy classes of linear support — the single most reusable technical input.
3. Settle exclusion on $K_N$ with **bounded** $k$ (e.g. $k=2$), where $\min(k,N-k)$ does not tend to infinity and cutoff is expected to fail.
4. Extend limit profiles to separation and $L^p$ distances, $1<p<\infty$, where even the $k=1$ picture is incomplete.
5. Transfer results from the complete graph to expanders and to $K_{n,n}$, testing whether $\frac14 N\log\min(k,N-k)$ is graph-universal.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take $n=2$: two urns of 2 balls, 2 red and 2 black in total. States $x\in\{0,1,2\}$ = number of red balls in urn I.

**Stationary law.** $\pi=\big(\tfrac16,\tfrac46,\tfrac16\big)$.

**Transitions.** From $x=2$ (urn I all red): urn I gives a red with probability 1, urn II gives a black with probability 1, so $P(2,1)=1$. From $x=1$: $P(1,2)=\big(\tfrac12\big)^2=\tfrac14$, $P(1,0)=\tfrac14$, $P(1,1)=\tfrac12$.

**Eigenvalues** from $\beta_j=1-\frac{j(2n+1-j)}{n^2}=1-\frac{j(5-j)}{4}$:
$$\beta_0=1,\qquad \beta_1=1-\tfrac{4}{4}=0,\qquad \beta_2=1-\tfrac{6}{4}=-\tfrac12 .$$
Check: $\operatorname{tr}P=0+\tfrac12+0=\tfrac12=1+0-\tfrac12$. ✓

**Distance from the extreme start $x_0=2$.**
$$d(1)=\tfrac12\Big(\big|0-\tfrac16\big|+\big|1-\tfrac23\big|+\big|0-\tfrac16\big|\Big)=\tfrac13,$$
$$d(2)=\tfrac12\Big(\big|\tfrac14-\tfrac16\big|+\big|\tfrac12-\tfrac23\big|+\big|\tfrac14-\tfrac16\big|\Big)=\tfrac16,$$
and thereafter $d(t)\asymp 2^{-t}$, matching $|\beta_2|^t$.

**What this illustrates.** The decay rate here is set by $\beta_2=-\tfrac12$, not by $\beta_1$, because $\beta_1$ vanishes — a small-$n$ shadow of the $k=\theta n$ difficulty, where no single eigenvalue dominates. For large $n$ and $k=1$, the dominant eigenvalue is $\beta_1=1-\tfrac2n$ and the multiplicity $2n-1$ of the corresponding representation forces the $\log n$ term: $(2n)\,(1-\tfrac2n)^{2t}\approx 1$ exactly at $t=\tfrac14 n\log n$. Now take $k=n$ (swap both urns wholesale) at $n=2$: the chain becomes deterministic, $x\mapsto 2-x$, and is periodic — it never mixes. The open problem is precisely the interpolation between the clean $\frac{n}{4k}\log n$ cutoff at $k=o(n)$ and this degenerate endpoint at $k=n$.
