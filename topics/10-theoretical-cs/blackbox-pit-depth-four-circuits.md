# Blackbox Identity Testing for Depth-Four Circuits

> **Topic:** Theoretical Computer Science · **ID:** `10-theoretical-cs/blackbox-pit-depth-four-circuits` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

**Problem.** Construct an explicit hitting set of quasipolynomial (ideally polynomial) size for depth-four algebraic circuits, i.e. $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi$ circuits, over a field $\mathbb{F}$ of characteristic $0$ or sufficiently large characteristic.

Concretely: give a deterministic algorithm that, on input $(n, s)$, outputs in time $s^{O(\log s)}$ a finite set $\mathcal{H}_{n,s} \subset \mathbb{F}^n$ such that for **every** nonzero $n$-variate polynomial $f$ computable by a $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi$ circuit of size at most $s$, there exists $a \in \mathcal{H}_{n,s}$ with $f(a) \neq 0$. The algorithm sees only the circuit's size parameters, never its wiring — it must work given oracle (blackbox) access to $f$.

A complete solution is a construction plus a proof of the hitting property. A disproof would be a proof that no such set of subexponential size exists — which is not expected, since $\mathcal{H}$ of size $\mathrm{poly}(s)$ exists non-constructively by a counting argument; the difficulty is entirely one of *explicitness*.

The problem is the central bottleneck of algebraic derandomization: by the depth-reduction "chasm at depth four" (Agrawal–Vinay 2008; Koiran 2012; Gupta–Kamath–Kayal–Saptharishi 2013), a blackbox test for depth four running in time $s^{O(\log s)}$ (or even $s^{o(\log\log s)}$ in the bootstrapping regime) yields quasipolynomial-time blackbox PIT for *general* arithmetic circuits, and a super-polynomial lower bound for $\mathrm{VP}$.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Let $\mathbb{F}$ be a field and $f \in \mathbb{F}[x_1,\dots,x_n]$.

**Depth-four circuit.** A $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi$ circuit of top fan-in $k$ computes
$$ f(x_1,\dots,x_n) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{k}\ \prod_{j=1}^{d_i} Q_{ij}(x_1,\dots,x_n), \qquad \deg Q_{ij} \le t, $$
where each $Q_{ij}$ is given as an explicit sum of monomials. Size $s$ counts all wires; the two restricted parameters are the **top fan-in** $k$ and the **bottom fan-in** (degree) $t$. Writing $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi(k)$ fixes $k$; $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi^{[t]}$ fixes $t$. Setting $t=1$ recovers depth three, $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma(k)$.

**Hitting set / generator.** $\mathcal{H}\subset\mathbb{F}^n$ hits a class $\mathcal{C}$ if $\forall f\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\}\ \exists a\in\mathcal{H}: f(a)\ne 0$. Equivalently one seeks a *generator*: a polynomial map $G:\mathbb{F}^{\ell}\to\mathbb{F}^n$ with $\ell$ small and $\deg G$ small such that $f \ne 0 \Rightarrow f\circ G \ne 0$; then $\mathcal{H}=G(S^\ell)$ for any $S$ with $|S| > \deg(f\circ G)$.

**Randomized baseline (Schwartz–Zippel).** For $f\ne0$ of degree $d$ and finite $S\subseteq\mathbb{F}$,
$$ \Pr_{a\in S^n}[f(a)=0] \le d/|S|. $$
This gives a $\mathrm{co}\text{-}\mathrm{RP}$ blackbox test; derandomizing it is the problem.

**Normal form.** A $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi(k)$ identity $\sum_i T_i = 0$ is *minimal* if no proper nonempty sub-sum vanishes, and *simple* if $\gcd(T_1,\dots,T_k)=1$. PIT reduces to certifying that simple, minimal identities are structurally constrained.

**Rank bound (depth three).** For $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma(k)$ with $T_i = \prod_j \ell_{ij}$ linear, define $\mathrm{rank}$ as the dimension of the span of all $\ell_{ij}$. Dvir–Shpilka and Saxena–Seshadhri: a simple, minimal identity of degree $d$ has
$$ \mathrm{rank} \;=\; O(k^2\log d) \quad\text{(Saxena–Seshadhri, } k^3\log d \text{ improved to } O(k^2\log d)\text{)}, $$
which yields a $\mathrm{poly}(n)\, d^{O(k^2 \log d)}$-size hitting set for fixed $k$.

**Gupta's conjecture (ideal-theoretic Sylvester–Gallai).** Let $\mathcal{Q}_1,\dots,\mathcal{Q}_k$ be finite sets of irreducible polynomials of degree $\le t$ with $\bigcap_i \langle \mathcal{Q}_i\rangle$ having small codimension whenever every $Q\in\mathcal{Q}_1$ satisfies $\mathrm{rad}\big(\langle Q', Q''\rangle\big) \ni$ products from other sets. Gupta (ECCC 2014) conjectured that then the whole configuration lies in an ideal generated by $c(k,t)$ polynomials, $c$ independent of $n$ and of the number of polynomials. This conjecture implies polynomial-time blackbox PIT for $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi(k)$ with bounded $t$.

**Hardness–randomness.** Kabanets–Impagliazzo: blackbox PIT in subexponential time for $\mathrm{VP}$ implies $\mathrm{NEXP}\not\subseteq\mathrm{P}/\mathrm{poly}$ or $\mathrm{perm}\notin\mathrm{VP}$. Conversely, Guo–Kumar–Saptharishi–Solomon: an explicit hard polynomial for a class closed under the relevant operations yields hitting sets for that class.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1979–80.** Schwartz, Zippel, Heintz–Schnorr: randomized PIT; existence of small hitting sets by counting.
- **2004.** Kabanets–Impagliazzo: PIT $\Leftrightarrow$ circuit lower bounds, making derandomization unavoidable-hard.
- **2005–13.** Depth three resolved for bounded top fan-in: Dvir–Shpilka (rank bounds), Kayal–Saxena (whitebox poly time for $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma(k)$), Karnin–Shpilka (blackbox quasi-poly), Saxena–Seshadhri (JACM 2013, optimal $O(k^2\log d)$ rank bound, blackbox $\mathrm{poly}(n) d^{O(k^2\log d)}$).
- **2008.** Agrawal–Vinay: chasm at depth four — depth-four PIT is as hard as general PIT. This is what makes depth four *the* target.
- **2008–13.** Saxena's duality trick for diagonal depth four; Forbes–Shpilka quasipolynomial hitting sets for ROABPs, covering diagonal $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi$ and set-multilinear models.
- **2010–11.** Karnin–Mukhopadhyay–Shpilka–Volkovich: quasipolynomial blackbox PIT for *multilinear* $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi(k)$; Saraf–Volkovich improve to $\mathrm{poly}$ in $n$ for constant $k$.
- **2012.** Beecken–Mittmann–Saxena and Agrawal–Saha–Saptharishi–Saxena: Jacobian/algebraic-independence based hitting sets for depth-$D$ occur-$k$ formulas and bounded transcendence degree.
- **2018–19.** Agrawal–Ghosh–Saxena bootstrapping: a hitting set of size $s^{o(\log\log s)}$ for $\mathrm{poly}(\log s)$-variate circuits upgrades to a near-polynomial hitting set for all of $\mathrm{VP}$; Kumar–Saptharishi–Tengse push this to near-optimal form. Applies verbatim to depth four.
- **2019–22.** Sylvester–Gallai program: Shpilka (quadratics), Peleg–Shpilka (generalized SG for quadratics; **polynomial-time deterministic PIT for $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi(2)$ with quadratic bottom**, STOC 2021), Oliveira–Sengupta (radical SG for cubics, FOCS 2022).
- **2021.** Dutta–Dwivedi–Saxena (CCC 2021): quasipolynomial-time blackbox PIT for $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi(k)$ with *bounded top fan-in and arbitrary degree* — the strongest general depth-four result to date.
- **2021–22.** Limaye–Srinivasan–Tavenas: first superpolynomial lower bounds for constant-depth algebraic circuits; Andrews–Forbes leverage ideal-theoretic lower bounds to obtain deterministic subexponential-time blackbox PIT for constant-depth circuits over characteristic $0$.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

| Restricted class | Result | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi(k)$, any degree $d$, char $0$ | blackbox, quasipolynomial $s^{O_k(\log s)}$ | Dutta–Dwivedi–Saxena 2021 |
| $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi(2)$, bottom degree $\le 2$ | deterministic **polynomial** time | Peleg–Shpilka 2021 |
| $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi(3)$, bottom degree $\le 2$ | polynomial time (Edelstein–Kelly for quadratics) | Peleg–Shpilka 2020/21 |
| bottom degree $\le 3$, radical SG configurations | codimension bounds independent of $n$ | Oliveira–Sengupta 2022 |
| multilinear $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi(k)$ | $n^{k^{O(k)}}$ blackbox, i.e. poly for constant $k$ | Saraf–Volkovich 2011 |
| diagonal / set-multilinear depth four, ROABPs | quasipolynomial $ (nds)^{O(\log s)}$ | Saxena 2008; Forbes–Shpilka 2013 |
| depth four, transcendence degree $\le k$ (char $0$ or $>d$) | $\mathrm{poly}(s)^{O(k)}$ hitting sets | Beecken–Mittmann–Saxena 2013; ASSS 2012 |
| $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma(k)$, i.e. bottom degree $1$ | $d^{O(k^2\log d)}$ blackbox | Saxena–Seshadhri 2013 |
| constant depth, char $0$ | deterministic $s^{O(s^{\varepsilon})}$-type subexponential blackbox | Andrews–Forbes 2022 |

Small-parameter checks: for $k=2$, $t=2$, $n$ arbitrary, the problem is fully solved. For $k$ constant and $t$ constant, it is solved modulo Gupta's conjecture, which is proven for $t\le 2$ and (in radical form) $t\le 3$.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Rank bounds fail beyond degree one.** The depth-three method rests on linear algebra: a vanishing sum of products of *linear* forms forces the forms into a low-dimensional space. For $t\ge 2$ the analogue must be ideal-theoretic (codimension of $\bigcap\langle\mathcal{Q}_i\rangle$), and no linear-algebraic substitute is known. Products of quadratics do not factor uniquely into a rigid geometric configuration.
- **Sylvester–Gallai over varieties.** The classical SG theorem — a finite point set with every line through two points containing a third is collinear — has robust proofs. Its polynomial version needs a statement about radical ideals $\mathrm{rad}(\langle Q_i,Q_j\rangle)$ containing other $Q_\ell$; existing proofs use heavy commutative algebra tailored degree-by-degree and blow up combinatorially at $t\ge 4$.
- **Unbounded top fan-in is a wall.** All techniques (rank, SG, Jacobian, duality) degrade at least exponentially in $k$. The chasm results require $k = s^{\Theta(1)}$, so the resolved regimes are provably far from the regime that implies general PIT.
- **Chicken-and-egg with lower bounds.** Kabanets–Impagliazzo makes any subexponential depth-four blackbox test imply a superpolynomial $\mathrm{VP}$-type lower bound. Known depth-four lower bounds ($n^{\Omega(\sqrt d)}$ for homogeneous depth four, LST's superpolynomial constant-depth bounds) are not of the *ideal-theoretic / robust* form the hardness-to-randomness converters need at the required strength.
- **Sparsity blowup.** Expanding a $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi$ circuit into monomials is exponential: $\prod_{j=1}^{d}(x_j+y_j)$ has $2^d$ terms. Sparse-PIT hitting sets are therefore useless directly.
- **Border complexity.** Approximative closures $\overline{\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi}$ can be strictly larger; hitting sets must be robust to limits, which several algebraic-geometric arguments are not.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: quasipolynomial hitting sets when $k = O(1)$ (any degree), polynomial when $(k,t)$ are both tiny, quasipolynomial for structured (multilinear, diagonal, low-transcendence-degree) depth four.

Wanted: quasipolynomial hitting sets when $k$ and $t$ are both polynomial in $s$ — the unrestricted class.

The exact step: prove Gupta's conjecture, or a suitable robust generalization, with codimension bound $c(k,t)$ growing at most polylogarithmically rather than being merely finite, **and** with $k$ allowed to grow. Equivalently, exhibit a polynomial map $G$ of degree $\mathrm{poly}(s)$ in $O(\log s)$ variables preserving nonzeroness of all size-$s$ depth-four circuits. Every current proof produces $c(k,t)$ that is at best tower-type in $t$ and requires $k$ bounded; both dependencies must be broken simultaneously.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Sylvester–Gallai school** (Peleg, Shpilka, Oliveira, Sengupta, Garg): pushing radical SG theorems from degree $3$ to arbitrary constant degree, with the goal of unconditional polynomial-time PIT for $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi(k)$ at constant $k,t$. *(frontier — verify)* extensions to degree $\ge 4$ and to Edelstein–Kelly configurations of higher arity remain the announced target.
- **IIT Kanpur / Saxena group** (Dutta, Dwivedi): incremental "paradigms" — logarithmic derivatives, DiDIL (divide-and-derive induction on top fan-in), and border PIT for depth three and four.
- **Lower-bounds-to-PIT** (Limaye, Srinivasan, Tavenas; Andrews, Forbes; Kumar, Saptharishi): converting constant-depth and polynomial-ideal lower bounds into hitting sets; the open target is bringing the running time from subexponential down to quasipolynomial.
- **Bootstrapping** (Agrawal, Ghosh, Saxena; Kumar, Saptharishi, Tengse): reducing the whole problem to hitting sets for $O(1)$-variate depth-four circuits — a formally sufficient and very narrow target.
- **Border/approximative complexity**: characterizing $\overline{\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi}$ and building hitting sets stable under degeneration.

## 8. Future Work

- Prove Gupta's conjecture for bottom degree $t=4$ and then for all constant $t$, with explicit codimension bounds.
- Obtain hitting sets for $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi(k)$ with $k = (\log s)^{\omega(1)}$ — the first genuinely unbounded-top-fan-in case.
- Construct a $\mathrm{poly}(s)$-size hitting set for $3$-variate depth-four circuits of size $s$ and degree $s$; by bootstrapping this nearly settles $\mathrm{VP}$.
- Prove ideal-theoretic (robust) lower bounds against depth four strong enough to feed Guo–Kumar–Saptharishi–Solomon at quasipolynomial loss.
- Settle whether $\overline{\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi(k)} = \Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi(k')$ for some $k'=f(k)$, which would transfer all existing hitting sets to the border.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**A depth-four identity with top fan-in 2 and quadratic bottom.** Over $\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z,w]$ take
$$ T_1 = (xy - zw)(xy + zw), \qquad T_2 = (x^2y^2 - z^2w^2)\cdot 1 . $$
Then $f = T_1 - T_2$ is a $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi(2)$ circuit with bottom degree $2$, and
$$ f = (x^2y^2 - z^2w^2) - (x^2y^2 - z^2w^2) = 0 . $$
No individual factor vanishes; the identity comes entirely from a factorization coincidence. This is exactly the phenomenon a hitting set must survive: the certificate of vanishing is *ideal-theoretic*, since $\mathrm{rad}\langle xy-zw,\, xy+zw\rangle = \langle xy, zw\rangle \ni x^2y^2 - z^2w^2$, which is the shape Gupta's conjecture abstracts.

**Why sparse hitting sets do not apply.** Perturb to
$$ g = (xy-zw)(xy+zw) - (x^2y^2 - z^2w^2) + \varepsilon\, x y z w = \varepsilon\, xyzw \neq 0 . $$
The circuit for $g$ has $O(1)$ gates and degree $4$, but the two big terms each expand to $3$ monomials that cancel completely. In general $\prod_{j=1}^{d}(x_j + y_j)$ expands to $2^d$ monomials, so any test that first writes the polynomial in the monomial basis costs $2^{\Omega(d)}$.

**A hitting set for this instance.** $g$ has degree $4$ in $4$ variables, so by Schwartz–Zippel the grid $S^4$ with $S=\{0,1,2,3,4\}$, $|S^4| = 625$, contains a non-root: at $a=(1,1,1,1)$, $g(a) = \varepsilon \ne 0$. Crucially this grid is derived from the *degree*, not the circuit — for degree $d = s$ it has size $(s+1)^n$, exponential in $n$. The Peleg–Shpilka theorem removes the $n$-dependence in this case: for $\Sigma\Pi\Sigma\Pi(2)$ with quadratic bottom, any simple minimal identity forces all the $Q_{ij}$ into a subspace of dimension $c$ for an absolute constant $c$, so one may substitute $x \mapsto A\,u$ with $u \in \mathbb{F}^{c}$ and $A$ a generic $n\times c$ matrix from a small explicit family, reducing to $\mathrm{poly}(s)$ evaluations. Extending precisely this substitution step from $(k,t) = (2,2)$ to $k,t = \mathrm{poly}(s)$ is the open problem.
