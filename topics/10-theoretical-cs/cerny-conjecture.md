# Černý Conjecture

> **Topic:** Theoretical Computer Science · **ID:** `10-theoretical-cs/cerny-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $\mathscr{A} = (Q, \Sigma, \delta)$ be a deterministic finite automaton (DFA) with $|Q| = n$ states, a finite input alphabet $\Sigma$, and a **total** transition function $\delta : Q \times \Sigma \to Q$. A word $w \in \Sigma^*$ is a **reset word** (synchronizing word) if it maps every state to one and the same state:
$$\bigl|\, Q \cdot w \,\bigr| = 1, \qquad \text{where } Q\cdot w = \{\delta(q,w) : q \in Q\}.$$
$\mathscr{A}$ is **synchronizing** if some reset word exists. Write $\mathrm{rt}(\mathscr{A})$ (the *reset threshold*) for the length of a shortest reset word.

**Conjecture (Černý, 1964/1971).** Every synchronizing DFA on $n$ states satisfies
$$\mathrm{rt}(\mathscr{A}) \;\le\; (n-1)^2 .$$

The bound is stated with no restriction on $|\Sigma|$, and it is tight: the Černý automata $\mathscr{C}_n$ (Section 10) attain $(n-1)^2$ exactly. A complete resolution requires either a proof of the $(n-1)^2$ upper bound for all $n$ and all alphabets, or an explicit synchronizing DFA with $\mathrm{rt}(\mathscr{A}) > (n-1)^2$. The weaker **Černý–Pin problem** — prove *any* upper bound of the form $O(n^2)$ — is also open and is the target of most current work.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Transition monoid.** Each letter $a \in \Sigma$ induces a map $\hat a : Q \to Q$. The monoid $M(\mathscr{A}) \le \mathrm{Full}(Q) \cong T_n$ (the full transformation monoid on $n$ points) generated by $\{\hat a\}_{a\in\Sigma}$ is the *transition monoid*. Then
$$\mathscr{A} \text{ is synchronizing} \iff M(\mathscr{A}) \text{ contains a constant map}.$$
Define the **rank** (or *deficiency-complement*) of $w$ as $\mathrm{rk}(w) = |Q\cdot w|$. Synchronization is the problem of driving $\mathrm{rk}$ from $n$ to $1$.

**Pair-merging criterion (Černý).** $\mathscr{A}$ is synchronizing iff for every pair $\{p,q\} \subseteq Q$ there is $w$ with $\delta(p,w) = \delta(q,w)$. Equivalently, the **pair automaton** on $\binom{Q}{2} \cup \{\Delta\}$ has $\Delta$ reachable from every vertex. This yields Eppstein's greedy algorithm: repeatedly merge one pair, each merge costing a word of length $\le \binom{n}{2}$ obtained by breadth-first search, giving
$$\mathrm{rt}(\mathscr{A}) \;\le\; (n-1)\binom{n}{2} \;=\; \tfrac{n(n-1)^2}{2} \;=\; O(n^3),$$
and a decision procedure running in time $O(n^2|\Sigma|)$.

**Extension / avoiding-word method (Frankl; Pin; Klyachko–Rystsov–Spivak).** For $S \subseteq Q$ and $w \in \Sigma^*$ let $S w^{-1} = \{q : \delta(q,w) \in S\}$. The key lemma is: if $\emptyset \ne S \subsetneq Q$ and $\mathscr{A}$ is synchronizing, there is $w$ with $|S w^{-1}| > |S|$ and $|w| \le n - |S|$. Iterating from a singleton to $Q$ and reversing gives the **Frankl–Pin bound**
$$\mathrm{rt}(\mathscr{A}) \;\le\; \sum_{k=1}^{n-1} k \cdot \frac{?}{} \;\le\; \frac{n^3 - n}{6} \approx 0.1667\,n^3 .$$

**Linear-algebraic model.** Identify $S \subseteq Q$ with its characteristic vector $\chi_S \in \mathbb{Q}^Q$ and each letter with its $0/1$ transition matrix $A_a$ (row-stochastic). Then $\chi_{Sw^{-1}} = A_w \chi_S$ where $A_w = A_{a_1}\cdots A_{a_k}$. Synchronization corresponds to reaching a rank-one matrix $A_w = \mathbf{1}e_q^{\top}$. Kari's method studies the subspace $V \le \mathbb{Q}^Q$ spanned by reachable $\chi_S$ and exploits invariant vectors: for **Eulerian** automata the all-ones vector is a left eigenvector of every $A_a$, which forces a flow/counting identity strong enough to give $(n-1)^2$.

**Complexity backdrop.** Deciding synchronizability is in $\mathrm{P}$; computing $\mathrm{rt}(\mathscr{A})$ is NP-hard (Eppstein 1990), the decision version "$\mathrm{rt} \le k$" is $\mathrm{DP}$-complete, and approximating $\mathrm{rt}$ within $n^{1-\varepsilon}$ is NP-hard (Gawrychowski–Straszak 2015). Hardness of *computation* is not an obstacle to the *bound*, but it rules out approaches that require exhibiting shortest words.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1964.** Ján Černý, studying "homogeneous experiments" on finite automata, constructs the family $\mathscr{C}_n$ with $\mathrm{rt} = (n-1)^2$ and proves the $2^n - n - 1$ upper bound.
- **1971.** Černý, Pirická and Rosenauerová state the conjecture $\mathrm{rt} \le (n-1)^2$ in print.
- **1970s.** Starke, Kohavi, and others obtain the first polynomial bounds; Černý's own $O(2^n)$ is superseded.
- **1982–1983.** Péter Frankl proves an extremal set-theoretic lemma yielding $\frac{n^3-n}{6}$; Pin translates it into automata language. Klyachko, Rystsov and Spivak give an independent proof (1987). This bound stands, unimproved in its leading constant, for **35 years**.
- **1990.** Eppstein: polynomial-time synchronizability test, NP-hardness of the shortest reset word, and the $(n-1)^2$ bound for *monotonic* (cyclic-order-preserving) automata.
- **2003.** Kari proves the conjecture for Eulerian automata.
- **2008.** Volkov's LATA survey becomes the standard reference; Trahtman resolves the closely related **Road Coloring Problem** (2009), showing the surrounding toolbox is not powerless.
- **2018.** Marek Szykuła (STACS) achieves the first improvement of the cubic constant: $\mathrm{rt} \le \frac{114}{685}n^3 + O(n^2) \approx 0.1664\,n^3$.
- **2019.** Yaroslav Shitov lowers it further to $\approx 0.1654\,n^3$. This remains the SOTA general bound.

**SOTA summary.** Best general upper bound $\approx 0.1654\,n^3$; best lower bound $(n-1)^2$; the gap is a full factor of $\Theta(n)$.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

Proven classes (all achieve $O(n^2)$ or better):

| Class | Bound | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Eulerian automata (all states in/out-degree $|\Sigma|$) | $(n-1)^2$ | Kari 2003 |
| Circular automata (some letter acts as an $n$-cycle) | $(n-1)^2$ | Dubuc 1998 |
| Monotonic automata (preserve a cyclic order) | $(n-1)^2$ | Eppstein 1990 |
| Automata preserving a linear order | $n-1$ | Ananichev–Volkov 2004 |
| Aperiodic automata ($M(\mathscr{A})$ $\mathcal{H}$-trivial) | $\tfrac{n(n-1)}{2}$ | Trahtman 2007 |
| One-cluster automata, prime cycle length | $2n^2 - 6n + 5$ | Steinberg 2011 |
| One-cluster automata (general) | $2n^2 - 7n + 7$ | Béal–Berlinkov–Perrin 2011 |
| Automata with a letter of deficiency 1 | $O(n^2)$ | Rystsov 1997 |

**Computational verification.** Exhaustive/branch-and-bound searches confirm the conjecture and identify all extremal automata for: $n \le 12$ with $|\Sigma| = 2$; $n \le 10$ with $|\Sigma| = 3$; $n \le 7$ with $|\Sigma| = 4$ (Kisielewicz–Kowalski–Szykuła 2015; de Bondt–Don–Zantema 2019). In every verified range the *only* automata attaining $(n-1)^2$ are $\mathscr{C}_n$ plus a short, now-classified list of sporadic examples with $n \le 6$ (Ananichev–Gusev–Volkov 2013).

**Random automata.** For a uniformly random $n$-state DFA on 2 letters, synchronization holds with probability $1 - \Theta(1/n)$ (Berlinkov 2016) and $\mathrm{rt} = O(n \log^3 n)$ with high probability (Nicaud 2016). So the conjecture is true for *almost all* automata; the difficulty is concentrated on a measure-zero set.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **The rank-drop accounting loses a factor $n$.** Every known general argument decrements $\mathrm{rk}$ (or increments $|Sw^{-1}|$) $n-1$ times, and can only bound the cost of a single step by $\Theta(n)$ *on average over the step index*, giving $\sum_{k} \Theta(k) \cdot \Theta(n) = \Theta(n^3)$. To reach $(n-1)^2$ one needs the *total* cost of all $n-1$ steps to be $O(n^2)$, i.e. an amortization across steps. No mechanism is known that reuses work between successive merges — after a merge the subset structure is essentially reset.
- **Extremal set theory saturates.** Frankl's lemma is tight as a statement about set systems; Szykuła's and Shitov's improvements come from finding *slack* in special configurations, not from a new principle. The gains ($0.1667 \to 0.1654$) are third-decimal, indicating the method's ceiling is a cubic bound.
- **Algebraic invariants exist only in special cases.** Kari's Eulerian proof needs $\mathbf{1}^{\top}A_a = \mathbf{1}^{\top}$; Steinberg's needs a primitive cyclic structure. General DFAs have transition matrices with no common eigenvector beyond the trivial one, so the linear-algebraic subspace $V$ can be all of $\mathbb{Q}^Q$ with no usable filtration.
- **No natural potential function.** Attempts to define $\Phi(S)$ decreasing by $\Omega(1)$ per letter all fail: single letters can *increase* every reasonable measure (entropy, subset size, spanning-tree count), and the loss is not bounded by the gain.
- **Kari's counterexample to the natural strengthening.** Pin conjectured that any $k$-element subset can be extended in $\le n-k$ steps in a uniform "$k$-compressibility" sense; Kari (2001) refuted it, killing the most attractive inductive route.
- **Hardness signals.** $n^{1-\varepsilon}$-inapproximability means no efficiently constructible witness word can be near-optimal in general, so constructive proofs must build *inefficient* certificates.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: $\mathrm{rt}(\mathscr{A}) \le 0.1654\,n^3(1+o(1))$ in general; $\mathrm{rt}(\mathscr{A}) \le (n-1)^2$ under a structural hypothesis (Eulerian, circular, monotonic, aperiodic, one-cluster) or for $n \le 12$, $|\Sigma|=2$. Conjectured: $(n-1)^2$ unconditionally.

The exact missing step is a **quadratic extension lemma**. Currently, growing a reachable-preimage set $S$ from size $k$ to $k+1$ is known to cost $\le n-k$ letters, and the sum $\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}(n-k)\cdot k$-type accounting is cubic. Černý's bound would follow from either:

1. a lemma showing $|S| \mapsto |S|+1$ costs $O(1)$ *amortized* letters over the whole run (total $O(n)$ merges $\times$ $O(n)$ letters), or
2. a single global argument producing a reset word directly, bypassing the merge-by-merge decomposition.

Even removing the $\Theta(n)$ factor down to $O(n^{3-\varepsilon})$ for some fixed $\varepsilon > 0$ would be a landmark; nothing beyond constant-factor improvements is currently known.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Wrocław (Szykuła, Kisielewicz, Kowalski).** Computer-assisted improvement of the cubic constant via exhaustive analysis of "hard" subset configurations, plus large-scale enumeration of slowly synchronizing automata. Their branch-and-bound codebase is the source of the $n \le 12$ verification.
- **Ural Federal University (Volkov, Gusev, Ananichev).** Structural classification of extremal and near-extremal automata; the working hypothesis is that automata with $\mathrm{rt} \ge n^2/2$ form a finite, describable list. Volkov's 2022 *Russian Mathematical Surveys* article is the current definitive survey.
- **Berlinkov (probabilistic).** Sharpening "almost all automata synchronize quickly" toward explicit structural dichotomies — separating the generic quadratic-or-better regime from a hoped-for small exceptional family.
- **Béal–Perrin (symbolic dynamics).** Reset thresholds via road colorings and constrained-channel coding; the one-cluster results came from this school.
- **Frontier.** Attempts to bound $\mathrm{rt}$ by $O(n^2 \log n)$ using entropy-compression / Lovász-Local-Lemma-style arguments have been circulated but no such bound has been verified *(frontier — verify)*. Likewise, SAT/SMT-based verification pushing exhaustive checks to $n = 13$ with $|\Sigma| = 2$ is claimed but not independently confirmed *(frontier — verify)*.

## 8. Future Work

- **Amortized extension.** Find a lemma bounding the *total* length needed for all $n-1$ extensions by $O(n^2)$, e.g. by showing the extension words for successive $S$ can be chosen with large common prefixes.
- **Widen the algebraic classes.** Interpolate between Eulerian and general: bound $\mathrm{rt}$ in terms of the deviation of $\sum_a A_a$ from a doubly stochastic matrix, so that Kari's argument degrades gracefully rather than vanishing.
- **Finish the extremal classification.** Prove Volkov's programme: that $\mathrm{rt}(\mathscr{A}) > n^2/2$ forces $\mathscr{A}$ to contain a $\mathscr{C}_n$-like circular sub-structure. This would reduce Černý to a finite check plus the circular case (already solved by Dubuc).
- **Attack the weak form.** A proof of $\mathrm{rt} = O(n^2)$ with any constant is regarded as the realistic next milestone; the exact constant $1$ in $(n-1)^2$ may be much harder than the exponent.
- **Lower-bound search.** Continue exhaustive and heuristic search for $\mathrm{rt} > (n-1)^2$ over $n \le 15$; the absence of any counterexample in $\sim 60$ years of search over increasingly large ranges is the main empirical evidence.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**The Černý automaton $\mathscr{C}_n$.** Take $Q = \mathbb{Z}_n = \{0,1,\dots,n-1\}$, $\Sigma = \{a,b\}$, and
$$\delta(q,a) = \begin{cases} 1 & q = 0\\ q & q \ne 0\end{cases}, \qquad \delta(q,b) = q+1 \bmod n .$$
So $b$ rotates, and $a$ is the single "collapsing" letter that merges $0$ into $1$ and fixes everything else.

**Upper bound for $\mathscr{C}_n$.** The word $w_n = (a\,b^{\,n-1})^{\,n-2}\,a$ resets it. Its length is $(n-2)\cdot n + 1 = n^2 - 2n + 1 = (n-1)^2$.

**Trace for $n = 4$** ($w_4 = a\,b^3\,a\,b^3\,a$, length $9$):

| step | letter | current image set |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | — | $\{0,1,2,3\}$ |
| 1 | $a$ | $\{1,2,3\}$ |
| 2 | $b$ | $\{2,3,0\}$ |
| 3 | $b$ | $\{3,0,1\}$ |
| 4 | $b$ | $\{0,1,2\}$ |
| 5 | $a$ | $\{1,2\}$ |
| 6 | $b$ | $\{2,3\}$ |
| 7 | $b$ | $\{3,0\}$ |
| 8 | $b$ | $\{0,1\}$ |
| 9 | $a$ | $\{1\}$ |

Nine letters, and $(4-1)^2 = 9$.

**Why nothing shorter works.** Only $a$ reduces cardinality, and it does so exactly when the current set $S$ contains $0$ but not $1$ — otherwise $|S\cdot a| = |S|$. Starting from $|S| = n$ we need $n-1$ applications of $a$ that actually reduce. Between two successive reducing applications, the set must be rotated by $b$ until the unique "hole pattern" places $0 \in S,\; 1 \notin S$ again. For $\mathscr{C}_4$ one checks that after each reduction the required number of $b$'s is exactly $3 = n-1$, and there are $n-2 = 2$ such gaps, giving $2\cdot 3 + 3 = 9$ letters at minimum. The general argument (Černý 1964) formalizes this rotation-counting to show $\mathrm{rt}(\mathscr{C}_n) = (n-1)^2$ exactly.

**What the example says about the conjecture.** $\mathscr{C}_n$ is circular and Eulerian-like, so both Dubuc's and Kari's theorems cover it. The conjecture's difficulty is not in $\mathscr{C}_n$ but in showing that *no other structure* — in particular no automaton lacking a cyclic permutation letter — can force more than $(n-1)^2$. Exhaustive search up to $n=12$ (2 letters) found nothing beating $\mathscr{C}_n$; the proof that nothing ever will is missing.
