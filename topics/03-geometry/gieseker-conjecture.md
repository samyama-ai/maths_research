# Gieseker Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebraic & Differential Geometry · **ID:** `03-geometry/gieseker-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p>0$ and $X$ a smooth variety over $k$. A **stratified bundle** on $X$ is a coherent module over the full ring of Grothendieck differential operators $\mathcal{D}_{X/k}$ that is $\mathcal{O}_X$-coherent — the characteristic-$p$ substitute for a flat vector bundle.

**Gieseker's conjecture (1975).** If $X$ is smooth projective over $k$ and the étale fundamental group $\pi_1^{\text{ét}}(X,x)$ is trivial, then every stratified bundle on $X$ is trivial, i.e. isomorphic to $(\mathcal{O}_X^{\,r}, d)$.

This statement was **proved** by Esnault and Mehta in 2010. The entry stays **open** because the conjecture is now understood as the proper case of a family of statements, and the natural generalizations remain unresolved:

- **(G1, open) Tame/open case.** $X$ smooth proper, $D\subset X$ a normal crossings divisor, $U=X\setminus D$. If the tame fundamental group $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U)$ is trivial, is every **regular singular** stratified bundle on $U$ trivial?
- **(G2, open) Proper non-projective case.** Does the theorem hold for smooth proper $X$ that is not projective? *(frontier — verify)*
- **(G3, open) Structural refinement.** Describe $\pi^{\mathrm{strat}}(X,x)$ in general in terms of $\pi_1^{\text{ét}}(X,x)$ and $F$-periodicity data; no conjectural dictionary is known beyond the trivial case.
- **(G4, open) Effectivity.** The Esnault–Mehta proof is non-effective. Bound, in terms of $\dim X$, $\operatorname{rank}$ and a polarization, the data forcing triviality.

A complete resolution of (G1) means a proof or a counterexample: a smooth $U$ with $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U)=1$ carrying a non-trivial regular singular stratified bundle.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Differential operators.** For $X$ smooth over $k$, $\mathcal{D}_{X/k}=\bigcup_n \mathcal{D}^{(n)}_{X/k}$ is generated by divided-power derivations $\partial^{[m]}$; it is not generated by $\mathcal{T}_{X/k}$ in characteristic $p$.

**Stratified bundle.** An $\mathcal{O}_X$-coherent $\mathcal{D}_{X/k}$-module $E$; such $E$ is automatically locally free.

**Katz's equivalence ($F$-divided sheaves).** Let $F:X\to X$ be the absolute Frobenius. The category of stratified bundles is equivalent to that of sequences
$$
E_\bullet=\big((E_i)_{i\ge 0},\ \sigma_i: F^*E_{i+1}\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }E_i\big),
$$
with $E_i$ locally free of constant rank $r$. Triviality means $E_\bullet\cong(\mathcal{O}_X^{\,r},\mathrm{id})$.

**Tannakian structure.** $\mathrm{Strat}(X)$ is a $k$-linear rigid tensor category; a rational point $x$ gives a fiber functor $E_\bullet\mapsto E_0|_x$, so
$$
\mathrm{Strat}(X)\ \simeq\ \mathrm{Rep}_k\big(\pi^{\mathrm{strat}}(X,x)\big)
$$
for a pro-affine group scheme $\pi^{\mathrm{strat}}(X,x)$, which is **pro-smooth** (dos Santos, 2007). Gieseker's conjecture reads $\pi_1^{\text{ét}}(X)=1\Rightarrow\pi^{\mathrm{strat}}(X,x)=1$.

**Lange–Stuhler.** $E_\bullet$ has finite monodromy iff it is $F$-periodic, i.e. $F^{n*}E\cong E$ for some $n\ge1$ compatibly; equivalently $E$ is trivialized by a finite étale cover. Hence
$$
\pi^{\mathrm{strat}}(X,x)^{\text{finite quotients}}\ \cong\ \pi_1^{\text{ét}}(X,x),
$$
so the whole difficulty is ruling out **infinite, non-étale** monodromy.

**Regular singularity.** For $U=X\setminus D$, $E$ is regular singular along $D$ if it extends to an $\mathcal{O}_X$-coherent module over $\mathcal{D}_{X/k}(\log D)$ — the log differential operators. This is the correct condition matching tame ramification (Kindler, 2015).

**Slope/boundedness input.** For a polarization $H$ and semistable $E$ of rank $r$ on $X$ of dimension $d$, Langer's restriction and boundedness theorems control the family $\{E_i\}$; since $\deg_H F^*E_{i+1}=p\cdot\deg_H E_{i+1}$, the constraint
$$
\deg_H E_i \;=\; p\,\deg_H E_{i+1}\qquad\text{for all } i
$$
forces $\deg_H E_i=0$ once degrees are bounded, and analogously for $c_2$-type invariants.

## 3. History & State of the Art

- **1975.** D. Gieseker, *Flat bundles and the fundamental group in non-zero characteristics*, Ann. Scuola Norm. Sup. Pisa. He introduces $\mathcal{D}$-module/$F$-divided bundles, proves the equivalence with Katz (appendix), and formulates the conjecture as the char-$p$ analogue of: a simply connected complex manifold has no non-trivial flat bundle.
- **1977.** Lange–Stuhler characterize étale-trivializable bundles by $F$-periodicity — the finite-monodromy half of the picture.
- **1982.** Nori's fundamental group scheme $\pi^N(X,x)$ classifies essentially finite bundles; $\pi^{\mathrm{strat}}$ is the "infinite-dimensional" companion.
- **2004–2011.** Langer's boundedness and restriction theorems for semistable sheaves in char $p$, plus the $S$-fundamental group scheme, provide the numerical engine.
- **2007.** dos Santos: smoothness of $\pi^{\mathrm{strat}}$, and computation of $\pi^{\mathrm{strat}}$ for abelian varieties — showing $\pi^{\mathrm{strat}}$ can be far larger than $\pi_1^{\text{ét}}$-related data would suggest.
- **2010.** **Esnault–Mehta**, *Simply connected projective manifolds in characteristic $p>0$ have no nontrivial stratified bundles*, Invent. Math. 181. Proof: reduce to a finitely generated $\mathbb{F}_p$-algebra model, use boundedness to place all $E_i$ in one moduli space of semistable sheaves, then apply **Hrushovski's theorem** on density of Frobenius-twisted fixed points in difference varieties to produce a Frobenius-periodic point; Lange–Stuhler then makes the bundle étale-trivializable, and $\pi_1^{\text{ét}}=1$ kills it.
- **2012–2016.** Kindler builds the regular singular theory and the tame dictionary; Esnault–Kindler prove Lefschetz theorems for tame coverings, reducing parts of (G1) to surfaces/curves.
- **2013.** Esnault–Langer prove an equicharacteristic analogue of the $p$-curvature conjecture, using the same circle of ideas.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **Smooth projective $X$ over $\bar{\mathbb{F}}_p$ or any algebraically closed $k$, any dimension $d\ge1$, any rank $r$, with $\pi_1^{\text{ét}}(X)=1$: theorem** (Esnault–Mehta 2010). Includes $\mathbb{P}^d$, smooth complete intersections of dimension $\ge3$ in $\mathbb{P}^N$, smooth rational and Fano varieties with vanishing $\pi_1^{\text{ét}}$.
- **$X=\mathbb{P}^1$ (and $\mathbb{P}^d$):** elementary, via Grothendieck splitting plus Lang's theorem — see §10.
- **Rank $r=1$, any smooth projective $X$:** stratified line bundles are governed by $\varprojlim(\mathrm{Pic}(X),\times p)$; triviality of $\pi_1^{\text{ét}}$ forces $\mathrm{Pic}^\tau$-torsion-freeness arguments to close the case without Hrushovski.
- **Finite monodromy, any $X$:** Lange–Stuhler (1977) — unconditional.
- **Abelian varieties:** $\pi^{\mathrm{strat}}$ computed by dos Santos (2007); consistent with, but not implied by, the conjecture (here $\pi_1^{\text{ét}}\neq1$).
- **Tame case (G1), known instances:** $\dim U=1$, i.e. smooth affine curves — regular singular stratified bundles with trivial tame $\pi_1$ are trivial (Kindler 2015). Also known when $U$ admits a good compactification with a suitable ample boundary and after Lefschetz reduction (Esnault–Kindler 2016). Open for general $U$ with $\dim\ge2$.
- **Counterexample boundary:** without regular singularity, (G1) fails — $\mathbb{A}^1$ and $\mathbb{G}_m$ carry irregular stratified bundles of infinite non-étale monodromy.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No Riemann–Hilbert correspondence.** In char $p$ there is no topological space whose representations classify $\mathcal{D}$-modules. The étale $\pi_1$ only sees the finite quotients (Lange–Stuhler), so the entire content is invisible to étale homotopy theory.
- **$\mathcal{D}_{X/k}$ has infinite $p$-filtration.** Unlike char $0$, $\mathcal{D}$ is not generated in degree $1$; one must control the whole tower $(E_i)$ at once, not a single connection. Perturbation and deformation arguments act on one level and do not propagate.
- **Non-effectivity of the model-theoretic step.** Hrushovski's density theorem is an existence statement about $\mathbb{F}_q$-points of difference schemes; it yields a periodic point but no bound on the period or on $q$. This is why (G4) is untouched.
- **Boundedness fails on open varieties.** The Esnault–Mehta argument needs a projective moduli space of semistable sheaves with fixed numerical invariants. On $U=X\setminus D$ there is no such space; log-semistability with respect to $(X,D)$ does not give the required uniform boundedness, and the degrees can drift along the tower. This is the main block on (G1) and, in a different form, on (G2), where the absence of an ample line bundle removes slope stability altogether.
- **Simpson-type non-abelian Hodge theory** works over $\mathbb{C}$ (flat bundles $\leftrightarrow$ Higgs bundles); the Ogus–Vologodsky char-$p$ analogue is limited to levels $<p$ and does not see the full stratified tower.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: triviality of $\mathrm{Strat}(X)$ for **smooth projective** $X$ with $\pi_1^{\text{ét}}(X)=1$.

Open: the same statement when properness is replaced by a **tameness hypothesis at the boundary**. Precisely, the missing step is a substitute for the chain

$$
\text{boundedness of }\{E_i\}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{one moduli space}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{Frobenius-periodic point}\ \Longrightarrow\ F\text{-periodicity},
$$

for regular singular stratified bundles on $U$. One needs a **projective moduli space of log-semistable parabolic/logarithmic sheaves on $(X,D)$** whose numerical invariants are stable under the identification $\sigma_i$, together with an equality of the form $\deg_H E_i=p\deg_H E_{i+1}$ for parabolic degrees, so that Hrushovski's theorem can again be applied. No such compatibility between Frobenius pullback and parabolic weights has been established in dimension $\ge2$.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Berlin / Freie Universität (Esnault school)** and collaborators continue on the tame conjecture (G1) and on companions/$\ell$-adic analogues; the guiding heuristic is that regular singular stratified bundles should behave like tame $\ell$-adic local systems. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Warsaw (Langer)** — semipositivity and nearby-cycle techniques in char $p$, aimed at extending boundedness to logarithmic settings; a log-version of the restriction theorem is the announced target. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Kindler and coauthors** — parabolic and log-$\mathcal{D}$-module formalism, and Lefschetz-type reductions from $\dim\ge3$ to surfaces for tame coverings.
- **dos Santos and the Tannakian group school (IMPA / Brazil)** — explicit computation of $\pi^{\mathrm{strat}}$ for concrete families (abelian varieties, homogeneous spaces, curves), feeding (G3).
- **Model-theoretic side** — attempts to make the difference-scheme step effective for fixed rank; nothing beyond rank $2$ is announced. *(frontier — verify)*

## 8. Future Work

1. Construct a projective coarse moduli space of $\mu$-semistable **logarithmic/parabolic** sheaves on $(X,D)$ that is stable under $F^*$ up to weight rescaling; then rerun the Esnault–Mehta argument verbatim for (G1).
2. Prove a boundedness statement for stratified towers on smooth proper non-projective $X$ using Kollár–Nakayama positivity substitutes rather than an ample $H$ — this attacks (G2).
3. Make Hrushovski's step effective for fixed rank $r$ and fixed Hilbert polynomial: an explicit $N(r,d,H)$ with $F^{N*}E\cong E$.
4. Classify $\pi^{\mathrm{strat}}$ for surfaces with $\pi_1^{\text{ét}}$ finite, testing whether $\pi^{\mathrm{strat}}$ is always an extension of $\pi_1^{\text{ét}}$ by a pro-unipotent/pro-reductive part determined by Frobenius-semistable Higgs data.
5. Relate (G1) to de Jong's conjecture on arithmetic fundamental groups, which predicts analogous rigidity for $\overline{\mathbb{F}_p(t)}$-representations.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Claim.** Every stratified bundle on $X=\mathbb{P}^1_k$, $k=\bar k$, $\operatorname{char}k=p>0$, is trivial. (Here $\pi_1^{\text{ét}}(\mathbb{P}^1)=1$.)

Let $E_\bullet=((E_i),\sigma_i)$ have rank $r$. By Grothendieck's splitting theorem,
$$
E_i\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}(a_{i,j}),\qquad a_{i,1}\ge\cdots\ge a_{i,r}.
$$
Since $F^*\mathcal{O}(a)=\mathcal{O}(pa)$, the isomorphism $\sigma_i:F^*E_{i+1}\cong E_i$ gives, comparing splitting types,
$$
a_{i,j}\;=\;p\,a_{i+1,j}\qquad\text{for all }i\ge0,\ 1\le j\le r .
$$
Hence $a_{0,j}=p^{n}a_{n,j}$ for every $n$, so $p^n \mid a_{0,j}$ for all $n$, forcing $a_{0,j}=0$; the same for every $i$. Thus $E_i\cong\mathcal{O}^{\,r}$ for all $i$.

Now the stratification is the data of isomorphisms
$$
\sigma_i:\ F^*\mathcal{O}^{\,r}=\mathcal{O}^{\,r}\ \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }\ \mathcal{O}^{\,r},
\qquad \sigma_i\in \mathrm{GL}_r\big(H^0(\mathbb{P}^1,\mathcal{O})\big)=\mathrm{GL}_r(k).
$$
Changing the trivialization of $E_{i+1}$ by $g_{i+1}\in\mathrm{GL}_r(k)$ replaces $\sigma_i$ by $g_i^{-1}\sigma_i\,g_{i+1}^{(p)}$, where $g^{(p)}=(g_{uv}^{\,p})$. Solve inductively: set $g_0=\mathrm{id}$; given $g_i$, we need $g_{i+1}$ with
$$
g_i^{-1}\sigma_i\,g_{i+1}^{(p)}=\mathrm{id}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad g_{i+1}^{(p)}=\sigma_i^{-1}g_i .
$$
The map $\mathrm{GL}_r(k)\to\mathrm{GL}_r(k)$, $g\mapsto g^{(p)}$, is surjective because $k$ is algebraically closed (extract $p$-th roots entrywise via the Lang isogeny for $\mathrm{GL}_r$). So $g_{i+1}$ exists, and $E_\bullet\cong(\mathcal{O}^{\,r},\mathrm{id})$. $\square$

**Contrast showing what the general conjecture must overcome.** On $U=\mathbb{G}_m=\operatorname{Spec}k[t,t^{-1}]$, the trivial line bundle admits stratifications $\sigma_i=\lambda_i t^{n_i}$ with $\lambda_i\in k^\times$, $n_i\in\mathbb{Z}$; the divisibility argument above collapses because $\mathrm{Pic}(\mathbb{G}_m)=0$ and there is no degree to bound. The resulting rank-$1$ stratified bundles are non-trivial exactly when $(n_i)$ is not of the form $(m_i-p\,m_{i+1})$, and the regular singular ones match the prime-to-$p$ Kummer characters of $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(\mathbb{G}_m)=\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{(p')}$. This is exactly the phenomenon that (G1) must control in dimension $\ge2$: without properness, the numerical invariants that drive the $\mathbb{P}^1$ proof and the Esnault–Mehta proof simply vanish.
