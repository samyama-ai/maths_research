# Grothendieck p-Curvature Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebraic & Differential Geometry · **ID:** `03-geometry/grothendieck-p-curvature-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $X$ be a smooth geometrically connected variety over a number field $K$, and let $(\mathcal{E}, \nabla)$ be a vector bundle with an integrable (flat) connection on $X$. Spread out to a smooth model $\mathcal{X} \to \operatorname{Spec} \mathcal{O}_K[1/N]$ carrying $(\mathcal{E},\nabla)$. For each prime $\mathfrak{p}$ of good reduction one obtains a connection on the reduction $\mathcal{X}_{\mathfrak{p}}$ in characteristic $p$, which has a **$p$-curvature** $\psi_{\mathfrak{p}}$.

**Conjecture (Grothendieck).** The connection $(\mathcal{E},\nabla)$ has a full set of algebraic solutions — equivalently, the differential Galois group of $\nabla$ is finite, equivalently $\nabla$ becomes trivial on a finite étale cover of $X$ — **if and only if** $\psi_{\mathfrak{p}} = 0$ for almost all primes $\mathfrak{p}$.

The "only if" direction is elementary. The content is: *vanishing $p$-curvature mod almost all $p$ forces algebraicity in characteristic zero.* A complete proof must produce, from the mod-$p$ data alone, a finite étale cover $Y \to X_{\bar K}$ trivializing $\nabla$; a disproof needs a flat connection with $\psi_{\mathfrak p}=0$ for a density-one set of $\mathfrak p$ and infinite monodromy.

A stronger form, the **Grothendieck–Katz conjecture**, predicts that the Lie algebra of the differential Galois group is the smallest algebraic Lie subalgebra whose reductions mod $\mathfrak p$ contain the $p$-curvatures for almost all $\mathfrak p$.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**$p$-curvature.** Let $S$ be a scheme of characteristic $p>0$, $X/S$ smooth, and $(\mathcal E,\nabla)$ a module with integrable connection. For a derivation $D \in \operatorname{Der}(\mathcal O_X)$, the operator $D^p$ is again a derivation. Define
$$\psi(D) \;=\; \nabla(D)^p - \nabla(D^p) \;\in\; \operatorname{End}_{\mathcal O_X}(\mathcal E).$$
Integrability plus Jacobson's formula show $\psi(D)$ is $\mathcal O_X$-linear and that $D \mapsto \psi(D)$ is $p$-linear, giving
$$\psi \in H^0\!\left(X, \; F^*\Omega^1_{X/S} \otimes \operatorname{End}(\mathcal E)\right),$$
where $F$ is the absolute Frobenius. This is the **$p$-curvature** of $\nabla$.

**Cartier's theorem.** $\psi = 0$ if and only if $\mathcal E$ is generated by its horizontal sections locally for the Zariski topology; equivalently $(\mathcal E,\nabla) \cong (F^*\mathcal F, \nabla_{\mathrm{can}})$ for some $\mathcal F$ on $X^{(p)}$. Thus $\psi=0$ is exactly "as many solutions as possible in characteristic $p$."

**Scalar case.** For $L = \partial^n + a_{n-1}\partial^{n-1} + \cdots + a_0$ over $\mathbb F_p(t)$ with $\partial = d/dt$, $\psi = 0$ iff $L$ divides $\partial^{p}$ in the noncommutative ring $\mathbb F_p(t)\langle \partial\rangle$ — iff the space of solutions in $\mathbb F_p(t)[x]/(x^p-t)$-type extensions has dimension $n$.

**Characteristic-zero side.** Over $\mathbb C$, $(\mathcal E,\nabla)$ on $X^{\mathrm{an}}$ corresponds to a representation $\rho: \pi_1(X^{\mathrm{an}}, x) \to GL_n(\mathbb C)$ (Riemann–Hilbert). "Algebraic solutions" $\iff$ $\rho$ has finite image $\iff$ the differential Galois group $G_{\mathrm{dif}} \subset GL_n$ is finite $\iff$ $G_{\mathrm{dif}}$ has trivial identity component.

**Katz's reformulation.** Let $\mathfrak g \subset \mathfrak{gl}_n$ be the Lie algebra of $G_{\mathrm{dif}}$. Grothendieck–Katz asserts $\mathfrak g$ is the smallest algebraic Lie subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ defined over the base with $\psi_{\mathfrak p} \in \mathfrak h \otimes \kappa(\mathfrak p)$ for almost all $\mathfrak p$. The original conjecture is the case $\mathfrak h = 0$.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- Grothendieck formulated the conjecture in the 1960s; it circulated orally and appears in print through Katz. Katz, *Nilpotent connections and the monodromy theorem: applications of a result of Turrittin* (Publ. Math. IHÉS 39, 1970), sets the $p$-curvature machinery in place.
- **Katz (1972)**, *Algebraic solutions of differential equations ($p$-curvature and the Hodge filtration)*, Invent. Math. 18: proves the conjecture for **Picard–Fuchs equations** — Gauss–Manin connections arising from families of smooth projective varieties. This remains the deepest general theorem toward the conjecture.
- **Katz (1982)**, *A conjecture in the arithmetic theory of differential equations*, Bull. SMF 110: states and studies the Lie-algebra strengthening; proves it for connections whose $p$-curvatures are "of Hodge type".
- **The Chudnovsky brothers (1985)** proved the rank-one case over $\mathbb C(t)$ by Padé/Diophantine approximation methods; André and Bost later gave conceptual proofs.
- **André (2004)** and **Bost (2001)** gave algebraicity criteria for foliations and leaves from $p$-adic/Arakelov data (Bost's *Algebraic leaves of algebraic foliations over number fields*, Publ. IHÉS 93), yielding the rank-one and abelian cases and much more.
- **Farb–Kisin (2009)**, *Rigid local systems and the Grothendieck–Katz $p$-curvature conjecture* (Amer. J. Math. 131): proves the conjecture for connections on certain locally symmetric varieties $X = \Gamma \backslash G/K$ with $\mathbb{Q}$-rank $\geq 2$, using superrigidity.
- **Papanikolas–Ramachandran**, **Bost–Chambert-Loir**, **Esnault–Langer**, **Shankar (2018)** contribute further special cases; Esnault–Groechenig relate the conjecture to $F$-isocrystals and to the integrality of cohomologically rigid local systems.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

Established cases, with the parameters that make them work:

| Class | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Gauss–Manin connections** (Picard–Fuchs equations of any rank, any base dimension) | Conjecture true | Katz 1972 |
| **Rank 1** ($n=1$) over any smooth $X/K$ | True; equivalent to a statement about torsion in $\operatorname{Pic}$ / abelian-variety points | Chudnovsky–Chudnovsky 1985; André 2004; Bost 2001 |
| **Connections with $\mathfrak g$ solvable** | True (reduces to rank-one by dévissage) | André 2004; Bost |
| **Hodge-type $p$-curvature / $\mathbb Z$-variation of Hodge structure** | Lie-algebra form true | Katz 1982 |
| **$X$ a locally symmetric variety $\Gamma\backslash G/K$, $G$ semisimple $\mathbb Q$-rank $\geq 2$** (or rank 1 with extra hypotheses), $\rho$ arbitrary | True via Margulis superrigidity | Farb–Kisin 2009 |
| **$X$ with $\pi_1$ having only finitely many rank-$n$ representations up to conjugacy (rigid local systems)** on such $X$ | True | Farb–Kisin 2009 |
| **Abelian schemes / connections coming from 1-motives** | True | Bost 2001; André 2004 |
| **$X = \mathbb P^1 \setminus \{0,1,\infty\}$, hypergeometric ${}_2F_1(a,b;c;t)$ with rational parameters** | True: $\psi_p=0$ for almost all $p$ $\iff$ Schwarz list condition $\iff$ algebraic | Classical (Schwarz 1873) + Katz |
| **Numerical/computational** | Vanishing $p$-curvature checked algorithmically (Bostan–Caruso–Schost, van der Put) for scalar operators up to large $p$; no counterexample found | Computer algebra literature |

Also: the conjecture is known when the connection underlies an $F$-isocrystal structure at almost all $p$ (Esnault–Langer type arguments), and for connections on abelian varieties by Bost's theorem on algebraic leaves.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No characteristic-zero invariant is visible.** Vanishing of $\psi_{\mathfrak p}$ is a statement about the mod-$p$ fibers only. There is no known functorial construction turning $\{\psi_{\mathfrak p} = 0\}_{\mathfrak p}$ into a finite étale cover in characteristic zero. Cartier descent produces a cover for each $p$, but the covers do not obviously glue or bound in degree.
- **Local-to-global failure.** $p$-curvature is a local condition at each $\mathfrak p$; finiteness of monodromy is global and topological. The bridge in every proved case is an *external* rigidity input — Hodge theory (Katz), Diophantine approximation and Arakelov slope inequalities (Bost, André, Chudnovsky), or superrigidity of lattices (Farb–Kisin). Absent such input, nothing constrains $\pi_1$.
- **Transcendence-theoretic weight.** The rank-one case over $\mathbb{C}(t)$ is already equivalent to nontrivial Diophantine statements; the general case would imply, for instance, strong forms of the "algebraicity of formal leaves" and results in the direction of the Grothendieck period conjecture. This suggests the problem is not accessible by soft algebraic geometry alone.
- **Degree bounds are absent.** Even granting a finite cover exists, no effective bound on its degree is known in terms of $n$, $\deg \mathcal E$, and $\dim X$. Without such a bound one cannot run a limiting/compactness argument over $\mathfrak p$.
- **Nilpotence is not enough.** Connections with nilpotent but nonzero $p$-curvature for all $p$ (regular singular, unipotent monodromy) show that weakening the hypothesis destroys the conclusion, so any proof must use vanishing sharply, not just nilpotence.
- **$\pi_1$ is too large in general.** For $X = \mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0,1,\infty\}$, $\pi_1$ is free of rank 2 and has huge representation varieties; the superrigidity strategy has no analogue.

## 6. The Gap

Proved (Sect. 4) covers connections that are *geometric* (Gauss–Manin), *low rank* ($n=1$) or *solvable*, or live on bases whose fundamental group is superrigid. The general statement asks nothing of the connection's origin, rank, or base.

The exact missing step: given $(\mathcal E,\nabla)$ on a general $X/K$ with $\psi_{\mathfrak p}=0$ for almost all $\mathfrak p$, produce **either** a Hodge/motivic structure (so Katz 1972 applies), **or** an effective bound on the degree of a trivializing cover, **or** a global $\mathbb{Z}$- or $\hat{\mathbb Z}$-structure on the local system. Concretely, the smallest genuinely open test case is a rank-2 irreducible connection on $\mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0,1,\infty\}$ with irregular singularities, or a rank-3 connection on a curve of genus $\geq 2$, not known to be of geometric origin.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Esnault–Groechenig program.** Integrality and $F$-isocrystal structures for cohomologically rigid local systems; their theorem that rigid integral local systems on projective $X$ are (after twist) unitary/crystalline feeds directly into $p$-curvature questions. Continued work relates the conjecture to the companions conjecture of Deligne. *(frontier — verify current scope of the rank hypotheses)*
- **Arakelov and Bost-style methods.** Bost's algebraicity criteria for formal subschemes, extended by Bost–Chambert-Loir and by Chen, are being pushed to handle non-solvable differential Galois groups. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Nonabelian Hodge in characteristic $p$.** Ogus–Vologodsky's correspondence interprets $p$-curvature as a Higgs field; work of Lan–Sheng–Zuo and Langer on Higgs–de Rham flows gives new mechanisms for producing periodic (hence finite-monodromy) objects, and is an active route to the conjecture for higher rank.
- **Computational verification.** Algorithms for $p$-curvature of $D$-finite operators (Bostan, Caruso, Schost, and successors) allow scanning large families of operators; used to certify absence of counterexamples and to guess algebraicity in creative-telescoping output.
- Groups at IHÉS, Berlin (FU/HU), Orsay, Chicago, MIT, and Bonn are the visible centers.

## 8. Future Work

- Prove the conjecture for rank 2 on open curves — the smallest case resisting all current machinery.
- Establish effective degree bounds for the trivializing cover under the vanishing hypothesis; this alone would allow a compactness argument.
- Determine whether $\psi_{\mathfrak p}=0$ for almost all $\mathfrak p$ forces the local system to be **integral** (defined over $\overline{\mathbb Z}$); integrality plus boundedness would give finiteness by a Kronecker-style argument.
- Use Higgs–de Rham flow periodicity as a mod-$p$ substitute for Hodge theory, aiming to place any $\psi=0$ connection inside a motivic family.
- Settle the Lie-algebra (Katz) form for semisimple $\mathfrak g$, which by dévissage would combine with the solvable case to cover everything.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take $X = \mathbb{G}_m = \operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Q}[t,t^{-1}]$ and the rank-one connection on $\mathcal{O}_X$ given by
$$\nabla\left(\tfrac{d}{dt}\right)(f) \;=\; f' - \frac{a}{t}f, \qquad a \in \mathbb{Q}.$$
The formal solution is $y = t^{a}$. Monodromy around $0$ multiplies $y$ by $e^{2\pi i a}$, so the differential Galois group is finite $\iff a \in \mathbb{Q}$ with the cover $t = s^{d}$, $d$ the denominator of $a$ — and infinite as soon as $a \notin \mathbb Q$.

**Compute the $p$-curvature.** Work mod $p \nmid \mathrm{denom}(a)$, so $a \in \mathbb{F}_p$. Set $D = d/dt$ and $\nabla_D = D - a/t$. Then $D^p = 0$ as a derivation of $\mathbb{F}_p[t,t^{-1}]$, so
$$\psi(D) \;=\; \nabla_D^{\,p} - \nabla_{D^p} \;=\; \left(D - \tfrac{a}{t}\right)^{p}.$$
A standard identity in the ring of differential operators over $\mathbb F_p$ gives, for a rank-one operator $D - g$,
$$(D-g)^p \;=\; D^p - \left(g^p + D^{p-1}(g)\right).$$
With $g = a/t$: $g^p = a^p/t^p = a/t^p$ (Fermat), and
$$D^{p-1}\!\left(\frac{a}{t}\right) = a\,(-1)^{p-1}(p-1)!\,t^{-p} = a\,(p-1)!\,t^{-p} = -\frac{a}{t^{p}}$$
by Wilson's theorem $(p-1)! \equiv -1$. Hence
$$\psi(D) \;=\; -\left(\frac{a}{t^p} - \frac{a}{t^p}\right)\cdot(-1) \;=\; \frac{a - a^{p}}{t^{p}} \;=\; \frac{a-a}{t^p} \;=\; 0 .$$
More precisely, before reducing $a$: $\psi(D) = (a^p - a)/t^p$, which vanishes exactly when $a^p = a$ in the residue field, i.e. when $a \bmod p$ lies in $\mathbb{F}_p$.

**Reading the conjecture off this.** If $a = r/s \in \mathbb{Q}$, then for all $p \nmid s$ the reduction $a \bmod p \in \mathbb{F}_p$, so $\psi_p = 0$ for almost all $p$ — and indeed $y=t^{r/s}$ is algebraic, trivialized by the degree-$s$ cover $t \mapsto t^s$. Conversely, if $a$ is irrational, say $a \in K = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})$, then $a \bmod \mathfrak p \in \mathbb{F}_p$ only at primes inert-vs-split conditions failing: $a^p \equiv \bar a \neq a$ at the inert primes, a set of density $1/2$. So $\psi_{\mathfrak p} \neq 0$ on a positive-density set, matching the transcendental monodromy. This is exactly the rank-one case, and it already shows the mechanism: **$\psi_{\mathfrak p}=0$ for almost all $\mathfrak p$ is a Chebotarev-type rigidity forcing rationality of exponents.** The open problem is to find the analogue of "$a^p = a$ forces $a \in \mathbb Q$" when the exponent is replaced by a non-abelian monodromy representation.
