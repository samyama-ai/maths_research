# Flux Conjecture for Symplectic Manifolds

> **Topic:** Algebraic & Differential Geometry · **ID:** `03-geometry/flux-conjecture` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $(M,\omega)$ be a closed connected symplectic manifold, $\mathrm{Symp}_0(M,\omega)$ the identity component of its symplectomorphism group, and $\mathrm{Ham}(M,\omega)$ the subgroup of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms. The **flux group** is
$$\Gamma_\omega \;=\; \mathrm{Flux}\big(\pi_1(\mathrm{Symp}_0(M,\omega))\big)\;\subset\; H^1(M;\mathbb{R}).$$

**Flux Conjecture (Lalonde–McDuff–Polterovich, 1998).** $\Gamma_\omega$ is a *discrete* subgroup of $H^1(M;\mathbb{R})$.

Equivalently: $\mathrm{Ham}(M,\omega)$ is closed in $\mathrm{Symp}_0(M,\omega)$ in the $C^\infty$ (indeed $C^1$) topology.

**Status.** For *closed* symplectic manifolds the conjecture is a **theorem** of Kaoru Ono (GAFA, 2006). The page remains `partially-solved` because the natural strengthenings are open:

- **$C^0$ Flux Conjecture.** $\mathrm{Ham}(M,\omega)$ is closed in $\mathrm{Symp}_0(M,\omega)$ for the $C^0$ topology. Open in general.
- **Open/non-compact and infinite-type settings.** Discreteness of $\Gamma_\omega$ for open manifolds with controlled behaviour at infinity, and for symplectic manifolds of infinite topological type, is not settled.
- **Quantitative form.** No general bound is known for $\mathrm{rank}\,\Gamma_\omega$ in terms of $b_1(M)$, $[\omega]$ and the topology of $M$; a complete proof would compute $\Gamma_\omega$, not merely certify discreteness.

A resolution of the $C^0$ version requires either a proof that a $C^0$-limit of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms which is symplectic has zero flux, or an explicit $(M,\omega)$ with a symplectic isotopy of nonzero flux arising as a $C^0$-limit of Hamiltonian ones.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Symplectic setting.** $\omega \in \Omega^2(M)$ is closed and nondegenerate, $\dim M = 2n$, $\omega^n$ a volume form. A diffeomorphism $\phi$ is symplectic if $\phi^*\omega = \omega$.

**Flux homomorphism (Calabi 1970, Banyaga 1978).** For a smooth path $\{\phi_t\}_{t\in[0,1]}$ in $\mathrm{Symp}_0$ with $\phi_0 = \mathrm{id}$, generated by the time-dependent vector field $X_t$ (so $\frac{d}{dt}\phi_t = X_t\circ\phi_t$ and $d(\iota_{X_t}\omega)=0$ by Cartan's formula), define
$$\mathrm{Flux}\big(\{\phi_t\}\big) \;=\; \int_0^1 [\,\iota_{X_t}\omega\,]\; dt \;\in\; H^1(M;\mathbb{R}).$$
This depends only on the homotopy class rel endpoints, giving a surjective homomorphism
$$\mathrm{Flux}:\ \widetilde{\mathrm{Symp}_0}(M,\omega)\ \longrightarrow\ H^1(M;\mathbb{R})$$
from the universal cover. Geometrically, for a loop $\gamma:S^1\to M$,
$$\langle \mathrm{Flux}(\{\phi_t\}),[\gamma]\rangle \;=\; \int_{[0,1]\times S^1} \Phi^*\omega ,\qquad \Phi(t,s)=\phi_t(\gamma(s)),$$
the symplectic area swept by $\gamma$ under the isotopy.

**Banyaga's theorem (1978).** $\ker \mathrm{Flux} $ projects onto $\mathrm{Ham}(M,\omega)$, which is a simple group when $M$ is closed, and
$$\mathrm{Symp}_0(M,\omega)/\mathrm{Ham}(M,\omega)\ \cong\ H^1(M;\mathbb{R})/\Gamma_\omega .$$
Hence $\Gamma_\omega$ discrete $\iff$ the quotient is a torus $\times \mathbb{R}^k$ (a Hausdorff Lie group) $\iff$ $\mathrm{Ham}$ is $C^1$-closed in $\mathrm{Symp}_0$.

**Novikov machinery.** For $a\in H^1(M;\mathbb{R})$ and a symplectic isotopy of flux $a$, the fixed points of the time-1 map are critical points of a multivalued action functional on the free loop space $\mathcal{L}M$ whose periods lie in the group generated by $\langle a, \cdot\rangle$ and $\langle[\omega],\pi_2(M)\rangle$. The associated Floer theory is **Floer–Novikov cohomology** $HF^*_{\mathrm{FN}}(\phi;\Lambda)$ over a Novikov ring $\Lambda$; when defined, it is isomorphic to Novikov cohomology $H^*_{\mathrm{Nov}}(M;a)$ of the closed $1$-form representing $a$.

**Elementary discreteness criterion.** If $[\omega]\in H^2(M;\mathbb{R})$ has rational (or integral) periods, then $\Gamma_\omega \subset H^1(M;\mathbb{Q}$-lattice$)$-type constraints force discreteness: the area swept out is valued in the discrete period group of $[\omega]$ pulled back over $2$-tori.

**$C^0$ setting.** $\overline{\mathrm{Ham}}^{\,C^0}(M,\omega)$ denotes the $C^0$-closure inside $\mathrm{Symp}_0$; Fathi's *mass flow homomorphism* on measure-preserving homeomorphisms is $C^0$-continuous and detects the image of $\mathrm{Flux}$ in $H^1(M;\mathbb{R})$ under the map induced by $\omega^n/n!$.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1970 / 1978.** Calabi introduces the invariant later reformulated as flux; Banyaga proves the structure theorem for $\mathrm{Symp}_0$, simplicity of $\mathrm{Ham}$, and identifies $\Gamma_\omega$ as the obstruction to $\mathrm{Ham}$ being closed.
- **1990s.** The problem is named and organised by **Lalonde, McDuff and Polterovich**, "On the flux conjectures" (CRM Proc. Lecture Notes 15, 1998), who split it into the *flux conjecture* ($\Gamma_\omega$ discrete) and the *$C^0$ flux conjecture*, prove countability of $\Gamma_\omega$, and settle several classes using quantum homology and Gromov–Witten theory (LMP, *Topological rigidity of Hamiltonian loops and quantum homology*, Invent. Math. 135 (1999)). Kędra (Math. Res. Lett. 5, 1998) obtains rank restrictions for particular families.
- **2006.** **Ono** proves the flux conjecture for all closed symplectic manifolds, using Floer–Novikov cohomology with Novikov-ring coefficients and virtual/Kuranishi perturbation to handle non-semi-positive targets. Published as *Floer–Novikov cohomology and the flux conjecture*, GAFA 16 (2006).
- **2014–present.** Attention moves to the $C^0$ statement. Buhovsky, *Towards the $C^0$ flux conjecture* (Algebr. Geom. Topol. 14, 2014), reduces the conjecture to a statement about $C^0$-limits and proves it in special classes. $C^0$ symplectic topology (Humilière, Le Roux, Seyfaddini, Buhovsky, Opshtein) develops spectral invariants and the "$C^0$ Arnold" circle of results that bear directly on it.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **All closed symplectic manifolds (any dimension $2n$): $\Gamma_\omega$ is discrete.** Ono 2006. This is the full smooth conjecture.
- **Rational symplectic forms.** If $[\omega]\in H^2(M;\mathbb{Q})$, discreteness is elementary: swept areas take values in a discrete period group. Covers all Kähler manifolds with rational Kähler class, hence all smooth projective varieties with $\omega$ a Hodge form.
- **Hard Lefschetz manifolds.** LMP 1998/1999 prove discreteness for closed $(M,\omega)$ satisfying the hard Lefschetz condition (in particular all closed Kähler manifolds), by showing that a loop in $\mathrm{Ham}$ acts trivially on quantum/ordinary homology in a way that pins the flux to a lattice.
- **Surfaces.** For a closed surface $\Sigma_g$, $g\ge 1$, $\Gamma_\omega$ is computed exactly: $\Gamma_\omega = 0$ for $g\ge 2$ (since $\pi_1(\mathrm{Symp}_0(\Sigma_g))=1$), and $\Gamma_\omega \cong H^1(T^2;\mathbb{Z})\cdot A \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ (scaled by total area $A$) for $T^2$. For $S^2$, $H^1=0$ and the statement is vacuous.
- **Symplectically aspherical manifolds** ($\omega|_{\pi_2(M)}=0=c_1|_{\pi_2(M)}$): the Floer–Novikov argument avoids virtual techniques entirely; discreteness is classical here.
- **$C^0$ flux conjecture.** Known for closed surfaces and for tori, where Fathi's $C^0$-continuous mass flow homomorphism sees all of $H^1$; Buhovsky (2014) establishes it for further classes and shows the general case follows from a $C^0$-rigidity statement for the flux of limits. *(frontier — verify the exact hypothesis list in the published version.)*

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Loss of derivative control under $C^0$ limits.** Flux is defined by integrating $\iota_{X_t}\omega$, a first-derivative object. A $C^0$-convergent sequence $\phi^k\to\phi$ gives no control on $X^k_t$, so the flux of the limit is not obviously the limit of the fluxes. Every proof strategy must replace the differential definition by a $C^0$-continuous invariant (spectral invariants, mass flow, action spectra).
- **Mass flow is not enough.** Fathi's mass flow factors through $H_1(M;\mathbb{R})$ via the volume form and is blind to the part of $\Gamma_\omega$ that is not detected by the volume class; in dimension $\ge 4$ the symplectic and volume information genuinely differ.
- **Non-Hausdorff limits of Floer data.** In the smooth case, the difficulty Ono had to overcome was that a non-discrete $\Gamma_\omega$ yields fluxes $a_k\to 0$ with each associated Novikov ring changing discontinuously; Floer–Novikov cohomology is not obviously continuous in $a$, and the Novikov ring of an irrational class can be a field, killing the invariant. Ono's proof requires an invariance-of-Floer-Novikov-cohomology-under-small-flux statement plus virtual perturbation to define it without semi-positivity.
- **Absence of a $C^0$ Floer theory.** Spectral invariants $c(\alpha,H)$ are Lipschitz for the Hofer norm but only conditionally $C^0$-continuous; the machinery producing $C^0$ statements (Buhovsky–Humilière–Seyfaddini) is currently strongest in dimension $2$ and degrades in higher dimensions.
- **No counterexample-building technology.** Constructing a symplectic manifold with a non-discrete flux group would require infinitely many essentially distinct loops in $\mathrm{Symp}_0$ with fluxes accumulating at $0$ — but $\pi_1(\mathrm{Symp}_0)$ is countable and hard to compute beyond rational homotopy in low dimensions and toric/ruled examples.

## 6. The Gap

Proven (smooth): for closed $(M,\omega)$, $\Gamma_\omega\subset H^1(M;\mathbb{R})$ is discrete, so $\mathrm{Ham}$ is $C^1$-closed.

Not proven: that $\mathrm{Ham}(M,\omega)$ is $C^0$-closed in $\mathrm{Symp}_0(M,\omega)$. The precise missing step:

> If $\phi\in\mathrm{Symp}_0(M,\omega)$ is a $C^0$-limit of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms and $\phi$ lies on a symplectic isotopy of flux $a$, must $a\in\Gamma_\omega$ (equivalently $a=0$ in $H^1/\Gamma_\omega$)?

Since $\Gamma_\omega$ *is* discrete, the smooth quotient is Hausdorff; failure could only come from $C^0$-limits producing flux not visible to any $C^0$-continuous invariant. Bridging the gap means constructing a flux invariant defined on $\overline{\mathrm{Ham}}^{\,C^0}$ — e.g. by proving that the action spectrum, or a suitable Lagrangian-shadow/spectral quantity, varies continuously in $C^0$ and detects $\langle a,[\gamma]\rangle$ for every $[\gamma]\in H_1(M;\mathbb{Z})$.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **$C^0$ symplectic topology school** (Buhovsky, Humilière, Seyfaddini, Opshtein, Le Roux, Cristofaro-Gardiner): spectral invariants and action-spectrum rigidity as $C^0$-continuous surrogates for flux. The $C^0$-continuity of the action spectrum (Buhovsky–Humilière–Seyfaddini, Math. Ann. 2021) is the closest existing tool. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Periodic Floer homology / PFH-spectral invariants** used by Cristofaro-Gardiner, Humilière, Seyfaddini and collaborators for surface dynamics; extensions to higher-genus and higher-dimensional flux detection are an active thread. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Computations of $\Gamma_\omega$** for explicit families — toric manifolds, ruled surfaces, symplectic fibrations — using $\pi_1(\mathrm{Symp})$ computations (Anjos, Pinsonnault, Li–Li–Wu). These test the quantitative form of the problem.
- **Quantitative flux / Hofer geometry.** Relating $\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{Hofer}}$-type lower bounds to the shortest nonzero element of $\Gamma_\omega$, giving effective discreteness constants rather than a pure existence statement.
- **Groups of homeomorphisms.** Study of $\overline{\mathrm{Ham}}^{\,C^0}$ as a topological group — normality, simplicity, and whether it equals the kernel of a mass-flow-type homomorphism.

## 8. Future Work

1. **Build a $C^0$-continuous flux.** Define $\widetilde{\mathrm{Flux}}$ on $\overline{\mathrm{Ham}}^{\,C^0}$ from spectral invariants over the Novikov ring; the technical target is continuity of $a\mapsto c(\alpha, \cdot)$ under $C^0$ perturbation in dimension $\ge 4$.
2. **Dimension 4 first.** Settle the $C^0$ flux conjecture for closed symplectic $4$-manifolds with $b_1>0$ (e.g. $T^4$, $T^2\times\Sigma_g$, Kodaira–Thurston), where both $\pi_1(\mathrm{Symp}_0)$ and Floer theory are tractable.
3. **Effective bounds.** Prove $\mathrm{rank}\,\Gamma_\omega \le b_1(M)$ with an explicit lower bound on the shortest nonzero flux in terms of Gromov width or the Hofer diameter.
4. **Non-compact analogues.** Formulate and prove discreteness for open manifolds with convex/cylindrical ends, where the flux lives in relative cohomology.
5. **Counterexample search.** Systematically compute $\pi_1(\mathrm{Symp}_0)$ for families with large $b_1$ and irrational $[\omega]$; a non-discrete $C^0$ flux group would falsify the remaining statement.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**The two-torus.** Let $M=T^2=\mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2$ with coordinates $(x,y)$ and $\omega = A\,dx\wedge dy$, total area $A>0$.

*Step 1 — a loop of symplectomorphisms.* Translation in $x$: $\phi_t(x,y)=(x+t,y)$, $t\in[0,1]$. Since $\phi_1=\mathrm{id}$, this is a loop in $\mathrm{Symp}_0(T^2,\omega)$. Its generating field is $X_t=\partial_x$ (autonomous).

*Step 2 — compute the flux.*
$$\iota_{\partial_x}\omega \;=\; A\,\iota_{\partial_x}(dx\wedge dy) \;=\; A\,dy ,\qquad
\mathrm{Flux}(\{\phi_t\}) = \int_0^1 [A\,dy]\,dt = A\,[dy].$$
Symmetrically the $y$-translation loop has flux $-A\,[dx]$.

*Step 3 — check by swept area.* Take $\gamma(s)=(0,s)$, the loop generating $[dy]$-duality. The trace $\Phi(t,s)=(t,s)$ sweeps the whole torus once, so $\int \Phi^*\omega = A = \langle A[dy],[\gamma]\rangle$. Consistent.

*Step 4 — the flux group.* $\pi_1(\mathrm{Symp}_0(T^2))\cong \pi_1(T^2)=\mathbb{Z}^2$ (translations; the rest of $\mathrm{Symp}_0(T^2)$ deformation-retracts to $T^2$). Hence
$$\Gamma_\omega \;=\; A\cdot\big(\mathbb{Z}[dx]\oplus\mathbb{Z}[dy]\big)\;\cong\;\mathbb{Z}^2 \subset H^1(T^2;\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}^2 ,$$
a lattice — **discrete**, as the conjecture predicts. By Banyaga's theorem,
$$\mathrm{Symp}_0(T^2,\omega)/\mathrm{Ham}(T^2,\omega)\ \cong\ \mathbb{R}^2/A\mathbb{Z}^2\ \cong\ T^2 ,$$
a Hausdorff torus, so $\mathrm{Ham}(T^2,\omega)$ is closed.

*Step 5 — what failure would look like.* Suppose instead $\Gamma_\omega$ contained $\{A\alpha_k [dx]\}$ with $\alpha_k\to 0$, $\alpha_k\neq 0$. Then $\phi^k := $ (time-$\alpha_k$ translation) would be non-Hamiltonian (nonzero flux) yet $\phi^k\to\mathrm{id}\in\mathrm{Ham}$ smoothly, and the quotient $H^1/\Gamma_\omega$ would be non-Hausdorff. Concretely, $\phi^k$ displaces the circle $\{y=0\}$ to $\{y=0\}$ shifted, sweeping area $A\alpha_k\neq 0$; no Hamiltonian isotopy can sweep nonzero net area against a $1$-cycle. Ono's theorem rules this out for every closed $(M,\omega)$. The **open** question is whether such a $\phi$ with $\alpha\neq 0$ could nonetheless be a *uniform* ($C^0$) limit of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms in higher dimensions; on $T^2$ it cannot, because Fathi's mass flow homomorphism is $C^0$-continuous and equals $\mathrm{Flux}$ up to the Poincaré duality $H^1(T^2;\mathbb{R})\cong H_1(T^2;\mathbb{R})$.
