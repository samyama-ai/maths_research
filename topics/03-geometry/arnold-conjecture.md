# Arnold Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebraic & Differential Geometry · **ID:** `03-geometry/arnold-conjecture` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $(M,\omega)$ be a closed (compact, boundaryless) symplectic manifold and let $\phi = \phi^1_H$ be the time-one map of a Hamiltonian isotopy generated by a smooth time-dependent Hamiltonian $H: M \times S^1 \to \mathbb{R}$. Arnold conjectured that $\phi$ has at least as many fixed points as a smooth function on $M$ has critical points.

Two precise forms:

- **Nondegenerate form.** If every fixed point $p$ of $\phi$ is nondegenerate, i.e. $\det(\mathrm{id} - d\phi_p) \neq 0$, then
$$\\#\,\mathrm{Fix}(\phi) \;\ge\; \sum_{i=0}^{\dim M} \dim_{\mathbb{F}} H_i(M;\mathbb{F})$$
for a field $\mathbb{F}$ — the Morse-inequality count.
- **Degenerate form.** Without nondegeneracy,
$$\\#\,\mathrm{Fix}(\phi) \;\ge\; \mathrm{cl}(M) + 1,$$
where $\mathrm{cl}(M)$ is the cup-length (largest $k$ with $a_1\smile\cdots\smile a_k \neq 0$ for $a_i \in H^{>0}(M)$); stronger versions replace $\mathrm{cl}(M)+1$ by the Lusternik–Schnirelmann category $\mathrm{cat}(M)$.

A complete proof must handle arbitrary closed $(M,\omega)$, in particular manifolds where $\omega$ and $c_1(TM)$ pair nontrivially with $\pi_2(M)$. A disproof requires a Hamiltonian diffeomorphism violating one of the two bounds. Both are sharp: $C^2$-small autonomous $H$ realizes equality.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Hamiltonian dynamics.** $\omega$ is a closed nondegenerate 2-form. For $H_t$, the Hamiltonian vector field $X_{H_t}$ is defined by
$$\iota_{X_{H_t}}\omega = dH_t,$$
and its flow $\phi^t_H$ preserves $\omega$. Fixed points of $\phi^1_H$ correspond to contractible 1-periodic orbits $x: S^1 \to M$ solving $\dot x(t) = X_{H_t}(x(t))$.

**Action functional.** On the space $\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}M$ of pairs $(x,u)$, $x$ a contractible loop and $u:D^2 \to M$ a capping disc,
$$\mathcal{A}_H(x,u) \;=\; -\int_{D^2} u^*\omega \;-\; \int_0^1 H_t(x(t))\,dt .$$
Critical points of $\mathcal{A}_H$ are exactly the capped 1-periodic orbits. $\mathcal{A}_H$ is multivalued on $\mathcal{L}M$; its periods form the group $\omega(\pi_2(M))$, so classical Morse theory does not apply directly.

**Floer's equation.** Fix an $\omega$-compatible almost complex structure $J_t$, giving metric $g = \omega(\cdot,J\cdot)$. The formal negative gradient flow of $\mathcal{A}_H$ is
$$\partial_s u + J_t(u)\big(\partial_t u - X_{H_t}(u)\big) = 0, \qquad u: \mathbb{R}\times S^1 \to M .$$
Solutions with finite energy $E(u)=\int |\partial_s u|^2 < \infty$ connect periodic orbits.

**Floer homology.** For nondegenerate $H$, $CF_*(H)$ is generated by capped orbits over the Novikov ring
$$\Lambda = \Big\{ \textstyle\sum_i a_i T^{\lambda_i} \;:\; a_i \in R,\ \lambda_i \in \mathbb{R},\ \\\\#\{i: a_i\neq 0,\ \lambda_i \le C\} < \infty \ \forall C \Big\},$$
graded by the Conley–Zehnder index $\mu_{CZ}$. The differential counts index-1 Floer trajectories modulo $\mathbb{R}$:
$$\partial \langle x \rangle = \sum_{\mu_{CZ}(y)=\mu_{CZ}(x)-1} \\#\big(\mathcal{M}(x,y)/\mathbb{R}\big)\,\langle y\rangle .$$
The central theorem (Floer; Piunikhin–Salamon–Schwarz) is
$$HF_*(M,\omega) \;\cong\; H_{*}(M;R)\otimes_R \Lambda ,$$
whence $\\#\mathrm{Fix}(\phi) \ge \mathrm{rank}_\Lambda HF_* = \sum_i \dim H_i(M;R)$. The difficulty is entirely in making $\partial$ well-defined and $\partial^2=0$ when the moduli spaces $\mathcal{M}(x,y)$ are not cut out transversally, because of bubbling of $J$-holomorphic spheres (Gromov compactness) and of multiply covered curves with negative index.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1965.** Arnold states the conjecture for the torus (C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris **261**); the general formulation appears as Appendix 9 of *Mathematical Methods of Classical Mechanics* (1978). Motivation: Poincaré's last geometric theorem on the annulus, proved by Birkhoff (1913).
- **1983.** Conley–Zehnder prove the nondegenerate conjecture for $M = \mathbb{T}^{2n}$ with the standard form, using a finite-dimensional reduction and a Conley-index argument.
- **1985.** Gromov introduces pseudoholomorphic curves; compactness and bubbling analysis become available.
- **1988–89.** Floer builds Floer homology and proves the conjecture for monotone symplectic manifolds ($\omega|_{\pi_2} = \lambda\, c_1|_{\pi_2}$, $\lambda \ge 0$) and for symplectically aspherical $M$ ($\omega|_{\pi_2}=0$).
- **1995–99.** Hofer–Salamon and Ono extend to weakly monotone (semipositive) $M$. Fukaya–Ono, Liu–Tian, and Ruan independently prove the nondegenerate conjecture over $\mathbb{Q}$ for all closed $(M,\omega)$, using Kuranishi structures / virtual cycles.
- **2016.** Pardon gives an algebraic virtual-cycle construction reproving the $\mathbb{Q}$-statement; Hofer–Wysocki–Zehnder's polyfold theory supplies a second foundational route.
- **2018.** Buhovsky–Humilière–Seyfaddini construct a Hamiltonian *homeomorphism* of any closed symplectic manifold of dimension $\ge 4$ with exactly one fixed point — the conjecture is false in the $C^0$ category.
- **2021–22.** Abouzaid–Blumberg prove the nondegenerate conjecture with $\mathbb{F}_p$ coefficients via Morava K-theory; Bai–Xu announce the statement over $\mathbb{Z}$ (all Betti numbers plus torsion) using FOP (finite-orbit-preserving) perturbations of derived orbifold charts.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Transversality vs. symmetry.** The Floer moduli spaces contain multiply covered $J$-holomorphic spheres whose expected dimension can be negative. Generic $J$ cannot remove them because the reparametrization group acts with isotropy; the standard Sard–Smale argument fails. Only virtual techniques (Kuranishi structures, polyfolds, derived orbifolds) recover a fundamental class.
- **Rational coefficients are forced by multivalued perturbations.** Virtual cycles are built from multisections; their zero counts are rational numbers with denominators equal to orbifold isotropy orders. This is why the classical proofs give $\mathbb{Q}$, not $\mathbb{Z}$ or $\mathbb{F}_p$ — the $p$-torsion in $H_*(M)$ is invisible. Abouzaid–Blumberg circumvent this by replacing ordinary homology with Morava K-theory $K(n)$, whose Euler classes exist for the relevant non-orientable-in-$K(n)$-sense bundles; Bai–Xu instead build integral perturbations compatible with the isotropy.
- **Degenerate case has no Morse-theoretic model.** For degenerate $\phi$ one needs Lusternik–Schnirelmann theory for a multivalued, non-Palais–Smale functional $\mathcal{A}_H$ on an infinite-dimensional loop space. The Novikov ring makes cup-length arguments awkward: products in $HF$ over $\Lambda$ can vanish for filtration reasons even when they are nonzero in $H^*(M)$.
- **No $C^0$ rigidity.** The BHS counterexample shows the bound is not a consequence of topology plus measure preservation; any proof must use the smooth (or at least $C^1$) structure, ruling out purely topological-dynamics approaches in dimension $\ge 4$.

## 6. The Gap

Two gaps remain.

1. **Degenerate general case.** Proven only for symplectically aspherical and monotone $M$; open for a general closed $(M,\omega)$. Missing step: a Floer-theoretic Lusternik–Schnirelmann theory over $\Lambda$ that produces $\mathrm{cl}(M)+1$ distinct critical *values* (hence orbits) even when the virtual machinery only delivers homology ranks. Passing from ranks to a cup-length count requires a well-defined, associative product on filtered Floer homology with integral coefficients and control of spectral invariants $c(a,H)$ under degeneration.
2. **Integral nondegenerate case.** The $\mathbb{Z}$-statement rests on recent, technically heavy foundations (FOP perturbations, Morava K-theory Floer homotopy types) whose independent verification is ongoing; the community consensus is that $\mathbb{F}_p$ (Abouzaid–Blumberg) is settled and $\mathbb{Z}$ is credible but not yet folklore.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Floer homotopy theory.** Abouzaid, Blumberg, McLean, Smith: stable-homotopy refinements of Floer theory, complex-oriented Gromov–Witten invariants, and Morava K-theoretic Arnold bounds. Active at Stanford, Columbia, Oxford, Cambridge.
- **Derived orbifolds and integral virtual cycles.** Bai (Simons Center/Michigan) and Xu (Rutgers/Westlake) extend FOP perturbation theory toward Hamiltonian Floer homology over $\mathbb{Z}$ and toward degenerate counts. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Polyfolds.** Hofer–Wysocki–Zehnder school (Institute for Advanced Study, Augsburg): sc-Fredholm foundations, with efforts to obtain integral counts from polyfold branched ensembles.
- **Quantitative and $C^0$ symplectic topology.** Humilière, Seyfaddini, Le Roux, Cristofaro-Gardiner (Paris, Maryland): barcodes, spectral invariants, and the boundary between smooth rigidity and $C^0$ flexibility; links to the simplicity conjecture for $\mathrm{Ham}(D^2)$ (resolved 2020) and the Conley conjecture on infinitely many periodic points.
- **Degenerate case via spectral invariants.** Work using Oh–Schwarz spectral numbers and persistence modules to produce lower bounds on the number of distinct action values.

## 8. Future Work

- Build an $E_\infty$- or $A_\infty$-structured Floer homotopy type carrying an integral cup product, then run Lusternik–Schnirelmann theory internally to attack the degenerate conjecture in full.
- Prove the degenerate conjecture for all monotone-plus-torsion cases, then remove the sphere-bubbling hypothesis by controlling the Novikov filtration of $\mathrm{cl}(M)$-fold products.
- Determine the sharp $C^0$ threshold: for which regularity class $C^r$ does the BHS counterexample cease to exist? Nothing is known between $C^0$ and $C^1$.
- Extend the count to the Lagrangian Arnold conjecture: $\\#(L \cap \phi(L)) \ge \sum \dim H_i(L;\mathbb{F})$ for monotone/exact $L$, still open in the presence of obstructed Floer theory.
- Independent write-ups and formalization of virtual-cycle foundations, so that the Arnold bound over $\mathbb{Z}$ has a checkable proof of record.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

Take $M = \mathbb{T}^2 = \mathbb{R}^2/\mathbb{Z}^2$ with coordinates $(x,y)$ and $\omega = dx \wedge dy$. Betti numbers over any field: $b_0=1$, $b_1=2$, $b_2=1$, so the nondegenerate bound predicts $\\#\mathrm{Fix}(\phi) \ge 4$, and $\mathrm{cl}(\mathbb{T}^2)=2$ gives the degenerate bound $\ge 3$.

**Autonomous Hamiltonian.** Let
$$H(x,y) = \varepsilon\big(\cos 2\pi x + \cos 2\pi y\big), \qquad \varepsilon > 0 \text{ small}.$$
From $\iota_{X_H}\omega = dH$ with $\omega = dx\wedge dy$ one gets $X_H = (\partial_y H,\, -\partial_x H)$, i.e.
$$\dot x = -2\pi\varepsilon \sin 2\pi y, \qquad \dot y = 2\pi\varepsilon \sin 2\pi x .$$
Zeros of $X_H$ = critical points of $H$: $\sin 2\pi x = \sin 2\pi y = 0$, so
$$(x,y) \in \{0,\tfrac12\} \times \{0,\tfrac12\},$$
four points. The Hessian is $\mathrm{Hess}\,H = -4\pi^2\varepsilon\,\mathrm{diag}(\cos 2\pi x, \cos 2\pi y)$, with determinant $16\pi^4\varepsilon^2 \cos 2\pi x \cos 2\pi y \neq 0$ at each: $(0,0)$ is a maximum, $(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$ a minimum, $(0,\tfrac12)$ and $(\tfrac12,0)$ saddles — Morse indices $2,0,1,1$, matching $b_2,b_0,b_1$ exactly.

**Fixed points of the time-1 map.** All four critical points are fixed by $\phi^1_H$. Nondegeneracy: linearizing at a critical point $p$ gives $d\phi^1_p = \exp(J_0\,\mathrm{Hess}\,H(p))$ where $J_0 = \begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$. At $(0,0)$, $J_0\mathrm{Hess}\,H = -4\pi^2\varepsilon J_0$, so $d\phi^1$ is rotation by angle $4\pi^2\varepsilon$; for $0<\varepsilon<1/(2\pi^2)$ the eigenvalues $e^{\pm 4\pi^2 \varepsilon i}\ne 1$, hence $\det(\mathrm{id}-d\phi^1)\neq 0$. The saddle points give real eigenvalues $e^{\pm 4\pi^2\varepsilon} \neq 1$. So all four are nondegenerate.

**Sharpness and no extras.** For $\varepsilon$ small, a 1-periodic orbit must stay in a ball of radius $O(\varepsilon)$ (since $|X_H| = O(\varepsilon)$) and a fixed-point argument on the lift to $\mathbb{R}^2$ shows it is constant; hence $\mathrm{Fix}(\phi^1_H)$ is exactly these four points. Equality holds: the bound $4 = b_0+b_1+b_2$ cannot be improved.

**Where the general case breaks.** Here $\pi_2(\mathbb{T}^2)=0$, so no holomorphic spheres exist, all Floer moduli spaces are cut out transversally for generic $J$, and $HF_*(\mathbb{T}^2) \cong H_*(\mathbb{T}^2;\mathbb{Z})$ follows by Floer's original argument with no virtual technology. Replace $\mathbb{T}^2$ by, say, a general closed symplectic 8-manifold with $\omega(\pi_2)\ne 0$ and $c_1(\pi_2)$ of the wrong sign, and negative-index multiple covers appear in the compactification of $\mathcal{M}(x,y)$ — this is precisely the point where the elementary argument above must be replaced by Kuranishi/derived-orbifold machinery, and where the integral count was only recently recovered.
