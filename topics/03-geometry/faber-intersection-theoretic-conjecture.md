# Faber Intersection-Theoretic Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebraic & Differential Geometry · **ID:** `03-geometry/faber-intersection-theoretic-conjecture` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

The Faber Intersection-Theoretic Conjecture (or simply Faber's Conjecture) concerns the algebraic and combinatorial structure of the tautological ring $R^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$ of the moduli space of smooth algebraic curves of genus $g$, denoted $\mathcal{M}_g$. Proposed by Carel Faber in 1993, the conjecture asserts that the tautological ring—a subring of the rational Chow ring $CH^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$ generated by natural characteristic classes—behaves algebraically like the even cohomology ring of a compact, nonsingular projective manifold of dimension $g-2$. This is a striking prediction because $\mathcal{M}_g$ itself is a non-compact Deligne-Mumford stack (or orbifold) of complex dimension $3g-3$.

Specifically, the conjecture is articulated through four exact structural claims regarding $R^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$:
1. **Vanishing Bound:** The ring is zero above degree $g-2$. That is, $R^i(\mathcal{M}_g) = 0$ for all $i > g-2$.
2. **One-Dimensional Socle:** In the top non-vanishing degree, the tautological ring is exactly one-dimensional: $R^{g-2}(\mathcal{M}_g) \cong \mathbb{Q}$.
3. **Perfect Pairing (The Gorenstein Property):** For each $0 \le i \le g-2$, the intersection product given by 
   $$R^i(\mathcal{M}_g) \times R^{g-2-i}(\mathcal{M}_g) \to R^{g-2}(\mathcal{M}_g) \cong \mathbb{Q}$$
   is a non-degenerate (perfect) pairing. This implies that $R^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$ is a Gorenstein Artinian local ring, exhibiting a strict algebraic analog of Poincaré duality.
4. **Generation Bound:** The entire tautological ring $R^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$ is generated as an algebra over $\mathbb{Q}$ by the first $\lfloor g/3 \rfloor$ Morita-Mumford-Faber classes: $\kappa_1, \kappa_2, \dots, \kappa_{\lfloor g/3 \rfloor}$.

While the vanishing, socle, and generation components have been rigorously proven, the perfect pairing (Gorenstein) property has been shown to decisively fail for sufficiently large genus (specifically for $g \ge 24$). This failure shifts the core open problem toward understanding the precise arithmetic or geometric origin of this breakdown, and establishing whether known tautological relations (such as Pixton's relations) are complete.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Let $\mathcal{M}_g$ be the moduli space of smooth, projective curves of genus $g \ge 2$ over the field of complex numbers $\mathbb{C}$. It is a Deligne-Mumford stack of complex dimension $3g-3$. The rational Chow ring, denoted $CH^*(\mathcal{M}_g) = CH^*(\mathcal{M}_g, \mathbb{Q})$, encodes the intersection theory of algebraic cycles on this space modulo rational equivalence.

To construct the tautological ring, one begins with the universal curve $\pi: \mathcal{C}_g \to \mathcal{M}_g$, which can be identified with the universal family (or the moduli space of curves with one marked point, $\mathcal{M}_{g,1}$). Let $\omega_\pi$ denote the relative dualizing sheaf (the relative cotangent bundle) of the morphism $\pi$. The first Chern class of this line bundle is the fundamental class $K = c_1(\omega_\pi) \in CH^1(\mathcal{C}_g)$. 

The **kappa classes** (Morita-Mumford-Faber classes) are defined via the Gysin pushforward map along $\pi$:
$$ \kappa_i = \pi_*(K^{i+1}) \in CH^i(\mathcal{M}_g) \quad \text{for } i \ge 1. $$
By convention, $\kappa_0 = \pi_*(K^1)$ evaluates to $2g-2$, the degree of the canonical divisor on a curve of genus $g$. 

The **tautological ring** $R^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$ is defined as the $\mathbb{Q}$-subalgebra of $CH^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$ generated by all kappa classes:
$$ R^*(\mathcal{M}_g) = \mathbb{Q}[\kappa_1, \kappa_2, \dots] \subset CH^*(\mathcal{M}_g). $$
Because the Chow ring is graded by codimension, $R^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$ naturally inherits a grading: 
$$ R^*(\mathcal{M}_g) = \bigoplus_{i=0}^{3g-3} R^i(\mathcal{M}_g). $$

Faber's conjecture stipulates that the quotient of the free polynomial ring by the ideal of tautological relations $I_g$ yields a Gorenstein algebra. The Gorenstein condition requires that the natural bilinear pairing has zero kernel: any non-zero tautological class $\alpha \in R^i(\mathcal{M}_g)$ must multiply with some $\beta \in R^{g-2-i}(\mathcal{M}_g)$ to produce a non-zero element $\alpha \cdot \beta \neq 0 \in R^{g-2}(\mathcal{M}_g)$.

Furthermore, Faber posited precise combinatorial proportionalities in the top degree $R^{g-2}(\mathcal{M}_g)$. For any integer partition $\vec{\lambda} = (\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_k)$ of $g-2$ (such that $\sum \lambda_j = g-2$), the monomial $\kappa_{\vec{\lambda}} = \kappa_{\lambda_1} \dots \kappa_{\lambda_k}$ can be explicitly scaled to $\kappa_{g-2}$. Specifically, if one formally defines intersection numbers $\int_{\mathcal{M}_g} \kappa_{\vec{\lambda}}$, Faber provided a conjectural formula linking these evaluations to sums over the symmetric group, effectively fully determining the top-degree intersection theory.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

Carel Faber formally introduced the conjecture in a 1993 publication, following earlier foundational work by Mumford and Morita. The boldest claim was that the tautological ring, constructed purely from intersection-theoretic algebraic cycles on a highly non-compact stack, miraculously mirrors the topological Poincaré duality of a compact Kähler manifold.

The conjecture acted as the primary framework for the intersection theory of moduli spaces for over two decades. Key milestones include:
- **1992–1995:** Witten's conjecture (proven by Kontsevich) established that intersection numbers of $\psi$-classes on the compactification $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}$ satisfy the KdV hierarchy. Motivated by this, Eduard Looijenga (1995) proved the vanishing theorem $R^i(\mathcal{M}_g) = 0$ for $i > g-2$ and established that $\dim_\mathbb{Q} R^{g-2}(\mathcal{M}_g) \le 1$.
- **2000:** Faber and Zagier formulated a set of highly non-trivial conjectural relations in $R^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$ (the Faber-Zagier relations), constructed via extracting coefficients from a specific power series involving generating functions of Bernoulli numbers.
- **2013:** Rahul Pandharipande and Aaron Pixton proved that the Faber-Zagier relations hold true in the tautological ring. Pixton derived these relations by considering the Cohomological Field Theory (CohFT) associated with Witten's $r$-spin class, taking the limit as $r \to \infty$.
- **2014 (Generation Bound):** The property that $R^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$ is generated by $\kappa_1, \dots, \kappa_{\lfloor g/3 \rfloor}$ was proven by multiple researchers (including Ionel, and later definitively via the Pixton relations by Pandharipande, Pixton, and Zvonkine).

However, the perfect pairing (Gorenstein) property was dismantled in higher dimensions:
- **2014:** Dan Petersen and Orsola Tommasi proved that the analogous Gorenstein property fails for the moduli space of *stable* curves $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2,n}$ for $n \ge 20$. 
- **Computations for $g \ge 24$:** Extensive computer algebra calculations by Faber, based on generating the full matrix of Pixton's relations, showed that for $\mathcal{M}_g$, the intersection pairing matrix becomes degenerate exactly at $g = 24$. In $R^{12}(\mathcal{M}_{24})$, the number of independent Pixton relations strictly exceeds what is mathematically permitted if the ring were to pair perfectly with $R^{10}(\mathcal{M}_{24})$. This decisively shattered the Gorenstein conjecture for all $g \ge 24$.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

Faber's conjecture is "partially solved," meaning heavily structural subsets of the conjecture are universally true, while the specific algebraic structure of the Gorenstein property is only true in a finite, verified range.

The following components are rigorously proven for **all** genera $g \ge 2$:
1. **Vanishing Bound:** $R^i(\mathcal{M}_g) = 0$ for $i > g-2$ (Looijenga, 1995).
2. **Socle:** $R^{g-2}(\mathcal{M}_g) \cong \mathbb{Q}$ is proven for all $g$ (consequence of Looijenga's bound and the Faber-Zagier relations).
3. **Generation:** $R^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$ is generated by $\kappa_1, \dots, \kappa_{\lfloor g/3 \rfloor}$.
4. **Proportionalities:** The exact numerical proportionalities between kappa monomials in the top degree have been unconditionally proven for all $g$ (by Liu-Xu and Buryak-Shadrin, independently resolving Faber's intersection number predictions).

The **Perfect Pairing (Gorenstein Property)** has been computationally and theoretically verified for all $g \le 23$. For these genera, massive Gröbner basis computations of Pixton's relations demonstrate that the ideal of relations perfectly matches the Gorenstein ideal, confirming that no "ghost" relations exist in this range. 

## 5. Principal Obstacles

The fundamental obstacle to fully resolving the tautological ring's structure is the absence of an intrinsic geometric mechanism that explains the failure of the Gorenstein property at $g \ge 24$. 

When the perfect pairing fails, it implies the existence of non-trivial elements in the kernel of the pairing—classes in $R^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$ that are not identically zero, yet have an intersection product of zero with every element of the complementary degree. 

The primary technical bottleneck is the gap between the tautological ring $R^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$ and the full rational Chow ring $CH^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$, as well as the cycle map to singular cohomology $H^{2i}(\mathcal{M}_g, \mathbb{Q})$. Because $CH^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$ is not finitely generated, and because we lack a complete understanding of non-tautological classes, standard algebraic topology machinery (like the Hard Lefschetz theorem or Hodge theory on compact manifolds) fails to apply to the non-compact stack $\mathcal{M}_g$. 

Petersen and Tommasi's disproof for $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{2,n}$ utilized the cohomology of local systems and the representation theory of the symplectic group $Sp(2g, \mathbb{Q})$ to detect non-tautological singular cohomology classes. A Gorenstein property rigidly forces the Betti numbers of the tautological ring to be symmetric. If one finds a non-tautological cohomology class in a degree where the tautological ring already exhausts the Gorenstein Betti number constraint, the perfect pairing must necessarily break. Executing this logic for $\mathcal{M}_{24}$ directly remains computationally intractable due to the astronomical size of the dual complexes of stable curves.

## 6. The Gap

The boundary between what is known (Section 4) and a full resolution of the problem is precise: We know Pixton's relations provide a massive, rigorously proven set of true relations in $R^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$. We know these relations are sufficient to prove the Gorenstein property for $g \le 23$. We also know these same relations algebraically predict the *failure* of the Gorenstein property for $g \ge 24$.

The ultimate gap is the **Completeness of Pixton's Relations**. Does the ideal generated by Pixton's relations equal the entire ideal of true tautological relations $I_g$ for all $g$? 
- If Pixton's relations are complete, then the algebraic structure of $R^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$ is formally solved. The failure of the Gorenstein property is simply a mathematical reality encoded in the Pixton ideal.
- If Pixton's relations are *not* complete, there must exist some exotic, undetected geometric construction that yields novel tautological relations—relations entirely unseen by the Cohomological Field Theory methods of Witten, Kontsevich, and Pixton. Proving completeness requires crossing a massive barrier in bounding the rank of tautological groups from below.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

Current research is heavily focused on proving Pixton's Conjecture (the completeness of the $r$-spin relations) and exploring the boundary between tautological and non-tautological cohomology. Active hubs include the schools around ETH Zürich (Pandharipande's research group), the University of Amsterdam, KTH Royal Institute of Technology, and Stanford University.

Key ongoing directions include:
- **Cohomology of Moduli Spaces:** Groups are actively computing the exact singular cohomology of $\mathcal{M}_{g,n}$ using highly optimized computer searches and representation theory to find the lowest-degree non-tautological classes for various $(g,n)$.
- **Derived Algebraic Geometry:** Researchers are utilizing derived stacks and non-commutative geometry to attempt to construct an intrinsic geometrical framework for Pixton's relations, hoping to show that no other relations can exist.
- **Arithmetic Motives:** A frontier claim `*(frontier — verify)*` suggests that the failure of the Gorenstein property at $g=24$ is strictly tied to the existence of weight-zero boundary motives related to specific arithmetic structures (such as K3 surfaces or level-2 modular forms) that inject non-tautological elements into the cohomology ring, forcing the tautological pairing to degenerate.

## 8. Future Work

Leading mathematicians have outlined several long-term strategies to complete the theory:
1. **Prove Pixton's Completeness:** Establishing a formal, unconditional proof that Pixton's relations generate the full ideal $I_g$ is the most critical open challenge in intersection theory on moduli spaces.
2. **Characterize the Substitute for Gorenstein:** If $R^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$ is not Gorenstein, what exact algebraic property *does* it satisfy? Some researchers hypothesize that $R^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$ might be a module over a well-behaved Gorenstein subring, or that a geometrically natural larger ring containing $R^*(\mathcal{M}_g)$ restores the perfect pairing.
3. **Map the Non-Tautological Frontier:** Systematically trace the non-tautological singular cohomology of $\mathcal{M}_g$. Understanding exactly where and how non-tautological classes appear (for instance, mapping the exact Betti numbers where the cycle map injects into non-tautological components) could fully explain the dimensional anomalies causing the tautological intersection pairing to fail.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

To ground the abstract formulation, consider the moduli space of curves of genus $g=4$, denoted $\mathcal{M}_4$. By Faber's Conjecture (which holds perfectly here since $4 \le 23$), we can precisely map out the entire tautological ring $R^*(\mathcal{M}_4)$.

1. **Top Degree (Vanishing & Socle):** The maximum non-zero degree is $g-2 = 4-2 = 2$. Therefore, $R^{>2}(\mathcal{M}_4) = 0$, and the socle is $R^2(\mathcal{M}_4) \cong \mathbb{Q}$.
2. **Generators:** The generation bound asserts that the ring is generated by $\kappa_1, \dots, \kappa_{\lfloor 4/3 \rfloor}$. Since $\lfloor 4/3 \rfloor = 1$, the *entire* tautological ring $R^*(\mathcal{M}_4)$ is generated by a single class, $\kappa_1 \in R^1(\mathcal{M}_4)$.
3. **Ring Structure:** The tautological ring is distributed across degrees 0, 1, and 2:
   - $R^0(\mathcal{M}_4) \cong \mathbb{Q} \cdot 1$
   - $R^1(\mathcal{M}_4) \cong \mathbb{Q} \cdot \kappa_1$
   - $R^2(\mathcal{M}_4) \cong \mathbb{Q} \cdot \kappa_1^2$

Because $R^*(\mathcal{M}_4)$ is fully generated by $\kappa_1$, the class $\kappa_2 \in R^2(\mathcal{M}_4)$ must be mathematically proportional to $\kappa_1^2$. Faber's combinatorial proportionalities provide the exact relation between these top-degree intersections. For $g=4$, the relations force $\kappa_2$ to be a specific non-zero rational multiple of $\kappa_1^2$, reducing the dimension of $R^2(\mathcal{M}_4)$ to exactly 1. 

The **Gorenstein (perfect pairing) property** is elegantly visible in this low-dimensional case. The intersection pairing between degree 1 and degree 1 is simply multiplication: 
$$ R^1 \times R^1 \to R^2 $$
$$ \kappa_1 \cdot \kappa_1 = \kappa_1^2 $$
Since $\kappa_1^2$ is the non-zero basis element of the socle $R^2 \cong \mathbb{Q}$, the pairing has no kernel. Any non-zero element in $R^1$ (which is a scalar multiple $c\kappa_1$) paired with $\kappa_1$ yields $c\kappa_1^2 \neq 0$. Thus, it satisfies algebraic Poincaré duality. The full tautological ring for genus 4 is structurally isomorphic to the truncated polynomial ring $\mathbb{Q}[\kappa_1] / (\kappa_1^3)$.
