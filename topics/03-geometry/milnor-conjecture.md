# Milnor Conjecture (Fundamental Groups of Manifolds with Nonnegative Ricci Curvature)

> **Topic:** Algebraic & Differential Geometry · **ID:** `03-geometry/milnor-conjecture` · **Status:** solved-recently

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

**Milnor's conjecture (1968).** Let $(M^n, g)$ be a complete, connected Riemannian manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature, $\mathrm{Ric}_g \geq 0$. Then the fundamental group $\pi_1(M)$ is finitely generated.

Milnor stated this as an open question immediately after proving that every *finitely generated* subgroup of $\pi_1(M)$ has polynomial growth of degree $\le n$. The gap he left open is exactly whether $\pi_1(M)$ itself can fail to be generated by finitely many elements — which can only happen if $M$ is noncompact (compact manifolds have finitely presented $\pi_1$).

A disproof requires exhibiting one complete $(M^n,g)$ with $\mathrm{Ric}_g \ge 0$ and $\pi_1(M)$ not finitely generated. A proof requires a general structural argument bounding the number of generators by a function of $n$ alone.

**Resolution status.** The conjecture is **false in general**: Bruè, Naber and Semola (2023) constructed a complete $M^7$ with $\mathrm{Ric} > 0$ and $\pi_1(M) \cong \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$, later pushed down to dimension $6$. It remains **true in dimensions $n \le 3$** and **open in dimensions $4$ and $5$**.

*(This page concerns the differential-geometric Milnor conjecture. The unrelated Milnor conjecture on quadratic forms and mod-2 Galois cohomology, proved by Voevodsky, and the Milnor conjecture on the slice genus of torus knots, proved by Kronheimer–Mrowka, are separate problems.)*

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

Let $(M^n,g)$ be complete with Levi-Civita connection $\nabla$ and curvature $R$. The Ricci tensor is
$$\mathrm{Ric}(X,Y) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \langle R(e_i, X)Y, e_i \rangle ,$$
and $\mathrm{Ric} \ge 0$ means $\mathrm{Ric}(X,X)\ge 0$ for all tangent vectors $X$.

**Bishop–Gromov.** For $p \in M$ and $\mathrm{Ric}\ge 0$, the function
$$r \longmapsto \frac{\mathrm{vol}\,B_r(p)}{\omega_n r^n}$$
is nonincreasing, where $\omega_n = \mathrm{vol}(B_1 \subset \mathbb{R}^n)$. Its limit $\mathrm{AVR}(M)=\lim_{r\to\infty}\mathrm{vol}\,B_r(p)/(\omega_n r^n) \in [0,1]$ is the **asymptotic volume ratio**.

**Milnor's growth theorem.** If $\Gamma \le \pi_1(M)$ is generated by a finite set $S$, then the word-growth function $\beta_S(k)=\\#\{\gamma \in \Gamma : |\gamma|_S \le k\}$ satisfies $\beta_S(k) \le C k^n$. Proof: lift to the universal cover $\tilde M$, which also has $\mathrm{Ric}\ge0$; $\Gamma$ acts freely by deck transformations, and Bishop–Gromov applied to disjoint balls around the orbit $\Gamma\cdot \tilde p$ forces polynomial orbit growth.

Combining with **Gromov's polynomial-growth theorem**, every finitely generated subgroup of $\pi_1(M)$ is virtually nilpotent. Critically, this says nothing about $\pi_1(M)$ globally: a group all of whose finitely generated subgroups are finite (a **locally finite** group) satisfies every conclusion above yet may be infinitely generated. The canonical example is
$$\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z} \;=\; \varinjlim_k \; \tfrac{1}{k!}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z} \;\cong\; \varinjlim \big(\mathbb{Z}/1! \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/2! \hookrightarrow \cdots\big),$$
abelian, torsion, locally finite, not finitely generated.

**Cheeger–Gromoll splitting.** If $\mathrm{Ric}\ge0$ and $M$ contains a line, then $M \cong N^{n-1}\times \mathbb{R}$ isometrically. This is the main rigidity input for low-dimensional cases.

**Cheeger–Colding theory.** Pointed rescalings $(M, r_i^{-2}g, p)$ with $r_i\to\infty$ subconverge in pointed Gromov–Hausdorff topology to **asymptotic cones** $(X,d,x)$, metric measure spaces with $\mathrm{Ric}\ge0$ in the $\mathrm{RCD}(0,n)$ sense. Non-uniqueness of the asymptotic cone is the mechanism exploited by the counterexample.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1968.** Milnor, *A note on curvature and fundamental group* (J. Differential Geometry 2, 1–7), proves the polynomial growth bound and poses the finite-generation question.
- **1971–72.** Cheeger–Gromoll prove the splitting theorem and the soul theorem. The soul theorem settles the *sectional* curvature case: $\sec \ge 0 \Rightarrow M$ deformation retracts to a compact totally geodesic soul $S$, so $\pi_1(M)\cong\pi_1(S)$ is finitely presented.
- **1981.** Gromov's polynomial growth theorem upgrades Milnor's bound to "virtually nilpotent".
- **1986–90.** Li, and independently Anderson, prove $\mathrm{AVR}(M)>0 \Rightarrow |\pi_1(M)| \le \mathrm{AVR}(M)^{-1} < \infty$.
- **2000.** Sormani: small linear diameter growth of ends implies finite generation. Wilking: any counterexample can be replaced by one with abelian (indeed torsion or $\mathbb{Q}$-like) fundamental group — a decisive reduction.
- **2013.** Gang Liu settles $n=3$.
- **2019–22.** Jiayin Pan, and Pan–Wei, build increasingly wild examples: fundamental groups with infinitely many "scales", Ricci limit spaces of non-integer Hausdorff dimension. These map the failure modes of the conjecture without breaking it.
- **2023–25.** Bruè–Naber–Semola construct the counterexample, first in $n=7$ with $\mathrm{Ric}>0$ and $\pi_1 = \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$, then in $n=6$. Published in the *Annals of Mathematics* line of work *(frontier — verify final volume/pagination)*.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Milnor's own theorem is vacuous against locally finite groups.** Polynomial growth constrains each finitely generated subgroup separately. For $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ every finitely generated subgroup is a finite cyclic group, growth degree $0$. No growth estimate can see the failure.
- **Volume-comparison arguments are scale-local.** Bishop–Gromov compares one scale to the next; a group generated at infinitely many separated scales $r_1 \ll r_2 \ll \cdots$ pays a bounded volume cost at each. The costs must be summed against a *fixed* budget, and there is no mechanism forcing that sum to diverge unless volume growth is Euclidean ($\mathrm{AVR}>0$, which is precisely why Li–Anderson works).
- **Asymptotic cones are not unique.** Under only $\mathrm{Ric}\ge0$, different rescaling sequences can converge to non-isometric cones, and the cones need not be metric cones. Every finite-generation proof to date (Sormani, Pan) secretly assumes some form of stability at infinity; without it the induction over scales has no base.
- **No topological obstruction in high dimension.** In dimension $\le 3$ the classification of $3$-manifolds plus the splitting theorem pins down the topology. From dimension $6$ up there is enough room to build infinite towers of lens-space-like quotients smoothly glued, with curvature errors absorbed in a shrinking direction.

## 6. The Gap

The remaining gap is dimensions $4$ and $5$. Below dimension $4$ the splitting theorem plus $3$-manifold topology closes the problem. From dimension $6$ up the construction exists. The open question is whether $4$- and $5$-dimensional geometry has enough room for the Bruè–Naber–Semola mechanism, which requires: (i) a base with a family of shrinking, topologically nontrivial circle fibrations, (ii) enough transverse directions to keep $\mathrm{Ric}\ge0$ while the fiber degenerates, and (iii) a compatible tower of covers realizing $\varinjlim \mathbb{Z}/a_k$. Known constructions need at least $6$ dimensions for (ii). The gap to close is: either a dimension-specific rigidity theorem for $n=4,5$ (likely via $4$-dimensional collapsing theory and the Gauss–Bonnet/Chern–Gauss–Bonnet constraint), or a sharper construction.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Bruè (Bocconi), Naber (Northwestern), Semola (Vienna/ETH):** extending the counterexample technology to constrain which groups arise; the conjectural picture is that $\pi_1(M^n)$ for $\mathrm{Ric}\ge0$ is always a finitely generated-by-locally-finite extension, with an explicit bound on the "finitely generated part" in terms of $n$ *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Jiayin Pan (UC Santa Cruz) and Guofang Wei (UCSB):** classification of the escape rate / critical rescaling invariants that force finite generation; sharpened versions of the stability-at-infinity criterion.
- **Dimensions 4–5:** groups working on $4$-dimensional Ricci limit spaces (Naber–Zhang, Cheeger–Jiang–Naber $\varepsilon$-regularity) are the natural source of a positive answer, since codimension-4 singular set estimates are strongest exactly there.
- **RCD analogue:** whether an $\mathrm{RCD}(0,N)$ space can have non-finitely-generated "revised fundamental group"; Mondino–Wei and collaborators.
- **Wilking's reduction** remains the organizing principle: any surviving low-dimensional case may be assumed abelian.

## 8. Future Work

1. **Settle $n=4$.** Combine Cheeger–Colding codimension-$4$ regularity with the classification of $4$-dimensional collapsed limits; a positive answer here would be the last "true" dimension.
2. **Classify realizable groups.** Determine exactly which countable groups occur as $\pi_1(M^n)$ with $\mathrm{Ric}\ge0$ for given $n$ — is every countable locally finite abelian group realizable in some dimension?
3. **Quantitative finite generation.** Prove $\pi_1$ is generated by $C(n)$ elements *whenever* the asymptotic cone is unique, with explicit $C(n)$.
4. **Sharpen Milnor's growth theorem** to a statement about the whole group: e.g. is $\pi_1(M)$ always an extension $1 \to L \to \pi_1(M) \to N \to 1$ with $L$ locally finite and $N$ finitely generated virtually nilpotent?
5. **Positive scalar / positive Ricci variants.** Does $\mathrm{Ric}>0$ with additional volume or diameter control restore finite generation?

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**(a) Why the conjecture is true when $\mathrm{AVR}(M) > 0$.** Let $\mathrm{Ric}_M \ge 0$ with $\mathrm{AVR}(M) = v > 0$, and let $\tilde M \to M$ be the universal cover with deck group $\Gamma = \pi_1(M)$. Since $\mathrm{Ric}_{\tilde M}\ge 0$ too, Bishop–Gromov gives for any $p\in\tilde M$
$$\mathrm{vol}\, B_r(\tilde p) \le \omega_n r^n .$$
A fundamental domain $F$ for $\Gamma$ has $\mathrm{vol}(F) = \mathrm{vol}(M)$. If $\Gamma$ contains $k$ elements, then $k$ disjoint copies of $F$ sit inside $\tilde M$, so for large $r$
$$k \cdot \mathrm{vol}\,B_r(p) \big/ (\omega_n r^n) \;\lesssim\; \mathrm{vol}\,B_{r+D}(\tilde p)/(\omega_n r^n) \le 1 + o(1),$$
giving $k \cdot v \le 1$, i.e. $|\Gamma| \le 1/v$. So $\pi_1(M)$ is finite — in particular finitely generated. **The whole argument dies when $v=0$**: the inequality becomes $k\cdot 0\le 1$, vacuous for every $k$.

**(b) The group that breaks it.** Take $a_k = k!$ and the tower of cyclic groups
$$\mathbb{Z}/1! \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/2! \hookrightarrow \mathbb{Z}/3! \hookrightarrow \cdots, \qquad \varinjlim_k \mathbb{Z}/k! \;\cong\; \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}.$$
Each inclusion $\mathbb{Z}/k! \to \mathbb{Z}/(k+1)!$ is multiplication by $k+1$. Check the two properties:

- *Not finitely generated.* Any finite set $\{q_1,\dots,q_m\} \subset \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$ with $q_i = p_i/n_i$ generates a subgroup inside $\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{Z} \cong \mathbb{Z}/N$ where $N=\mathrm{lcm}(n_i)$, a **finite** group. So no finite set generates all of $\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$.
- *Milnor's theorem is satisfied.* Each such subgroup is $\mathbb{Z}/N$, with growth function $\beta_S(k)\le N$ — bounded, hence polynomial of degree $0 \le n$. No contradiction with $\mathrm{Ric}\ge0$.

Geometrically, Bruè–Naber–Semola realize this group as follows: build $M^7$ (later $M^6$) carrying a sequence of scales $r_1 \gg r_2 \gg \cdots$ at which the metric looks like a circle bundle whose fiber length shrinks, with the $k$-th stage contributing a $\mathbb{Z}/(k+1)$ lens-space-type quotient glued to the previous one. Positivity of $\mathrm{Ric}$ is maintained because at each stage the shrinking fiber direction is compensated by curvature in the remaining $\ge 5$ transverse directions — the reason $n\ge 6$ is needed. The resulting $\pi_1$ is $\varinjlim \mathbb{Z}/k! = \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}$, and the asymptotic cone is non-unique, which is exactly the hypothesis Pan's finite-generation criterion forbids.
