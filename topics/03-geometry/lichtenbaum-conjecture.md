# Lichtenbaum Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebraic & Differential Geometry · **ID:** `03-geometry/lichtenbaum-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $F$ be a number field with ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_F$, $r_1$ real embeddings, $r_2$ pairs of complex embeddings, and Dedekind zeta function $\zeta_F(s)$. Lichtenbaum (1972) conjectured that the special values of $\zeta_F$ at negative integers are computed by the orders of Quillen's higher $K$-groups of $\mathcal{O}_F$.

**Conjecture (Lichtenbaum, totally real case).** If $F$ is totally real and $n \ge 2$ is even, then $K_{2n-2}(\mathcal{O}_F)$ and $K_{2n-1}(\mathcal{O}_F)$ are finite and

$$\bigl|\zeta_F(1-n)\bigr| \;=\; 2^{r_1}\,\frac{\\#K_{2n-2}(\mathcal{O}_F)}{\\#K_{2n-1}(\mathcal{O}_F)}.$$

**Conjecture (general number field).** For any $F$ and any $n \ge 2$, the leading coefficient $\zeta_F^{*}(1-n)$ of the Taylor expansion at $s = 1-n$ satisfies

$$\zeta_F^{*}(1-n) \;=\; \pm\, 2^{?}\,\frac{\\#K_{2n-2}(\mathcal{O}_F)}{\\#K_{2n-1}(\mathcal{O}_F)_{\mathrm{tors}}}\;R_n^{B}(F),$$

where $R_n^{B}(F)$ is the Borel regulator, the covolume of the image of $K_{2n-1}(\mathcal{O}_F)/\mathrm{tors} \cong \mathbb{Z}^{d_n}$ under the Borel regulator map, $d_n = r_1 + r_2$ for $n$ odd and $d_n = r_2$ for $n$ even.

A complete solution means proving the identity **on the nose** (including the $2$-primary factor and the exact power of $2$) for all number fields, or exhibiting a field and an $n$ where it fails. Lichtenbaum's later "Weil-étale" reformulation asks for a Grothendieck topology on $\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_F)$ whose cohomology is finitely generated and computes $\zeta_F^{*}(1-n)$ as an Euler characteristic; that existence statement is a second, still-open half of the problem.

Two related conjectures should not be confused with the above. The **Quillen–Lichtenbaum conjecture** (comparison of algebraic and étale $K$-theory) is now a theorem, a consequence of the Rost–Voevodsky norm residue theorem. The **Lichtenbaum conjecture over finite fields** (Weil-étale cohomology computing $\zeta_X(s)$ for $X/\mathbb{F}_q$ smooth projective) is known only conditionally on the Tate conjecture.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**$K$-groups.** $K_i(\mathcal{O}_F) = \pi_i\bigl(BGL(\mathcal{O}_F)^{+} \times K_0\bigr)$ (Quillen 1973). Borel computed the ranks:

$$\mathrm{rank}\,K_i(\mathcal{O}_F) = \begin{cases} 1 & i = 0,\\ r_1 + r_2 - 1 & i = 1,\\ r_1 + r_2 & i \equiv 1 \bmod 4,\ i>1,\\ r_2 & i \equiv 3 \bmod 4,\\ 0 & i \text{ even},\ i>0.\end{cases}$$

So for even $n$ the numerator group $K_{2n-2}(\mathcal{O}_F)$ is finite, and $K_{2n-1}(\mathcal{O}_F)$ is finite exactly when $r_2 = 0$, i.e. $F$ totally real — matching the functional-equation fact that $\zeta_F(1-n) \ne 0$ precisely then.

**Roots-of-unity weights.** Set

$$w_n(F) = \\#H^0\bigl(F, \mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}(n)\bigr) = \max\{ N : \mathrm{Gal}(F(\mu_N)/F) \text{ has exponent dividing } n\}.$$

For $F = \mathbb{Q}$: $w_1 = 2$, $w_2 = 24$, $w_3 = 2^4\cdot 3^2 \cdot 5 = 720$? — concretely $w_2(\mathbb{Q}) = 24$, $w_4(\mathbb{Q}) = 240$, $w_6(\mathbb{Q}) = 504$.

**Étale/motivic description.** For $p$ odd and $\mathcal{O}_F[1/p]$, the Quillen–Lichtenbaum theorem gives, for $n \ge 2$,

$$K_{2n-2}(\mathcal{O}_F)\otimes \mathbb{Z}_p \cong H^2_{\text{ét}}\bigl(\mathcal{O}_F[1/p], \mathbb{Z}_p(n)\bigr), \qquad K_{2n-1}(\mathcal{O}_F)\otimes\mathbb{Z}_p \cong H^1_{\text{ét}}\bigl(\mathcal{O}_F[1/p], \mathbb{Z}_p(n)\bigr),$$

turning the conjecture into the **$p$-adic Euler-characteristic formula**

$$\bigl|\zeta_F(1-n)\bigr|_p^{-1} \;=\; \frac{\\#H^2_{\text{ét}}(\mathcal{O}_F[1/p],\mathbb{Z}_p(n))}{\\#H^1_{\text{ét}}(\mathcal{O}_F[1/p],\mathbb{Z}_p(n))_{\mathrm{tors}}},$$

which for totally real $F$ is exactly the statement that the Iwasawa **Main Conjecture** holds and that the characteristic ideal of the relevant Iwasawa module is generated by the $p$-adic $L$-function $L_p(s,\omega^{1-n})$ (Deligne–Ribet, Cassou-Noguès).

**$n=2$ specialization (Birch–Tate).** $\zeta_F(-1) = \pm\,\\#K_2(\mathcal{O}_F)/w_2(F)$, with $K_2(\mathcal{O}_F)$ the tame kernel.

**Weil-étale formulation.** Lichtenbaum (2009) posits a topos $\overline{\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}_F}_{W}$ with cohomology $H^i_W(\cdot,\mathbb{Z}(n))$ finitely generated, vanishing for $i \gg 0$, and

$$\mathrm{ord}_{s=1-n}\zeta_F(s) = \sum_{i} (-1)^i\, i\cdot \mathrm{rank}\,H^i_W, \qquad \zeta_F^{*}(1-n) = \pm \prod_i \bigl(\\#H^i_W{}_{\mathrm{tors}}\bigr)^{(-1)^i}\cdot \bigl(\text{regulator}\bigr).$$

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1970–71.** Tate and Birch formulate $\zeta_F(-1) \doteq \\#K_2(\mathcal{O}_F)/w_2(F)$, guided by Serre's computation of $K_2$ and Klingen–Siegel rationality of $\zeta_F(1-n)$.
- **1972–73.** Lichtenbaum, at the Battelle conference (*Algebraic K-theory II*, LNM 342), generalizes to all $n$ and links the values to étale cohomology of $\mathcal{O}_F$, immediately after Quillen's definition of higher $K$-theory.
- **1974.** Borel computes $K_*(\mathcal{O}_F)\otimes\mathbb{Q}$ and the regulator, confirming the *rank* half of the conjecture and the order of vanishing.
- **1984.** Mazur–Wiles prove the Main Conjecture over $\mathbb{Q}$ for odd $p$ — the odd part for abelian $F$.
- **1990.** Wiles proves the Main Conjecture for totally real $F$ and odd $p$.
- **1996.** Kolster–Nguyen Quang Do–Fleckinger (Duke) derive the odd-part Lichtenbaum conjecture from the Main Conjecture, up to an ambiguity later removed.
- **2000.** Rognes–Weibel compute the full $2$-primary $K$-theory of $\mathcal{O}_F$ using Voevodsky's Milnor conjecture, settling the $2$-part for **abelian** totally real fields and pinning the exact power $2^{r_1}$.
- **2003.** Huber–Kings, and Burns–Greither, prove the equivariant Tamagawa number conjecture for Tate motives over abelian fields, which subsumes Lichtenbaum's identity there.
- **2009–2018.** Lichtenbaum's Weil-étale topology (Annals 2009); Geisser and Flach–Morin build Weil-étale cohomology for proper regular arithmetic schemes and prove the expected special-value formalism conditionally.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **Ranks and order of vanishing:** proven unconditionally for all $F$, all $n \ge 2$ (Borel 1974).
- **Odd primes, totally real $F$:** the $p$-part holds for every odd $p$ and every totally real $F$ (Wiles 1990 + Kolster–Nguyen Quang Do–Fleckinger 1996). This is the largest known class.
- **Abelian $F$ over $\mathbb{Q}$:** the full statement, all primes including $p=2$, all $n\ge 2$ (Mazur–Wiles; Rognes–Weibel for $p=2$; Huber–Kings and Burns–Greither via ETNC). In particular $F = \mathbb{Q}$, real quadratic $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})$, real cyclotomic $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m)^{+}$.
- **$n = 2$ (Birch–Tate):** proven for all abelian totally real $F$; proven up to the $2$-part for all totally real $F$.
- **Explicit numerics:** $K_i(\mathbb{Z})$ is known for $i \le 8k$ ranges with $2$-torsion fully determined (Rognes–Weibel); tables of $\\#K_2(\mathcal{O}_F)$ for hundreds of quadratic and cubic fields (Browkin, Belabas–Gangl) match Birch–Tate exactly.
- **Function-field analogue:** for $X$ smooth projective over $\mathbb{F}_q$, the Weil-étale special-value formula is a theorem for $\dim X \le 1$ and, in general, is equivalent to the Tate conjecture for $X$ (Milne 1986; Geisser 2004).

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **The prime $2$ outside abelian fields.** Iwasawa Main Conjecture machinery (Eisenstein ideals, Hida families, Euler systems) is built for odd $p$; at $p = 2$ the relevant Galois cohomology has extra $H^0$ and $H^2$ contributions from the real places ($H^*(\mathbb{Z}/2,\mathbb{Z}_2(n))$ is $2$-periodic and nonzero in all degrees), so the étale descent spectral sequence does not degenerate and no clean Euler characteristic emerges. Wiles' proof simply excludes $p=2$.
- **Non-abelian totally real fields.** The proofs for abelian $F$ use cyclotomic units / Beilinson elements as an explicit Euler system. No analogue of cyclotomic units exists for a general totally real field; Stark units are conjectural, so the "explicit reciprocity" input is missing.
- **Regulator irrationality.** For fields with $r_2 > 0$ the conjecture involves the Borel regulator, a transcendental period. Comparing an archimedean volume to a $p$-adic Euler characteristic requires a $p$-adic period comparison of Bloch–Kato type; the $p$-adic and complex sides are only linked through the (still-conjectural for general motives) Tamagawa number formalism.
- **Weil-étale existence.** Lichtenbaum's topos gives the right answer in weight $0$, but for $n \ne 0$ the required finite generation of $H^i_W(\mathcal{O}_F, \mathbb{Z}(n))$ is currently known only modulo Beilinson–Soulé vanishing and finiteness statements as strong as the conjecture itself — the construction is not yet independent leverage.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: the $p$-part for all odd $p$ and all totally real $F$; everything for abelian $F$. Open: (i) the $2$-primary part for totally real $F$ not abelian over $\mathbb{Q}$ — equivalently the $2$-adic Main Conjecture for such $F$; (ii) the full statement for fields with complex places, where the identity is equivalent to the Tamagawa number conjecture for the Tate motive $\mathbb{Q}(n)$ over $F$, known only for abelian $F$; (iii) an unconditional Weil-étale cohomology theory realizing the value as an Euler characteristic. The single sharpest missing step is an Euler system (or a $2$-adic Hida-theoretic argument) for non-abelian totally real fields at $p = 2$.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Equivariant Tamagawa number conjecture (ETNC).** Burns, Flach, Macias Castillo, Sano and collaborators continue to reduce Lichtenbaum-type formulas to ETNC and to prove ETNC in new equivariant settings (dihedral and $\ell$-adic Lie extensions). *(frontier — verify)*
- **Weil-étale cohomology.** Flach–Morin's program for proper regular arithmetic schemes; Morin's work on $\mathbb{Z}(n)$-complexes and the "fundamental line". Extensions to singular and non-proper schemes are active. *(frontier — verify)*
- **$2$-adic Iwasawa theory.** Groups at Bordeaux, Besançon (Nguyen Quang Do school) and McMaster (Kolster) on $2$-adic descent, wild kernels and the $2$-part of Birch–Tate for non-abelian totally real fields. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Computation.** Continued verification of $\\#K_2(\mathcal{O}_F)$ and $\\#K_{4k+2}(\mathcal{O}_F)$ against zeta values for large families of number fields, using Belabas–Gangl style tame-kernel algorithms and PARI/Magma zeta evaluations.

## 8. Future Work

- Construct an Euler system for totally real fields at $p = 2$, or prove the $2$-adic Main Conjecture by deformation of Hilbert modular forms.
- Prove Beilinson–Soulé vanishing for number fields, which would let Lichtenbaum's motivic complexes $\mathbb{Z}(n)$ be pinned down independently and make the Weil-étale Euler characteristic unconditional.
- Establish the Tate conjecture for surfaces/threefolds over finite fields, which would give the geometric Lichtenbaum conjecture unconditionally in those dimensions.
- Develop a uniform framework treating archimedean regulators and $p$-adic Euler characteristics simultaneously (a "global Tamagawa measure" for $\mathrm{Spec}\,\mathcal{O}_F$).

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Case $F = \mathbb{Q}$ ($r_1 = 1$, $r_2 = 0$).** Here the conjecture is a theorem, and one can check it numerically against Quillen–Borel–Rognes–Weibel computations of $K_*(\mathbb{Z})$.

*$n = 2$.* $\zeta(-1) = -\tfrac{1}{12}$. The $K$-groups are $K_2(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/2$ (Milnor: generated by the symbol $\{-1,-1\}$) and $K_3(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/48$. Then

$$2^{r_1}\frac{\\#K_2(\mathbb{Z})}{\\#K_3(\mathbb{Z})} = 2\cdot\frac{2}{48} = \frac{1}{12} = |\zeta(-1)|. \checkmark$$

Equivalently in Birch–Tate form: $w_2(\mathbb{Q}) = 24$ (the largest $N$ with $(\mathbb{Z}/N)^\times$ of exponent $2$), and $\\#K_2(\mathbb{Z})/w_2 = 2/24 = 1/12$. $\checkmark$

*$n = 4$.* $\zeta(-3) = \tfrac{1}{120}$. $K_6(\mathbb{Z}) = 0$ (order $1$), $K_7(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/240$. Then $2\cdot \tfrac{1}{240} = \tfrac{1}{120}$. $\checkmark$

*$n = 6$.* $\zeta(-5) = -\tfrac{1}{252}$. $K_{10}(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/2$, $K_{11}(\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/1008$. Then $2\cdot\tfrac{2}{1008} = \tfrac{1}{252}$. $\checkmark$

**Case $F = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt5)$, $n = 2$.** By Siegel's formula for a real quadratic field of discriminant $D$,
$$\zeta_F(-1) = \frac{1}{60}\sum_{\substack{|b| < \sqrt{D} \\ b \equiv D \bmod 2}} \sigma\!\left(\frac{D-b^2}{4}\right).$$
With $D = 5$ the admissible $b$ are $\pm 1$, each contributing $\sigma(1) = 1$, so $\zeta_F(-1) = 2/60 = 1/30$. Since $\sqrt5 \in \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_5)$, the extension $F(\mu_{120})/F$ has Galois group of exponent $2$, giving $w_2(F) = 120$. Birch–Tate then predicts $\\#K_2(\mathcal{O}_F) = 120 \cdot \tfrac{1}{30} = 4$, and correspondingly $\\#K_3(\mathcal{O}_F) = 2^{r_1} w_2 = 4\cdot 120 = 480$. Because $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt5)$ is abelian over $\mathbb{Q}$, this instance is proven; replacing $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt5)$ by a totally real non-Galois cubic field leaves the $2$-part of the same computation conjectural — that is precisely the gap of Section 6.
