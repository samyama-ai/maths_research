# Green Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebraic & Differential Geometry · **ID:** `03-geometry/green-conjecture` · **Status:** partially-solved

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $C$ be a smooth irreducible projective curve of genus $g \ge 4$ over an algebraically closed field, non-hyperelliptic, canonically embedded by $|K_C|$ into $\mathbf{P}^{g-1}$. Green's conjecture asserts that the Koszul (syzygy) cohomology of the canonical ring detects the Clifford index exactly:

$$K_{p,2}(C, K_C) = 0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad p < \operatorname{Cliff}(C).$$

Equivalently, in terms of the graded Betti numbers $b_{p,q}$ of the homogeneous coordinate ring of the canonical curve:

$$\operatorname{Cliff}(C) \;=\; \min\{\, p \;:\; b_{p,2} \neq 0 \,\}.$$

The "$\Leftarrow$ fails" direction — nonvanishing $K_{p,2} \neq 0$ for all $p \ge \operatorname{Cliff}(C)$ — is a theorem of Green and Lazarsfeld (1984). The open content is the **vanishing statement**: a curve with large Clifford index has no linear syzygies of the second kind in low degrees. A complete proof must establish this vanishing for *every* curve, not just a general one in moduli; a disproof requires a single curve $C$ with $K_{p,2}(C,K_C) \neq 0$ for some $p < \operatorname{Cliff}(C)$.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Clifford index.** For a line bundle $L$ on $C$ set $\operatorname{Cliff}(L) = \deg L - 2(h^0(L) - 1)$. Then

$$\operatorname{Cliff}(C) = \min\{\operatorname{Cliff}(L) : h^0(L) \ge 2,\ h^1(L) \ge 2\}.$$

Clifford's theorem gives $\operatorname{Cliff}(C) \ge 0$, with $=0$ iff $C$ is hyperelliptic and $=1$ iff $C$ is trigonal or a smooth plane quintic. Always $\operatorname{Cliff}(C) \le \lfloor (g-1)/2 \rfloor$, with equality for a general curve. If $\operatorname{gon}(C)$ is the gonality (minimal degree of a map to $\mathbf{P}^1$) then

$$\operatorname{gon}(C) - 3 \;\le\; \operatorname{Cliff}(C) \;\le\; \operatorname{gon}(C) - 2,$$

and the left inequality is strict equality only in the exceptional cases classified by Coppens–Martens.

**Koszul cohomology.** Let $V = H^0(C, K_C)$, $\dim V = g$, and $S = \operatorname{Sym} V^\vee$. The group $K_{p,q}(C,K_C)$ is the cohomology at the middle of

$$\textstyle\bigwedge^{p+1} V \otimes H^0((q-1)K_C) \;\xrightarrow{\ d\ }\; \bigwedge^{p} V \otimes H^0(qK_C) \;\xrightarrow{\ d\ }\; \bigwedge^{p-1} V \otimes H^0((q+1)K_C),$$

with the Koszul differential $d(v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge v_p \otimes s) = \sum_i (-1)^i v_1\wedge\cdots\widehat{v_i}\cdots\wedge v_p \otimes (v_i s)$. Then $b_{p,q} = \dim K_{p,q}(C,K_C)$ are the graded Betti numbers of $S/I_C$. For a canonical curve the resolution is Gorenstein of length $g-2$, all rows vanish except $q = 1,2$, and duality gives

$$K_{p,1}(C,K_C) \cong K_{g-2-p,2}(C,K_C)^\vee .$$

So the conjecture may be restated: $K_{p,1} \ne 0$ exactly for $1 \le p \le g-2-\operatorname{Cliff}(C)$; the linear strand of the resolution has length $g - 2 - \operatorname{Cliff}(C)$.

**Property $N_p$.** $K_{i,2}=0$ for $i \le p$ means the ideal is generated by quadrics with linear syzygies up to step $p$. The case $p=1$ is the Enriques–Babbage–Petri theorem: $I_C$ is generated by quadrics unless $C$ is trigonal or a plane quintic, i.e. unless $\operatorname{Cliff}(C) = 1$.

**Related conjectures.** The Green–Lazarsfeld *gonality conjecture* predicts $K_{p,1}(C,L) \ne 0$ for $p \le h^0(L)-\operatorname{gon}(C)-1$ when $\deg L \gg 0$; the *secant conjecture* predicts $K_{p,1}(C,L) = 0$ precisely when $L$ is $(p+1)$-very ample under a numerical hypothesis.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1984.** Mark Green, *Koszul cohomology and the geometry of projective varieties* (J. Diff. Geom. 19), introduces Koszul cohomology $K_{p,q}$ and states the conjecture, unifying Noether, Petri, Enriques–Babbage and Andreotti–Mayer-type statements.
- **1984/86.** Green–Lazarsfeld prove the nonvanishing half: a pencil computing the Clifford index produces a nonzero class in $K_{\operatorname{Cliff}(C),2}$ (appendix to *On the projective normality of complete linear series*, Invent. Math. 83).
- **1986–88.** Schreyer verifies the conjecture for $\operatorname{Cliff}(C)\le 3$ and, by computer algebra, for all curves of genus $\le 8$ and low-gonality strata; Voisin settles the tetragonal case ($\operatorname{Cliff}=2$, J. Reine Angew. Math. 387, 1988).
- **1998.** Hirschowitz–Ramanan (Ann. Sci. ÉNS 31) show that for odd genus $g = 2k+1$, the "syzygy divisor" $\{K_{k-1,2}\neq0\}$ and the gonality divisor $\mathcal{M}^1_{g,k+1}$ have proportional classes on $\overline{\mathcal{M}}_g$; hence the generic statement implies the conjecture for *every* curve of maximal gonality in odd genus.
- **2002, 2005.** Voisin proves the generic case by degenerating to curves on K3 surfaces and computing on Hilbert schemes of points: even genus (J. Eur. Math. Soc. 4, 2002) and odd genus (Compositio Math. 141, 2005). Combined with Hirschowitz–Ramanan this yields Green's conjecture for all curves of maximal gonality.
- **2011.** Aprodu–Farkas extend to arbitrary K3 surfaces (Compositio Math. 147), proving Green's conjecture for every smooth curve on a K3 surface — a case where the Clifford index can drop below the generic value.
- **2019–2022.** Aprodu–Farkas–Papadima–Raicu–Weyman (Invent. Math. 218) give a proof of the generic conjecture via **Koszul modules** and the geometry of K3 carpets, valid in characteristic $0$ and characteristic $p \ge (g-2)/2$; Raicu–Sam (Compositio Math. 158, 2022) refine the bigraded machinery and sharpen the characteristic hypotheses.
- **2015.** Ein–Lazarsfeld prove the Green–Lazarsfeld gonality conjecture for line bundles of sufficiently large degree (Publ. IHÉS 122), by a vector-bundle/secant-sheaf argument that is strikingly short compared with the canonical case.

| Every curve of maximal gonality $\lceil (g+3)/2 \rceil$ | Proven | Voisin + Hirschowitz–Ramanan |
| General $d$-gonal curve, $g \ge 3d-7$ (and refinements to all $d < \lfloor g/2\rfloor+1$) | Proven | Teixidor i Bigas 2002; Aprodu 2005 |
| Any smooth curve lying on a K3 surface | Proven | Aprodu–Farkas 2011 |
| Generic curve in char. $0$ and char. $p \ge (g-2)/2$ | Proven via Koszul modules | AFPRW 2019; Raicu–Sam 2022 |
| Large-degree line bundles (gonality variant) | Proven | Ein–Lazarsfeld 2015 |

Statements are for algebraically closed fields; in small positive characteristic the vanishing is genuinely delicate and the characteristic bounds above are not known to be removable *(frontier — verify)*.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No cohomological vanishing theorem computes $K_{p,2}$ directly.** Kodaira–Nakano-type vanishing controls $H^i$ of line bundles, but syzygy groups are cohomology of a *complex* of tensor products; their vanishing is not implied by any positivity of $K_C$.
- **Semicontinuity runs the wrong way.** Betti numbers are upper semicontinuous in families, so a generic vanishing does not descend to a special curve of the *same* Clifford index. Every extension from the general curve to an arbitrary curve requires a separate degeneration argument in which the Clifford index is controlled — and $\operatorname{Cliff}$ is not itself semicontinuous in a usable direction.
- **Voisin's method needs a K3.** The proof identifies $K_{p,2}$ with cohomology of a tautological bundle on $C^{[k]}$ or on $S^{[k]}$ for a K3 surface $S$, and requires that $C$ deform inside $S$ with the right Picard lattice. Curves whose Clifford index is not computed by a pencil, or that admit no K3 embedding with the correct Brill–Noether behaviour, are outside its reach.
- **Failure of the naive dual.** The nonvanishing half is produced by an explicit geometric cycle (a scroll swept by the pencil). There is no known "converse construction" that turns a nonzero syzygy class into a low-degree pencil — the map from syzygies to linear series is not injective in any known sense.
- **Exceptional curves.** Curves with $\operatorname{Cliff}(C) = \operatorname{gon}(C)-3$ (Eisenbud–Lange–Martens–Schreyer examples, plane curves, some curves on rational surfaces) break the gonality/Clifford dictionary that most inductive proofs use as a bookkeeping device.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: the vanishing $K_{p,2}=0$ for $p < \operatorname{Cliff}(C)$ whenever $C$ is general in moduli, general in a gonality stratum, lies on a K3, or has $\operatorname{Cliff}(C)\le 3$. Open: an arbitrary curve whose Clifford index is *smaller* than generic and which is not realized on a K3 surface — for instance curves with several distinct minimal pencils, curves on surfaces of general type, or curves in high-codimension Brill–Noether strata. The precise missing step is a mechanism that converts a hypothetical nonzero class in $K_{p,2}(C,K_C)$ with $p<\operatorname{Cliff}(C)$ into a line bundle $L$ with $\operatorname{Cliff}(L)\le p$ — a "syzygy $\Rightarrow$ special linear series" implication valid without genericity. Equivalently: prove that the locus $\{[C] : K_{p,2}\neq 0\}$ in $\mathcal{M}_g$ is *set-theoretically* the Clifford-index stratum, not merely divisorially proportional to it (Hirschowitz–Ramanan gives the divisor-class statement only in the top case).

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Koszul-module school** (Aprodu, Farkas, Raicu, Sam, Weyman; Berlin, Bucharest, Notre Dame, UCSD): representation-theoretic reformulation via the Weyman module $W(V,K)$ and resonance varieties; ongoing effort to lower the characteristic bound below $(g-2)/2$ and to handle non-generic curves by deforming K3 carpets *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Ein–Lazarsfeld asymptotic syzygies** (Chicago, Stony Brook): the secant-sheaf technique that settled the gonality conjecture is being pushed toward canonical (non-asymptotic) bundles and toward higher-dimensional analogues (syzygies of $\mathbf{P}^n$-embeddings and of abelian varieties).
- **Prym–Green and Prym-canonical variants** (Farkas–Kemeny, Chiodo–Eisenbud–Farkas–Schreyer): the Prym–Green conjecture is known to *fail* in genera $8$ and $16$, a cautionary datum for the general shape of syzygy conjectures.
- **Computational verification** (Schreyer, Bopp; Saarbrücken): Macaulay2/`Singular` random-curve experiments over finite fields, including positive-characteristic tests of Green-type statements, extending machine confirmation into genus $\ge 15$ *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Tropical and Berkovich approaches** (Jensen, Payne, Cools, Draisma): tropical Brill–Noether theory has reproved Green's conjecture for general curves of even genus and is being adapted to gonality strata.

## 8. Future Work

- Prove the "syzygy $\Rightarrow$ pencil" implication in general, perhaps by showing that the scheme structure on the syzygy locus in $\mathcal{M}_g$ is reduced and equal to the gonality stratum.
- Remove the characteristic hypotheses in AFPRW/Raicu–Sam by finding a characteristic-free resolution of the K3 carpet, or produce a genuine counterexample in small characteristic — either outcome is decisive.
- Extend Aprodu–Farkas beyond K3s: prove Green's conjecture for curves on Enriques, abelian, or general-type surfaces where the Clifford index is computed by the ambient geometry.
- Understand the exceptional Eisenbud–Lange–Martens–Schreyer curves, where $\operatorname{Cliff} = \operatorname{gon}-3$, as a test family for any proposed general argument.
- Formulate and test a higher-dimensional Green conjecture relating $K_{p,2}$ of a canonically embedded variety to a Clifford-type invariant.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Genus $5$: the conjecture in its smallest nontrivial form.** Here $g=5$, the canonical model is a curve of degree $2g-2 = 8$ in $\mathbf{P}^4$, and $\lfloor (g-1)/2 \rfloor = 2$, so $\operatorname{Cliff}(C) \in \{1,2\}$ (excluding hyperelliptic). The Betti table has two rows with duality $b_{p,1} = b_{3-p,2}$, and Green's prediction is exactly:

$$b_{1,2} = 0 \iff \operatorname{Cliff}(C) = 2 \iff C \text{ is not trigonal.}$$

*Count the quadrics.* From $0 \to I_C(2) \to \mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^4}(2) \to \mathcal{O}_C(2K_C) \to 0$ and Riemann–Roch, $h^0(2K_C) = \deg(2K_C) - g + 1 = 8 - 5 + 1 \cdot$ — precisely $h^0(2K_C) = 3g-3 = 12$ — while $h^0(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{P}^4}(2)) = \binom{6}{2} = 15$. Since canonical curves are projectively normal (Noether), $\dim I_2 = 15 - 12 = 3$: every genus-5 canonical curve lies on exactly three quadrics.

*Non-trigonal case ($\operatorname{Cliff}=2$).* The three quadrics meet in a complete intersection of degree $2^3 = 8$ and arithmetic genus $1 + \tfrac12 \cdot 8 \cdot (2+2+2-5) = 5$, matching $C$; so $C$ is the complete intersection $Q_1 \cap Q_2 \cap Q_3$ and the minimal free resolution is the Koszul complex

$$0 \to S(-6) \to S(-4)^3 \to S(-2)^3 \to S \to S/I_C \to 0,$$

giving $b_{1,1}=3$, $b_{2,2}=3$, $b_{3,3}=1$ and $b_{1,2} = 0$. The first nonvanishing $b_{p,2}$ occurs at $p = 2 = \operatorname{Cliff}(C)$. ✓

*Trigonal case ($\operatorname{Cliff}=1$).* Let $g^1_3 = |D|$ with $\deg D = 3$, $h^0(D)=2$; then $\operatorname{Cliff}(D) = 3 - 2 = 1$. The planes $\langle D_t \rangle \subset \mathbf{P}^4$ spanned by the divisors of the pencil sweep out a rational normal scroll $X$ of degree $3$ and dimension $2$. Every quadric through $C$ contains $X$ (a quadric meets each plane $\langle D_t\rangle$ in a conic containing $3$ collinear-free points of $C$ plus, by degree count, the whole plane), and $\dim I_2(X) = 3$. Hence $I_2(C) = I_2(X)$ and the quadrics cut out the surface $X$, not $C$: a cubic generator is required, so $b_{1,2} \ne 0$. The first nonvanishing row-2 Betti number sits at $p = 1 = \operatorname{Cliff}(C)$. ✓

Both directions are visible here; the difficulty of the general conjecture is that for $g \gg 0$ there is no analogue of the "count the quadrics" dimension argument, and the scroll construction only ever *produces* syzygies, never excludes them.
