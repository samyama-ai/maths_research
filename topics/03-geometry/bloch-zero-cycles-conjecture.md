# Bloch's Conjecture on Zero Cycles

> **Topic:** Algebraic & Differential Geometry · **ID:** `03-geometry/bloch-zero-cycles-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $S$ be a smooth complex projective surface with geometric genus $p_g(S) = \dim_{\mathbb C} H^0(S, \Omega^2_S) = 0$. Bloch's conjecture asserts that the Albanese map on degree-zero zero-cycles

$$\mathrm{alb}_S \colon A_0(S) := \mathrm{CH}_0(S)_{\deg = 0} \longrightarrow \mathrm{Alb}(S)$$

is an isomorphism. Equivalently, the **Albanese kernel** $T(S) := \ker(\mathrm{alb}_S)$ vanishes. When $q(S) = h^{1,0} = 0$ as well, the claim is the sharp statement $\mathrm{CH}_0(S) \cong \mathbb{Z}$: every degree-zero zero-cycle is rationally equivalent to $0$.

A proof must produce, for each such $S$, an explicit rational-equivalence trivialization (or a motivic argument implying it); a disproof requires one surface with $p_g = 0$ and $T(S) \neq 0$ — necessarily an infinite-dimensional group, by the structure theory below.

**Generalized (Bloch–Beilinson) form.** For $X$ smooth projective of dimension $n$: if $H^{i,0}(X) = 0$ for all $i > k$, then $\mathrm{CH}_0(X)_{\mathbb Q}$ is supported on a closed subvariety of dimension $\le k$. Also standard is the *correspondence form*: if $\Gamma \in \mathrm{CH}^n(X \times X)$ satisfies $\Gamma^* = 0$ on $H^{i,0}(X)$ for all $i > 0$, then $\Gamma^* = 0$ on $A_0(X)$.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Chow groups.** $\mathrm{CH}_0(X) = Z_0(X)/\!\sim_{\mathrm{rat}}$, where $Z_0(X)$ is the free abelian group on closed points and $\sim_{\mathrm{rat}}$ is generated by $\mathrm{div}(f)$ for $f \in \mathbb{C}(C)^\times$ on curves $C \subset X$.

**Albanese.** $\mathrm{Alb}(S) = H^0(S,\Omega^1_S)^\vee / H_1(S,\mathbb{Z})$, and $\mathrm{alb}_S(\sum n_i p_i) = \sum n_i \int_{p_0}^{p_i}$, well defined modulo periods. It is the universal map to an abelian variety, so $T(S)$ carries no abelian-variety quotient.

**Mumford's theorem (1968).** If $p_g(S) > 0$ then $A_0(S)$ is *infinite dimensional*: for every $m$, the map $S^{(m)} \to \mathrm{CH}_0(S)$ has all fibres of dimension $< m$ (no $m$-dimensional family of rationally equivalent cycles). Hence $T(S) \ne 0$ whenever $p_g > 0$. Bloch's conjecture is the exact converse for surfaces.

**Roitman (1980).** $\mathrm{alb}_S$ is an isomorphism on torsion: $T(S)$ is torsion-free and uniquely divisible. So $T(S)$ is a $\mathbb{Q}$-vector space, and Bloch's conjecture is insensitive to $\mathbb{Q}$-coefficients.

**Bloch–Srinivas decomposition of the diagonal.** If $\mathrm{CH}_0(X)_{\mathbb Q}$ is supported on a surface (in particular if $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}=0$), then in $\mathrm{CH}^n(X\times X)_{\mathbb Q}$

$$N\cdot\Delta_X = \Gamma_1 + \Gamma_2, \qquad \mathrm{supp}\,\Gamma_1 \subset D \times X,\ \ \mathrm{supp}\,\Gamma_2 \subset X \times W,$$

with $D \subsetneq X$ a divisor and $\dim W \le k$. For a surface with $T(S)_{\mathbb Q}=0$ this forces $H^{2,0}=0$, giving the (easy) converse direction and showing Bloch's conjecture is equivalent to a diagonal decomposition.

**Motivic reformulation.** Murre's Chow–Künneth decomposition $h(S) = \bigoplus_{i=0}^{4} h^i(S)$ exists for surfaces; the motive splits further as
$$h(S) = \mathbf{1} \oplus h^1(S) \oplus (\mathrm{NS}(S)\otimes \mathbb{L}) \oplus t_2(S) \oplus h^3(S) \oplus \mathbb{L}^2,$$
where $t_2(S)$ is the **transcendental motive**, with $\mathrm{CH}_0(t_2(S)) = T(S)_{\mathbb Q}$ and $H^*(t_2(S)) = H^2_{\mathrm{tr}}(S)$. Bloch's conjecture for $p_g=q=0$ says $t_2(S) = 0$.

**Kimura finite-dimensionality.** A motive $M$ is finite dimensional if $M = M_+ \oplus M_-$ with $\wedge^n M_+ = 0$, $\mathrm{Sym}^m M_- = 0$ for some $n,m$. Kimura's theorem: a finite-dimensional motive with trivial homological realization is itself zero. Since $H^2_{\mathrm{tr}}(S)$ is $\mathbb{Q}$-Hodge-theoretically trivial when $p_g=0$ and $b_2 = \rho$, finite-dimensionality of $h(S)$ *implies* Bloch's conjecture for $S$. Finite-dimensionality is known for motives generated by curves (products, quotients, abelian varieties) and is itself conjectural in general.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1968** — Mumford proves infinite-dimensionality of $A_0$ when $p_g>0$, killing the pre-1968 expectation that $\mathrm{CH}_0$ is always "finite dimensional".
- **1976** — Bloch, Kas and Lieberman settle the conjecture for all surfaces with $p_g=0$ of Kodaira dimension $\kappa \le 1$, using the Enriques–Kodaira classification.
- **1979–1980** — Bloch formulates the conjecture in its general and motivic forms in *Lectures on Algebraic Cycles*; Roitman proves the torsion theorem.
- **1979–1985** — Inose–Mizukami and Barlow prove it for specific general-type families with $p_g=0$ ($K^2 = 1,\dots$), by exhibiting dominating rational or quotient structures.
- **1983** — Bloch–Srinivas make the diagonal-decomposition technique the standard engine.
- **1992** — Voisin proves it for quotients of hypersurfaces by automorphism groups (Godeaux-type surfaces), introducing the "spread over the moduli of the family" method.
- **2003–2005** — Guletskii–Pedrini reduce the classical Godeaux surface to finite-dimensionality; Kimura's finite-dimensionality theory gives a uniform criterion.
- **2012–2016** — Voisin proves Bloch for Catanese and Barlow surfaces (2014) and the K3 symplectic-involution case (2012, independently Huybrechts); Pedrini–Weibel and Bauer et al. extend to many product-quotient and Burniat/Inoue families.

| Classical Godeaux surface ($K^2=1$, $\pi_1=\mathbb{Z}/5$) | **Proved** | Guletskii–Pedrini 2003 |
| Barlow surface ($K^2=1$, $p_g=q=0$, simply connected) and Catanese surfaces | **Proved** | Barlow 1985; Voisin 2014 |
| Inoue surfaces with $K^2=7$; generalized Burniat-type surfaces | **Proved** | Bauer 2014; Bauer–Gleissner and Pedrini–Weibel 2016 |
| Determinantal Barlow surfaces, several $K^2=2,3$ families | **Proved** | Pedrini–Weibel 2016 |
| Symplectic involutions (and finite symplectic automorphisms) on K3s acting trivially on $\mathrm{CH}_0$ | **Proved** | Voisin 2012; Huybrechts 2012 |

| Fake projective planes, general surfaces with $p_g=0$, $K^2=2,\dots,9$ of unknown motive type | **Open** | — |

The residual open cases are all of general type with $p_g = q = 0$ and $1 \le K^2 \le 9$, precisely those whose motives are not known to be generated by curves.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **No finiteness on the Chow side.** $\mathrm{CH}_0$ is a huge, non-representable functor: $T(S)$ is a uniquely divisible group with no algebraic structure, so no topology, no dimension count, and no compactness argument applies. Cohomological vanishing gives no *a priori* handle.
- **The conjecture is the hard direction of Mumford.** Mumford's method (pull back a holomorphic $2$-form along $S^{(m)}$ and detect nonvanishing) produces obstructions; it has no converse mechanism that manufactures rational equivalences from the absence of $2$-forms.
- **Finite-dimensionality is itself open.** The Kimura route is complete *modulo* proving $h(S)$ finite dimensional, and finite-dimensionality is known only for motives in the tensor subcategory generated by curves (abelian varieties). For a simply connected general-type surface with $p_g=0$ there is no curve correspondence to exploit.
- **Diagonal decomposition needs input, not output.** Bloch–Srinivas converts $T(S)=0$ into a decomposition of $\Delta_S$; constructing that decomposition directly requires knowing enough cycles on $S \times S$, and $\mathrm{CH}^2(S\times S)$ is at least as inaccessible as $\mathrm{CH}_0(S)$.
- **Spread/deformation arguments need moduli.** Voisin's method requires a positive-dimensional family in which the cycle-theoretic statement can be varied and a Hodge-theoretic vanishing propagated. Rigid or nearly rigid surfaces (fake projective planes are rigid) give the method nothing to move.
- **No Bloch–Beilinson filtration.** The conjectural filtration $F^\bullet \mathrm{CH}^*$ with $\mathrm{Gr}^i$ controlled by Ext groups in mixed motives — which would make Bloch's conjecture formal — has not been constructed.

## 6. The Gap

Everything proven falls into two buckets: (a) surfaces whose motive is provably generated by curves, so Kimura applies; (b) surfaces sitting in families with enough moduli or enough group actions for Voisin's spread argument. The general statement covers surfaces in neither bucket — rigid, simply connected, general-type surfaces with $p_g=q=0$, e.g. fake projective planes and several $K^2 \le 4$ families.

The exact missing step: **derive the vanishing of the transcendental motive $t_2(S)$ from the Hodge-theoretic vanishing $H^{2,0}(S)=0$, without a curve model and without a deformation.** Concretely, one must either (i) prove Kimura finite-dimensionality for an arbitrary surface, or (ii) construct a Bloch–Beilinson filtration whose $\mathrm{Gr}^2_F \mathrm{CH}_0$ is governed by $H^{2,0}$, or (iii) find a direct geometric construction producing, for each pair $p,q \in S$, an explicit chain of rational equivalences. No current technique does any of the three.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Finite-dimensionality attacks.** Extending Kimura–O'Sullivan beyond curve-generated motives; work at Regensburg, Duisburg-Essen and Milan on Murre's conjectures for product-quotient surfaces.
- **Voisin school (Jussieu/Collège de France).** Spread, decomposition of the small diagonal, and the "generalized Hodge $\Leftrightarrow$ generalized Bloch" programme for complete intersections and hyper-Kähler manifolds; the multiplicative Chow–Künneth (MCK) framework of Shen–Vial.
- **Automorphism actions.** A large body of work (Laterveer, Vial, Bülles, Huybrechts) proving the correspondence form for finite-order symplectic/anti-symplectic automorphisms of K3s, Calabi–Yau and hyper-Kähler varieties; derived-category methods (Fourier–Mukai kernels acting on $\mathrm{CH}_0$) are gaining traction.
- **Fake projective planes.** Attempts using the arithmetic (Prasad–Yeung) classification and $p$-adic uniformization to build correspondences. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Motivic homotopy / cdh methods.** Efforts to compute $\mathrm{CH}_0$ of $p_g=0$ surfaces via Suslin homology and $K$-theoretic descent. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Computational/database work.** Systematic verification across the classification of surfaces with $p_g=0$ and $K^2 = 1,\dots,9$ (Bauer–Catanese–Pignatelli census), narrowing the open list family by family.

## 8. Future Work

1. **Prove finite-dimensionality for surfaces of general type with $p_g=0$** — the single step that would settle the conjecture uniformly (Kimura's stated goal).
2. **Construct a Bloch–Beilinson filtration** on $\mathrm{CH}^*(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}$ with the predicted functoriality (Beilinson, Murre, Jannsen), which would make the conjecture a formal consequence.
3. **Complete the family-by-family programme** for the residual $p_g=q=0$ general-type surfaces, exploiting bicanonical maps and torsion in $\pi_1$.
4. **Rigid cases** — develop a substitute for spread arguments where moduli are $0$-dimensional; possibly via arithmetic specialization or $\ell$-adic/crystalline correspondences.
5. **Derived-category reformulation** — express $t_2(S)=0$ as a statement about $D^b(S)$ (existence of a full exceptional collection or a quasi-phantom killing $\mathrm{CH}_0$); several surfaces with $p_g=0$ do admit quasi-phantom subcategories, tying Bloch's conjecture to $K_0$ computations.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**The Godeaux surface $S = X_5 / \mu_5$.** Take the Fermat quintic
$$X = \{x_0^5 + x_1^5 + x_2^5 + x_3^5 = 0\} \subset \mathbb{P}^3,$$
with $g \in \mu_5$ acting by $g\cdot(x_0 : x_1 : x_2 : x_3) = (x_0 : \zeta x_1 : \zeta^2 x_2 : \zeta^3 x_3)$, $\zeta = e^{2\pi i/5}$.

*Step 1 — the action is free.* A fixed point of $g$ on $\mathbb{P}^3$ has all but one coordinate zero (the weights $0,1,2,3$ are pairwise distinct mod $5$), i.e. is a coordinate point $e_i$. But $e_i \notin X$ since $1 \neq 0$. So $S = X/\mu_5$ is a smooth surface and $X \to S$ is étale of degree $5$.

*Step 2 — invariants of $X$.* For a smooth quintic surface, $K_X = \mathcal{O}_X(1)$, so $K_X^2 = 5$, $p_g(X) = h^0(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^3}(1)) = 4$, $q(X) = 0$, $\chi(\mathcal{O}_X) = 5$.

*Step 3 — invariants of $S$.* Étale degree $5$ gives $\chi(\mathcal{O}_S) = \chi(\mathcal{O}_X)/5 = 1$ and $K_S^2 = K_X^2/5 = 1$. Since $q(S) \le q(X) = 0$, we get $q(S)=0$ and $p_g(S) = \chi(\mathcal{O}_S) - 1 + q(S) = 0$.

*Step 4 — check $p_g(S)=0$ by characters.* Under the residue isomorphism $H^0(X,K_X) \cong \{\,\mathrm{Res}\,(\ell \,\Omega / f) : \ell \in H^0(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^3}(1))\,\}$ with $\Omega = \sum_i (-1)^i x_i\, dx_0 \wedge \cdots \widehat{dx_i} \cdots \wedge dx_3$: the quintic $f$ is $\mu_5$-invariant (each $x_i^5$ has weight $5i \equiv 0$), and $\Omega$ has weight $0+1+2+3 = 6 \equiv 1 \pmod 5$. So $\ell\,\Omega/f$ is invariant iff $w(\ell) \equiv -1 \equiv 4$. The available weights are $\{0,1,2,3\}$, none equal to $4$; hence
$$H^0(S, K_S) = H^0(X, K_X)^{\mu_5} = 0.$$

*Step 5 — what Bloch predicts and what is known.* With $p_g = q = 0$, Bloch's conjecture predicts $\mathrm{CH}_0(S) = \mathbb{Z}$. By Mumford, the cover $X$ has $p_g = 4 > 0$, so $A_0(X)$ is infinite dimensional — the invariant part $A_0(X)^{\mu_5} \otimes \mathbb{Q} \cong A_0(S)_{\mathbb Q}$ must nevertheless vanish. Set $\Gamma = \frac{1}{5}\sum_{i=0}^{4} \Gamma_{g^i} \in \mathrm{CH}^2(X\times X)_{\mathbb{Q}}$, the projector onto invariants. Step 4 says $\Gamma^* = 0$ on $H^{2,0}(X)$, so the correspondence form of Bloch's conjecture predicts $\Gamma^* = 0$ on $A_0(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}$, i.e. $A_0(S)_{\mathbb Q} = 0$; Roitman's torsion theorem then upgrades this to $A_0(S) = 0$ integrally.

*Step 6 — the theorem.* Voisin (1992) proves exactly this by spreading the cycle over the family of $\mu_5$-invariant quintics: the vanishing of the invariant part of $H^{2,0}$ propagates, via the infinitesimal variation of Hodge structure of the family, to the vanishing of $\Gamma^*$ on $A_0$. So $\mathrm{CH}_0(S) = \mathbb{Z}$ for this Godeaux surface — a confirmed instance. The construction also shows why the general case resists: the argument consumed both a group action and a positive-dimensional family of deformations, and a rigid simply connected surface with $p_g=0$ supplies neither.
