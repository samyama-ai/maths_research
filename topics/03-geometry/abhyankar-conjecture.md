# Abhyankar Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebraic & Differential Geometry · **ID:** `03-geometry/abhyankar-conjecture` · **Status:** solved-recently

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p > 0$, let $X$ be a smooth connected projective curve over $k$ of genus $g$, let $S \subset X$ be a finite set of $r \geq 1$ closed points, and set $U = X \setminus S$.

For a finite group $G$, write $p(G)$ for the **quasi-$p$ part**: the subgroup generated by all Sylow $p$-subgroups of $G$. It is normal, and $G/p(G)$ is the largest quotient of $G$ of order prime to $p$. Call $G$ **quasi-$p$** if $G = p(G)$.

**Abhyankar's Conjecture (1957).** $G$ is a quotient of the étale fundamental group $\pi_1(U)$ — equivalently, $G$ occurs as the Galois group of a connected Galois covering of $X$ étale over $U$ — **if and only if** $G/p(G)$ can be generated by $2g + r - 1$ elements.

In the base case $X = \mathbb{P}^1$, $S = \{\infty\}$, so $U = \mathbb{A}^1_k$ and $2g+r-1 = 0$: the assertion is that the finite quotients of $\pi_1(\mathbb{A}^1_k)$ are exactly the quasi-$p$ groups.

**Status.** The conjecture is a theorem. Raynaud (1994) proved the affine-line case; Harbater (1994) deduced the general case. The page is retained because the surrounding program — determining $\pi_1(U)$ as a profinite group, and Abhyankar's **Inertia Conjecture** refining the theorem with ramification data — remains open.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Étale fundamental group.** For $U$ a connected scheme with geometric point $\bar u$, $\pi_1(U,\bar u)$ is the profinite automorphism group of the fibre functor on finite étale covers (Grothendieck, SGA 1). Its finite quotients classify connected Galois covers.

**Characteristic $0$ / tame model.** If $\operatorname{char} k = 0$, Riemann existence plus GAGA gives
$$\pi_1(U) \;\cong\; \widehat{\Big\langle a_1,b_1,\dots,a_g,b_g,\ c_1,\dots,c_r \ \Big|\ \prod_{i=1}^g [a_i,b_i]\prod_{j=1}^r c_j = 1 \Big\rangle},$$
the profinite completion of a group on $2g+r$ generators with one relation; for $r \geq 1$ it is free profinite of rank $2g+r-1$.

**Tame quotient in characteristic $p$.** Grothendieck's specialization theorem gives a surjection from the char-$0$ group onto $\pi_1^{\mathrm{t}}(U)$, the tame fundamental group (covers with ramification indices prime to $p$ at $S$). Hence every tame quotient of $\pi_1(U)$ is generated by $2g+r-1$ elements.

**Necessity.** If $G$ is a quotient of $\pi_1(U)$, the intermediate cover with group $G/p(G)$ has degree prime to $p$, hence is tame, hence $G/p(G)$ is generated by $2g+r-1$ elements. The content of the conjecture is **sufficiency**.

**Wild ramification.** In char $p$, $\pi_1(\mathbb{A}^1_k)$ is not topologically finitely generated: Artin–Schreier theory gives
$$H^1_{\text{ét}}(\mathbb{A}^1_k,\mathbb{Z}/p) \;\cong\; k[x]/\wp(k[x]), \qquad \wp(f) = f^p - f,$$
an infinite-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_p$-vector space. So $\mathbb{Z}/p$ covers of $\mathbb{A}^1$ exist in unbounded supply, and the prime-to-$p$ generation bound is the only constraint.

**Ramification filtration.** At a point of $S$ the inertia group $I$ has the form $I = P \rtimes C$ with $P$ the wild inertia (a $p$-group, the unique Sylow $p$-subgroup of $I$) and $C$ cyclic of order prime to $p$. Conductor and genus are controlled by Riemann–Hurwitz with the higher ramification filtration $I = I_0 \supseteq I_1 \supseteq \cdots$:
$$2g_Y - 2 \;=\; |G|\,(2g_X - 2) \;+\; \sum_{y} d_y, \qquad d_y = \sum_{i \ge 0}\big(|I_i(y)| - 1\big).$$

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1957.** Abhyankar, *Coverings of algebraic curves* (Amer. J. Math. 79), formulates the conjecture after computing Galois groups of explicit trinomial equations in char $p$ and observing that the char-$0$ generation count survives only after quotienting by $p(G)$.
- **1980s.** Serre, Nori, Abhyankar and others verify scattered families. Harbater develops **formal/rigid patching** (*Galois coverings of the arithmetic line*, 1987), the tool that later glues local covers into global ones.
- **1990.** Serre proves the affine-line case for **solvable** quasi-$p$ groups (C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 311), by induction on the derived length using Artin–Schreier and embedding problems.
- **1994 — Raynaud.** *Revêtements de la droite affine en caractéristique $p>0$ et conjecture d'Abhyankar* (Invent. Math. 116, 425–462): every quasi-$p$ group is a quotient of $\pi_1(\mathbb{A}^1_k)$. Method: semistable reduction of covers of $\mathbb{P}^1$ over a mixed-characteristic base, degeneration to a tree of components, and induction on $|G|$ via the "new part / old part" dichotomy for the special fibre.
- **1994 — Harbater.** *Abhyankar's conjecture on Galois groups over curves* (Invent. Math. 117, 1–25): the general $(g,r)$ case. Method: patch a Raynaud cover (supplying $p(G)$) with a tame cover of $U$ (supplying $G/p(G)$) along a formal annulus, plus induction using "adding branch points".
- **1995 — Pop.** *Étale Galois covers of affine smooth curves* (Invent. Math. 120, 555–578): the geometric case of Shafarevich's conjecture, giving strong freeness/embedding-problem statements beyond finite quotients.
- **2001.** Abhyankar (Bull. AMS 38) states the **Inertia Conjecture**, refining the theorem by prescribing the inertia group at $\infty$. Still open.
- **2018.** Harbater–Obus–Pries–Stevenson, *Abhyankar's conjectures in Galois theory: current status and future directions* (Bull. AMS 55, 239–287) — the reference survey.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

Chronologically, before the full theorem, and for the still-open refinements:

- **Solvable quasi-$p$ $G$, $U = \mathbb{A}^1$:** Serre, 1990.
- **$G$ a $p$-group:** classical, via iterated Artin–Schreier extensions; every $p$-group is a quotient of $\pi_1(\mathbb{A}^1)$.
- **$G = A_p$, $\mathrm{PSL}_2(p)$, $\mathrm{PSL}_2(2^n)$, symplectic and unitary groups over $\mathbb{F}_p$:** explicit trinomial models $Y^n - a X^s Y^t + 1$ computed by Abhyankar (1957 and *Nice equations for nice groups*, Israel J. Math. 88, 1995).
- **Full theorem:** $U = \mathbb{A}^1$ (Raynaud); all $g \geq 0$, $r \geq 1$ (Harbater). No restriction on $p$, $|G|$, or $g$.
- **Beyond finite quotients:** $\pi_1(U)$ for $U$ affine over algebraically closed $k$ is projective (Serre) and every finite embedding problem with $|k|$-many solutions is solvable (Pop 1995; Harbater 1995), so $\pi_1(U)$ is quasi-free of rank $\operatorname{card}(k)$.
- **Inertia Conjecture, verified cases:** $G$ a $p$-group; $G = A_{p}$ and $A_{p+1}$ for various $p$; $\mathrm{PSL}_2(p)$ under conditions on the Sylow normalizer; families obtained by Bouw–Pries rigidity/reduction arguments and by Kumar's wild-ramification-killing technique.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

The historical obstacles, all of which the 1994 proofs had to route around:

- **Non-finite generation.** $\pi_1(\mathbb{A}^1_k)$ has infinitely many $\mathbb{Z}/p$-quotients; no presentation is available, so one cannot argue by generators and relations as in char $0$.
- **No Riemann existence theorem.** Topological/transcendental construction of covers is unavailable; every cover must be built algebraically or by degeneration.
- **Obstructed lifting.** A wildly ramified cover in char $p$ generally does not lift to characteristic $0$ (the local lifting obstruction, cyclic-group case resolved only by the Oort conjecture, proved 2014). So one cannot simply reduce char-$0$ covers.
- **Semistable reduction is delicate.** Raynaud's argument requires controlling the special fibre of the stable model of a $G$-cover, where components can carry inseparable or non-reduced behaviour; the combinatorics of "new" components carrying $p$-Sylow subgroups is the technical heart.
- **Cohomological invariants are blind.** $H^1(\mathbb{A}^1,\mathbb{Z}/p)$ being infinite-dimensional means étale cohomology gives no finiteness leverage; deformation-theoretic dimension counts replace it.
- **For the open refinements:** ramification breaks (upper-numbering jumps) are not deformation-invariant in a usable way, so patching, which is indifferent to fine ramification data, cannot by itself prescribe the inertia group.

## 6. The Gap

The gap in the original conjecture is closed. What remains:

1. **The Inertia Conjecture (open).** Let $G$ be quasi-$p$ with Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$. Abhyankar conjectures that a subgroup $I \leq G$ occurs as the inertia group at $\infty$ for some $G$-Galois cover of $\mathbb{P}^1$ étale over $\mathbb{A}^1$ **iff** $I = P' \rtimes C$ with $P'$ a Sylow $p$-subgroup of $G$, $C$ cyclic of prime-to-$p$ order, and the conjugates of $P'$ under $I$ generating $G$. Necessity is easy; sufficiency is unknown even for $G = A_p$ in general.
2. **The Purely Wild Inertia Conjecture (open).** The special case $C = 1$: every Sylow $p$-subgroup of a quasi-$p$ group $G$ occurs as inertia group at $\infty$.
3. **Structure of $\pi_1(U)$ (open).** Quasi-freeness plus projectivity does not determine $\pi_1(U)$; it is not known whether $\pi_1(U)$ depends on $(g,r)$ and on $U$ itself beyond the invariant $\operatorname{card}(k)$, and Harbater–Stevenson's isomorphism question for $\pi_1$ of two affine curves of different genus is unresolved.

Crossing (1) requires a construction that simultaneously realizes a prescribed group **and** a prescribed higher-ramification filtration — patching gives the first, deformation of Artin–Schreier–Witt data gives the second, and no method gives both.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Inertia Conjecture.** Work of Das and Kumar (ISI Bangalore) on alternating and symmetric group covers, extending the "killing wild ramification" degeneration technique; new families for $A_{p+\ell}$ *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Rigidity and reduction.** Bouw–Pries-style arguments that reduce char-$0$ rigid triples modulo $p$ to control both the group and the inertia; Colorado State (Pries) and collaborators.
- **Lifting problems.** Obus, Wewers, Pop and collaborators on the local lifting problem beyond cyclic groups (post-Oort), which feeds directly into inertia control.
- **Fundamental groups of higher-dimensional and arithmetic analogues.** Abhyankar's local conjecture for $\pi_1$ of punctured spectra of complete local rings; Harbater–Stevenson quasi-freeness in dimension two.
- **Anabelian direction.** Whether $\pi_1(U)$ recovers $U$; Tamagawa's and Saïdi's results on the anabelian geometry of curves in char $p$ constrain but do not settle the isomorphism question.

## 8. Future Work

- Develop a deformation theory of $G$-covers that tracks the **conductor/ramification filtration** as an explicit parameter, so that patching can be performed within a fixed inertia stratum.
- Settle the Purely Wild Inertia Conjecture for simple quasi-$p$ groups of Lie type in characteristic $p$, where Sylow subgroups are unipotent radicals and explicit models exist.
- Extend Raynaud's semistable-reduction machine to families over higher-dimensional bases, aiming at Abhyankar-type statements for $\pi_1$ of affine varieties of dimension $\geq 2$.
- Compute the full ramification data of Abhyankar's trinomial covers systematically, to test the Inertia Conjecture at scale computationally.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**Setting.** $k = \overline{\mathbb{F}_p}$, $p = 5$, $U = \mathbb{A}^1_k = \operatorname{Spec} k[x]$, so $g = 0$, $r = 1$, and the generation bound is $2g + r - 1 = 0$. The criterion reads: $G$ is a quotient of $\pi_1(\mathbb{A}^1)$ iff $G/p(G) = 1$, i.e. $G$ is quasi-$5$.

**(a) $G = \mathbb{Z}/5$ is realized.** Take the Artin–Schreier curve
$$Y: \quad y^5 - y = x .$$
The map $Y \to \mathbb{A}^1$, $(x,y)\mapsto x$, is a $\mathbb{Z}/5$-torsor: the deck action is $y \mapsto y + c$, $c \in \mathbb{F}_5$, and $\frac{d}{dy}(y^5-y) = -1$ is a unit, so the cover is étale over all of $\mathbb{A}^1$. It is totally (wildly) ramified over $\infty$, with a single jump at $m = 1$, so $d_\infty = \sum_{i\ge0}(|I_i|-1) = (5-1) + (5-1) = 8$. Riemann–Hurwitz on the smooth projective model $\bar Y \to \mathbb{P}^1$:
$$2g_{\bar Y} - 2 = 5\cdot(-2) + 8 = -2 \implies g_{\bar Y} = 0 .$$
So $\bar Y \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ — the cover is the map $y \mapsto y^5 - y$, consistent with $\mathbb{Z}/5 = p(\mathbb{Z}/5)$.

**(b) $G = \mathbb{Z}/5 \times \mathbb{Z}/5$ is realized.** Take the fibre product of $y_1^5 - y_1 = x$ and $y_2^5 - y_2 = x^2$. Since $x$ and $x^2$ are $\mathbb{F}_5$-independent modulo $\wp(k[x]) = \{f^5 - f\}$, the two torsors are independent classes in $H^1(\mathbb{A}^1,\mathbb{Z}/5)$ and the fibre product is connected with group $(\mathbb{Z}/5)^2$. Here $g_{\bar Y} = 2$ by Riemann–Hurwitz with $d_\infty$ computed from jumps $1$ and $2$. This is quasi-$5$ and no generation bound applies — note $(\mathbb{Z}/5)^2$ needs $2$ generators, exceeding $2g+r-1 = 0$, which is exactly the point: the bound constrains only $G/p(G)$.

**(c) A non-example: $G = \mathbb{Z}/3$.** Here $p(G) = 1$ and $G/p(G) = \mathbb{Z}/3$ needs $1 > 0$ generators, so no connected $\mathbb{Z}/3$-cover of $\mathbb{P}^1$ étale over $\mathbb{A}^1$ exists. Directly: such a cover is tame at $\infty$, and Kummer theory gives $y^3 = c$ with $c \in k^\times$, which is split.

**(d) Changing $(g,r)$.** Let $X$ be a supersingular elliptic curve ($g = 1$) and $S = \{O\}$, $r = 1$; the bound is $2\cdot1 + 1 - 1 = 2$. Then $G = \mathbb{Z}/3 \times \mathbb{Z}/3$ **is** a quotient of $\pi_1(U)$ (needs $2$ generators), realized by the tame $3$-torsion isogeny $X \to X$; but $G = (\mathbb{Z}/3)^3$ is **not**, since $G/p(G) = G$ needs $3 > 2$ generators. So $\pi_1^{\mathrm t}(U)$ is free profinite of rank $2$ on the prime-to-$3$ side, while the wild part remains infinite.
