# Orlik-Solomon Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebraic & Differential Geometry · **ID:** `03-geometry/orlik-solomon-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $\mathcal{A} = \{H_1,\dots,H_n\}$ be a central, essential arrangement of hyperplanes in $\mathbb{C}^\ell$, with defining polynomial $Q(\mathcal{A}) = \prod_{i=1}^n \alpha_i$, where $\alpha_i$ is a linear form with $\ker \alpha_i = H_i$. The **Milnor fiber** of the arrangement is

$$F(\mathcal{A}) \;=\; Q^{-1}(1) \subset \mathbb{C}^\ell .$$

**Conjecture (Orlik–Solomon).** The Betti numbers $b_q(F(\mathcal{A})) = \dim_{\mathbb{C}} H^q(F(\mathcal{A});\mathbb{C})$ are determined by the intersection lattice $L(\mathcal{A})$ alone. Equivalently, the characteristic polynomial of the algebraic monodromy $h^*: H^q(F;\mathbb{C}) \to H^q(F;\mathbb{C})$ is a combinatorial invariant.

A proof requires an algorithm producing $b_q(F)$ from $L(\mathcal{A})$ together with a theorem that it is correct for all $\mathcal{A}$. A disproof requires two arrangements $\mathcal{A}_1, \mathcal{A}_2$ with lattice isomorphism $L(\mathcal{A}_1) \cong L(\mathcal{A}_2)$ and $b_q(F(\mathcal{A}_1)) \ne b_q(F(\mathcal{A}_2))$ for some $q$. The first open case is $q=1$, $\ell=3$ (line arrangements in $\mathbb{P}^2$).

Attribution note: the question grew out of Orlik and Solomon's 1980 computation of $H^*(M(\mathcal{A}))$ and is recorded in Orlik–Terao's monograph; it also circulates as "Orlik's conjecture on the Milnor fiber" and, in the generic case, as the Orlik–Randell conjecture. It is distinct from *Terao's conjecture* (combinatoriality of freeness) and from the "Orlik conjecture" on restrictions of free arrangements, disproved by Edelman–Reiner (1993).

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Intersection lattice.** $L(\mathcal{A}) = \{\bigcap_{H \in \mathcal{B}} H : \mathcal{B} \subseteq \mathcal{A}\}$, ordered by reverse inclusion, ranked by codimension, with Möbius function $\mu$. The characteristic polynomial is $\chi(\mathcal{A},t) = \sum_{X \in L} \mu(X)\, t^{\ell - r(X)}$.

**Orlik–Solomon algebra.** Let $E = \Lambda(e_1,\dots,e_n)$ and $\partial e_{i_1}\cdots e_{i_k} = \sum_j (-1)^{j-1} e_{i_1}\cdots \widehat{e_{i_j}} \cdots e_{i_k}$. Let $I$ be the ideal generated by $\partial e_S$ for all dependent $S$. Orlik–Solomon (1980):

$$H^*(M(\mathcal{A});\mathbb{Z}) \;\cong\; A(\mathcal{A}) := E/I, \qquad \sum_q b_q(M) t^q = (-t)^\ell \chi\!\left(\mathcal{A}, -t^{-1}\right),$$

where $M(\mathcal{A}) = \mathbb{C}^\ell \setminus \bigcup H$. So the complement's cohomology **is** combinatorial. The conjecture asks whether this persists for the cyclic cover $F$.

**Milnor fibration.** Since $Q$ is homogeneous of degree $n$, $Q: M(\mathcal{A}) \to \mathbb{C}^*$ is a smooth fiber bundle with fiber $F$ and geometric monodromy $h(x) = e^{2\pi i/n}x$, so $h^n = \mathrm{id}$ and $F \to M$ is an $n$-fold cyclic cover. Writing $U = \mathbb{P}(M)$ for the projectivized (deconed) complement, $M \cong U \times \mathbb{C}^*$ and

$$H^q(F;\mathbb{C}) \;=\; \bigoplus_{k=0}^{n-1} H^q(F;\mathbb{C})_{\lambda^k}, \qquad H^q(F;\mathbb{C})_{\lambda^k} \;\cong\; H^q\!\left(U; \mathcal{L}_k\right), \quad \lambda = e^{2\pi i / n},$$

with $\mathcal{L}_k$ the rank-one local system on $U$ whose monodromy around each hyperplane is $\lambda^k$. The $k=0$ summand is $H^q(U;\mathbb{C})$, which is combinatorial. **The whole difficulty lies in $k \ne 0$.**

**Resonance and characteristic varieties.** The Aomoto complex $(A(\mathcal{A}), a\cdot)$ for $a \in A^1$ has resonance varieties $\mathcal{R}^q_d(\mathcal{A}) = \{a : \dim H^q(A,a\cdot) \ge d\}$ — purely combinatorial. The characteristic varieties $\mathcal{V}^q_d(U) \subseteq \mathrm{Hom}(\pi_1(U),\mathbb{C}^*)$ record local-system jumps. The tangent cone theorem (Cohen–Suciu; Dimca–Papadima–Suciu) gives $TC_1(\mathcal{V}^q_d) = \mathcal{R}^q_d$, but only *components through the origin* are controlled this way, and $\mathcal{L}_k$ for $k \ne 0$ is a torsion point that may sit on a **translated** component.

**Multinets.** A $(k,d)$-multinet on a line arrangement is a partition into $k \ge 3$ classes with multiplicities plus a base locus, arising from a pencil of degree-$d$ curves. Falk–Yuzvinsky: positive-dimensional components of $\mathcal{R}^1_1$ correspond exactly to multinets. Multinets force nontrivial monodromy eigenvalues of order dividing $k$.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1968.** Milnor establishes the fibration theorem for hypersurface singularities.
- **1980.** Orlik–Solomon prove $H^*(M) \cong A(\mathcal{A})$ (*Invent. Math.* 56). The natural follow-up — is the cyclic cover $F$ also combinatorial? — is posed.
- **1992.** Orlik–Terao's *Arrangements of Hyperplanes* records the problem; Esnault–Schechtman–Viehweg prove vanishing for generic local systems, giving the main analytic tool.
- **1995.** Cohen–Suciu compute the monodromy for large families via braid monodromy, producing the first systematic evidence and the first arrangements with $h^*|_{H^1} \ne \mathrm{id}$.
- **2002–2003.** Libgober's divisibility theorem restricts eigenvalue orders to divisors of multiplicities of dense edges. Dimca–Papadima prove the Orlik–Randell conjecture for generic arrangements (*Ann. of Math.* 158).
- **2003.** Cohen–Denham–Suciu find **torsion** in $H_*(F;\mathbb{Z})$ — so the integral homology question is strictly harder than the Betti-number question.
- **2007.** Falk–Yuzvinsky's multinet theorem turns the degree-one resonance side fully combinatorial.
- **2013–2017.** Papadima–Suciu's modular resonance machinery gives combinatorial upper and lower bounds for $b_1(F)$ and proves equality in wide classes; Denham–Suciu construct arrangements with prescribed torsion via parallel connections.
- **2010s–2020s.** Computer algebra (Singular/Macaulay2, Dimca–Sticlaru) verifies combinatoriality for all known small line arrangements; no counterexample has emerged.

**Status:** open in general; solved for several structured families; no counterexample known.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **Eigenvalue 1, all $q$, all $\mathcal{A}$:** $H^q(F)_1 \cong H^q(U;\mathbb{C})$ is combinatorial (Orlik–Solomon algebra of the decone).
- **Generic arrangements** ($n$ hyperplanes in $\mathbb{C}^\ell$ in general position, $n \ge \ell$): $F$ is $(\ell-2)$-connected and all $b_q(F)$ are explicit binomial expressions in $n,\ell$ — Orlik–Randell conjecture, proved by Dimca–Papadima (2003).
- **Pencils** (all hyperplanes containing a common codimension-2 flat), $\ell = 2$: $b_1(F) = (n-1)^2$ — see §10.
- **Line arrangements with only double and triple points:** $b_1(F)$ is combinatorially determined; the only possible non-unit eigenvalues have order 3 and their multiplicity is read off from $(3,q)$-multinets / modular resonance mod 3 (Papadima–Suciu, *Proc. LMS* 114 (2017)).
- **Real line arrangements** with restricted multiplicities: Yoshinaga (2013) computes $b_1(F)$ from the combinatorics using minimality and the real structure.
- **Graphic arrangements** $\mathcal{A}_G \subset \mathbb{C}^\ell$: the algebraic monodromy on $H^1(F)$ is trivial, hence $b_1(F) = b_1(M)$ (Măcinic–Papadima, 2009).
- **Reflection arrangements:** monodromy on $H^1$ computed for all irreducible complex reflection groups (Măcinic–Papadima–Popescu, *Doc. Math.* 2016).
- **Arrangements with no dense edge of multiplicity divisible by a small prime:** Libgober's divisibility forces $h^*|_{H^1} = \mathrm{id}$.
- **Computational:** exhaustive checks over catalogued line arrangements with $n \le 13$ lines (including Rybnikov's lattice-isomorphic, non-homotopy-equivalent pair of 13 lines) find no discrepancy in $b_1(F)$.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Translated components.** $b_q(F)$ is a sum of local-system Betti numbers at the $n$-torsion points $\mathcal{L}_k$. The tangent cone theorem makes only the components of $\mathcal{V}^1_d$ through $\mathbf{1}$ combinatorial. Translated (positive-dimensional, non-origin) components exist — Suciu's deleted $B_3$ arrangement is the standard example — and no method is known to decide combinatorially whether a torsion point lies on one.
- **The Aomoto–de Rham comparison is only generic.** Esnault–Schechtman–Viehweg vanishing needs weights avoiding resonance; $\mathcal{L}_k$ has weights $k/n$ at every hyperplane, i.e. the most resonant possible choice. The Aomoto complex computes $H^*(U;\mathcal{L})$ only outside a bad locus that always contains the points we care about.
- **Characteristic-$p$ obstruction.** Papadima–Suciu's bounds pass through $H^1$ of the Aomoto complex over $\mathbb{F}_p$. The mod-$p$ and characteristic-zero resonance can differ, and the gap between the combinatorial upper bound and the true value is not known to vanish.
- **Failure of $\pi_1$-invariance.** Rybnikov's example shows $\pi_1(M)$ is not a lattice invariant, so any proof strategy routing through the fundamental group (braid monodromy, presentations) cannot be purely combinatorial.
- **Torsion pollutes integral statements.** Cohen–Denham–Suciu's torsion classes show the universal-coefficient bookkeeping is genuinely non-formal; over $\mathbb{Z}$ the analogous conjecture is false-flavoured and unproven.
- **No deformation argument.** Moduli of arrangements with fixed lattice are typically disconnected (again Rybnikov), so one cannot connect two realizations by a path of homeomorphic complements.

## 6. The Gap

Proven: $H^*(F)_1$ for all $\mathcal{A}$; all eigenvalues for generic, graphic, reflection, pencil, and double/triple-point line arrangements; combinatorial *bounds*

$$b_1(M) \;\le\; b_1(F) \;\le\; b_1(M) + \sum_{p \text{ prime}} \sum_{k \ge 1} (p^k - p^{k-1})\,\theta_{p}(\mathcal{A}),$$

with $\theta_p$ a modular resonance count that is combinatorial.

The gap is exactly: **does the upper bound above (or any combinatorial refinement of it) attain the true $b_1(F)$ for every arrangement?** Concretely, one must decide, from $L(\mathcal{A})$ alone, whether the torsion character $\mathcal{L}_k$ lies on a translated component of $\mathcal{V}^1_1(U)$ and with what depth. No mechanism links translated components to lattice data. For $q \ge 2$ even the bounds are missing.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Modular resonance / Aomoto over $\mathbb{F}_p$.** Continuation of the Papadima–Suciu program at Northeastern and IMAR Bucharest, extending exact computation of $b_1(F)$ beyond multiplicity 3 to arrangements with points of multiplicity 4 and 5 *(frontier — verify)*.
- **$D$-module and spectrum methods.** Budur–Saito-style computations of jumping coefficients, Bernstein–Sato roots and Hodge spectra of $Q(\mathcal{A})$ at Leuven and RIMS; the mixed Hodge structure on $H^*(F)$ constrains eigenvalue multiplicities.
- **Perverse-sheaf / Kashiwara–Malgrange approaches** to nearby cycles of $Q$, aiming to express $H^*(F)_{\lambda^k}$ as an Euler characteristic over $L(\mathcal{A})$.
- **Combinatorial classification of multinets and nets** (Bartz, Yuzvinsky, Torielli): finiteness results for $k$-multinets with $k \ge 5$ would close off whole eigenvalue ranges.
- **Large-scale computation.** Machine enumeration of line arrangements with $n \le 15$ and matroid-level databases, testing lattice-isomorphic pairs for differing $b_1(F)$ *(frontier — verify)*.
- **Milnor fibers of matroids.** Attempts to define $F$ for non-realizable matroids via Chow rings / augmented Chow rings after Adiprasito–Huh–Katz; a matroid-level invariant matching $b_q(F)$ would prove the conjecture.

## 8. Future Work

- Prove or refute that $H^1(U;\mathcal{L}_k) \ne 0$ for a torsion character implies $\mathcal{L}_k$ lies on a component of $\mathcal{V}^1_1$ through $\mathbf{1}$ — i.e. rule out torsion points on translated components.
- Extend the modular bound to a matching lower bound for arrangements with a point of multiplicity $\ge 4$.
- Develop a Hodge-theoretic obstruction: show the eigenvalue-$\lambda^k$ part carries a weight filtration whose graded pieces are lattice-determined.
- Settle the $q \ge 2$ case for $\ell = 3,4$, where nothing beyond the generic result is known.
- Build a matroid-theoretic surrogate of $F$ (a "combinatorial Milnor fiber") and prove a comparison theorem for realizable matroids.
- Search deliberately for a counterexample among arrangements admitting several essentially different realizations, following Rybnikov's construction.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**A pencil of $n$ lines in $\mathbb{C}^2$.** Take $\mathcal{A} = \{H_1,\dots,H_n\}$, $H_i = \ker(y - a_i x)$ with distinct $a_i$, so $Q = \prod_{i=1}^n (y - a_i x)$, homogeneous of degree $n$. The lattice $L(\mathcal{A})$ has $\hat{0}$, the $n$ atoms, and the single rank-2 element $\{0\}$; $\mu(\{0\}) = n-1$.

*Route 1 — singularity theory.* $Q$ is a homogeneous plane curve singularity with $n$ distinct branches; its Milnor number is $\mu = (n-1)^2$, and $F$ is connected and homotopy equivalent to a wedge of $\mu$ circles. Hence

$$b_0(F) = 1, \qquad b_1(F) = (n-1)^2 .$$

*Route 2 — local systems, the method the conjecture is about.* Decone: $U = \mathbb{P}(M) = \mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{n \text{ points}\} \simeq \mathbb{C} \setminus \{n-1 \text{ points}\}$, homotopy equivalent to a wedge of $n-1$ circles, so $\pi_1(U) = F_{n-1}$ and $\chi(U) = 2-n$.

- $k = 0$: $H^0(U;\mathbb{C}) = \mathbb{C}$, $H^1(U;\mathbb{C}) = \mathbb{C}^{n-1}$.
- $k \ne 0$: $\mathcal{L}_k$ is a nontrivial rank-one local system on a wedge of circles, so $H^0(U;\mathcal{L}_k) = 0$ and, since $\dim H^0 - \dim H^1 = \chi(U) = 2-n$, we get $\dim H^1(U;\mathcal{L}_k) = n-2$.

Summing over $k = 0,\dots,n-1$:

$$b_1(F) = (n-1) + (n-1)(n-2) = (n-1)^2 ,$$

matching Route 1. Every eigenvalue $\lambda^k$, $k \ne 0$, occurs with multiplicity $n-2$; the monodromy $h^*$ on $H^1(F)$ has characteristic polynomial $(t-1)^{n-1}\left(\frac{t^n-1}{t-1}\right)^{n-2}$.

**Why this case is easy and the general one is not.** Here $U$ is a curve, so $\dim H^1(U;\mathcal{L})$ is forced by the Euler characteristic — a lattice invariant — for *every* nontrivial $\mathcal{L}$. There are no translated components to worry about: $\mathcal{V}^1_1(U) = \mathrm{Hom}(F_{n-1},\mathbb{C}^*)$ minus a point.

**Contrast: the braid arrangement $\mathcal{A}_3$**, $Q = xyz(x-y)(y-z)(x-z)$, $n = 6$, $\ell = 3$. Projectively this is six lines with four triple points and three double points, and it supports a $(3,2)$-net. By Falk–Yuzvinsky the net produces a two-dimensional component of $\mathcal{R}^1_1(\mathcal{A})$, which by the tangent cone theorem lifts to a positive-dimensional component of $\mathcal{V}^1_1(U)$ through $\mathbf{1}$ containing the order-3 characters $\mathcal{L}_2, \mathcal{L}_4$. So $h^*$ on $H^1(F)$ has nontrivial cube-root-of-unity eigenvalues, and their multiplicity is computed from the net — combinatorially. The conjecture is the assertion that this bookkeeping never fails; the unresolved case is a torsion character sitting on a component of $\mathcal{V}^1_1(U)$ *not* passing through $\mathbf{1}$, where no combinatorial predictor exists.
