# Atiyah-Floer Conjecture

> **Topic:** Algebraic & Differential Geometry · **ID:** `03-geometry/atiyah-floer-conjecture` · **Status:** open

## 1. Problem Statement / Conjecture

Let $Y$ be a closed oriented integral homology $3$-sphere with a Heegaard splitting
$$Y = H_0 \cup_\Sigma H_1,$$
where $\Sigma$ is a closed surface of genus $g \ge 2$ and $H_0, H_1$ are handlebodies. Two Floer-type invariants are attached to this data:

- **Gauge-theoretic side.** $HF^{\mathrm{inst}}_*(Y)$, Floer's instanton homology: Morse homology of the Chern–Simons functional on the space of $SU(2)$-connections on $Y$, graded over $\mathbb{Z}/8$.
- **Symplectic side.** $HF^{\mathrm{symp}}_*\big(L_{H_0}, L_{H_1}\big)$, Lagrangian Floer homology inside the moduli space $\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)$ of flat $SU(2)$-connections on $\Sigma$, where $L_{H_i} \subset \mathcal{M}(\Sigma)$ is the subset of flat connections that extend over $H_i$.

**Conjecture (Atiyah 1988, after Floer).** There is an isomorphism of $\mathbb{Z}/8$-graded abelian groups
$$HF^{\mathrm{inst}}_*(Y) \;\cong\; HF^{\mathrm{symp}}_*\big(L_{H_0}, L_{H_1}\big),$$
natural in the sense that it is independent of the Heegaard splitting and compatible with the maps induced by $4$-dimensional cobordisms.

A complete proof must (i) give a definition of the right-hand side that is well posed despite the singularities of $\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)$ and $L_{H_i}$, (ii) prove invariance of that definition, and (iii) construct the isomorphism — expected to arise from an adiabatic degeneration of the anti-self-duality (ASD) equation on $\mathbb{R} \times Y$ to the Cauchy–Riemann equation on $\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)$. A disproof would exhibit a $Y$ and a splitting with non-isomorphic sides for every admissible regularisation.

## 2. Mathematical Foundations

**Chern–Simons functional.** For $G = SU(2)$ and the trivial bundle $P \to Y$, let $\mathcal{A}(Y) = \Omega^1(Y; \mathfrak{su}(2))$ and
$$CS(a) \;=\; \frac{1}{8\pi^2}\int_Y \operatorname{tr}\!\left(a \wedge da + \tfrac{2}{3}\, a \wedge a \wedge a\right).$$
$CS$ descends to $\mathcal{A}(Y)/\mathcal{G}$ modulo $\mathbb{Z}$, and $\nabla CS(a) = \tfrac{1}{4\pi^2} \ast F_a$, so critical points are flat connections, i.e. conjugacy classes of representations $\rho: \pi_1(Y) \to SU(2)$. Since $Y$ is a homology sphere, the only reducible flat connection is the trivial one, and it is isolated. Gradient flow lines are ASD connections on $\mathbb{R}\times Y$:
$$\partial_s a + \ast F_a = 0, \qquad F_A^+ = 0 .$$
$HF^{\mathrm{inst}}_*(Y)$ is generated by irreducible flat connections, graded by the mod-$8$ spectral flow of the odd signature operator, with differential counting index-$1$ ASD trajectories (Floer 1988).

**Moduli space of flat connections on $\Sigma$.** $\mathcal{M}(\Sigma) = \operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1\Sigma, SU(2))/SU(2)$ carries the Atiyah–Bott symplectic form
$$\omega(\alpha,\beta) = -\frac{1}{4\pi^2}\int_\Sigma \operatorname{tr}(\alpha \wedge \beta), \qquad \alpha,\beta \in H^1(\Sigma;\mathfrak{su}(2)).$$
By Narasimhan–Seshadri it is homeomorphic to the moduli space of semistable rank-$2$ holomorphic bundles with trivial determinant, of complex dimension $3g-3$; it is **singular** along reducibles. The handlebody loci
$$L_{H_i} = \{\, \rho \in \mathcal{M}(\Sigma) : \rho \text{ extends to } \pi_1 H_i \,\} \cong \operatorname{Hom}(F_g, SU(2))/SU(2)$$
are singular Lagrangian subvarieties of dimension $3g-3$.

**Twisted (admissible) version.** Replace $\Sigma$ by $\Sigma$ with a marked point and impose holonomy $-\mathbb{1}$ around it — equivalently use a nontrivial $SO(3)$-bundle with $w_2 \ne 0$. The resulting
$$\mathcal{M}^{tw}(\Sigma) = \{ (A_i,B_i) : \textstyle\prod_i [A_i,B_i] = -\mathbb{1} \}/SU(2)$$
is a **smooth** monotone symplectic manifold of dimension $6g-6$, simply connected with minimal Chern number $N=2$. This is the setting in which both sides are unambiguously defined, and it is the form in which the conjecture has actually been proved in cases.

**Adiabatic limit.** Equip $Y_\varepsilon$ (or a mapping torus) with metrics scaling $\Sigma$ by $\varepsilon$. The heuristic is that as $\varepsilon \to 0$, ASD connections converge to holomorphic strips $u:\mathbb{R}\times[0,1] \to \mathcal{M}(\Sigma)$ with $u(\cdot,i) \subset L_{H_i}$, giving a chain-level identification of the two complexes.

## 3. History & State of the Art (SOTA)

- **1988.** Floer defines instanton homology for homology spheres and Lagrangian intersection Floer homology in the same year. Atiyah, in *New invariants of 3- and 4-dimensional manifolds* (Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. 48), proposes the identification, motivated by the correspondence between Yang–Mills on $\mathbb{R}\times Y$ and the sigma model into $\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)$.
- **1994.** Dostoglou–Salamon prove the mapping-torus analogue by a full adiabatic-limit analysis — the first and still the deepest verified case.
- **2005–2008.** Wehrheim develops elliptic theory and bubbling analysis for ASD instantons with Lagrangian boundary conditions; Salamon–Wehrheim construct instanton Floer homology for $3$-manifolds with boundary and Lagrangian boundary condition, providing an intermediate invariant designed to bridge the two sides.
- **2010–2012.** Wehrheim–Woodward's quilted Floer theory yields a *quilted Atiyah–Floer conjecture*, splitting the problem across a Heegaard-type decomposition into elementary cobordisms; Manolescu–Woodward define $HSI(Y)$ on an *extended* moduli space, resolving the singularity by adding a $\mathfrak{su}(2)^*$ factor.
- **2017–2024.** Daemi–Fukaya give a precise formulation and a strategy using $A_\infty$ / Fukaya-categorical methods; Daemi–Fukaya–Lipyanskiy develop the "mixed equation" interpolating between ASD and Cauchy–Riemann and establish the required compactness and gluing in the $SO(3)$ setting.

Computationally, instanton homology is known for large families (Seifert fibered spaces, surgeries on torus knots), whereas symplectic-side computations remain sparse — a persistent asymmetry.

## 4. Partial Results / Verified Cases

- **Mapping tori (proved).** Dostoglou–Salamon, *Ann. of Math.* 139 (1994): for $\Sigma$ closed of genus $g \ge 2$, $\phi \in \operatorname{Diff}^+(\Sigma)$, and the mapping torus $Y_\phi$ with a nontrivial $SO(3)$-bundle, $HF^{\mathrm{inst}}_*(Y_\phi) \cong HF^{\mathrm{symp}}_*(\phi_*, \mathcal{M}^{tw}(\Sigma))$, the symplectic Floer homology of the induced symplectomorphism. Both sides are $\mathbb{Z}/8$-graded; the proof is an $\varepsilon\to 0$ degeneration with uniform elliptic estimates.
- **Analytic foundation for splittings (partial).** Salamon–Wehrheim (*Geom. Topol.* 12, 2008) define $HF^{\mathrm{inst}}(H, L)$ for a handlebody with Lagrangian boundary condition; Wehrheim's two papers (2005) supply the elliptic theory and rule out boundary bubbling for the relevant monotone targets.
- **Quilted reformulation (partial).** Wehrheim–Woodward prove that quilted Floer homology of the associated Lagrangian correspondences is independent of the Heegaard splitting, so the symplectic side is a $3$-manifold invariant in the twisted setting; Duncan established the compactness needed for the quilted version.
- **Mixed equation (partial→strong).** Daemi–Fukaya–Lipyanskiy (2021–2024) prove Fredholm, compactness, and gluing results for the mixed ASD/holomorphic equation and deduce an Atiyah–Floer-type isomorphism for $SO(3)$-bundles with $w_2 \neq 0$ restricted nontrivially to $\Sigma$. *(frontier — verify final published scope.)*
- **Genus bounds.** The twisted results require $g \ge 2$; $g = 1$ degenerates (the twisted moduli space is a point) and carries no information.
- **Related identifications.** Abouzaid–Manolescu's sheaf-theoretic $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ Floer homology satisfies the analogous Atiyah–Floer statement in the complex-group setting, and Kronheimer–Mrowka's instanton knot homology has verified pillowcase (traceless character variety) analogues for two-bridge knots.

## 5. Principal Obstacles

- **Singularities of $\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)$ and $L_{H_i}$.** For the *untwisted* $SU(2)$ theory, reducible representations make $\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)$ a singular variety and $L_{H_i}$ a singular Lagrangian. Standard Lagrangian Floer theory requires smooth, embedded, monotone or exact Lagrangians; none of the hypotheses hold. There is no accepted definition of the right-hand side in the original $SU(2)$ formulation.
- **Non-transversality of the trivial connection.** On the instanton side the trivial flat connection is excluded by the homology-sphere condition, but on the symplectic side its analogue lies exactly at the singular stratum where $L_{H_0} \cap L_{H_1}$ is worst behaved. Holonomy perturbations improve transversality only away from that stratum.
- **Failure of naive adiabatic limits.** Instanton bubbling at $\varepsilon \to 0$ produces energy concentration at points of $\Sigma$ that does not match sphere bubbling in $\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)$; the exchange of bubble types is only controlled by delicate annulus/neck estimates, which Dostoglou–Salamon achieve for mapping tori but not for arbitrary splittings, where the ends of the handlebodies add non-product geometry.
- **Boundary/corner analysis.** The quilted and mixed-equation approaches replace one hard limit with a boundary-value problem whose linearised operator is not of standard elliptic boundary type; establishing Fredholm and gluing near corners is the bulk of the technical work.
- **Absence of algebraic shortcuts.** No purely topological axiomatisation (à la a TQFT uniqueness theorem) is known that would force the two theories to agree without constructing the map, since both sides are only partially computed.

## 6. The Gap

The proven statements are all in the **twisted/admissible** category ($w_2 \neq 0$ on $\Sigma$, smooth $\mathcal{M}^{tw}$, monotone Lagrangians), and predominantly for **mapping tori** or for **reformulated** invariants (Salamon–Wehrheim, quilted, extended moduli space) whose equivalence with classical Floer instanton homology of a homology $3$-sphere is itself conjectural. The general statement of Section 1 concerns the **untwisted $SU(2)$** theory on a homology sphere, where $w_2 = 0$ forces reducibles and the symplectic side has no definition.

The precise missing steps are:
1. A construction of $HF^{\mathrm{symp}}_*(L_{H_0}, L_{H_1})$ for singular $L_{H_i}$ — e.g. via virtual/Kuranishi structures, a resolution of $\mathcal{M}(\Sigma)$, or an extended moduli space — proved independent of all choices.
2. A proof that the intermediate invariants (Salamon–Wehrheim, $HSI$, quilted) agree with $HF^{\mathrm{inst}}_*(Y)$; presently only maps in one direction, or isomorphisms after twisting, are available.
3. Naturality: compatibility with cobordism maps and hence with the $4$-manifold Donaldson theory that motivated the conjecture.

## 7. Current Research (as of June 2026)

- **Daemi–Fukaya–Lipyanskiy programme.** The mixed equation $\;\bar\partial_J u + \text{(ASD on the neck)}\;$ is now equipped with compactness and gluing in the $SO(3)$ setting; ongoing work targets removal of the admissibility hypothesis by inserting singular Lagrangians into Fukaya $A_\infty$-categories with Kuranishi structures. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Fukaya's $SO(3)$-Floer homology of $3$-manifolds with boundary**, an $A_\infty$-category-valued invariant of surfaces, is being used to reformulate Atiyah–Floer as a statement about a functor from a $3$-cobordism category to Fukaya categories. *(frontier — verify)*
- **Pillowcase / traceless character variety school** (Herald, Kirk, Hedden, Cazassus, Kotelskiy): computes the symplectic side for tangle decompositions and matches it against Kronheimer–Mrowka instanton knot homology; this is the most computationally productive strand.
- **Sheaf-theoretic and complexified models** (Abouzaid, Manolescu, and successors) test the conjecture in the $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ and $\mathcal{P}$-framed settings, where singularities can be handled by perverse-sheaf technology.
- Active groups: Washington University in St. Louis (Daemi), Simons Center/Stony Brook and Tsinghua (Fukaya), Rutgers (Woodward), UCLA/Stanford (Manolescu), Indiana (Kirk), ETH Zürich (Salamon's school).

## 8. Future Work

- Construct Floer homology for the **singular** Lagrangians $L_{H_i}$ using virtual fundamental chains, and show the outcome is a Heegaard-splitting invariant.
- Prove the **equivalence of intermediate theories**: $HF^{\mathrm{inst}}(Y) \cong HF^{SW}(H_0, L_{H_1}) \cong HF^{\mathrm{quilt}}$.
- Extend the isomorphism to **cobordism-functoriality**, delivering the original goal of computing Donaldson invariants by cut-and-paste.
- Develop **computable models** on the symplectic side (e.g. bordered/pillowcase algebras) so both sides can be evaluated on infinite families and compared numerically.
- Transport the statement to other structure groups ($SU(n)$, $PU(n)$) and to sutured/knot versions, where singular strata may be avoidable.

## 10. Worked Example / Concrete Special Case

**The Poincaré homology sphere $Y = \Sigma(2,3,5) = S^3/I^*$.**

*Instanton side (computed).* $\pi_1(Y) = I^*$, the binary icosahedral group of order $120$. Its irreducible $2$-dimensional complex representations number exactly two, both unitary and hence valued in $SU(2)$; call them $\rho_1, \rho_2$. Every non-trivial $SU(2)$-representation of $I^*$ is irreducible, so the flat connection space is
$$\mathcal{R}(Y) = \{\theta\} \sqcup \{[\rho_1],[\rho_2]\},$$
with $\theta$ the trivial (reducible) connection, discarded. Both $[\rho_i]$ are non-degenerate critical points of $CS$, with Chern–Simons invariants $\tfrac{1}{120}$ and $\tfrac{49}{120}$ mod $1$. Fintushel–Stern's spectral-flow computation for $\Sigma(p,q,r)$ gives gradings $1$ and $5$ mod $8$. Since the two generators sit in gradings of the same parity difference $4 \neq \pm 1$, the differential vanishes for degree reasons:
$$HF^{\mathrm{inst}}_k(\Sigma(2,3,5)) = \begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}, & k = 1,\,5 \\ 0, & \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$
Total rank $2$; the Casson invariant is $\lambda = \tfrac12 \chi\big(HF^{\mathrm{inst}}\big) \cdot (-1)$-normalised $= -1$, consistent.

*Symplectic side (prediction).* $\Sigma(2,3,5)$ has Heegaard genus $2$. Take $Y = H_0 \cup_{\Sigma_2} H_1$. Then $\mathcal{M}(\Sigma_2)$ has dimension $3g-3 = 3$ (complex $3$, real $6$ — for $SU(2)$ and $g=2$, $\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{M} = 6g-6 = 6$), and $L_{H_0}, L_{H_1}$ are $3$-dimensional. In the twisted model $\mathcal{M}^{tw}(\Sigma_2)$ is smooth: it is the intersection of two quadrics in $\mathbb{CP}^5$, a Fano $3$-fold with $b_2=1$ and minimal Chern number $N=2$. The conjecture predicts
$$\operatorname{rank} HF^{\mathrm{symp}}_*(L_{H_0}, L_{H_1}) = 2,$$
with intersection points in relative gradings $1$ and $5$ mod $8$. Concretely, the two intersection points must correspond bijectively to $\rho_1, \rho_2$ under restriction $\pi_1\Sigma_2 \twoheadrightarrow$ image in $\pi_1 Y$: a representation of $\pi_1 Y$ restricts to $\Sigma_2$ and lands in $L_{H_0} \cap L_{H_1}$, since it extends over both handlebodies. Van Kampen gives $\pi_1 Y = \pi_1 H_0 \ast_{\pi_1 \Sigma} \pi_1 H_1$, so
$$L_{H_0} \cap L_{H_1} \;=\; \operatorname{Hom}(\pi_1 Y, SU(2))/SU(2) \;=\; \{\theta, [\rho_1], [\rho_2]\}.$$
This is exactly the *chain-level* statement: the generators match on the nose. The unresolved content is analytic — that the trivial point $\theta$ can be excised consistently (it is the singular point of both Lagrangians), and that the counts of holomorphic strips equal the counts of ASD trajectories. Here both differentials vanish for grading reasons, so the isomorphism holds once the symplectic side is *defined*; for general $Y$ neither the excision nor the differential-matching is known.
